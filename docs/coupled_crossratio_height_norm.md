# A simultaneous height norm for all projective cross-ratio units

## Status

The balanced core profile determines more than the height of each
individual cross-ratio. It determines, with one uniform relative error,
the height of **every product and quotient of cross-ratios**, including
ones with arbitrarily large integer exponents. The limiting height is
an explicit norm on an integer lattice of rank `k(k-3)/2`.

In particular the evaluated projective units attain their full possible
multiplicative rank. There are no accidental multiplicative relations
of any coefficient size in a sufficiently accurate extracted tuple.

This is a new simultaneous height identity, not a residue-growth theorem.
The additive relations of `M_0,k`, including the Plucker exchanges,
remain compatible with this norm. Section 5 identifies the part of
those relations that the height data already enforce and the additional
arithmetic input still missing.

## 1. The invariant edge-weight lattice

Take `k>=4` distinct common-unit circle points labelled `0,...,k-1`.
For an integer edge vector `c=(c_ij)_(i<j)`, require

```text
sum_(j!=i) c_ij=0          for every row i.       (1)
```

Here `c_ji=c_ij` when convenient. Denote this lattice by `L_k`.
Equation (1) implies `sum_(i<j)c_ij=0`. Define

```text
Lambda_c=product_(i<j)(z_i-z_j)^c_ij.             (2)
```

These quantities are invariant under a common fractional-linear change
of the complex radial coordinate. Indeed every difference acquires one
common determinant factor and one denominator factor at each endpoint;
their exponents all cancel by (1). The complex radial coordinate is
fractional-linear in the rational half-angle parameter, so the same
assertion applies to the rational projective changes in the earlier
notes.

The signless vertex-edge incidence matrix of the complete graph has
rank `k` over the rationals: a vector annihilating its columns would
satisfy `u_i+u_j=0` for every edge, and a triangle forces all `u_i=0`.
Consequently

```text
rank L_k=binom(k,2)-k=k(k-3)/2.                  (3)
```

This is exactly the lattice generated by quadruple cross-ratios.
For an elementary integral spanning argument, fix vertices zero and
one. Four-cycle vectors eliminate every edge between the other
vertices. The remaining support is on edges `01,0i,1i`. Vertex balance
forces `c_0i=-c_1i`, `c_01=0`, and `sum_i c_0i=0`. The remaining
vector is a sum of four-cycles `0,i,1,j,0`. Thus no passage from integer
to rational coefficients is needed.

## 2. An exact rational conductor factorization

Use the original Gaussian allocations and common unit

```text
z_i=epsilon product_p pi_p^a_ip bar(pi_p)^(e_p-a_ip).
```

The primitive chord formula gives, with one consistent signed-residue
convention,

```text
Lambda_c=product_p p^b_p(c) product_(i<j)t_ij^c_ij,
b_p(c)=sum_(i<j)c_ij min(a_ip,a_jp).             (4)
```

To check rationality directly, the exponent of `pi_p` from the chord
gcds is the displayed `b_p(c)`. The exponent of `bar(pi_p)` is
`sum c_ij(e_p-max(a_ip,a_jp))`. Use
`max(a_i,a_j)=a_i+a_j-min(a_i,a_j)` and the zero row degrees to
obtain the same exponent. The factors `2i epsilon` cancel because
`sum c_ij=0`. Formula (4) is exact for arbitrary prime powers and
retains the signs of every residue.

Now use central truncation from
[endpoint_central_truncation.md](endpoint_central_truncation.md):

```text
a_ip=b'_ip+c'_ip,
b'_ip in {0,e'_p},       0<=c'_ip<=2r_p,
e'_p=e_p-2r_p,
s=sum_p r_p log p.
```

Index the mutually coprime core blocks by the side `S` not containing
the anchor zero. Put

```text
C_S(c)=sum_(i<j, i,j in S)c_ij.                  (5)
```

Vertex balance implies `C_S(c)=C_(S complement)(c)`, so this does
not depend on the chosen side of an unoriented cut. At a prime whose
core block is `H_S`, the core version of `b_p(c)` is exactly
`e'_p C_S(c)`.

For each edge,

```text
0<=min(a_ip,a_jp)-min(b'_ip,b'_jp)<=2r_p.
```

Let `||c||_1=sum_(i<j)|c_ij|`, and suppose all nonzero primitive
residues satisfy `|t_ij|<=T`, `T>=1`. Formula (4) therefore becomes

```text
Lambda_c = U_c R_c,
U_c=product_S N(H_S)^C_S(c),
h(R_c)<=(2s+log T)||c||_1.                      (6)
```

Here `h(n/q)=log max(|n|,q)` for a reduced rational number with
positive denominator. The correction estimate charges the sum of
the absolute prime-valuation errors and the absolute residue exponents.
It does not presume that these corrections avoid the core primes.

## 3. The complete cut norm

Assume the core block weights lie in a common window

```text
(1-eta)w<=log N(H_S)<=(1+eta)w,
0<=eta<1.                                      (7)
```

Include the empty cut, whose coefficient is zero. Since the reference
vertex is excluded from `S`, each nonreference edge belongs to one
quarter of all cuts. The zero degrees imply that the sum of these
edge coefficients is zero. Consequently

```text
sum_S C_S(c)=0.
```

Define

```text
ell(c)=(1/2)sum_S |C_S(c)|
      =sum_(C_S>0) C_S
      =sum_(C_S<0) (-C_S).                      (8)
```

The norms of distinct core blocks are coprime rational integers.
There is therefore no cancellation between positive and negative
terms in `U_c`. Each of its reduced numerator and denominator has
logarithmic size between `(1-eta)w ell(c)` and
`(1+eta)w ell(c)`. The elementary rational-height inequalities
`h(xy)<=h(x)+h(y)` and `h(x)<=h(xy)+h(y)` now prove

```text
(1-eta)w ell(c)-(2s+log T)||c||_1
 <= h(Lambda_c)
 <= (1+eta)w ell(c)+(2s+log T)||c||_1.           (9)
```

This single estimate applies to every `c in L_k`, with no restriction
on its coefficient sizes.

The function `ell` is a genuine norm on the real zero-degree edge
space. For a nonreference pair, the two-element cut gives
`C_{i,j}(c)=c_ij`. Moreover,

```text
sum_(i!=0)|c_0i|
 <=2 sum_(i<j, i,j!=0)|c_ij|.
```

It follows that

```text
||c||_1 <=3 sum_(i<j, i,j!=0)|c_ij|
         <=3 sum_S |C_S(c)|=6ell(c).            (10)
```

Thus, with

```text
delta=eta+6(2s+log T)/w,
```

equation (9) gives the uniform comparison

```text
(1-delta)w ell(c)<=h(Lambda_c)<=(1+delta)w ell(c)
                    for every c in L_k.        (11)
```

In the growing central reduction, `eta ->0` and
`s+log T=o(w)`. Hence `delta ->0`, uniformly over the whole
integer lattice, including vectors depending on the original radius.

For a single quadruple cross-ratio, exactly two cut types have
coefficient `+1` or `-1`. On four rows `ell(c)=1` and `w=W/8+o(W)`;
padding with more rows gives the same leading `W/8` height. Thus
(9) extends, rather than replaces, the earlier individual calculation.

## 4. No accidental multiplicative relations

If `delta<1`, (11) implies `h(Lambda_c)>0` for every nonzero
`c in L_k`. In particular `Lambda_c` is neither one nor minus one.
The evaluation homomorphism

```text
L_k -> Q^*/{1,-1},       c -> Lambda_c
```

is injective. The subgroup generated by the evaluated cross-ratios
therefore has rank exactly `k(k-3)/2`.

This conclusion concerns **multiplicative** relations only. It does
not say that the cross-ratios are algebraically independent. Their
many prescribed additive equations remain exact. Full multiplicative
rank alone is consistent with such equations: two rational numbers
can be multiplicatively independent and still have sum one.

## 5. What the additive exchanges still require

Consider the Plucker exchange

```text
lambda=(z_0-z_1)(z_2-z_3)/((z_0-z_2)(z_1-z_3)),
mu=(z_0-z_3)(z_1-z_2)/((z_0-z_2)(z_1-z_3)),
lambda+mu=1.                                  (12)
```

At its three two-against-two cut types, the conductor orders of
`(lambda,mu,1)` are respectively

```text
01|23:  ( 1, 0,0),
02|13:  (-1,-1,0),
03|12:  ( 0, 1,0).                             (13)
```

At every other cut all three orders are zero. In each case the
smallest order occurs at least twice, exactly as required by an
additive identity in a valued field. The denominator cancellation
at the second cut is real; counting its two denominator contributions
as separate restrictions would be incorrect.

The same compatibility holds for every universal rational-function
identity among projective units on `M_0,k`.
To see it without invoking tropical geometry, take a formal rational
configuration with rows in `S` of the form `t+epsilon u_i`, and all
other rows fixed and distinct. An edge difference has order one if
both endpoints are in `S`, and order zero otherwise. Hence `Lambda_c`
has order `C_S(c)`. In any valid sum of such units, the smallest order
must be attained by at least two summands. This applies to every cut
independently and therefore to the complete balanced cut profile used
in (9).

This formal specialization does not apply to a further additive relation
that happens to hold only at one evaluated arithmetic configuration.
Such additional relations remain possible sources of information.

Thus the leading valuation constraints from the additive relations
are already compatible with the entire simultaneous height norm.
They do not supply an extra positive power of the core scale. What
remains to be proved is an arithmetic lower bound on the corrections
`R_c`, or on the original primitive residues, that exploits the
simultaneous **unit values after these valuation cancellations**.
The norm comparison itself gives no such bound.

The exact CRT construction in
[plucker_compatibility_route.md](plucker_compatibility_route.md) realizes
all the rational algebraic identities and prescribed collision cuts,
but pays large residues. It therefore illustrates the same unresolved
distinction; it is not an endpoint counterexample. No small-residue
construction with the full balanced Gaussian profile has been supplied
here, and the radius-independent point bound remains unproved.
