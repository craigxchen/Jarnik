# A limitation of polynomial elimination in the Plucker system

The aim here was to use all rank-two compatibility equations, rather
than one four-point relation at a time, on a nearly uniform extracted
prime profile. The result is a precise barrier for one broad class of
such arguments. It does **not** prove that all arithmetic uses of the
Plucker system fail, and it does not construct an endpoint lattice
counterexample.

The existing small-cofactor system is in Section 6.8 of
`codex_uniform_bound_research.md`. The new cross-ratio height estimates
are in Section 7 of `uniformity_audit_parallel.md`. The present note
does not replace their missing simultaneous Gaussian arithmetic by an
assumption about unit equations or number-field uniformity.

## 1. The polynomial collision-order theorem

Let `F` be a multihomogeneous polynomial in the rank-two brackets

```text
[ij] = X_i Y_j-X_j Y_i,       1<=i,j<=m,
```

homogeneous of bracket degree `B`. Assume `F` is nonzero as a
function of the `2 by m` matrix; a Plucker relation that is identically
zero is excluded. Write its row degrees as `e_i`, so
`sum_i e_i=2B`.

Dehomogenize by setting `(X_i,Y_i)=(1,t_i)`. This gives a nonzero
translation-invariant homogeneous polynomial

```text
f(t_1,...,t_m)=F(1,t_1,...,1,t_m)
```

of total degree `B`. For a subset `S` of the row labels, let `nu_S`
be its order of vanishing along the small diagonal

```text
t_i=t_j for all i,j in S.
```

Equivalently, if `I_S` is the ideal generated by these differences,
`nu_S` is the largest integer with `f in I_S^(nu_S)`. Put
`nu_S=0` when `|S|<=1`.

Then

```text
(1/2^m) sum_(S subset [m]) nu_S <= B/4.                (1)
```

Products of brackets attain equality. Consequently no polynomial
combination of minors can increase the *average* prescribed diagonal
vanishing order beyond that of a product of `B` individual minors.
An improved order at some subsets must be paid for elsewhere.

### Proof by multiplicities on a binary cube

At the binary vertex `1_S`, the coordinates in `S` are equal to one
and those in its complement are equal to zero. The ideals `I_S` and
`I_(S^c)` are generated by disjoint sets of linear difference variables
after choosing one anchor in each nonempty group. Their power
intersection equals their power product. Hence

```text
mult_(1_S)(f) >= nu_S+nu_(S^c).                       (2)
```

The same statement holds when one group is empty, using its zero order.
For a nonzero polynomial `g` of total degree at most `d`,

```text
sum_(a in {0,1}^m) mult_a(g) <= d 2^(m-1).            (3)
```

Here is an elementary proof of (3), included to make the argument
self-contained. Induct on the number of variables. Write the degree in
the last variable as `t`, with leading coefficient `h` of total degree
at most `d-t`. At a point `a` of the first `m-1` coordinates, put
`s=mult_a(h)`. Choose a partial derivative in those coordinates of
total order `s` for which the differentiated `h` does not vanish at
`a`. Applying the same derivative to `g` and specializing at `a` gives
a nonzero univariate polynomial of degree `t`. At each `b in {0,1}`,
its multiplicity is at least `mult_(a,b)(g)-s`. Therefore

```text
sum_(b in {0,1}) mult_(a,b)(g) <= 2s+t.
```

Summing and using the induction hypothesis gives (3).

Sum (2) over all `S` and apply (3) with `d=B`. The left side is
twice the sum of the `nu_S`, which proves (1).

For a bracket monomial `product [ij]^(b_ij)`, its diagonal order is
`nu_S=sum_(i,j in S) b_ij`. A uniformly random subset contains both
ends of any fixed edge with probability `1/4`, proving equality in
(1).

## 2. Exactly which Gaussian coordinates the theorem applies to

Use the half-angle products from Section 6.8 of the earlier note.
Index Gaussian blocks by subsets `S` of `m` nonreference rows. Assume
the blocks `H_S` are pairwise coprime and coprime to all their
conjugates. Empty blocks can be units; a nonunit empty block is a
common factor in the original circle configuration. Put

```text
G=product_S H_S,
z_0=conj(G),
A_j=product_(S containing j) H_S,
z_j=z_0 A_j/conj(A_j).
```

All `z_j` are Gaussian integers of radius
`R=|G|`. Write `A_j=X_j+iY_j`; these are the rank-two rows in
Section 1. They are **half-angle Gaussian products**, not the radial
coordinate rows `(Re z_j,Im z_j)`. No rational projective
transformation sending three points to `0,1,infinity` is being made.

If `H_S` divides `A_j`, then at a prime factor of `H_S` the
projective row `[X_j:Y_j]` is the isotropic direction `[-i:1]`.
For the conjugate prime it is `[i:1]`. All conjugate partners are
units, by the block coprimality assumptions. Thus the order `nu_S`
forces

```text
N(H_S)^(nu_S) divides F((X_j,Y_j)_j).                 (4)
```

For precision, this can be checked using the local coordinates
`A_j,conj(A_j)` instead of `X_j,Y_j`. At a prime of `H_S`, every
`conj(A_j)` is a unit and `A_j/conj(A_j)` vanishes for `j in S`.
The bracket identity

```text
[ij] = (conj(A_i) A_j-A_i conj(A_j))/(2i)
```

reduces (4) to membership in the small-diagonal ideal power. The
denominator `2i` is a unit at these primes: conjugate-coprimality
excludes the ramified prime `1+i`. The conjugate argument gives the
other half of the norm factor. Blocks may be powers of distinct primes;
the same proof multiplies the vanishing order by the block valuation.

There is no claim that the complementary rows also collide at this
prime. The two complementary collision groups in (2) are only an
auxiliary polynomial argument over characteristic zero, bounding the
sum of the *one-group* orders used in (4).

## 3. The endpoint exponents agree exactly

Let

```text
W=log(R^2),
w_S=log N(H_S).
```

At the fully uniform inherited profile, `w_S=W/2^m` for every
subset, including the empty subset. Thus

```text
log|A_j|=W/4.
```

If the circle points occupy an arc of angular width
`Delta<=C/sqrt(R)`, their half-angle directions, after selecting
signs, occupy an interval of width at most `Delta/2`. Every minor
therefore satisfies

```text
|[ij]| <= |A_i A_j| Delta/2.
```

By multihomogeneity and the row degrees, for a constant depending only
on the fixed polynomial and on `C`,

```text
log|F(A)| <= BW/4+O_(F,C)(1),                         (5)
```

whenever `F(A)` is nonzero. On the other hand, the guaranteed
divisor in (4) has logarithm

```text
sum_S w_S nu_S = W average_S(nu_S) <= BW/4.           (6)
```

So the best possible average prescribed divisibility equals the
archimedean endpoint exponent. Products of minors already attain
this equality. Taking increasingly elaborate fixed polynomial
combinations of Plucker relations does not create a positive multiple
of `W` beyond (5) using these collision orders alone.

This explains a specific obstruction to the requested fixed-neighborhood
exclusion. The argument would need a strictly positive logarithmic
margin that persists for small relative profile errors. No such margin
is available from the prescribed diagonal orders of any one nonzero
bracket polynomial, or from any fixed finite family of them.

This is an exponent statement. At an *exact* uniform profile, a small
constant in (5) can still force a particular value to vanish. It does
not follow that the same constant argument excludes a fixed relative
neighborhood when the error multiplied by `W` is unbounded. Also,
unexpected extra prime divisibility of a particular value, beyond the
generic `nu_S`, is not ruled out by (1); exploiting such simultaneous
residue arithmetic is precisely outside this barrier.

## 4. Exact rational compatibility by CRT, with the size failure retained

There is also an elementary way to make every Plucker equation hold
while prescribing all the one-cluster valuation patterns. It illustrates
why the small residue bounds must remain an explicit part of the problem.

Fix `m` and choose a distinct odd prime `p_S>m` for each subset
`S` of `[m]`. For a large real parameter `T`, put

```text
a_S=floor(T/log p_S),
Q=product_S p_S^(a_S+1).
```

For each row `j`, prescribe modulo `p_S^(a_S+1)`

```text
t_j = p_S^(a_S) j       if j in S,
t_j = j                if j not in S.
```

For large `T`, these prescriptions give exactly

```text
v_(p_S)(t_i-t_j) = a_S  if i,j are both in S,
v_(p_S)(t_i-t_j) = 0    otherwise.
```

The Chinese remainder theorem gives integer representatives `r_j`
in `[0,Q)`. Set `t_j=r_j+2Qj`. These are distinct, increasing, and
their pair differences have magnitude between `Q` and `(2m+1)Q`.
The rank-two rows `(1,t_j)` then give an actual rational projective
configuration. All Plucker and cross-ratio identities hold exactly,
and all the indicated valuations are exact. Furthermore

```text
a_S log p_S = T+O_m(1),
```

so the prescribed pattern weights approach the fully uniform profile.

But the quotient sizes fail the endpoint requirement. With

```text
E_ij=product_(S containing i,j) p_S^(a_S),
u_ij=(t_i-t_j)/E_ij,
```

one has

```text
log|u_ij| = (3/4+o(1)) log Q.
```

The factor `3/4` comes from the fact that one quarter of all subsets
contain a specified pair. These residues are large, in contrast with
the `exp(O(eta W))` residues demanded by the extracted endpoint
problem.

Thus CRT is an exact compatibility construction, **not** a construction
with all the required small residues, and certainly not a short-arc
lattice counterexample. It separates the algebraic equations and
prescribed valuations from the unresolved archimedean size condition.

The same construction can retain genuine Gaussian prime prescriptions.
Choose the fixed primes to be split primes `p_S=1 mod 4`, all larger
than `2m+5`, and choose a Gaussian prime `pi_S` above each. Let
`iota_S` be the image of `i` modulo `pi_S^(a_S+1)`, regarded as an
integer modulo `p_S^(a_S+1)`. Replace the inside-class residue by

```text
t_j = iota_S+p_S^(a_S)j          if j in S.
```

For the outside rows use distinct residues modulo `p_S` avoiding
`iota_S` and `-iota_S`, and lift them arbitrarily. Then the Gaussian
rows `U_j=1+i t_j` have valuation exactly `a_S` at `pi_S` for
`j in S`, valuation zero there otherwise, and valuation zero at
`conj(pi_S)` for every row. All the prescribed minor valuations
remain exactly as above. Thus this is actual Gaussian factor
compatibility, not merely formal prime labels.

The size loss remains visible: `|U_j|` has logarithm `log Q+O_m(1)`,
whereas the prescribed Gaussian product in row `j` has logarithm
`(1/4+o(1)) log Q`. The unprescribed Gaussian quotient in each row
therefore has logarithm `(3/4+o(1)) log Q`. It cannot be discarded
or counted as a subpower residue. A common circle realization can be
obtained by the exact lcm/gcd formula in `fresh_algebraic_parallel.md`,
but neither its full prime profile nor its endpoint arc bound has
been established by this construction.

As an exact arithmetic check of the construction, three rows with all
eight subsets, split primes `13,17,29,37,41,53,61,73`, and parameters
`T=10,20` satisfied every prescribed Gaussian norm valuation and every
minor valuation. Their CRT moduli had 40 and 76 decimal digits. These
checks supplement the construction proof; they do not test the missing
endpoint estimate.

## 5. Scope of the conclusion

The new proved statement is (1), together with its precise Gaussian
application (4)--(6). It rules out a fixed positive exponent gain from
generic diagonal divisibility of bracket-polynomial values alone.

It does not rule out using the Hermitian rank-one metric lift, special
cancellations in the integer residues, changing auxiliary coefficients
with a controlled height argument, or an arithmetic theorem exploiting
several values simultaneously. The exact CRT construction does not
supply the small residues. No height-independent bound on the number
of circle points has been proved here.

The primary agent subsequently extended the cube-multiplicity method
to arbitrary row-multihomogeneous forms in the half-angle coordinates,
using reciprocal variables and the Taylor order at the short-phase
point. That broader statement is developed separately in
`hermitian_polynomial_barrier.md`; it is not an additional arithmetic
assumption in the proof above.
