# Higher-power separation in the signed Gaussian phase group

## Status

This extends the square-root argument in
[norm_squareclass_separation.md](norm_squareclass_separation.md).
The useful congruences for powers greater than two concern **signed
Gaussian allocation exponents**, rather than rational norm classes.

For fourth powers, no balanced signed relation involving at most
24 extracted nonanchor rows can vanish modulo four. In particular,
all sums of 12 distinct phase labels are different. The canonical
Gaussian-unit normalization is essential to this improvement.

These are additional restrictions on a hypothetical endpoint
cluster, not a uniform bound. The correction-conductor consequence
still grows only logarithmically in the number of selected rows.

## 1. The correct group and the failure of norm-only congruences

Use common-unit selected rows and fixed Gaussian prime
representatives, as in the central-truncation note:

```text
z_i=epsilon product_p pi_p^a_ip bar(pi_p)^(e_p-a_ip),
z_i/z_0=product_p (pi_p/bar(pi_p))^(a_ip-a_0p).
```

For an integer `ell>=2`, define the signed phase label

```text
v_i^(ell)=(a_ip-a_0p mod ell)_p
          in (Z/ell Z)^P,       1<=i<=k-1,      (1)
```

where `P` is the finite set of original split primes. Let `G_ell`
be the subgroup generated by these labels. We use its cardinality,
not a vector-space rank: `Z/4 Z`, for example, is not a field.

If integers `c_i`, not all zero, satisfy

```text
sum_i c_i v_i^(ell)=0,
beta_p=sum_i c_i(a_ip-a_0p),
g=product_p pi_p^((beta_p)_+) bar(pi_p)^((-beta_p)_+),
```

then every `beta_p` is divisible by `ell`, and literally

```text
g=gamma^ell  for a Gaussian integer gamma,
g/bar(g)=product_i (z_i/z_0)^c_i.                (2)
```

There is no residual unit in the first identity. This follows from
the displayed canonical product, including the fixed prime
representatives and the cancellation of the original common unit.
If units have not been made common, their torsion must instead be
retained in the full rational Gaussian norm-one group modulo
`ell`-th powers. Discarding that torsion can invalidate the phase
estimate in Section 3.

In contrast, a relation among the individual rational norms does
not in general give (1). Norms record `|a_ip-a_0p|`, whose sign
can be discarded modulo two but not modulo four. For a concrete
example, take distinct split primes `pi,rho` of norms `p,q` and

```text
h_1=pi rho,
h_2=pi^3 bar(rho)^3.
```

Their norm product is `(pq)^4`. Their signed exponent vectors are
`(1,1)` and `(3,-3)`, however, so neither their sum `(4,-2)` nor
their difference `(-2,4)` vanishes modulo four. Even allowing a
choice of the two signs does not repair this example.

Also, unlike the squareclass case, changing a coefficient from
`+1` to `-1` may destroy a relation modulo four. The higher-power
theorem below starts with an already balanced signed relation;
it does not balance an arbitrary relation after the fact.

## 2. An elementary cyclotomic phase bound

Put

```text
delta_ell=[Q(i,zeta_ell):Q(i)]
         =phi(lcm(ell,4))/2
         =phi(ell)       if 4 does not divide ell,
          phi(ell)/2     if 4 divides ell.       (3)
```

For every nonzero Gaussian integer `gamma` such that
`Im(gamma^ell)!=0`,

```text
|Im(gamma^ell)|/|gamma|^ell
 >= c_ell |gamma|^(-delta_ell),
c_ell=2^(-delta_ell) sin(pi/ell)^(ell-1)>0.       (4)
```

Here is a direct proof that keeps the field and constants fixed.
Let `r=|gamma|>=1`, `u=gamma/bar(gamma)`, and choose an `ell`-th
root of unity `zeta` closest to `u`. The irreducible polynomial
`f(T)` of `zeta` over `Q(i)` is monic with coefficients in `Z[i]`:
its roots are algebraic integers and its coefficients lie in the
integrally closed ring's fraction field. Its degree `d` is at most
`delta_ell`, since its cyclotomic field is contained in the field
in (3).

Homogenizing and evaluating gives the nonzero Gaussian integer

```text
bar(gamma)^d f(gamma/bar(gamma)).
```

It has modulus at least one. All roots of `f` have modulus one,
so

```text
1 <= r^d |u-zeta| 2^(d-1),
|u-zeta| >= 2^(1-delta_ell) r^(-delta_ell).       (5)
```

For any other `ell`-th root `xi`, the nearest-root property and
the triangle inequality give

```text
|u-xi| >= |zeta-xi|/2 >= sin(pi/ell).
```

Taking the product over all roots in `u^ell-1` and using
`|Im(gamma^ell)|/r^ell=|u^ell-1|/2` proves (4).
The maximal degree is attained at a primitive `ell`-th root, so
the relevant orders divide `ell`, not `2ell`. The latter orders
can arise if an extra unit twists the power.

Equivalently, for the exact power `g=gamma^ell` in (2),

```text
|Im g|/|g| >= c_ell |g|^(-delta_ell/ell).        (6)
```

This proof uses integer values of fixed cyclotomic factors. It
does not invoke Roth's theorem or a moving number-field estimate.

## 3. Why the fourth-power unit cannot be suppressed

For `ell=4`, `delta_4=1`, so (6) has exponent `1/4`.
One can also see the bound directly from

```text
Im(a+i b)^4=4ab(a-b)(a+b).
```

An arbitrary unit-twisted fourth power need not have this exponent.
There is an explicit primitive counterexample. Let

```text
x_n+b_n sqrt(2)=(3+2sqrt(2))^n,
a_n=x_n+b_n,
gamma_n=a_n+i b_n,
g_n=i gamma_n^4.
```

Then `x_n^2-2b_n^2=1`, `a_n` is odd, `b_n` is even, and the two
coordinates of `gamma_n` are coprime. Thus `g_n` is coprime to its
conjugate. But

```text
Im g_n=Re(gamma_n^4)
 =(a_n^2-2a_n b_n-b_n^2)(a_n^2+2a_n b_n-b_n^2)
 =1+4a_n b_n.
```

Since `a_n/b_n -> 1+sqrt(2)`, this gives

```text
|Im g_n|/|g_n| asymptotic to a positive constant times
|g_n|^(-1/2).                                  (7)
```

Consequently a bound `c|g|^(-1/4)` is false for general
`g=i gamma^4`, even with conjugate primitivity. The literal power
in (2) is what permits the stronger separation. For square norms,
all four units happened to give the same exponent; that feature
does not extend to fourth powers.

## 4. The finite signed-relation obstruction

Let `s=log D`, `W_core=W-2s`, and `eta=eta_core` in the central
truncation. Write

```text
w'_S=log N(H'_S),
|w'_S-W_core/2^(k-1)|<=eta W_core/2^(k-1).
```

For a nonzero integer coefficient vector `c=(c_i)`, put

```text
L=sum_i |c_i|,
mu(c)=E |sum_i c_i X_i|,
```

where the `X_i` are independent Bernoulli bits with parameter
`1/2`. If the signed phase relation (1) vanishes modulo `ell`,
the canonical numerator in (2) satisfies

```text
log N(g) <= F(c),
F(c)=(1+eta)W_core mu(c)+2L s.                  (8)
```

Indeed, on core block `S` its signed exponent is, up to the
block's orientation, `e'_p sum_(i in S)c_i`, plus an error of
magnitude at most `2L r_p`. Sum absolute values and use the
uniform profile, exactly as in the squareclass proof.

If

```text
min_S w'_S > 2L s,                             (9)
```

then `g` is nonunit. Choose a block meeting the coefficient
support in exactly one nonzero position; its nonzero core
exponent cannot be canceled by the total error in (9). Since
`g` is coprime to its conjugate, it cannot be a real nonunit.
Thus `Im g!=0`, as required in (6).

The original angles, meanwhile, imply

```text
|Im g|/|g| <= (L C/2) exp(-W/4).                (10)
```

Equations (6), (8), and (10) are incompatible whenever

```text
(L C/(2c_ell))
 * exp(delta_ell F(c)/(2ell)-W/4) < 1.          (11)
```

In the growing central reduction, for any fixed coefficient
pattern and fixed `ell`, conditions (9) and (11) follow from

```text
mu(c) < ell/(2delta_ell).                      (12)
```

The strict inequality is essential. Equality gives no positive
exponent gap by this argument.

## 5. Fourth powers exclude balanced relations on 24 rows

Take coefficients `+1,-1` with the numbers of the two signs
differing by at most one, and let their support size be `q`.
For `q=2r-1` or `q=2r`, the absolute Bernoulli expectation is

```text
m_q=r binom(2r,r)/4^r.                         (13)
```

This also correctly incorporates the anchor bit when the support
size is odd, as explained in the squareclass note. The values
increase with `r`. Since the threshold in (12) is two for
`ell=4`,

```text
m_23=m_24=2028117/1048576 < 2,
m_25=m_26=16900975/8388608 > 2.                 (14)
```

Therefore, for all sufficiently large original clusters, no
balanced signed sum of at most 24 nonanchor phase labels can
vanish modulo four. At support size 24 the exponent gap is

```text
1/4-m_24/8 = 69035/8388608 > 0.                (15)
```

This is a statement about balanced signed relations, not
24-wise linear independence of all labels over `Z/4 Z`.

Let `n=k-1>=12`. If two different 12-element subsets of the
nonanchor indices had equal sums in `G_4`, canceling their
intersection would give a balanced signed relation of support
at most 24. Hence

```text
|G_4| >= binom(n,12).                           (16)
```

More generally, for fixed `ell` and any fixed `r` satisfying
`r binom(2r,r)/4^r < ell/(2delta_ell)`, all sums over distinct
`r`-element subsets are different, and

```text
|G_ell| >= binom(n,r).                          (17)
```

Repeated indices in those sums are not included: allowing them
can make the coefficient magnitudes, and hence `mu(c)`, much
larger. Equations (16)--(17) are counts of restricted subset
sums, not claims about all multisets.

## 6. Localizing the congruences on the small correction conductor

If every core exponent `e'_p` is divisible by `ell`, its
contribution to (1) vanishes. Only primes with `r_p>0`, which
are exactly the primes dividing `D`, can then support a phase
label. Consequently

```text
|G_ell| <= ell^omega(D).                        (18)
```

For a fourth-power core, (16) gives

```text
4^omega(D) >= binom(k-1,12),
omega(D) >= 6 log_2(k-1)-log_4(12!)+o(1),
log D >= (6 log 5/log 2)log k-O(1).             (19)
```

The divisibility assumption on the core is substantive. A
square core has exponents divisible by two, which need not
vanish modulo four; its phase labels cannot generally be
localized on `D` for (18).

There is, however, a harmless modification when the original
exponents are all divisible by four. Replace each central
truncation radius `r_p` by

```text
rhat_p=2 ceil(r_p/2).
```

Because `e_p/2` is even, `rhat_p<=e_p/2`; also `rhat_p<=2r_p`,
including the case `r_p=0`. The new core exponent
`ehat_p=e_p-2rhat_p` is divisible by four. The common correction
modulus has

```text
Dhat=product_p p^rhat_p,
log Dhat <= 2 log D,
supp(Dhat)=supp(D).                             (20)
```

All Gaussian exponent identities remain integral, ties and
`ehat_p=0` are permitted, and the removed weight increases by
at most a fixed factor. Since `log D` is negligible relative
to each block, all blocks still retain their full relative
weight. Thus (19) applies with this modified truncation to the
original fourth-power-norm case. No new primes are discarded
merely because they occur in a correction.

## 7. Scope and remaining arithmetic requirement

The higher-power restriction concerns the signed phase group.
It does not follow from a fourth-power relation among ordinary
norms, and it does not survive an unrecorded unit twist.
Both failures have explicit examples above.

For every fixed power, the counting consequence remains a
lower bound of order `log k` on the number or weight of prime
coordinates. This is compatible with the central estimate
`log D<=kW/sqrt(M)`, even while that upper bound is negligible
relative to every individual block. A uniform endpoint proof
would need a further bound on the available phase-group size,
or a stronger relation forced by the actual arithmetic. No
such bound is supplied here.

## Verification

An independent audit checked the Gaussian-integral minimal-polynomial
argument, the degree in (3), and the explicit constant in (4).
It also independently supplied the norm-only counterexample in
Section 1 and checked that the packing statement must use group
cardinality rather than a vector-space rank modulo four. The
displayed values in (14)--(15) were evaluated with exact rational
arithmetic. This is a prose theorem and has not been formalized
in Lean.
