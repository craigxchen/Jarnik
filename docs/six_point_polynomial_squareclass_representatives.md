# Polynomial-height representatives for square descent

The squareclass representatives in the branch descent can be chosen with
polynomially bounded conjugates, inverse conjugates, and denominator ideals.
Large fundamental units do not obstruct this: replace the representative
and its accompanying ideal together. This strengthens the earlier
representative-height caution in
[six_point_branch_square_descent_audit.md](six_point_branch_square_descent_audit.md).
It does not bound the circle parameter height H.

## 1. A fixed-degree theorem with explicit dependencies

Let K be a number field of degree `n<=12`, of absolute discriminant D_K.
Let Qbad be a positive integer divisible by two, and let S consist of all
prime ideals above its prime divisors. Suppose `eta in K*` satisfies

```
v_P(eta) even for every P outside S.
```

There is an integral representative `eta' in O_K` of its squareclass such
that, with a constant depending only on n and

```
M=max(2, C_n D_K^2 4^n Qbad^n),
```

we have for every embedding `tau:K->C`

```
M^(-(n-1)) <= |tau(eta')| <= M.                       (1)
```

Write `a=product_(P in S) P^(v_P(eta) mod 2)`. There is an integral ideal b'
with

```
(eta')=a b'^2,
Norm(a)<=rad(Qbad)^n,
Norm(b')<=M^(n/2).                                    (2)
```

If an integral element L has `L/eta` square in K, it consequently has a
representation

```
L=eta' beta'^2,      beta' in b'^(-1),
N beta' in O_K,      N=Norm(b')<=M^(n/2).               (3)
```

All bounds are independent of the original height of eta or L. In
particular, if `D_K<=B^d` and `Qbad<=B^b` for fixed d,b and `B>=2`, then
M, all inverse conjugates, and N are `B^O(1)`. One may take the exponent
of M to be `2d+nb`, up to the dimension-dependent constant, and that of
N to be `(n/2)(2d+nb)`.

## 2. Local discriminants, including primes above two

If eta is a square, use `eta'=1`, `a=b'=O_K`; all conclusions are immediate.
Otherwise let `E=K(sqrt(eta))`, a field of degree `2n<=24`, and let sigma
be its nontrivial automorphism over K.

At each finite prime P, multiplication of eta by a local square writes
it as `pi^epsilon u`, with u a unit and `epsilon in {0,1}`. The local
integral order generated by `sqrt(pi^epsilon u)` has discriminant
`4 pi^epsilon u`. The maximal-order discriminant divides an order
discriminant, so

```
v_P(disc(E/K)) <= 2 v_P(2)+epsilon.
```

Outside S, epsilon is zero and two is a unit, so the exponent is zero.
At a split local extension it is also zero. Hence, uniformly,

```
v_P(disc(E/K)) <= 2 v_P(2)+1_(P in S),
|Disc(E)| <= D_K^2 4^n rad(Qbad)^n
          <= D_K^2 4^n Qbad^n.                         (4)
```

For the norm bound, the product of norms of all primes above a rational
prime p is at most `p^n`, since the sum of their residue degrees is at
most n. Thus (4) includes ramified base primes, all wild quadratic costs
above two, and nonsquare unit classes. No regulator bound is used.

## 3. A small primitive integral element

For any number field F of fixed degree N and discriminant D_F, there is
an integral primitive element gamma with

```
max_tau |tau(gamma)| <= C_N sqrt(D_F).                 (5)
```

Here is a proof retaining the needed dependence. Embed O_F as its standard
lattice in `R^(r1) x C^(r2)`, viewed as a real N-dimensional Euclidean
space. Its covolume is `2^(-r2) sqrt(D_F)`. Every nonzero integral element
has a nonzero integer norm, so at least one conjugate has modulus at least
one and its Euclidean norm is at least one. All successive minima lambda_i
for the Euclidean unit ball are therefore at least one. Minkowski's second
theorem gives

```
product_i lambda_i <= C_N sqrt(D_F),
lambda_N <= C_N sqrt(D_F).
```

The precise inequality is checked in equation (1.1) of
[Henk, Henze and Hernandez Cifre, On extensions of Minkowski's theorem on
successive minima](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.4993). It supplies N linearly
independent integral elements `a_1,...,a_N` of that size. They need not be
an integral basis; a Q-basis is enough for the next step.

For each pair of distinct embeddings tau_i,tau_j, the equation

```
sum_l c_l (tau_i(a_l)-tau_j(a_l))=0
```

is a proper hyperplane in the coefficient variables. Put
`J=N(N-1)/2`. Each such hyperplane contains at most `(J+1)^(N-1)` points
of the grid `{0,...,J}^N`, because some coefficient in its equation is
nonzero. Their union contains strictly fewer than `(J+1)^N` grid points.
A point outside their union gives an integral gamma with all N conjugates
distinct, hence primitive, and proves (5). Degree one is immediate.

Apply (5) to E and set

```
delta=gamma-sigma(gamma),            eta'=delta^2.
```

Because gamma is primitive over Q, it is not fixed by sigma, so delta is
nonzero. It is integral and anti-invariant. Thus `eta' in O_K`, and writing
`delta=c sqrt(eta)` in the anti-invariant K-line shows `eta'/eta=c^2`.
Every conjugate of delta has modulus at most `2C_(2n) sqrt(|Disc(E)|)`;
every conjugate of eta' is therefore at most a constant times `|Disc(E)|`.
Together with (4), this gives the upper bound in (1). Since
`|Norm_K/Q(eta')|` is a positive integer, multiplying its other n-1 conjugate
bounds gives the lower bound in (1). Complex embeddings and negative real
values of eta' present no exception: only absolute values are used.

## 4. The new ideal and denominator

Squareclasses have identical valuation parities. Since eta' is integral,
its ideal factors uniquely as `a b'^2` with b' integral. Its norm is at
most M^n, proving (2). If `L=eta' beta'^2` is integral, taking ideal
valuations gives

```
v_P(beta') >= -v_P(b')
```

at every P, including those where a has exponent one. Thus beta' belongs
to `b'^(-1)`. The positive integer `N=Norm(b')` annihilates the finite
abelian group `O_K/b'`, so `N O_K` is contained in b'. This gives
`N b'^(-1) subset O_K`, proving (3).

This replacement deliberately allows b' to differ from an initially
chosen small representative of its ideal class. Keeping that original
ideal and separately reducing fundamental units is unnecessary and need
not give polynomial archimedean control.

There are only polynomially many such squareclasses when D_K and Qbad
are polynomial in B: each has an integral representative in the fixed
Minkowski ball of radius `sqrt(n)M`. Distinct integral elements are at
Euclidean distance at least one, so disjoint balls of radius one-half
bound their number by `(1+2sqrt(n)M)^n`. This argument also avoids class
number and regulator estimates. For an etale algebra of total degree
12, apply the theorem to every field component; the product bounds
remain fixed powers, and the split squareclass components use eta'=1.

## 5. What this repairs, and what it still does not prove

In the branch descent, `L=a0 s-theta t` and
`Norm(L)=a0^11 d^2`. Its field discriminants and its bad integer are
polynomial in the branch coefficient height B, as already proved in
Section 1 of the descent audit. Its crude bounds `D_K<=c B^132` and
`Qbad<=c B^133` allow, in degree twelve, `M<=c B^1860`, inverse
conjugates at most `c B^20460`, and `N<=c B^11160`. These exponents
are deliberately unoptimized. The theorem therefore gives exact equations

```
a0 s-theta t=eta' beta'^2,
|tau(eta')| and |tau(eta')|^(-1) <= B^O(1),
N beta' integral,                  N<=B^O(1).          (6)
```

Consequently the old archimedean representative constant can genuinely be
taken to be `B^O(1)=U^O(1)`, after changing its accompanying ideal too.
This is a stronger conclusion than merely counting polynomially many
possibly large representatives.

Nevertheless, the irreducible degree-twelve case still has the following
compatible estimates near a simple real branch root in the endpoint
window. Eleven conjugates of beta' are at most `B^O(1) H^(1/2)`, and the
approached conjugate is at most `U^O(1) H^(-9/2)` (including the endpoint
constant). The denominator N and algebraic norm only force that last
conjugate to be at least `B^(-O(1)) H^(-11/2)`. The lower exponent is
strictly weaker. Thus the representation is now polynomially controlled
in U, but this norm comparison still supplies no upper bound on H and
no uniform circle-arc bound. The two-plane constraint on `eta' beta'^2`
retains the unresolved Diophantine content.
