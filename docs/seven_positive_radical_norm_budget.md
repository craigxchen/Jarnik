# The seven-row positive radical: conjugates and ramification budget

The positive divided-difference identity gives a nonzero algebraic integer
with a very small distinguished real value. Its field norm excludes small
squareclass rank, but the complete norm budget does not give a contradiction
at the possible full degree. Clean-cut primes do not universally divide
this norm, even on the exact square-quartic Gale locus.

## 1. Integral normalization and every conjugate

Use the intrinsic normalization in
[`seven_positive_radical_height.md`](seven_positive_radical_height.md).
Orient marked vectors `v_i` into a common real affine chart and put

```text
Q_i=q(v_i),       D_i=product_(j!=i)det(v_i,v_j),
rho_i^2=Q_i^5/D_i^2,       tau=gcd_Q(rho_1^2,...,rho_7^2),
c_i=rho_i^2/tau,           sigma_i=sign(D_i).
```

The `c_i` are the coprime positive integer metric values on a saturated
integral Gale basis.  The exact scaling formulas in the height note show
that these signs also retain the zero row-sum relation; if `epsilon_i`
orient the primitive rows of that basis, then
`sigma_i=-(product_j epsilon_j)epsilon_i`.  The positive radical is

```text
R = sum_(i=1..7) sigma_i sqrt(c_i).
```

It is an algebraic integer. Neither the rational coefficients of `q` nor
the denominators of affine slopes introduce a denominator in `R`.
The positive sixth-divided-difference identity, with its primitive scaling
retained, gives on the stated full fair reduced profile

```text
R != 0,
log |R| = -77w+o(w),
log c_i = 38w+o(w).                               (1)
```

Put `beta=R^2` and `d=[Q(beta):Q]`. Every embedding of `Q(beta)` extends
to `K=Q(sqrt(c_1),...,sqrt(c_7))`, and therefore every conjugate of `beta`
is the square of a signed sum of the seven positive radicals. In
particular, with `H=sum_i sqrt(c_i)`,

```text
0 < sigma(beta) <= H^2,
log H=19w+o(w).                                   (2)
```

The field of `beta` is contained in the multiquadratic field generated by
the pair radicals `sqrt(c_i c_j)`. This has degree at most `2^6=64`:
the even subsets of seven labels form a six-dimensional binary vector
space, and dependencies of squareclasses can only lower the degree.
Thus `d` is a power of two and `d<=64`.

There are exactly `d` conjugates in its field norm, counted once each.
Using the known small conjugate from (1) and the bound (2) for all the
others gives

```text
1 <= Norm_(Q(beta)/Q)(beta)
  <= |R|^2 H^(2(d-1)),
log Norm(beta) <= (38d-192)w+o(w).                 (3)
```

No lower estimate for the other conjugates is assumed. They may have
additional cancellation; (3) is an upper bound, not an asymptotic equality.
For `d=64` its coefficient is `2240`, so integrality supplies no
contradiction. For `d<=4` it is negative. Therefore any sequence satisfying
(1) must eventually have

```text
[Q(R^2):Q] >= 8.                                 (4)
```

This formulation also handles fields without an automorphism that negates
all seven radicals. If such an automorphism exists, the two small
conjugates `R` and `-R` in the norm of `R` are exactly the information
retained once in the norm of `R^2`; they must not be counted as two
independent small conjugates of `R^2`.

## 2. No universal clean-prime norm divisor

At a clean odd split prime with minority size `s=1,2,3` and depth `e`,
the normalized radicand valuations are

```text
v_p(c_i)=(7-2s)e  for minority i,
v_p(c_i)=0       for majority i.                  (5)
```

At least four radicals are therefore units. A common radical divisor
cannot be extracted from `R`. Nor does the square-quartic relation force
the sum of these unit radicals to vanish in the residue field.

Here is an algebraic verification of the latter claim. Work on the exact
Gale family

```text
q(t)=1+t^2,
P(t)=product_(i=1..7)(t-t_i),
a_i=q(t_i)^2/P'(t_i),
c_i=a_i^2 q(t_i)=q(t_i)^5/P'(t_i)^2.               (6)
```

At the generic cut specialize the minority nodes to a root `r` of `q`.
The minority `c_i` have positive valuation by (5). The majority limits
are

```text
c_j = q(t_j)^5 / [(t_j-r)^(2s) P_M'(t_j)^2],
P_M(t)=product_(j in majority)(t-t_j).             (7)
```

Their squareclasses are those of the independent functions `q(t_j)`.
Over the rational function field in the free majority nodes (with `r`
adjoined), these squareclasses are independent: the divisor `t_j=r` has
odd valuation for `q(t_j)` and zero valuation for every other `q(t_k)`.
Distinct squareclass radicals are linearly independent over that base
field. Thus no signed sum of the majority radicals in (7) vanishes.

The universal product over the `64` sign patterns modulo global reversal,

```text
P_rad(c) = product_(sigma in {+,-}^7 / {+,-})
                 (sum_i sigma_i sqrt(c_i))^2,     (8)
```

is a polynomial in the `c_i`; at the generic cut all its factors have
nonzero residue. Its generic clean-cut valuation is consequently zero
for each minority size. At an actual specialization with squareclass
dependencies it need not be the norm of the chosen nonzero radical, but
its being a unit implies that every permitted signed sum is a local unit.
This is enough to disprove a universal positive clean-cut norm order.

For a finite check, take `p=1009`, `r=540` (so `r^2=-1 mod p`), and choose
the majority nodes to be `0,...,6-s`. Formula (7) gives the following
unit radicands modulo `p`:

| s | Majority radicands | P_rad mod 1009 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 777, 15, 259, 806, 457, 795 | 74 |
| 2 | 755, 224, 201, 506, 801 | 227 |
| 3 | 28, 469, 829, 977 | 951 |

All minority radicands are zero in this residue calculation. None of the
64 signed sums modulo global reversal vanishes, with roots taken in
`F_(1009^2)`. Distinct lifts of the minority nodes to depth `e` realize
the exact valuation (5). Majority rows span rank two modulo `p`, so local
saturation changes only units. A common unit metric rescaling also
preserves the nonvanishing. These are local examples, not fair-height
endpoint constructions.

## 3. Ramification counts parity, not the full cut depth

Let `M=[K:Q]`, a power of two at most `128`. At an odd clean prime, the
extension `K/Q` is ramified exactly when at least one radicand valuation
is odd. By (5), this is exactly when `e` is odd. In that case its tame
inertia group has order two, and the field-discriminant exponent is

```text
v_p(Disc K)=M/2  if e is odd,
v_p(Disc K)=0    if e is even.                     (9)
```

For each of the 63 cuts define

```text
omega_S = sum_(p in that cut, depth e_p odd) log p.
```

The full fair profile gives `omega_S<=w+o(w)`, not an equality in general.
Subject to its stated negligible exceptional-prime error, (9) gives

```text
log |Disc K| = (M/2) sum_S omega_S + o(w)
            <= (63M/2)w+o(w).                     (10)
```

Equality in the leading upper bound holds for a profile consisting of
depth-one clean primes, but cannot be inferred if even or large depths
carry substantial mass. The prime two and exceptional primes must be
retained in the error budget. With at most seven quadratic generators,
their discriminant contribution is controlled by the given full-profile
error; no growing-degree limit is being taken here.

The discriminant measures the trace lattice and ramification. It does
not assert that an arbitrary nonzero algebraic integer, or its norm, is
divisible by that discriminant. The explicit units above show why such a
divisibility substitution would be invalid in this application.

Thus the exact positive radical supplies (4), but the norm and clean-prime
ramification calculations do not currently yield the additional small
conjugates or a norm divisor needed for a fair-profile contradiction.

The standard-library checker `check_positive_radical_norm_budget.py`
reproduces all three finite residue calculations and checks the derivative
and fair-count identities used in the odd-point extension.
