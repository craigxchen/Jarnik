# Weighted rank-one kernels: power classes and Roth packing

The rank-one argument in
[`rank_one_kernel_relative_roth_obstruction.md`](rank_one_kernel_relative_roth_obstruction.md)
extends beyond a kernel generated by signs.  For a primitive kernel vector
with nonzero coordinates, collisions are exact proportional-power classes.
Whole-row primitivity bounds the **total kernel weight** of each class, and
an exact fractional matching between the classes gives a Roth certificate.

For the smaller arc constants covered by
[linear allocation rigidity](linear_allocation_affine_rigidity.md),
the [integer pattern-lattice theorem](corank_one_local_bessel_pair_filter.md)
now rules out every proportional-power collision on an actual endpoint
arc, and supplies a separate local coefficient test. The present
transportation and power-class arguments retain their broader fixed-`C`
scope and can exclude profiles that pass that local test.

For the weighted transportation criterion, put

```text
a_j=|r_j|,                 A=sum_j a_j,
d_C=gcd_(j in C) a_j
```

for every power-direction class `C`.  There is a cross-class transportation
`y_jh` with vertex demands `a_j`.  Its amplified Roth mass is

```text
P(y)=sum_(j<h) y_jh/lcm(d_C(j),d_C(h)).                 (1)
```

If `P(y)>4`, the radius is bounded.  In particular, if
`1<=a_j<=B`, then `k>=9` suffices for `B=1`, and

```text
k >= 8B-3                                                (2)
```

suffices for every `B>=2`.  The bound is for each fixed sign matrix and arc
constant; Roth's constant is ineffective and is not uniform as the matrix
varies.

There is also an effective degeneration theorem with no bound on `r`: if
there are exactly two power-direction classes and at least four actual row
outputs are distinct, their containing arc has length `L` with

```text
L >= sqrt(2) R^(2/3),                R <= C^6/8.
```

The proof keeps the primitive normalization of the entire row tuple.  It
also keeps the common Gaussian multiplier, arbitrary row units, and the
units left outside Gaussian root extraction.  None of those may be removed
coordinatewise.

## 1. Weighted phase normal form

Let `S in {+1,-1}^(m x k)`, choose row zero, and let `D` have rows
`S_i-S_0`.  Assume

```text
rank_Q(D)=k-1,                 ker_Q(D)=Q r,
r in Z^k primitive,            r_j!=0.                    (3)
```

Use conjugate-primitive nonunit Gaussian integers `kappa_j`, arbitrary row
units `u_i`, and a nonzero common multiplier `d`.  The normalized row
products and the actual points are

```text
Z_i=product_j kappa_j^((1+S_ij)/2)
                    bar(kappa_j)^((1-S_ij)/2),
z_i=d u_i Z_i,                 R=|d| product_j |kappa_j|. (4)
```

Assume throughout that

```text
gcd_i Z_i=1 in Z[i], up to units.                         (5)
```

Primitivity here belongs to the whole normalized row tuple.  It is not an
individual assertion about a single `Z_i`, and the common multiplier `d`
is not silently included in (5).

If the points have angular span `Delta` inside a containing arc, their
lifted phases have the
form

```text
S theta+(pi/2)t=c 1+2 pi l+e,       |e_i|<=Delta/2,       (6)
```

where the integral vector `t` retains the arbitrary row units and `c`
retains the common phase, including `arg(d)`.  The rational right-inverse
argument from the rank-one note does not use `r_j in {+1,-1}`.  It gives a
finite set of rational torsion vectors and

```text
theta_j=r_j tau+beta_j+O_S(Delta).                       (7)
```

Conjugate `kappa_j` and reverse column `j` of `S` when `r_j<0`.  Denote the
new block by `lambda_j` and the new sign matrix by `S'`.  Every product
`Z_i` in (4) is unchanged, while

```text
ker_Q(D')=Q a,                    a=(a_1,...,a_k)>0.       (8)
```

Thus every row of `S'` has the same weighted plus load.  For fixed integers
`q` and `A-q`,

```text
sum_(S'_ij=+1) a_j=q,       sum_(S'_ij=-1) a_j=A-q.      (9)
```

The phase normal form is now

```text
arg(lambda_j)=a_j tau+beta'_j+O_S(Delta).                (10)
```

## 2. The exact collision classes

Choose one Gaussian prime `pi_p` above every rational split prime occurring
in the blocks.  Conjugate primitivity gives

```text
lambda_j=epsilon_j product_p pi_p^((nu_jp)_+)
                         bar(pi_p)^((-nu_jp)_+),
epsilon_j in mu_4,              nu_jp in Z.              (11)
```

There are no inert or ramified prime factors.  Define

```text
j ~ h    iff    nu_jp/a_j=nu_hp/a_h for every p.         (12)
```

This is exactly the collision relation detected by the primitive pair
certificate.  If `g_jh=gcd(a_j,a_h)`, put

```text
v_jh=(a_h/g_jh)e_j-(a_j/g_jh)e_h.                       (13)
```

The vector belongs to `a^perp`, hence to the rational row-difference span.
Its two coefficients cancel the moving term in (10), so its phase is within
`O_(S,r)(Delta)` of a fixed finite set of rational multiples of `pi`.
Its reduced Gaussian product has signed prime exponents

```text
E_p(j,h)=(a_h/g_jh)nu_jp-(a_j/g_jh)nu_hp.               (14)
```

It is a unit precisely when `j~h`.  This includes every unit rotation:
the raw product may be a Gaussian unit times a positive rational integer,
but its conjugate-primitive direction is a unit exactly when all the
exponents (14) vanish.

Now fix a class `C` and put `d_C=gcd_(j in C)a_j`.  The common rational
numbers in (12) have denominators dividing `d_C`.  Therefore there is a
conjugate-primitive nonunit `rho_C` such that, exactly,

```text
lambda_j=epsilon_j rho_C^(a_j/d_C),       j in C.        (15)
```

The units `epsilon_j` stay outside the powers.  In general they cannot be
absorbed into Gaussian roots: for example, `i` has no square root in
`mu_4`.  This is harmless for phase separation because there are only four
possible unit angles, but it prevents an unqualified coordinatewise root
identity.

For distinct classes `C,D`, (15) supplies additional exact divisibility.
Writing `L_jh=lcm(a_j,a_h)`, every exponent in (14) is divisible by

```text
ell_CD(j,h)=L_jh/lcm(d_C,d_D).                           (16)
```

Indeed, if `t_Cp=d_C nu_jp/a_j`, then

```text
E_p(j,h)=(L_jh/d_C)t_Cp-(L_jh/d_D)t_Dp,
gcd(L_jh/d_C,L_jh/d_D)=L_jh/lcm(d_C,d_D).               (17)
```

After division by (16), some exponent remains nonzero because the classes
are distinct.  Thus the reduced product for (13) is literally a nonunit
`ell_CD(j,h)`-th power.  Its raw predecessor is that power times the
positive cancelled content and a recorded unit from (11).

The order in (16) is fixed once `r` and the collision partition are fixed.
Dividing its phase relation introduces only finitely many roots of each
fixed algebraic target, together with the four unit angles.  Roth therefore
gives, for every fixed `epsilon>0`,

```text
h(v_jh) >= ell_CD(j,h)/(2+epsilon) log R
            -O_(S,C,r,epsilon)(1)                       (18)
```

on cross-class pairs, where `h(v_jh)` is the logarithmic norm of the
conjugate-primitive reduced product.  This is the fixed-power amplification from
[`coupled_block_roth_packing.md`](coupled_block_roth_packing.md).  It makes
no uniform assertion for root orders that vary with `R`.

## 3. Whole-row primitivity gives a weighted class cap

Every collision class satisfies

```text
M_C:=sum_(j in C)a_j <= min(q,A-q) <= A/2.              (19)
```

Choose a Gaussian prime `pi` dividing `rho_C`.  Condition (5) supplies a
row with no `pi` factor and a row with no `bar(pi)` factor.  In the first
row all class coordinates must choose the conjugated block; in the second
they must all choose the unconjugated block.  Other blocks can add
valuations but cannot cancel them.  Formula (9) then gives respectively
`M_C<=A-q` and `M_C<=q`.

This is the correct replacement for the cardinality cap in the sign-kernel
case.  A class containing many coordinates of weight one may have more than
`k/2` members, so an ordinary perfect matching of coordinates need not
exist.  Its total demand can nevertheless be matched.

## 4. Weighted cross-class matching and the certificate inequality

The cap (19) gives nonnegative symmetric numbers `y_jh`, supported on pairs
from distinct classes, such that

```text
sum_(h!=j)y_jh=a_j.                                    (20)
```

One direct construction arranges each class as a contiguous interval of
length `M_C` on a circle of circumference `A`, subdivides it into vertex
intervals of lengths `a_j`, and pairs every point with its antipode.  No
class interval of length at most `A/2` meets its antipodal translate except
possibly at endpoints of measure zero.  The intersection measures give
(20).  In particular,

```text
sum_(j<h)y_jh=A/2.                                     (21)
```

Give certificate (13) the packing weight

```text
w_jh=y_jh/L_jh.                                        (22)
```

At coordinate `j`, its contribution is

```text
w_jh |v_jh,j|=(y_jh/L_jh)(a_h/g_jh)=y_jh/a_j.
```

Therefore (20) makes every coordinate load exactly one.  Combining (16)
and (22), the total power-amplified Roth mass is exactly (1):

```text
P(y)=sum_(j<h) w_jh ell_CD(j,h)
    =sum_(j<h) y_jh/lcm(d_C(j),d_C(h)).                 (23)
```

On the other hand, exact Gaussian cancellation and the coordinate loads
give

```text
sum_(j<h)w_jh h(v_jh)
 <=sum_j log N(lambda_j)
 <=2 log R.                                             (24)
```

Equations (18), (23), and (24) prove the following.

**Weighted rank-one power-class theorem.**  Fix `S`, `C`, and the primitive
kernel generator `r` in (3).  Suppose (4)--(6) hold with
`Delta<=C R^(-1/2)`, all blocks are conjugate-primitive nonunits, and the
whole normalized row gcd satisfies (5).  Form the collision classes (12).
If some cross-class transportation (20) has `P(y)>4`, then `R` is bounded
by a generally ineffective constant depending only on `S,C`.

Although the collision partition depends on the blocks, the coordinate set
is fixed and has only finitely many partitions.  For each partition the
transportation polytope and the functional (23) have fixed rational data,
so one may choose a maximizing vertex once and for all.  Taking the maximum
of the resulting finitely many Roth bounds over the partitions whose optimum
exceeds four removes any dependence of the final radius bound on the
realized partition.  For the universal estimates below, every perfect
transportation, including the interval construction, has the required lower
bound.

Two useful sufficient forms of (23) are

```text
P(y) >= A/[2 max_(C!=D) lcm(d_C,d_D)],                  (25)

P(y) >= [1/H] sum_C M_C/d_C,
H=max_(C!=D) (d_C+d_D)/gcd(d_C,d_D).                   (26)
```

For (26), use on each edge

```text
1/lcm(d_C,d_D)
 >= (1/H)(1/d_C+1/d_D)
```

and then sum the vertex demands (20).

## 5. An explicit bounded-weight class

Suppose `a_j<=B`.  The elementary maximum

```text
H_B=max_(1<=c,d<=B)(c+d)/gcd(c,d)
   =2                    if B=1,
    2B-1                if B>=2                           (27)
```

follows because distinct `c,d` have `c+d<=2B-1`, while equal values give
ratio two; equality for `B>=2` is attained at `B,B-1`.  Since
`a_j/d_C` is a positive integer, (26) gives

```text
P(y) >= (1/H_B)sum_j a_j/d_C(j) >= k/H_B.              (28)
```

Consequently the theorem applies when

```text
B=1 and k>=9,
B>=2 and k>=8B-3.                                      (29)
```

This recovers the nine-coordinate sign-kernel threshold and provides a
linear, rather than quadratic, block threshold for bounded primitive kernel
height.

The estimate (28) is sharp as an abstract matching bound.  For `B>=2`, take
two classes: `B-1` coordinates of weight `B` and `B` coordinates of weight
`B-1`.  Both class masses are `B(B-1)`, their class gcds are `B` and `B-1`,
and every transportation has

```text
P=B(B-1)/lcm(B,B-1)=1=k/(2B-1).                        (30)
```

Repeating both multiplicities by a common factor gives equality at every
scale.  This only shows sharpness of the universal certificate estimate; it
does not construct a sign matrix or an endpoint family.

## 6. When a reduction to unit weights is valid

Whole-row primitivity alone does not make `lambda_j` an `a_j`-th power.
For a singleton collision class, `d_C=a_j`, and (15) says only
`lambda_j=epsilon_j rho_C`.  Thus prime-power grouping does not reduce the
general weighted kernel to the `+/-1` theorem.

There is a clean conditional reduction.  If every block has an exact form

```text
lambda_j=epsilon_j xi_j^a_j,                            (31)
```

then every cross-class pair product in (13) is an
`L_jh`-th power up to its retained unit and positive content.  In (23) the
denominator disappears, so

```text
P(y)=sum_(j<h)y_jh=A/2.                                 (32)
```

Hence `A>8` suffices.  More generally, the same conclusion holds when every
collision class has `d_C=1`, because (15) then forces (31) class by class.
This is the precise scope of the root reduction.  The unrestricted theorem
must pay the class gcds in (23).

## 7. Two power classes give an effective one-parameter orbit

There is a stronger conclusion when (12) has exactly two classes `C,D` and
at least four of the actual points `z_i` are distinct.  In that case `R` is
bounded by an explicit function of `C` alone, with no bound on the
coordinates of `r`.

For row `i`, put

```text
p_iC=sum_(j in C, S'_ij=+1) a_j/d_C,
p_iD=sum_(j in D, S'_ij=+1) a_j/d_D.                   (33)
```

The common weighted plus load (9) says

```text
d_C p_iC+d_D p_iD=q.                                   (34)
```

Let `g_0=gcd(d_C,d_D)` and fix row zero.  There are integers `n_i` such
that

```text
p_iC-p_0C=(d_D/g_0)n_i,
p_iD-p_0D=-(d_C/g_0)n_i.                               (35)
```

Using the exact class roots (15), directionally reduce

```text
rho_C^(d_D/g_0) bar(rho_D)^(d_C/g_0)                  (36)
```

to a conjugate-primitive Gaussian integer `gamma`.  It is a nonunit because
`C,D` are distinct classes.  Equations (33)--(36), with every block and row
unit retained, give

```text
Z_i/Z_0=omega_i (gamma/bar(gamma))^n_i,
omega_i in mu_4.                                       (37)
```

Let `delta=gcd_i |n_i-n_0|`.  Unless all the row directions differ only by
units, `delta>0`.  Put

```text
Gamma=gamma^delta,
m_i=(n_i-n_0)/delta,
s=max_i m_i-min_i m_i.                                 (38)
```

Then the differences of the `m_i` have gcd one.  More importantly,
whole-row primitivity gives the exact modulus identity

```text
product_j |lambda_j|=|Gamma|^s,
R=|d| |Gamma|^s.                                       (39)
```

To verify (39), work one split prime at a time.  If its signed exponent in
`Gamma` is `f`, equation (37) says that its two row valuations vary by
`f m_i` and `-f m_i`.  The minimum of each valuation is zero by (5), so the
fixed sum of the two valuations is `|f|s`.  A prime absent from `Gamma` has
constant row valuations, which (5) forces to be zero.  Multiplying these
identities over the split primes proves (39).  The common multiplier `d`
remains in the second identity and is not assumed to be a unit.

After translating all `m_i`, put `t_i=m_i-min_h m_h`.  The same primewise
calculation strengthens (37)--(39) to the exact integral orbit

```text
Z_i=eta_i Gamma^t_i bar(Gamma)^(s-t_i),
eta_i in mu_4,                 0<=t_i<=s.               (40)
```

This form also gives a direct metric estimate which is stronger here than
Roth.  Write `n=N(Gamma)`.  For two distinct points with `t_h>t_j` and
`b=t_h-t_j`, factor their difference using (40).  The remaining bracket is
the difference of two unit multiples of `Gamma^b` and `bar(Gamma)^b`.  It is
a nonzero Gaussian integer between two Gaussian integers of equal norm, so
its norm is at least two.  Consequently

```text
|z_h-z_j|^2 >= 2 R^2/n^b.                              (41)
```

If the containing physical arc has length `L`, its chord bound
`|z_h-z_j|<=L` now gives

```text
n^b >= 2R^2/L^2.                                        (42)
```

If the angular span is less than `pi/2`, two distinct points cannot
have the same `t_i`: by (40) their quotient would be a nontrivial Gaussian
unit.  Choose four distinct points and order their exponents
`t_1<t_2<t_3<t_4`.  Apply (42) to the three adjacent gaps and multiply.
Since their sum is at most `s`, (39) and `|d|>=1` give

```text
(2R^2/L^2)^3 <= n^(t_4-t_1) <= n^s <= R^2,
L >= sqrt(2) R^(2/3).                                   (43)
```

If the angular span is at least `pi/2`, then `L>=pi R/2`, which is at
least `sqrt(2) R^(2/3)` for `R>=1`.  Thus (43) holds in both angular cases.
The endpoint hypothesis `L<=C sqrt(R)` immediately gives

```text
R <= C^6/8.                                             (44)
```

The metric estimate is the usual primitive rank-one orbit separation.  The
new point here is the exact reduction of two arbitrary weighted power
classes to (40), including whole-row content and the common multiplier.
Three or more power classes need not lie on one integer orbit.

## Verification

Run

```text
python3 docs/check_weighted_rank_one_power_class_packing.py
```

The checker constructs the antipodal transportation in exact half-integer
arithmetic, verifies its demands and cross-class support, verifies the
certificate loads and forced divisibility (16), exhausts (27) for bounded
weights, checks the sharp two-class family (30), and enumerates the integer
line relation (35) on exact two-class subset sums.  It also checks the
nonzero equal-norm Gaussian bracket used in (41) for every pair of Gaussian
units in several primitive power orbits.  Roth's theorem, the passage from
whole-row gcd one to (19), and the primewise modulus argument in (39) are
prose inputs, not numerical tests.
