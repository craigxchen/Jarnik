# Seven primitive Gale rows give the majority normalization

The primitive rows of the inverse seven-point low-moment lattice have
smaller heights than the original rows. On the full fair profile this
compression is exact, but it is the usual majority choice of Gaussian
representatives, rather than an additional height saving. This note uses
the notation of [the local gcd calculation](seven_local_gcd.md).

## 1. Exact primitive row contents

Write `b_i=l_i v_i`, where `l_i>0` is the gcd of the two coordinates of
`b_i` and `v_i` is primitive integral. Saturation of the rank-two lattice
means that the gcd of all maximal minors of `B` is one. Equivalently, the
rows generate `Z^2`. Therefore

```text
l_i=gcd_(j!=i)|p_ij|.                               (1)
```

Indeed the ideal generated by the determinants of `b_i` against the rows
equals the ideal of its determinants against every vector in `Z^2`; the
latter is precisely the coordinate-content ideal of `b_i`.

Set `C_i=q(v_i)=c_i/l_i^2` and
`d_ij=det(v_i,v_j)=p_ij/(l_i l_j)`. The `C_i` are positive rational
numbers; integrality must not be assumed before clearing the metric.

For a clean cut with conductor valuation `e`, the exact local table is:

| Minority size | Nonzero `v_p(l_i)` | Nonzero `v_p(C_i)` | Nonzero `v_p(d_ij)` |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | `2e` at its vertex | `e` at its vertex | none |
| 2 | `e` at its vertices | `e` at its vertices | `e` on its internal pair |
| 3 | none | `e` at its vertices | `e` on its internal triangle |

These follow by subtracting row contents from the primitive Plucker table
and from the coprime metric-value table. In particular they are valid at
every prime on which the stated clean profile holds.

## 2. Controlling the metric before reducing the basis

Assume the full signed squared-chord valuation profile differs from the
clean fair-cut profile by total absolute logarithmic error `o(w)`, as in
the full-profile hypothesis in the low-moment note. The fixed finite
maxima and minima defining the primitive Plucker valuations, the star
normalization, and (1) preserve this error bound.

The integer `G=gcd_(i<j<k)|p_ij p_ik p_jk|` has valuation zero on every
clean cut: choose three vertices outside the minority set. It follows
that `log G=o(w)`.

Put `t=4det(q)`. For any fixed pair the chord identity gives

```text
v_p(t)=v_p(c_i)+v_p(c_j)-2v_p(p_ij)+v_p(delta_ij^2).
```

The right side is zero on every clean cut, by direct substitution.
Consequently `sum_p |v_p(t)| log p=o(w)`, with any fixed two-adic
normalization adjustment contributing only `O(1)`. Thus `t`, and also
`sqrt(det(q))`, have subpower rational height. This statement uses the
full signed-edge error hypothesis; it cannot be inferred just from the
maximum conductor valuation or from archimedean minor heights.

The denominator of the three coefficients of `q`, in an arbitrary
integer lattice basis, divides `2G`. To see this, solve any three
equations `q(b_i)=c_i` by Cramer's rule. The determinant of their
coefficient matrix with rows `(x_i^2,2x_i y_i,y_i^2)` is, up to sign,
`2p_ij p_ik p_jk`. Each coefficient denominator divides every such
determinant and hence their gcd `2G`.

Therefore `Q=(2G)q` is an integral positive form of square determinant
`m^2`, where `m=exp(o(w))` is a positive integer. Integral Gauss reduction
gives coefficients `exp(o(w))` and eigenvalues bounded above and below
by `exp(±o(w))`. Dividing by `2G` preserves these bounds. The simultaneous
unimodular change of marked vectors preserves their contents and
determinants. This is precisely the covariant reduction required in
[the marked-node audit](gauss_reduction_marked_nodes_audit.md).

## 3. The resulting exponents and their meaning

On the fair profile every primitive Plucker coordinate has logarithm
`22w+o(w)`. Its six incident factors give `log sqrt(-D_i)=66w+o(w)`.
The common star square-root content has one unit from each singleton and
each size-two cut, so `log s=(7+21)w+o(w)=28w+o(w)`. Thus

```text
log c_i=38w+o(w),
log l_i=(2+6)w+o(w)=8w+o(w),
log C_i=22w+o(w),
log |d_ij|=6w+o(w).
```

In the reduced basis it follows that

```text
log ||v_i||=11w+o(w),
log sin(angle(v_i,v_j))=-16w+o(w),
```

where angles are understood projectively. The last identity also follows
directly in the physical metric from
`sin(angle)=sqrt(det(q)) |d_ij|/sqrt(C_i C_j)`.

The table identifies these numbers with majority normalization. Each
vertex lies in `1+6+15=22` minority sets of sizes one through three.
Each pair lies together in `1+5=6` minority sets. Choosing a common
Gaussian rotation so the majority side has zero exponent at each cut
therefore gives exactly the norm and determinant valuations above, up to
the same subpower error. Height `exp(11w)` and angular scale `exp(-16w)`
are the two halves of this existing normalization; neither is an extra
compression beyond it.

Finally the retained relation `sum_i l_i v_i=0` is the usual Gale
interpolation identity in these representatives. If `L=prod_i l_i`,
then exactly

```text
prod_(j!=i)d_ij = -s^2 c_i^2/(l_i^5 L),
q(v_i)^2/prod_(j!=i)d_ij = -l_i L/s^2.
```

Thus the weights `l_i` are proportional to the standard linear Gale
weights. Their simultaneous cancellation is real, but is not a further
independent relation created by primitive row division. No radius-height
inequality or endpoint improvement is established by this normalization.
