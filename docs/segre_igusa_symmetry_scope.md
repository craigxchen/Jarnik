# Segre--Igusa duality and the scope of a new rational involution

The six-row balanced invariant kernel is the Segre cubic's polar linear
system. Its primitive coordinate height drops from `18w` to `16w` on the
full integer profile. The standard duality and permutation operations
nevertheless return the same projective six-point configuration. A related
Richelot construction does change moduli, but need not preserve rational
projective point configurations, even when all fifteen pairings are available.
These observations do not prove endpoint uniformity.
Section 5 records a different rational involution found after the standard
duality audit. Subsequent arithmetic work proves that its arc-preserving
ordered version inflates the radius even after the original anchor is
removed. Every labeling of its fixed-three pullback loses bounded
normalized endpoint scale.

## 1. Identifying the polar system

Howard--Millson--Snowden--Vakil describe the six-point quotient as the Segre
cubic in projective four-space, generated by five degree-one invariants.
They identify its dual with the Igusa quartic; the duality sends the six
points through the Veronese embedding onto a conic. They also identify the
two permutation actions through the outer automorphism of `S_6`.
[Primary source, Section 1](https://math.stanford.edu/~vakil/files/0906.2437v1.pdf).

Here is an elementary check of the relevant linear system. In the standard
six-coordinate model, the Segre cubic is

```text
sum_(i=1..6) x_i=0,       sum_(i=1..6) x_i^3=0.
```

Its ten singular points are the sign vectors with three `+1` and three
`-1`, modulo simultaneous negation. The five independent polar quadrics
may be written `x_i^2-x_6^2`, `1<=i<=5`. They vanish at all ten nodes.
The ten nodes impose ten independent conditions on the fifteen-dimensional
space of quadrics in the hyperplane `sum x_i=0`. For example, substitute
`x_6=-sum_(i=1..5)x_i` and evaluate the fifteen monomials `x_i x_j` at
the ten sign representatives with `x_1=1`; exact rational row reduction
has rank ten. Thus the quadrics through the nodes form exactly the
five-dimensional polar space.

Under the point quotient, these ten nodes are the balanced configurations
with three rows on each of two directions. Therefore the balanced kernel
from [the small-relation lemma](small_invariant_relations_at_balanced_cuts.md)
is this polar space. Products of two complementary triangle Vandermondes
span the same space. The explicit integral change-of-basis certificate,
Segre equation, and primitive gradient calculations are recorded separately
in [segre_gradient_arithmetic.md](segre_gradient_arithmetic.md).

## 2. Exact height bookkeeping and its meaning

Take six nonanchor primitive numerators from a full seven-row extraction.
There are `64` oriented core blocks, each with log norm `w+o(w)`, and
`W=64w+o(w)`. Each actual bracket has log magnitude `16w+o(w)`;
all correcting contents and primitive residues contribute only `o(w)`.

A perfect-matching coordinate uses three brackets. At a cut containing
`s` of these six rows, its minimum common core order is

```text
g(s)=max(0,s-3),       s=0,...,6.
```

For a pair of triangle coordinates, the corresponding minimum is

```text
f(s)=(0,0,0,1,2,4,6).
```

These give the independently checked counts

| Coordinates | Raw log size | Common core log content | Primitive height |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Segre matchings | `48w` | `30w` | `18w` |
| Igusa triangle pairs | `96w` | `80w` | `16w` |

Every entry has an `o(w)` error. The common-content counts are
`sum_s binomial(6,s)g(s)=30` and
`sum_s binomial(6,s)f(s)=80`. Using the exact integral spanning families
retains the common gcds; a maximum-coordinate estimate without those gcds
would miss this reduction.

The forward quadratic gradient thus has new base content

```text
f(s)-2g(s)=(0,0,0,1,0,0,0),
```

supported on the twenty balanced three-row cuts. It accounts for
`2(18w)-20w=16w`. This is a real arithmetic height improvement in a
different projective embedding. It is not a decrease of a circle radius.

The inverse calculation is now certified as well. In the matching basis
`M=(A,B,C,D,E)`, write `Phi=BCE-AD(A+B+C+D+E)` and use dual coordinates
`(p,u,v,q,w_0)`. The quartic equation is

```text
J=(pq-uv-u w_0-v w_0)^2-4u v w_0(u+v+w_0-p-q).
```

The exact identity is
`grad J(grad Phi(M))=-4(product_(i<j) Delta_ij) M`.
Let `g=gcd(M)` and `h=gcd(grad Phi(M))`; the primitive inverse therefore
has scalar content `-4(product Delta_ij)g/h^3`. This is an integer, since
its product with the primitive vector `M/g` is integral. At a cut of size
`s`, its core exponent is

```text
binom(s,2)+g(s)-3f(s)=(0,0,1,0,1,0,0).
```

The thirty cuts of sizes two and four give inverse content `30w+o(w)`.
Consequently the inverse cubic gradient has raw height `48w+o(w)` and
returns height `18w+o(w)` after its gcd is removed. This proves the full
`18w -> 16w -> 18w` cycle, including the two different boundary contents.
The polynomial certificate and exact integral normalizations are in
[the inverse-gradient calculation](segre_gradient_arithmetic.md#5-exact-inverse-quartic-and-the-return-of-the-original-point).

In particular, a choice of rational parameter on the resulting conic gives
the same six projective points: any two degree-one parameterizations of a
rational conic differ by a fractional-linear transformation. A different
coordinate size after reparameterization must still be charged to the
least Gaussian realization radius. Merely calling the target coordinates
smaller does not supply a new integral cluster.

## 3. Why the standard symmetry loop gives no new moduli

The Veronese construction retains the original labels and incidence data.
Parametrizing the same conic back by a projective line recovers the original
six-point moduli. Likewise, the dual map followed by its rational inverse
is the identity on its common domain. Inserting a coordinate permutation
between these maps gives a row permutation under the outer-automorphism
dictionary, so it still supplies no new projective six-tuple. Gale duality
also fixes the six-point conic locus in this construction.
[The geometric identifications are in HMSV, Figure 1 and Section 1](https://math.stanford.edu/~vakil/files/0906.2437v1.pdf).

This does not classify arbitrary birational self-maps of either threefold.
A useful further operation would have to be specified explicitly and shown
to produce rational labeled points, a suitable arc, and a genuinely smaller
least circle radius after every common factor is removed. No such operation
has been established here.

## 4. Richelot gives a genuine moduli change but can lose rational points

For three quadratic binary factors `A,B,C`, the Richelot construction uses
the derivative brackets

```text
[A,B]=A'B-AB',       [B,C],       [C,A].
```

When their coefficient determinant is nonzero, their product defines the
isogenous genus-two branch configuration. See Bröker--Gruenewald--Lauter,
Section 3.2, for the construction and its nonsingularity condition.
[Primary published source, page 503](https://msp.org/ant/2011/5-4/ant-v5-n4-s.pdf#page=73).

Rational coefficients of these three factors do not imply rational branch
points, or even a rational projective configuration of all six points.
The following fixed example shows that no choice among the fifteen pairings
automatically repairs this problem.

Start with the six rational projective points

```text
0,1,2,3,4,5.
```

For a pairing `(ab)(cd)(ef)`, take
`A=(t-a)(t-b)`, `B=(t-c)(t-d)`, `C=(t-e)(t-f)`.
The table lists the determinant in the basis `(1,t,t^2)` and the square
classes of the discriminants of `([B,C],[C,A],[A,B])`.

| Pairing | Determinant | Three discriminant square classes |
|---|---:|---|
| `(01)(23)(45)` | `32` | `3,15,3` |
| `(01)(24)(35)` | `19` | `-3,30,6` |
| `(01)(25)(34)` | `12` | `1,2,10` |
| `(02)(13)(45)` | `19` | `6,30,-3` |
| `(02)(14)(35)` | `21` | `-2,5,-2` |
| `(02)(15)(34)` | `23` | `3,6,-15` |
| `(03)(12)(45)` | `-12` | `2,10,1` |
| `(03)(14)(25)` | `4` | `-2,-5,-2` |
| `(03)(15)(24)` | `9` | `1,-2,-5` |
| `(04)(12)(35)` | `-23` | `6,-15,3` |
| `(04)(13)(25)` | `-9` | `-2,-5,1` |
| `(04)(15)(23)` | `7` | `3,3,-15` |
| `(05)(12)(34)` | `-28` | `3,6,6` |
| `(05)(13)(24)` | `-11` | `-3,6,6` |
| `(05)(14)(23)` | `0` | `1,1,1` |

The last pairing is degenerate. For each of the other fourteen, the square
classes admit a Galois sign choice that swaps the two roots of exactly one
factor and fixes the four roots of the other two factors. This is directly
visible for a repeated class and an independent third class. In the other
rows it follows from independence of the two or three nontrivial displayed
classes. The exact certificate checks this as linear algebra over `F_2`.
Some binary factors have a root at infinity; it is rational and causes no
exception to the argument.

Choose three of the fixed branch points and one of the swapped branch
points. Their cross-ratio changes under this Galois automorphism, since a
cross-ratio with three distinct entries fixed is an injective fractional-
linear function of its fourth entry. It is therefore not rational.
Consequently none of the fourteen nonsingular outputs is projectively
equivalent, even over the algebraic closure, to a six-tuple of rational
points. All projective cross-ratios of such a rational tuple would be
rational. This also rules out representing the whole output as six integer
points on any rationally parametrized circle.

For the consecutive pairing, the output factors are proportional to

```text
2t^2-14t+23,       2t^2-10t+5,       2t^2-6t+3.
```

Their roots involve `sqrt(3)` and `sqrt(15)`. The automorphism fixing
`sqrt(3)` and negating `sqrt(15)` already gives the stated obstruction.

The standard-library certificate
[check_richelot_rational_closure.py](check_richelot_rational_closure.py)
checks all fifteen pairings, the determinants and discriminant classes,
pairwise binary resultants, and the Galois sign condition. The argument
is a limitation of this proposed transformation, not an endpoint-profile
counterexample and not a proof that every specially chosen Richelot
operation must fail on endpoint data.

## 5. A different rational involution: the deck transformation

The preceding exclusions do not cover every rational joint operation.
Indeed, the quartic `J` in Section 2 is quadratic in its fifth coordinate:

```text
J=(u-v)^2 w_0^2
  -2((pq+uv)(u+v)-2uv(p+q)) w_0
  +(pq-uv)^2.
```

Thus, on the generic domain where the expressions are defined, there is
an actual involution

```text
(p,u,v,q,w_0) -> (p,u,v,q,(pq-uv)^2/((u-v)^2 w_0)).
```

It exchanges the two points above the projection to `[p:u:v:q]`.
Equivalently, this is projection from `[0:0:0:0:1]` on the quartic.
The discriminant of the displayed quadratic is exactly

```text
16uv(p-u)(p-v)(q-u)(q-v).
```

The involution therefore reverses the square root in this double-cover
description. Composing with the forward and inverse polar maps gives a
rational involution of the six-point moduli on a nonempty open domain.
It is a different construction from applying the inverse polar map
immediately, or inserting a coordinate permutation. The separate exact
chart, polynomial checks, and finite arithmetic height bounds are in
[joint_segre_involution.md](joint_segre_involution.md); no radius decrease
is asserted here.

For completeness, the conversion back to the actual circle has a precise
form. Write the original binary vectors as `v_i=(X_i,Y_i)`, their complex
representatives as `P_i=X_i+iY_i`, and
`Delta_ij=det(v_i,v_j)`. The normalization taking rows `1,2,3` to
`infinity,0,1` is

```text
t(v)=Delta(v,v_2) Delta_31 / (Delta(v,v_1) Delta_32).
```

If a new normalized point is `N/D`, an integral representative after
pulling back is

```text
W=N Delta_32 P_1-D Delta_31 P_2.                         (1)
```

This formula is independent of the common scaling of `(N,D)` after
primitive reduction. It fixes the first three original projective rows.
Its phase distance from the original anchor is governed exactly by

```text
|Im W|/|W|
 = |N Delta_32 Y_1-D Delta_31 Y_2|
   / |N Delta_32 P_1-D Delta_31 P_2|.                    (2)
```

To obtain the least integral circle radius while retaining the original
anchor, divide the real coordinate content out of every `W`, then divide
by `1+i` if both remaining coordinates are odd. Call the resulting
conjugate-primitive Gaussian integers `d_i`. The exact least radius is
the modulus of their Gaussian least common multiple, including the
unchanged rows. This follows from the integrality of each phase
`epsilon_i d_i/bar(d_i)`, as in
[the least-radius construction](projective_radius_inflation.md#4-primitive-reduction-and-the-exact-least-radius-construction).

Equations (1)--(2) isolate two costs that a moduli-coordinate height alone
does not settle. The denominator in (2) can suffer an additional real
cancellation, and the least common multiple depends on the Gaussian
contents of the lifted coordinates. These require an actual arithmetic
audit on the full cut profile. The involution is a concrete new joint
map. Its ordered real-arc behavior and its standalone six-point radius are now
settled in the companion notes summarized below.

The first height audit now has a rigorous correction to its generic
answer. In matching coordinates the five chart factors are

```text
F=C-B, H=C+B, U=B+C+2D, V=2A+B+C,
G=2A+B+C+2D+2E.
```

The transformed matching vector is proportional to
`(-AFUG,-BUGV,CUGV,-DFGV,-EF^3)`. Every pair of the five forms has
a sum or difference equal to twice one of the fifteen old matching
products. Hence at most one form can be small compared with all old
matching products, and some output monomial avoids it. The maximum raw
output coordinate has log magnitude `192w+o(w)`.

The generic common core content is `144w`, but the actual common content
can have an extra contribution at six cuts:

```text
{1,6}, {2,5}, {3,4}, and their complements in {1,...,6}.
```

At each, the exact valuation constraints permit one additional core
exponent. For instance, on the collision boundary `a=1`, the normalized
matching coordinates have `A=E=0` and `G=U=bc-1`. The additional condition
`bc=1` makes all five transformed matching coordinates vanish. This is an
actual extra base locus and cannot be removed by substituting generic
cut valuations.

[check_joint_segre_local_content.py](check_joint_segre_local_content.py)
verifies all individual two-matching decompositions, all ten pair
identities, and all sixty-four local valuation cases. The maximum common
core count is `150`, with one extra at exactly those six cuts. Its ceiling
argument covers real normalized valuations. For the error transfer, let
`B_p` be the sum of the fifteen bracket-correction valuations plus `v_p(2)`.
When the core exponent `e>B_p`, replace each actual form valuation `v`
by `ceil((v-B_p)/e)`. This satisfies the exact table constraints, including
the rule that unequal valuations in a pair have minimum equal to the
matching's core order. The output gcd valuation is at most the tabulated
count times `e`, plus `4B_p`. If `e<=B_p`, every pair of forms has minimum
at most `4B_p`, so an output avoiding the possible single larger form has
valuation at most `16B_p`. Since `sum B_p log p=o(w)`, these bounds charge
all residue and correction contributions to `o(w)`. Thus
the actual transformed projective height satisfies

```text
42w-o(w) <= h(new Segre point) <= 48w+o(w).                (3)
```

In particular, the tempting exact value `48w+o(w)` is not proved by this
audit. The lower bound yields only `log R_new>=28w-o(w)` for an endpoint
realization, leaving room below the parent scale `log R=32w+o(w)`.
These are verified restrictions on this new map, not a radius descent.
The subsequent [five-cut theorem](joint_involution_five_cut_inflation.md)
first proves `R_new>=R_old^(193/192-o(1))` with the original anchor
retained. The stronger [standalone theorem](joint_involution_standalone_inflation.md)
uses sixteen actual cut inequalities to prove
`R_new>=R_old^(49/48-o(1))` for the arc-preserving ordering after
deleting that anchor, allowing any common rotation. Every labeling of
the fixed-three pullback has normalized arc length at least
`exp(w/3-o(w))`. This resolves this joint-map candidate without assuming
its generic divisor table is actual.

## 6. The published quadratic self-map has uniform height growth

Mukai gives a degree-eight self-morphism of the Igusa quartic, represented
by five quadratic forms. His equation (5) is our equation `J` after the
substitution `(y_0,y_1,y_2,y_3,y_4)=(u,v,w_0,-p,-q)`. The linear change

```text
(x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=(u,v,w_0,u+v+w_0-p-q,p-q)
```

puts it in the Steiner form
`(x_4^2-Q(x))^2=64x_0x_1x_2x_3`, where
`Q(x)=sum_(i<4)x_i^2-2sum_(i<j<4)x_i x_j`.
In these coordinates the published map is

```text
(L_0^2,L_1^2,L_2^2,L_3^2,2(Q(x)-x_4^2)),
L_0=x_0+x_1+x_2+x_3,  L_1=x_0+x_1-x_2-x_3,
L_2=x_0-x_1+x_2-x_3,  L_3=x_0-x_1-x_2+x_3.
```

See [Mukai, Proposition 1 and equations (2)--(5)](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~mukai/paper/Igusa-Steiner.pdf).
This map is different from the deck involution in Section 5. Mukai's
linear reflection interchanges `p,q` in our coordinates.

Here is a complete elementary height check. The five quadrics have no
common projective zero over characteristic zero: vanishing of their first
four coordinates forces `L_i=0`, the invertible Hadamard matrix then
forces `x_0=...=x_3=0`, and the last coordinate forces `x_4=0`.

Take a primitive integral input with maximum coordinate `H`. The maximum
absolute value of the five raw outputs is at most `34H^2`. It is at least
`H^2/64`: if `max_(i<4)|x_i|>=H/8`, the inverse Hadamard formula gives
`max|L_i|>=H/8`; otherwise `|x_4|=H` and `|Q(x)|<=H^2/4`, so the last
output has magnitude at least `3H^2/2`.

An odd prime dividing all five outputs would divide all four `L_i`,
hence `x_0,...,x_3`, and then `x_4`; this contradicts primitivity.
If `2^5` divided all outputs, the four `L_i` would be divisible by `8`.
The inverse Hadamard formula makes `x_0,...,x_3` even; the last output
then makes `x_4` even as well. Consequently the raw coordinate gcd is
at most `16`. After primitive normalization the output height satisfies

```text
2 log H-log(1024) <= h(output) <= 2 log H+log(34).          (4)
```

The fixed rational linear changes of coordinates alter these logarithmic
heights only by bounded constants. Thus the Mukai map has
`h(output)=2h(input)+O(1)` uniformly on rational points. It does not
give projective-height descent. This conclusion does not identify
projective height with circle radius without the additional endpoint
profile hypotheses.

[check_mukai_height_map.py](check_mukai_height_map.py) verifies the exact
polynomial identity `S(map(x))=16S(x)(S(x)+4 product L_i)`, where `S` is
the defining Steiner quartic. It also checks the stated primitive-content
and height inequalities on all `16,322` primitive integral vectors in
`[-3,3]^5`. The unrestricted inequalities follow from the argument above,
not from the finite enumeration.

## Remaining arithmetic gap

The polar linear system supplies a useful exact height comparison and a
geometric interpretation of the invariant kernel. The standard symmetry
loop preserves moduli, while Richelot need not preserve rational point
configurations. The new deck involution preserves rational moduli and
admits an ordered arc-preserving choice, but that choice necessarily
inflates its least radius even after removing the original anchor.
Every labeling of the fixed-three pullback fails bounded normalized
endpoint scale. Subsequent arbitrary fractional-linear changes of the
output directions and other joint maps remain outside this theorem.
No uniform endpoint bound has been proved here.
