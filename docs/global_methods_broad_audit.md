# Broad audit of genuinely global methods

## Status

This note surveys several nonlocal frameworks for the endpoint arc problem. None currently proves the uniform bound. The point is to identify which global theories genuinely retain the Gaussian conductor and which collapse back to previously known critical obstructions.

## Setup

Write the split conductor as

```text
N = product_j p_j^(e_j),
H = log N,
```

and the normalized angular counting measure as

```text
mu_N = convolution_j mu_j,
```

where `mu_j` is uniform on the arithmetic progression of angles generated by the prime block `p_j^(e_j)`.

The target is an absolute bound for the number of atoms in an interval of length `C N^(-1/4)`.

---

## 1. Adelic potential theory and capacity

A natural global object is the Vandermonde energy

```text
E_v(A) = - sum_(i<j) log |u_i-u_j|_v
```

at every place `v`, coupled by the product formula.

At the complex place, an endpoint cluster gives a very large positive energy. At finite split places, the energy is determined by the threshold cuts of the exponent code.

However, summing the pair energies over places reproduces exactly the weighted cut functional already analyzed by the determinant and Plotkin arguments. Balanced binary cut systems attain the critical value. Standard logarithmic capacity and transfinite-diameter methods are therefore pairwise energies in disguise and cannot create a strict endpoint gain.

A genuinely higher-order adelic capacity might still be different, but the established one-variable potential theory is not enough.

Verdict: standard adelic capacity fails by reduction to pairwise cut energy.

---

## 2. Sum-product and incidence geometry

Let `U` be the set of norm-one Gaussian ratios represented by the cluster. Because `U` lies in a tiny complex arc, one might hope that it has small additive growth while remaining multiplicatively structured.

This is false in the relevant sparse regime. A finite subset of a strictly convex arc can have essentially maximal additive doubling, with nearly all sums distinct. Its quotient set can also be essentially maximal when the exponent vectors are Sidon-like.

Consequently generic complex sum-product inequalities are automatically satisfied and give no contradiction. Incidence bounds for chords or secants have the same issue: the set can have quadratically many distinct directions and intercepts.

Verdict: generic expansion theory is too coarse; the bad sparse regime already expands strongly.

---

## 3. Coding theory and list decoding

The threshold-layer expansion turns the exponent vectors into a weighted binary code. Endpoint closeness gives pairwise weighted distance at least `H/2-O(1)`.

This is exactly the Plotkin threshold. At relative distance `1/2`, binary codes can have arbitrarily large size through balanced sign systems, Hadamard-type configurations, or random codes. List-decoding and spherical-code bounds become effective only with a strict distance gap beyond the critical threshold.

Subspace-evasive or algebraic code ideas would require additional algebraic constraints on the set of codewords. The Gaussian realization does provide such constraints, but ordinary coding theory does not see them.

Verdict: abstract coding theory stops exactly at the known critical equality.

---

## 4. Concentration compactness and ultralimits

Assuming an unbounded bad family, one can pass to a limit of weighted cut distributions or orientation processes. Local errors become exact equalities, producing a balanced infinite sign process.

But abstract balanced processes exist. The limit forgets the actual Gaussian prime arguments unless one simultaneously retains an arithmetic structure at every scale. Without that extra data, compactness reconstructs the fake Plotkin extremizers rather than ruling them out.

Verdict: useful as an organizational framework, not a contradiction mechanism.

---

## 5. Optimal transport, log-Sobolev, and concentration inequalities

The divisor measure is a pushforward of a product measure under a linear angular map. Tensorized concentration inequalities control typical fluctuations in terms of coordinate Lipschitz constants.

The relevant arithmetic weight is `log p_j`, while the analytic Lipschitz size is the angle `theta_j`. These quantities can be almost unrelated. A very large prime can have a tiny angle or vice versa. Thus Gaussian concentration bounds are governed by the wrong metric.

Conditioning on the rare endpoint event creates dependence among all coordinates, and the available transport/entropy inequalities measure its total probability rather than its maximum fiber cardinality.

Verdict: probability concentration uses the wrong coordinate geometry.

---

## 6. Zero-sum and additive-combinatorial theorems

Quantizing the angular map modulo a fine grid turns an endpoint cluster into a large fiber of an approximate homomorphism. Zero-sum theorems can then produce short additive relations among the exponent differences.

The coefficients or support lengths produced are controlled by the modulus, which is of order `N^(1/4)`, or by the ambient rank. Clearing Gaussian denominators puts these relations back at the half-conductor threshold. This is another form of the linear-annihilator criticality.

Verdict: generic zero-sum relations are too expensive arithmetically.

---

## 7. Arithmetic unlikely intersections

The divisor points live in a high-dimensional multiplicative torus, while the angular map is one real character. One may ask whether many conductor-box points can lie near one real-analytic level set without lying in a proper algebraic subgroup.

Classical unlikely-intersection theorems concern exact algebraic intersections, bounded height, or fixed ambient dimension. Here the dimension and subgroup support vary with the conductor, and the condition is archimedean approximation rather than exact algebraic incidence.

This language may become useful after a bounded-rank reduction, but it currently does not produce that reduction.

Verdict: promising endgame language, not a present reduction theorem.

---

## 8. Inverse theory for rare fibers of product random walks

This is the strongest surviving direction.

Let independent variables `X_j` have laws `mu_j`, and let

```text
S = sum_j X_j mod 2 pi.
```

The endpoint problem asks for a worst-case bound on the cardinality of one fiber interval of `S`, not merely its probability under a fixed-density regime.

Existing inverse Littlewood--Offord theory says that abnormally large small-ball probability forces most coefficients into a low-rank generalized arithmetic progression. Existing inverse random-walk results similarly classify large discrepancy from uniformity in abelian groups.

The needed theorem must be stronger and weighted:

> If one interval of width `exp(-H/4)` contains an unbounded number of product atoms, then either a bounded set of prime blocks carries a positive fraction of `H`, or a positive fraction of the conductor-weighted angles lies in one bounded-rank rational progression with quantitative accuracy tied to `H`.

The first branch gives descent. In the second branch, Gaussian unique factorization or algebraic approximation to roots of unity could give a contradiction.

Unlike Shannon entropy, this framework targets the maximum concentration function directly. Unlike ordinary inverse Littlewood--Offord, it must use conductor weights `e_j log p_j` rather than coordinate count.

Verdict: live, but requires a new weighted rare-fiber inverse theorem.

---

## 9. A possible route to the weighted theorem

The natural concentration function is

```text
Q_delta(mu) = sup_x mu([x-delta,x+delta]).
```

Partition the prime blocks into packets of comparable conductor height. Apply a concentration-function inequality after each convolution. If a packet does not reduce `Q_delta`, an inverse step should place that packet near the approximate stabilizer of the current high-mass interval.

On the circle, approximate stabilizers of a short interval are neighborhoods of finite cyclic subgroups. Repeated failure of flattening would therefore force many packets to align with one common rational grid.

The essential novelty is persistence: the same approximate stabilizer must survive through many independent packets and many scales. This could amplify ordinary endpoint-scale closeness into supercritical closeness, which Gaussian algebraic separation can rule out.

The missing theorem is a concentration-function analogue of entropy flattening with constants uniform for a vanishing exceptional mass fraction.

---

## Conclusion

The broad audit eliminates several apparently global approaches because they secretly reduce to pairwise energy, abstract coding, or average concentration.

The strongest remaining program is:

```text
rare endpoint fiber
  -> repeated failure of concentration-function flattening
  -> persistent approximate stabilizer on the circle
  -> common rational grid across many conductor packets
  -> Gaussian arithmetic contradiction or heavy-block descent.
```

This is genuinely nonlocal and targets the worst interval directly. It is also not currently supplied by a standard theorem in the required weighted form.
