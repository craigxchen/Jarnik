# Prime-to-contact saturation is exactly the source Smith defect

This note isolates the part of the canonical-product index that can be
proved without making any assertion at primes dividing the contact moduli.
Let

\[
D_{\rm ct}=\prod_e d_e,
\qquad
L_i(X,Y)=\det(v_i,(X,Y)),
\]

where every source vector \(v_i\in\mathbf Z^2\) is primitive and the
source forms span rank two.  For fixed degree \(d\) and jet order \(h\),
write \(\Gamma_{d,h}\) for the contact-jet lattice and \(\Lambda_{d,h}\)
for the span of the canonical weighted products from
[signed_source_product_saturation.md](signed_source_product_saturation.md).

## Local statement

Fix a prime \(p\nmid D_{\rm ct}\), and put
\[
g=\gcd_{i<j}\left|\det(v_i,v_j)\right|,
\qquad a=v_p(g).
\]
Since every \(d_e\) is a \(p\)-adic unit, every canonical multiplier
\(d_e^{\max(h-n(C_e),0)}\) is a unit.  Moreover each contact ideal is the
unit ideal over \(\mathbf Z_p\), so
\[
\Gamma_{d,h}\otimes\mathbf Z_p
 =V_d:=\mathbf Z_p[X,Y]_d.
\]
If
\[
K_p=\sum_i\mathbf Z_pL_i\subset V_1:=\mathbf Z_pX+\mathbf Z_pY,
\]
then
\[
\Lambda_{d,h}\otimes\mathbf Z_p
 =\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}^d(K_p)
              \longrightarrow \operatorname{Sym}^d(V_1)=V_d\bigr).
\tag{1}
\]

For completeness, (1) uses ordinary binary forms.  If \(A_1,A_2\) is a
basis of \(K_p\), then the monomials
\[
A_1^{d-k}A_2^k,\qquad 0\leq k\leq d,
\]
are a \(\mathbf Z_p\)-basis of \(\operatorname{Sym}^d(K_p)\), and their
images are the usual products of linear forms.  There are no divided-power
or binomial normalizations here.  Every product of \(d\) elements of
\(K_p\) expands, by multilinearity, as a \(\mathbf Z_p\)-linear
combination of these monomials.  Since the \(L_i\) generate \(K_p\),
expanding each factor also shows that products of the source forms
generate all of \(\operatorname{Sym}^d(K_p)\).  This proves (1).

The Smith invariants of \(K_p\subset V_1\) are \(1,p^a\).  Indeed, form
the coefficient matrix whose columns are \((-s_i,r_i)^t\) when
\(v_i=(r_i,s_i)\).  Primitivity of each \(v_i\) makes the minimum valuation
of its entries zero, so the first Smith invariant is a unit.  The ideal of
two-by-two minors is generated by
\[
\det\bigl((-s_i,r_i),(-s_j,r_j)\bigr)=\det(v_i,v_j),
\]
so the valuation of the product of the two Smith invariants is
\(v_p(g)=a\).  Thus a pair of ambient and source bases puts \(K_p\) in the
form
\[
K_p=\mathbf Z_p e_1\oplus p^a\mathbf Z_p e_2,
\qquad V_1=\mathbf Z_p e_1\oplus\mathbf Z_p e_2.
\]

Taking ordinary symmetric powers gives the basis comparison
\[
e_1^{d-k}e_2^k
\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
p^{ak}e_1^{d-k}e_2^k,
\qquad 0\leq k\leq d.
\]
Consequently
\[
v_p[\Gamma_{d,h}:\Lambda_{d,h}]
 =v_p[V_d:\operatorname{im}\operatorname{Sym}^d(K_p)]
 =a\sum_{k=0}^d k
 =\frac{d(d+1)}2\,v_p(g).
\tag{2}
\]
The right side is independent of \(h\).  Formula (2) is asserted only for
\(p\nmid D_{\rm ct}\); it makes no claim at contact primes.

## Exact check against the existing Luna frame

For the frame used by the canonical-product checker,
\[
v_0=(1,2),\quad v_1=(4,5),\quad v_2=(7,8),
\]
the pair determinants are \(-3,-6,-3\), hence \(g=3\).  The contact
moduli are \(5,41,113\), so \(3\nmid D_{\rm ct}=5\cdot41\cdot113\).  Formula
(2) predicts the relative factors
\[
3^{1}=3,\qquad 3^{3}=27,\qquad 3^{6}=729
\]
for degrees \(d=1,2,3\), respectively, regardless of \(h\).  Running
`python3 docs/check_canonical_product_span.py` gives exactly these values:

```text
jet=1, degree=1: [Gamma:Lambda]=3
jet=1, degree=2: [Gamma:Lambda]=27
jet=1, degree=3: [Gamma:Lambda]=729
jet=2, degree=1: [Gamma:Lambda]=3
jet=2, degree=2: [Gamma:Lambda]=27
jet=2, degree=3: [Gamma:Lambda]=729
```

This identifies the observed power of \(3\) as the symmetric-power Smith
defect of the primitive source-form lattice.  It does not supply a
coefficient-height bound for signed representations and does not address
the nonkernel height estimate.
