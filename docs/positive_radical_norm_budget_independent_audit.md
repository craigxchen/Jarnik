# Independent audit of the positive-radical norm budget

The seven-point positivity and norm argument is sound after fixing its sign
normalization to the intrinsic one in
[`seven_positive_radical_height.md`](seven_positive_radical_height.md).
The odd-point extension is also algebraically sound under its stated full
marked-profile, reduced-metric, and bounded-chart hypotheses.  It is a
conditional norm budget, not an extraction theorem for larger point sets and
not a uniform circle bound.

## 1. Signs, primitivity, and the Gale zero sum

For chart-oriented marked vectors put

```text
Q_i=q(v_i),  D_i=product_(j!=i)det(v_i,v_j),
rho_i^2=Q_i^5/D_i^2,
tau=gcd_Q(rho_1^2,...,rho_7^2),
c_i=rho_i^2/tau,  sigma_i=sign(D_i).                (1)
```

The rational gcd takes the minimum at every prime.  Consequently every
`c_i` is a positive integer and their ordinary gcd is one.  The inverse Gale
construction identifies them with the coprime metric values on a saturated
integral basis.

The relation with an actual saturated basis removes a possible sign
ambiguity.  If its rows are `b_i`, put

```text
ell_i=gcd(b_i),  u_i=epsilon_i b_i/ell_i,
L=product_i ell_i,  E=product_i epsilon_i,
s=gcd_i sqrt(-product_(j!=i)det(b_i,b_j)).
```

Then `u_i` have positive first coordinate and

```text
K=-E s^2/L,
epsilon_i ell_i=K q(u_i)^2/D_i,
tau=L^2/s^4=1/K^2,
sigma_i=-E epsilon_i.                              (2)
```

These formulas follow directly by extracting the row contents and signs
from the negative square stars
`product_(j!=i)det(b_i,b_j)=-s^2 c_i^2`.  They retain

```text
sum_i epsilon_i ell_i u_i=sum_i b_i=0.              (3)
```

Thus the positive radical

```text
R=sum_i sigma_i sqrt(c_i)
```

is, up to the harmless global sign in (2), the basis-oriented radical.  The
same signs and normalization realize positivity and the actual Gale zero
row sum simultaneously.  The earlier wording with unspecified
`epsilon_i` could obscure this point; the norm note has been aligned with
the intrinsic `rho,tau,c,sigma` convention.

## 2. The divided difference is exactly nonzero

For odd `n`, chart orientation gives `x_i>0`.  With `t_i=y_i/x_i`,
`X=product_i x_i`, and `P(t)=product_i(t-t_i)`, parity gives exactly

```text
D_i=x_i^(n-2) X P'(t_i),
sum_i Q_i^((n-2)/2)/D_i
 =X^(-1)[t_1,...,t_n]q(1,t)^((n-2)/2).             (4)
```

There is no missing alternating sign: `n-1` is even.  Repeated
differentiation gives

```text
(d/dt)^(n-1) q(1,t)^((n-2)/2)
 =((n-2)!!)^2 det(q)^((n-1)/2)q(1,t)^(-n/2)>0.      (5)
```

The real divided-difference mean-value theorem therefore makes (4) strictly
positive.  For seven points, division by `sqrt(tau)` gives the integral
radical in (1).  Under the stated reduced-metric and bounded-chart profile,
all factors in (5) except `X^(-1)` have subpower size, so the distinguished
value has logarithm `-77w+o(w)` exactly.  This size conclusion depends on
those profile hypotheses; positivity alone gives no endpoint estimate.

## 3. Field norm and the degree-eight conclusion

The radical is an algebraic integer.  Its square lies in the multiquadratic
field generated by the pair radicals, so
`d=[Q(R^2):Q]` is a power of two and at most `64`.  Every embedding extends
to the full multiquadratic field, where a conjugate is a permitted signed
sum.  Hence every conjugate of `R^2` is positive and at most
`H^2`, where `H=sum_i sqrt(c_i)`.

The distinguished conjugate and the other `d-1` bounds give

```text
1 <= Norm(R^2) <= |R|^2 H^(2(d-1)),
log Norm(R^2) <= (38d-192)w+o(w).                  (6)
```

For `d=1,2,4` the leading exponent is negative; for `d=8` it is positive.
Since `d` is a power of two, a hypothetical seven-point full fair sequence
must eventually have `d>=8`.  Counting `R` and `-R` as two small conjugates
of `R^2` would be incorrect; (6) counts the one distinguished value of
`R^2` exactly once.

## 4. Clean primes and saturation

At a clean cut of minority size `s`, the odd radicand exponent is
`(n-2s)e` on the minority and zero on the majority.  In the exact family
`q(t)=1+t^2`, specialization of the minority nodes to a root of `q` leaves
the majority squareclasses `q(t_j)`.  The divisor `t_j=r` occurs to odd
order only in that class, so the majority classes are independent in the
generic function field.  Their signed radicals are therefore linearly
independent, and no signed majority sum vanishes generically.

The displayed `F_1009` specializations retain a unit two-by-two minor among
the majority Gale rows.  Thus their row lattice already has rank two modulo
the clean prime and passage to the saturated lattice changes the local data
only by units.  Direct calculation finds every signed sum nonzero in
`F_(1009^2)`; the universal signed-sum product is consequently a unit.
This validates the claimed absence of a universal clean-prime norm divisor.
It supplies local counterexamples to divisibility, not fair endpoint
configurations.

The ramification calculation is also correctly limited to parity.  At an
odd prime, a multiquadratic extension with at least one odd radicand
valuation has tame inertia two, giving discriminant exponent half the field
degree.  Even cut depth removes ramification rather than contributing its
full depth, and discriminant divisibility cannot be transferred to the norm.

## 5. Odd-point scope

For fixed odd `n=2k+1`, the fair incidence identities give

```text
A=(n-1)2^(n-3)-na/2,  B=na/2,  A+B=(n-1)2^(n-3).
```

The norm exponent is `2Ad-2(n-1)2^(n-3)`, so the necessary degree is the
first power of two at least

```text
(n-1)/[n*binomial(2k,k)/4^k-1].                    (7)
```

The threshold grows only on the order of `sqrt(n)`, while the permitted
multiquadratic degree is `2^(n-1)`.  The full-degree exponent is positive,
so increasing `n` gives no contradiction by itself.  Moreover, the note
assumes the full marked valuation profile, subpower denominator clearing,
a reduced metric with controlled inverse, and a bounded affine chart for
each fixed `n`.  None of those statements is proved there as a uniform
larger-point extraction.

The independent checker
[`check_positive_radical_norm_budget_independent.py`](check_positive_radical_norm_budget_independent.py)
uses exact Pythagorean configurations at seven and nine points to verify the
signs, rational gcd, integral radicands, positive divided difference, and—in
the seven-point case—the Gale zero sum and primitive square-star
normalization.  It separately checks clean-cut saturation and signed-sum
units modulo `1009`, the degree-eight transition, and every displayed
odd-point budget through `n=101`.  All checks pass.
