# A positive seven-radical defect and a low-degree height exclusion

There is a strictly positive arithmetic quantity attached to the seven
Gale lengths. On the full fair profile its logarithm is `-77w+o(w)`,
whereas each length has logarithm `19w+o(w)`. Its field norm gives an
actual height inequality and excludes radical-square degree at most
four. It does not exclude the general fair profile.

## 1. Normalize the integer radicals before taking the small sum

Choose rational-circle marked vectors `v_i=(x_i,y_i)` and a positive
rational quadratic `q`, with square determinant, representing the same
circle directions. Reorient the vectors so that all `x_i>0`, after
choosing a chart avoiding the seven directions. Write

```text
Q_i=q(v_i),       D_i=product_(j different from i)det(v_i,v_j),
rho_i^2=Q_i^5/D_i^2,
tau=gcd_Q(rho_1^2,...,rho_7^2),
c_i=rho_i^2/tau,        sigma_i=sign(D_i).           (1)
```

The rational gcd is defined valuation by valuation by the minimum.
Thus the `c_i` are coprime positive integers. They are precisely the
coprime metric values in the seven-point inverse construction: the raw
Gale row is `(Q_i^2/D_i)v_i`, whose squared length in `q` is `rho_i^2`.
Changing the rational Gale basis transports the metric and does not
change these normalized values.

For an explicit link to an integer basis `B` of the saturated lattice,
let its rows be `b_i`, normalize the transported metric by
`q(b_i)=c_i`, and put

```text
ell_i=gcd(coordinates of b_i),       L=product_i ell_i,
s=gcd_i sqrt(-product_(j different from i)det(b_i,b_j)).
```

Choose `epsilon_i` so that `u_i=epsilon_i b_i/ell_i` has positive first
coordinate, and put `E=product_i epsilon_i`. For these marked vectors
and this metric the exact scaling identities are

```text
K=-E s^2/L,
epsilon_i ell_i = K q(u_i)^2 / product_(j different from i)det(u_i,u_j),
tau=L^2/s^4=1/K^2.                                  (1a)
```

Indeed each signed star of `B` is `-s^2 c_i^2`, while extracting all row
contents and orientations from it gives
`E epsilon_i ell_i^5 L product_(j different from i)det(u_i,u_j)`.
Equation (1a) follows using `c_i=ell_i^2 q(u_i)`. It also preserves the
actual vector relation `sum_i epsilon_i ell_i u_i=sum_i b_i=0`.
The raw sign in (1) is `sigma_i=-E epsilon_i`. Thus the positive `R`
below is `-E sum_i epsilon_i sqrt(c_i)`; the signed sum using the basis
orientations has the same absolute value and equals `K/X` times the
divided difference. On the fair profile `log s=28w+o(w)` and
`log L=56w+o(w)`, so `log|K|=o(w)` exactly as required.

In particular the following number is an algebraic integer:

```text
R=sum_i sigma_i sqrt(c_i).                          (2)
```

The positive square roots are used. Ordering the parameters increasingly
makes the signs alternate, with four positive signs and three negative
signs. The normalization `tau` must be retained; a tiny raw divided
difference does not imply a tiny integral radical sum without it.

## 2. Exact positivity from the sixth derivative

Set `t_i=y_i/x_i`, `X=product_i x_i`, `P(t)=product_i(t-t_i)`, and
`Q(t)=q(1,t)`. Since there are six factors at each label,

```text
D_i=x_i^5 X P'(t_i).
```

Consequently

```text
R = 1/(X sqrt(tau)) sum_i Q(t_i)^(5/2)/P'(t_i)
  = 1/(X sqrt(tau)) [t_1,...,t_7] Q^(5/2).          (3)
```

For `Q(t)=A+2Bt+Ct^2`, direct differentiation gives

```text
d^6/dt^6 Q(t)^(5/2)
  =225(AC-B^2)^3 Q(t)^(-7/2).                       (4)
```

This is strictly positive. The real divided-difference mean-value
theorem therefore gives a point `xi` between the extreme parameters
such that

```text
R = (5/16) det(q)^3 /
      (X sqrt(tau) Q(xi)^(7/2)) > 0.                (5)
```

In particular the radical sum is nonzero, without any assumption that
its seven square classes are independent.

Let `q_min` and `q_max` be the minimum and maximum of `Q(t)` on that
interval. Formula (5) gives explicit upper and lower bounds by replacing
`Q(xi)` with these values. Every metric and chart factor remains visible.

## 3. An actual arithmetic height inequality

Let

```text
M=sum_i sqrt(c_i),          d=[Q(R^2):Q].
```

The number `R^2` is a nonzero algebraic integer in the multiquadratic
field generated by the pair products `sqrt(c_i c_j)`. Every conjugate
of `R^2` has absolute value at most `M^2`: extend its embedding to the
field generated by all seven square roots and use the triangle
inequality. Its nonzero integer norm hence proves

```text
R >= M^(-(d-1)).                                    (6)
```

Combining this with the upper bound in (5) yields the necessary global
inequality

```text
X sqrt(tau) <= (5/16) det(q)^3 q_min^(-7/2) M^(d-1). (7)
```

No generic finite-place divisor or polynomial cut-order gain is used.
Since every subfield of a multiquadratic extension is Galois of
power-of-two degree, `d` is a power of two, at most 64. Taking the degree
of `R^2` handles possible missing global sign reversal and any further
coincidences among its conjugates automatically.

## 4. The strict saving in the low-degree regime

Use the full marked-vector profile and its accumulated `o(w)` error
budget, as in the majority normalization note. With the reduced metric
of subpower coefficients and eigenvalues, choose one of the two
coordinate axes so that the clustered directions have
`x_i=exp(11w+o(w))` after reorientation. The coordinate change costs
only a fixed factor. Thus

```text
log X=77w+o(w),          log det(q)=o(w),
log q_min=o(w),          log q_max=o(w).
```

The normalization in (1) has no hidden leading cost. At a clean cut
with minority size `s<=3` and depth `e`,

```text
v_p(rho_i^2)=(7-2s)e if i is in the minority,
                 0 otherwise.
```

Their minimum is zero. The fixed finite minimum defining `tau`
therefore has total absolute logarithmic valuation `o(w)` under the
stated full-profile error hypothesis. Hence `tau` has subpower rational
height. This step would fail if one only controlled the selected core
primes and ignored extra conductor.

Equations (1), (5), and the known fair metric-value exponents now give

```text
log c_i=38w+o(w),       log M=19w+o(w),
log R=-77w+o(w).                                    (8)
```

If `d<=4`, (6) gives `log R>=-57w+o(w)`, contradicting (8).
Every sufficiently large hypothetical full fair configuration must
therefore satisfy

```text
[Q(R^2):Q] >= 8.                                    (9)
```

This is a strict exclusion of the low radical-degree symmetry regime.
Its scope is clarified by the subsequent
[squareclass identification](gale_radical_squareclass_identification.md):
the relative classes `[c_i c_0]` are exactly the full half-angle norm
classes of the original circle points. In the extracted common-unit
endpoint case the earlier six-wise separation theorem already makes
all six classes independent, so the degree is in fact 64. Thus (9)
does not exclude an additional extracted endpoint case.
For `d=8`, however, the norm lower bound is only
`R>=exp(-133w+o(w))`, leaving a `56w` gap. The general norm audit and
its lack of forced generic clean-prime content are treated separately
in [the odd-point norm note](odd_point_positive_radical_norm_budget.md).

## 5. Small squarefree parts alone do not close the gap

Write `c_i=a_i^2 b_i`, with `b_i` positive squarefree. If the `b_i`
have subpower height, the coefficient bound is still
`a_i<=exp(19w+o(w))`. Small field discriminant alone does not provide
the needed general lower bound for the resulting linear form.

For an elementary comparison, fix the seven radicals

```text
1, sqrt(5), sqrt(13), sqrt(17), sqrt(65), sqrt(85), sqrt(221).
```

They are linearly independent over `Q` in the fixed degree-eight field
`Q(sqrt(5),sqrt(13),sqrt(17))`; all the radicands use only split primes.
Place the `(H+1)^6` fractional parts of the combinations of the last
six radicals, with coefficients in `{0,...,H}`, into `(H+1)^6-1`
equal intervals. Two fall in one interval. Subtracting them and choosing
the coefficient of `1` gives a nonzero integer linear form with
coefficient size `O(H)` and absolute value `O(H^(-6))`. Independence
ensures that it is not zero. As `H` grows these furnish infinitely many
coefficient vectors, because a fixed finite collection of nonzero
values cannot tend to zero.

The fair smallness in (8), expressed in coefficient height, is only
`H^(-77/19+o(1))`, with `77/19` approximately `4.053`. Thus a lower
bound strong enough for the fair target cannot follow merely from
seven terms, small squarefree radicands, and a coefficient upper bound.
The pigeonhole vectors are **not** claimed to have the prescribed
Gale signs, seven comparable coefficient sizes, or the shared cut
factorizations. Those extra properties are exactly the remaining gap.

The valuation profile also does not force large squarefree height:
all dominant cut depths may be even. Adding one fixed odd prime to
each singleton block would keep seven independent square classes at
bounded squarefree cost. This is a statement about the permitted
profile, not a construction of an actual fair endpoint family.
