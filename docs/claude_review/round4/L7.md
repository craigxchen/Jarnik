# (L_7): residues vanish, a product criterion, and why the six-point mechanism does not extend

Task: decide `(L_7)` of `threshold/L8.md` §1 (and `growth/ptolemy.md` §7). Either refute it with a
Zariski-dense supply of anchored seven-point configurations, as Theorem B of L8 does for `(L_6)`, or
prove it. Scripts are in `scratchpad/round4/L7/` (Section 10).

Conventions (as in L8).

* *Theorem*, *Proposition*, *Lemma* and *Corollary* mean proved here, with all quantifiers, or cited
  with the exact place where the proof is written.
* *Data* means an exact finite computation (Python integers and `Fraction`).
* *NUMERICAL* means floating point. It is never used as a proof.
* *Parameter count* means a parameter-minus-condition count. It is a heuristic, not a dimension
  theorem.

Notation. `k = 7` and `m = 6`. `beta` is the balanced curve class (`beta.D_S = 1` for all 56
boundary divisors). For a curve class `gamma`:

* `pair(gamma)` is the sum of `D_S.gamma` over the 21 splits of type `2|5`;
* `triple(gamma)` is the sum over the 35 splits of type `3|4`;
* `B` is the total boundary, and `psi_i` is the Kapranov hyperplane class of label `i`.

## 0. Verdict

**`(L_7)` is not decided.** I found neither a dense construction nor a proof. The round produced
four rigorous results, which sharpen what a disproof must contain. It also produced a calibrated
negative numerical search.

1. **Theorem 1: residues vanish identically.** Normalize the anchor and two other labels to `∞`,
   `0` and `1`. Then the anchoring of `ptolemy.md` Lemma 5.2 gives the following for every
   `Phi` in `M_{0,7}(Q)`:
   * `Y_j = ±1`;
   * `det(P_i,P_j) = ±prod_{T ∋ i,j} N_T(Phi)` *exactly*, where `N_T(Phi) = prod_p p^{l_T(p)}` is the
     finite contact integer of `Phi` with `D_T`.

   So `H_Sigma = 0` whenever the `p`-adic trees are admissible (at most two nested chains). A
   disproof of `(L_7)` therefore never has to control residues. It needs only Zariski-dense points
   whose 56 finite boundary heights are balanced and whose non-admissible primes carry weight
   `o(w)` (Corollary 1). The exact check passes on 400 configurations at 5208 primes.
2. **Theorem 2: three refutation criteria.** Each is proved, and each would refute `(L_7)`.
   * (a) A set of `Q`-rational curves whose classes are (approximately) proportional to `beta` and
     whose union is dense.
   * (b) A dominant map `Psi: (P^1)^R ⇢ Mbar_{0,7}` with `beta` in the **cone** of the ruling
     classes `Psi_*[l_k]`. No single curve of class `∝ beta` is needed: mixtures of unbalanced
     families suffice. The proof uses the geometric sieve.
   * (c) A dense set of positive-rank genus-one curves of class `∝ beta`, by the proof of L8
     Lemma B.5.
3. **Proposition 3: what criterion (b) requires.**
   * Some ruling family is at least as triple-rich as `beta`: `triple >= (5/3) pair`.
   * For every label `i`, some ruling family has Kapranov degree `psi_i.gamma >= 5`.
   * These exclude every coordinate chart and every product of forgetful-fibre or low-degree
     families. For example, they exclude all products of rational curves of Kapranov degree at most
     4 in one fixed model.
   * They do **not** exclude everything. Twisted cubics meeting 13 of the 20 Kapranov planes pass
     both tests, and `beta` lies in the cone of their `S_7`-orbit together with the forgetful
     fibres. So the real obstruction is the product structure, not the numerics of classes.
4. **Theorem 4 and Lemma 5: structure of balanced rational seven-curves.**
   * A balanced rational curve has **at most two** single-label forgetful maps of degree two.
     Item 112 gives at most three for genus one.
   * With two, it is in an explicit **Klein normal form**.
   * With one, it is a balanced rational *six*-curve, a double cover of it, and a 7th point that
     solves a linear system.
   * In general the 7th point always solves a `25 x 18` linear system over the six-point image
     (Lemma 5).

   Parameter counts give these expected dimensions:

   | family | dimension |
   |---|---|
   | general rational | 8 |
   | one involution | 2 |
   | Klein | 0 |
   | general genus one | 7 |
   | genus one with three involutions (the L6 mechanism) | 1 |

   A dense family needs dimension at least 3. So **the Galois–Boolean mechanism that proved
   `(L_6)` false cannot by itself be dense at `k = 7`.** The structure is proved; the dimensions
   are parameter counts.
5. **NUMERICAL.**
   * *`k = 6`.* The Klein ansatz produces **genus-zero** balanced six-curves; all 10 found are
     nondegenerate, and they are smooth points of the expected 8-dimensional family. L8 needed
     genus one; Ptolemy §5.4 found real genus-zero ones only in another chart.
   * *`k = 7`.* Eleven solver variants across four formulations (general, Klein, one-involution,
     lift; Section 7) found **no** nondegenerate balanced rational curve. The `t`-coordinate Klein formulation alone failed on 844 starts. The
     same solver succeeds on 5% of starts at `k = 6`. Every convergent `k = 7` run ended at a
     degenerate configuration. This is inconclusive, not evidence of nonexistence: no geometric
     obstruction was found.
     * Real closed walks exist (exact).
     * The (log) Bogomolov–Miyaoka–Yau inequality has slack.
6. **Exact heuristic bookkeeping.**
   * The lattice count `e^{7w}` (`e^{8w}` with the top block) and the naive complete-intersection
     count `e^{-24w}` differ by exactly the 31 dependent congruences.
   * Adding one point to a fixed balanced six-configuration has expected count `e^{-9w}`. So
     fibration-by-lifting arguments must average over the base.

**Net effect.** The decision of `(L_7)` is reduced to one arithmetic-geometric question:

> Is there a `Q`-unirational (or at least Zariski-dense in `Q`-points) family of rational or
> positive-rank elliptic curves of class `∝ beta`, or a product structure as in Theorem 2(b)?

Everything weaker is now either proved or shown not to help:
* residue control (Theorem 1);
* curve classes alone (Proposition 3);
* forgetful involutions (Theorem 4 and the counts).

## 1. Setting

The setting is that of L8 §1. An anchored configuration consists of:
* primitive `P_0 = (1,0)` and `P_1..P_6` in `Z^2`;
* positive integers `n_T` for nonempty `T ⊆ [6]`;

with `det(P_i,P_j) = t_ij prod_{T ∋ i,j} n_T`.

* `(L_7)` asks for `c > 0`, `eta_0 > 0` and a proper Zariski-closed `Z ⊂ Mbar_{0,7}` with the
  following property. Every anchored configuration with `Phi ∉ Z`, admissible blocks and
  `eta <= eta_0` has `H_Sigma >= c w - O(1)`.
* *Admissible* at `p` means that the `T` with `p | n_T` form at most two nested chains on disjoint
  row sets.
* To refute `(L_7)` it suffices to find, for every proper closed `Z`, configurations off `Z` with
  `eta -> 0`, `w -> ∞` and `H_Sigma = O(1)`. If `(L_7)` is read with all blocks balanced, the top
  and singleton blocks are inflated as in L8 Theorem B.

**Dictionary** (`ptolemy.md` Theorem B(iv), Lemma 5.2).

* `l_T(p)` is the `p`-adic tree edge length of the split `T` (the side not containing the anchor
  0). It equals the local intersection number `(Phi.D_T)_p`.
* `N_T(Phi) = prod_p p^{l_T(p)}` is the **finite contact integer**.

## 2. Theorem 1: the residues vanish identically

**Theorem 1.** Let `Phi ∈ M_{0,7}(Q)`. Choose the representing points `w_0, ..., w_6 ∈ P^1(Q)`
(primitive integer vectors) so that `w_0 = (1,0)`, `w_1 = (0,1)` and `w_2 = (1,1)`; this is
possible by `PGL_2(Q)`. Let `P_0, ..., P_6` be the anchored vectors of `ptolemy.md` Lemma 5.2:

```text
D = lcm_j |y_j|,    y_j = det(w_0,w_j),    P_j = (D x_j/|y_j|, sgn y_j).
```

Then for every `1 <= i < j <= 6`:

```text
det(P_0,P_j) = ±1,      det(P_i,P_j) = ± prod_{T ⊆ [6], T ∋ i,j, |T| <= 5} N_T(Phi).      (2.1)
```

Hence, with `n_T := N_T(Phi)` for the visible `T`, `n_[6] := 1` and arbitrary singletons, all
`t_ij = ±1` and `H_Sigma = 0`.

*Proof.*

* Lemma 5.2 gives `v_p(det(P_i,P_j)) = delta_p + sum_{T ∋ i,j} l_T(p)` at every prime. Here
  `delta_p` is the distance, along the ray towards the end `0`, from the branch point `b_p` to the
  vertex `L_{r_p}`, where `r_p = max_j (0|j)_o` and `o` is the standard vertex.
* With ends `w_0, w_1, w_2 = ∞, 0, 1`, the standard vertex `o = Z_p^2` is their median. So `o` lies
  on the geodesic `(1,2)`, which is inside the subtree spanned by the ends `1..6`.
* For each `j >= 1`, the geodesic `(0,j)` meets the ray `[o,0)` in a segment `[x_j, 0)`, and
  `(0|j)_o = d(o,x_j)`. The point of the ray farthest from `o` that still lies in the subtree is
  `b_p = x_{j*}` for the maximizing `j*`. Hence `r_p = d(o,b_p)` and `L_{r_p} = b_p`, so
  `delta_p = 0`.
* The signs come from `sgn y_j`. `[]`

*Data* (`anchor_check.py`, exact):

* The check uses 400 random configurations normalized as above, and 5208 (configuration, prime)
  pairs. It computes `l_T(p)` from the Gromov products of the *original* vectors by the min-formula
  of `ptolemy.md` B(iv). It finds 0 mismatches in (2.1), and `Y_j = ±1` always.
* 568 of the 5208 trees were *non-admissible*, with three or more chains. So admissibility is a
  genuine restriction, but it is the only one.

**Corollary 1 (residue-free form).** For `Phi ∈ M_{0,7}(Q)`, define the **admissibility defect**

```text
delta(Phi) := sum over primes p whose tree is not admissible of  sum_{i<j} v_p(det(P_i,P_j)) log p,
```

with the normalized anchored rows of Theorem 1. Consider the statement:

> **`(L_7)*`.** There are `eps > 0`, a proper closed `Z` and `W_0` such that every
> `Phi ∈ M_{0,7}(Q) \ Z` satisfying
> `|log N_T(Phi) - w| <= eps w` for all 56 `T` and `delta(Phi) <= eps w`
> has `w <= W_0`.

Then `(L_7)` implies `(L_7)*`. In particular, to refute `(L_7)` it suffices to refute `(L_7)*`:
residues never need to be estimated.

*Proof.*

* Take `(c, eta_0, Z, C_0)` from `(L_7)`, and set `eps = min(eta_0, c)/2`.
* Given `Phi` as in `(L_7)*`, let `n_T := prod_{p admissible} p^{l_T(p)}`, and move the
  non-admissible `p`-parts into the `t_ij`.
* By (2.1), `t_ij = ±prod_{p bad} p^{v_p(det(P_i,P_j))}`. So
  `H_Sigma = sum_{i<j} log|t_ij| = delta(Phi) <= eps w`.
* Also `l_T(p) <= v_p(det(P_i,P_j))` for `i,j ∈ T`, so `0 <= log N_T - log n_T <= delta(Phi)`.
  Hence `|log n_T - w| <= 2 eps w <= eta_0 w`.
* The blocks are admissible. `(L_7)` gives `eps w >= H_Sigma >= c w - C_0`, so
  `w <= 2C_0/c =: W_0`. `[]`

*Remarks.*

1. Theorem 1 makes precise the sentence of `ptolemy.md` §5.2 that "residues come only from the
   non-Boolean part of the `p`-adic trees". With the median normalization there is not even a top
   block, so `delta_p = 0`.
2. `(L_7)*` is a statement about the distribution of the **finite boundary height vector**
   `(log N_T(Phi))_T` of rational points of `M_{0,7}`: such vectors cannot lie in an
   `eps`-neighbourhood of the ray `R_{>0}(1,...,1)` with large `w`, off `Z`.

## 3. Theorem 2: criteria that would refute (L_7)

**Theorem 2.**

**(a) Curve sets.** Let `F` be a set of morphisms `f: P^1_Q -> Mbar_{0,7}` such that each `f`:
* (i) has image not contained in `∂`;
* (ii) is birational onto its image;
* (iii) maps no point into two distinct boundary divisors;
* (iv) satisfies `|deg f^*D_S - q_f| <= eps_f q_f` for all `S`, for some integer `q_f >= 1`.

Suppose that for every `eps > 0` the union of the images of `{f ∈ F : eps_f <= eps}` is Zariski
dense. Then `(L_7)` is false, in both readings. The case `eps_f = 0` is a curve of class `q_f beta`.

**(b) Products.** Let `Psi: (P^1_Q)^R ⇢ Mbar_{0,7}` be dominant, and suppose no prime divisor of
`(P^1)^R` is mapped into `D_S ∩ D_{S'}` with `S ≠ S'`. Let `gamma_k = Psi_*[l_k]` be the class of
the image of a general line of the `k`-th ruling, with multiplicity. If `beta` lies in the convex
cone generated by `gamma_1, ..., gamma_R`, then `(L_7)` is false.

**(c) Genus one.** Let `G` be a set of pairs `(E,f)`, where:
* `E/Q` is smooth of genus one with `rank E(Q) >= 1`;
* `f: E -> Mbar_{0,7}` is birational onto an image not contained in `∂`;
* each `f^*D_S` is a reduced divisor of degree `q_f` (approximately balanced as in (a));
* the `56 q_f` points involved are pairwise distinct.

If the union of images is dense, then `(L_7)` is false.

*Proof of (a).* Fix a proper closed `Z` and `eta_0, c, C_0 > 0`. Choose `f ∈ F` with
`eps_f <= eta_0/4` and `f(P^1) ⊄ Z`, and write `e_S = deg f^*D_S`.

* **Binary forms.** `f^*D_S = div F_S` for primitive binary forms `F_S ∈ Z[u,v]` of degree `e_S`.
  By (iii) they are pairwise coprime, so `Res(F_S, F_{S'}) ≠ 0`.
* **Bad primes.** Let `Sigma` be the finite set of primes dividing some resultant, together with
  the primes outside which `f` extends to a morphism
  `P^1_{Z[1/Sigma]} -> Mbar_{0,7,Z[1/Sigma]}` with `f^*D_S = div F_S`. Such a set exists by
  spreading out; Knudsen's `Mbar_{0,7}` is smooth over `Z`.
* **Local conditions at `p ∈ Sigma`.** For each `p ∈ Sigma` pick `x_p ∈ P^1(Z_p)` with
  `f(x_p) ∈ M_{0,7}(Q_p)`. For `K` large, every `x ≡ x_p (mod p^K)` has
  `v_p(F_S(x)) = v_p(F_S(x_p))`. Its `p`-adic tree equals that of `f(x_p)`, because the tree is
  determined by the valuations of finitely many cross-ratios, and these are locally constant.
* **Archimedean condition.** Pick a real point `x_∞` with all `F_S(x_∞) ≠ 0`. Let `(u,v)` run
  through primitive integer pairs in that congruence class and in a small cone around `x_∞`. Then
  `|F_S(u,v)| ≍ X^{e_S}` with `X = max(|u|,|v|) -> ∞`.
* **Good primes.** For `p ∉ Sigma`, at most one `F_S(u,v)` is divisible by `p`, and
  `l_S(p) = (Phi.D_S)_p = v_p(F_S(u,v))` (`ptolemy.md` B(iv)(1)). So the tree is empty or a single
  edge.
* **Anchoring.** Theorem 1 gives `n_S := prod_{p ∉ Sigma} p^{v_p(F_S(u,v))}`, with
  `log n_S = e_S log X + O(1)`. The `Sigma`-parts are bounded and go into `t_ij`, so
  `H_Sigma = O_f(1)`.
* **Conclusion.** With `w = q_f log X`, the balance is `eta <= eps_f + O(1/log X) <= eta_0` for `X`
  large, and `H_Sigma < c w - C_0`. The admissibility condition holds trivially. Since `f(P^1) ∩ Z`
  is finite and `f` is birational, infinitely many distinct `Phi = f(u:v)` lie off `Z`. The top
  and singleton blocks are inflated as in L8 Theorem B (`Y = ±1`). `[]`

*Proof of (b).*

* **Reduction to equal weights.**
  * The cone is finitely generated, so for every `eps > 0` there are positive integers `a_k` and
    `q` with `|(sum a_k gamma_k).D_S - q| <= eps q` for all `S`. This needs only rational
    approximation of a nonnegative combination, plus a small positive multiple of every
    `gamma_k`.
  * Replace `Psi` by `Psi' = Psi ∘ mu`, where `mu: (P^1)^{R'} -> (P^1)^R` multiplies `a_k` copies
    of `P^1` into the `k`-th factor via `((u_1:v_1), ..., (u_a:v_a)) -> (u_1...u_a : v_1...v_a)`.
  * `mu` is flat and surjective, and it maps each new ruling isomorphically onto an old one. So
    `Psi'` is dominant, the hypothesis on divisors persists (preimages of codimension-2 sets under
    a flat map have codimension 2), and the new ruling classes sum to `sum a_k gamma_k`.
* **Multidegrees.** Let `U` be the domain of `Psi'`; its complement has codimension at least 2.
  Then `Psi'^*D_S|_U` extends to an effective divisor `div F_S` of `(P^1)^{R'}`. Its degree in each
  factor is `D_S.Psi'_*[l] = gamma_{k(l)}.D_S`, by restricting to a general ruling line, which
  avoids the codimension-2 indeterminacy. So the **total** degree is `deg F_S = q(1 + O(eps))`.
  Since no divisor maps into `D_S ∩ D_{S'}`, the `F_S` are pairwise coprime.
* **Bad locus.** Spread out over `Z[1/Sigma_0]`. Let `Y ⊂ A^{2R'}` be the union of the following
  closed subschemes, which together have codimension at least 2:
  * the cone over the indeterminacy locus;
  * the pairwise intersections `{F_S = F_{S'} = 0}`;
  * the loci `{u_l = v_l = 0}`.
* **Counting good points.** Use `a ∈ Z^{2R'} ∩ rB`, where `B` is a small ball around a real point
  at which all `F_S` and all coordinate pairs are nonzero.
  * By Ekedahl's geometric sieve (Bhargava, *The geometric sieve and the density of squarefree
    values of invariant polynomials*, Thm 3.3), together with the local densities
    `1 - O(p^{-2})` of the conditions "`a mod p ∉ Y(F_p)`" for small `p ∉ Sigma_0`, a positive
    proportion of the points in a fixed congruence class mod `prod_{p ∈ Sigma_0} p^K` avoid
    `Y(F_p)` for every `p ∉ Sigma_0`.
  * The congruence class is chosen at the primes of `Sigma_0` exactly as in (a).
* **Conclusion.** For these points the argument of (a) applies verbatim. Each good prime gives at
  most one edge, `log n_S = deg F_S log r + O(1)`, and `H_Sigma = O(1)`. Density: the integral
  points of `rB` on the proper closed set `Psi'^{-1}(Z)` number `O(r^{2R'-1})`, by
  Schwartz–Zippel. So most good points avoid `Z`. `[]`

*Proof sketch of (c).* The integer contact profile Lemma B.5 of L8, from
`disjoint_mixed_elliptic_integer_contact_profile.md`, uses only three inputs:
* a smooth model over `Z[1/Sigma]` with **disjoint** contact sections;
* compact recurrence of `N P` in `E(R) x prod_{p ∈ Sigma} E(Q_p)`;
* `div(det)` as a sum of contact divisors.

When each `D_S` pulls back to `q_f` distinct points, the contact integer of `D_S` is the product
of `q_f` point-contact integers. Each has logarithmic size `ĥ(P)N^2 + O(N)`, so
`log d_S(N) = q_f ĥ(P)N^2 + O(N)` for all `S`. Then L8 Lemma B.6, or Theorem 1 here, anchors with
bounded residues on a recurrent subsequence. Density is handled as in (a). This is the argument of
L8 Theorem B, with the single contact point replaced by a reduced divisor. I have not rewritten
B.5 in that generality, so (c) is labelled a sketch. `[]`

*Remarks.*

1. **(a) is how `(L_5)` falls.** The linear family C1 of `ptolemy.md` consists of rational curves
   of class `beta_5`. Prop C of L8 is the projective-space version: the integral chart
   `A^4 ⊂ P^4 ⇢ Mbar_{0,5}` pulls every boundary divisor back to a hyperplane, so the images of
   lines are curves of class `beta_5`, as in (a).
2. **(b) is genuinely weaker than (a).** No individual curve of class `∝ beta` is needed. Only the
   cone condition and a product structure are needed.
3. **What a product chart must satisfy algebraically.** Pull back the cross-ratio `chi_Q` of each
   quadruple `Q`. Since `Pic((P^1)^R) = Z^R`, it equals `lambda ∏_{T ⊢ M_1} F_T / ∏_{T ⊢ M_3} F_T`.
   Pulling back `chi + chi' = 1` then gives

   ```text
   lambda ∏_{T ⊢ M_1} F_T + lambda' ∏_{T ⊢ M_2} F_T = ∏_{T ⊢ M_3} F_T      (all 35 quadruples),
   ```

   that is, the matching relations `(3.1)` of `ptolemy.md` as identities between multihomogeneous
   forms. For `R = 1` these are the class-`q beta` curves. For `R >= 2` they are multinet-type
   identities among products of forms in several variables.

## 4. Proposition 3: what a product chart must satisfy

Two exact facts are used:
* `K_{Mbar_{0,7}} = -(1/3) sum_{|S|=2} D_S`, since the coefficient `s(7-s)/6 - 2` is `-1/3` for
  `s = 2` and `0` for `s = 3` (`classes7.py`);
* `sum_i psi_i = K + 2B`.

Hence, for every curve class `gamma`:

```text
-K.gamma = pair(gamma)/3,     B.gamma = pair + triple,     (B + 8K).gamma = triple - (5/3) pair,
-K.beta = 7,  B.beta = 56,  psi_i.beta = 15,  (K+2B).beta = 105,  (B+8K).beta = 0.
```

**Proposition 3.** In Theorem 2(b), write `beta = sum alpha_k gamma_k` with `alpha_k >= 0`.

* (i) Every nonzero `gamma_k` is a positive multiple of the class of a covering family of rational
  curves. Hence `-K.gamma_k >= 2`, that is, `pair(gamma_k) >= 6`.
* (ii) Some `k` with `alpha_k > 0` has `triple(gamma_k) >= (5/3) pair(gamma_k)`, so in particular
  `triple(gamma_k) >= 10`.
* (iii) For every label `i`, some `k` with `alpha_k > 0` has `psi_i.gamma_k >= 5`. In fact
  `7 psi_i.gamma_k >= 15(-K.gamma_k) >= 30`.
* (iv) Hence no product chart works if, for some fixed `i`, all its ruling families have
  `psi_i`-degree at most 4. The same holds if all its ruling families have `triple < (5/3) pair`.
  This excludes:
  * the coordinate charts `(x_4,...,x_7)`, whose rulings are forgetful fibres with `triple = 0`;
  * all charts whose rulings are lines meeting flats in one fixed Kapranov model (table below:
    `psi_7 = 1`, `triple <= 3`).

*Proof.*

* (i) The images of the `k`-th rulings form a dominating family of rational curves. By Kollár,
  *Rational curves on algebraic varieties*, II.3.11, a general member `g: P^1 -> X` is free, so
  `g^*T_X` is nef and contains `T_{P^1} = O(2)`. Hence `deg g^*T_X = -K.[g] >= 2`.
* (ii), (iii) Let `lambda` be the linear form `B + 8K`, or `7 psi_i + 15K`. In either case
  `lambda(beta) = 0`, so `sum alpha_k lambda(gamma_k) = 0`, and some term with `alpha_k > 0` has
  `lambda(gamma_k) >= 0`.
* (iv) This follows from (ii), (iii) and the table below. `[]`

*Data* (`classes7.py`, exact). Classes are written in the `psi_7` Kapranov model as
`e l - sum m_a e_a - sum m_ab e_ab - sum m_abc e_abc` and converted to `D`-vectors; every class
satisfies all Keel relations.

The last class has `psi_i`-degrees `(7,5,5,5,5,6,3)`. It satisfies both (ii) and (iii) for

**Proposition 3 is therefore not an obstruction in itself.** Whether such cubic families exist and
cover is not checked here; the count `16 - 13 = 3` suggests they do. The missing ingredient is a
*product* structure carrying different rulings. Remark 3 of Section 3 shows that any product chart
must satisfy the 35 matching identities among products of its pulled-back boundary forms. For one
variable these are the class-`q beta` curves. For two or more variables they are multinet-type
identities. I know no source of these over `Q`.

## 5. Theorem 4 and Lemma 5: structure of balanced rational seven-curves

Throughout, `f: P^1 -> Mbar_{0,7}` is birational onto its image, the image meets `M_{0,7}`, and
`f` has class `beta`. For a label `j`, let `pi_j` forget `j`, and let `r_j` be the degree of
`P^1 -> pi_j(f(P^1))`.

**Theorem 4.**

* **(i)** `r_j ∈ {1,2}` for every `j`.
* **(ii)** At most **two** labels have `r_j = 2`.
* **(iii) Klein normal form.** Suppose `r_a = r_b = 2`. Normalize three of the other labels to
  `0, 1, ∞` and call the remaining two `c, d`. Then, after a coordinate change on `P^1`:

  ```text
  a = A(v),  v = t + 1/t,  deg A = 4;      b = B(y),  y = t^2,  deg B = 4;
  c = C(z),  d = D(z),  z = t^2 + t^{-2},  deg C = deg D = 2,
  ```

  with deck involutions `tau_a: t -> -t` and `tau_b: t -> 1/t`.
* **(iv) One involution.** If `r_a = 2`, then `C_6 := pi_a(f(P^1))` is a balanced rational curve
  in `Mbar_{0,6}`, of class `beta_6`. The map `P^1 -> C_6` is a double cover. For each of the 25
  contact points `y_{S'}` of `C_6`, the 7th coordinate `a` equals the cluster value at exactly one
  of the two preimages. In particular the 25 fibres of the double cover over the `y_{S'}` are
  unramified.

*Proof.*

* **(i)** For a boundary divisor `D'` of `Mbar_{0,6}`,
  `pi_j^*D'_{S'} = D_{S'} + D_{S' ∪ j}`. Both summands are stable boundary divisors, each meeting
  `f` once, so `pi_{j*}f_*[P^1].D' = 2`. The image is a curve: otherwise `f` would be a fibre of
  `pi_j`, of class `f_j ≠ beta`. It meets `∂`, because `M_{0,6}` is affine. So
  `r_j [image].D' = 2` gives `r_j | 2`. This is the argument of
  `seven_row_quadratic_quotient_obstruction.md` (7).
* **(ii)** If `r_j = 2`, let `tau_j` be the deck involution. The argument of item 112 §3 applies
  verbatim, because it uses only birationality and anchors.
  * The `tau_j` are distinct and commute.
  * Any at most 4 of them are `F_2`-independent: three anchors outside their labels exist because
    `7 - 4 = 3`.
  * Hence `s` such labels give `(Z/2)^s ⊂ Aut(P^1) = PGL_2(C)`.
  * Finite subgroups of `PGL_2(C)` are cyclic, dihedral, `A_4`, `S_4` or `A_5`, and their
    elementary abelian 2-subgroups have rank at most 2. So `s <= 2`.
* **(iii)**
  * `<tau_a, tau_b>` is a Klein four-subgroup of `PGL_2(C)`. Every such subgroup is conjugate to
    `{±t^{±1}}`, and its three involutions are permuted transitively by the normalizer `S_4`. So
    we may take `tau_a: t -> -t` and `tau_b: t -> 1/t`.
  * In the anchor frame (anchors not in `{a,b}`), `tau_a` fixes every coordinate except `a`, and
    `tau_b` fixes every coordinate except `b`. Hence:
    * `b, c, d ∈ C(t)^{tau_a} = C(t^2)`;
    * `a, c, d ∈ C(t)^{tau_b} = C(t + 1/t)`;
    * `c, d ∈ C(t^2 + t^{-2})`.
  * Every mover has degree `2^{7-4} = 8` (item 112 (4)), which gives the stated degrees.
* **(iv)**
  * `pi_{a*}f_*[P^1] = 2[C_6]`, and `pi_{a*}f_*[P^1].D' = 2`, so `[C_6].D' = 1` for all 25 `D'`.
  * The two preimages of the contact point `y_{S'}` lie on `D_{S'}` and on `D_{S' ∪ a}`, one each,
    because each of these divisors meets `f` exactly once. They are distinct points unless
    `y_{S'}` is a branch point; in that case the curve would pass through
    `D_{S'} ∩ D_{S' ∪ a}`, which is excluded when every contact lies on a single divisor. If they
    coincided, `f^*D_{S'}` and `f^*D_{S'∪a}` would share a point.
  * On `D_{S' ∪ a}` the coordinate `a` equals the cluster value, and on `D_{S'}` it does not. `[]`

**Lemma 5 (the last point is linear).** Suppose `r_7 = 1`, and every contact of `f` lies on a
single boundary divisor. Then:

* `C' := pi_7(f(P^1))` has class `2 beta_6`. It meets each of the 25 divisors `D'_{S'}` at two
  distinct points `t'_{S'}, t''_{S'}` of the common parameter line.
* In a frame where three of the labels `1..6` are `0, 1, ∞`, write `x_7 = N/D` with
  `deg N, deg D <= 8`. Then there is `eps: {S'} -> {1,2}` such that

  ```text
  N(t^{eps(S')}_{S'}) - v_{S'} D(t^{eps(S')}_{S'}) = 0      for all 25 S',     (5.1)
  ```

  where `v_{S'}` is the value of the cluster on the side with at most one anchor (`D = 0` when
  `v = ∞`), while the same expression is nonzero at the other contact point.
* So `(N,D)` lies in the kernel of a `25 x 18` matrix `M_eps(C')`. A balanced lift exists only if
  `rank M_eps(C') <= 17`, which is 8 conditions.

*Proof.* Here `x_7` has degree 8 by item 112 (4). At the contact point on `D_{S' ∪ 7}`, label 7 is
in the cluster: its coordinate equals the common value of the other members of that cluster,
evaluated on the side with at most one anchor. At the point on `D_{S'}` it is not. `[]`

**Parameter counts** (heuristic, not proved). The same bookkeeping in each case: maps of given
degree, minus automorphisms, minus one condition per prescribed coincidence.

| structure | `k = 6` | `k = 7` | how counted |
|---|---|---|---|
| general rational, class `beta` | 8 | 8 | `-K.beta + dim - 3` |
| one forgetful involution (genus 0) | 5 | 2 | `beta_{k-1}` curve + 2 branch points − rank conditions (`k=7`: `8 + 2 - 8`) |
| Klein normal form (genus 0) | **3** | **0** | `k = 7`: `A, B ∈ Rat_4`, `C, D ∈ Rat_2`; 28 parameters, 28 conditions |
| general genus one | 8 | 7 | `-K.beta` |
| three involutions, genus one (the L6 mechanism) | **3** (= L6) | **1** | `k = 7`: 19 parameters, 18 conditions |
| lift of a fixed balanced 6-curve by a 7th point | — | `-8` | Lemma 5 |

* The `k = 6` column agrees with what is known or found:
  * L6 is 3-dimensional.
  * The general family has local dimension 8 at a Klein point, by an exact-rank Jacobian
    (Section 7).
  * Klein solutions exist at random values of the 3 free parameters.
* The `k = 7` counts are internally consistent. The Klein count, via (iv) and Lemma 5, is
  `5 + 1 - 6 = 0`.

**Corollary 6 (heuristic).**
* Families of balanced rational seven-curves with a forgetful involution have expected dimension
  at most 2. They sweep at most a threefold.
* Genus-one families with three involutions have expected dimension 1.
* A Zariski-dense family needs dimension at least 3.

So, if the counts are right, a dense construction at `k = 7` must use rational curves with **no**
degree-two forgetful map, or genus-one curves with at most two. The rigid Galois–Boolean mechanism
of L6 (common arguments of a quadratic pencil, `(Z/2)^3`-fibres) is not available. The structural
part (Theorem 4) is proved. The dimension statements are parameter counts.

## 6. Arithmetic remarks

1. **Rational contact points are automatic.** If `f: P^1_Q -> Mbar_{0,7}` has class `beta`, each
   `D_S` meets the image in exactly one point, which is fixed by Galois. So all 56 contact points
   are `Q`-rational. The binary forms `F_S` in Theorem 2(a) are linear.
2. **Split fibres are not automatic.** For a one-involution curve over `Q` (Theorem 4(iv)), the
   double cover `P^1 -> C_6` must split over all 25 rational contact points of `C_6`.
   * Given `C_6`, the branch data `(y_+, y_-, c)` must make the 25 numbers
     `c (y_j - y_+)(y_j - y_-)` squares.
   * These are rational points on a `(Z/2)^{25}`-cover of a 3-dimensional space.
   * In L6 the analogous splitting is automatic: every Boolean fibre `f(z) = y_j` contains the
     rational common argument `x_j`, so its other point is rational too. This trick is exactly
     what Theorem 4(ii) and the counts rule out at `k = 7`.
3. **Q-points of the general family.** The general class-`beta` family is expected to be smooth of
   dimension 8 at free curves. A single `Q`-rational member, or a `Q`-rational comb of free
   components, would give a smooth `Q`-point. It would not give density of `Q`-points: smoothness
   at a `Q`-point does not imply unirationality. This is the precise gap.

## 7. Numerical experiments (NUMERICAL; not used in any proof)

**Calibration at `k = 6`.**

* `klein6.py`, the Klein ansatz with 3 free parameters fixed at random complex values: 30 starts.
  Seed 3 gives a nondegenerate balanced rational six-curve: exactly 25 special fibres, each a single
  cluster, each of the 25 splits once. The other convergent seeds were degenerate.
* `klein6t.py`, in `t`-coordinates with a log-barrier: 10 of 200 starts converge, **all 10
  nondegenerate**, with 7 distinct solutions over the same parameters (`klein6_verify.py`).
* `gen6.py`: the Klein solution, rewritten in the general anchors-and-movers chart, has residual
  `9e-15`. The Jacobian has 17 equations, 28 unknowns and full rank 17. So the local dimension is
  `28 - 17 - 3 = 8`, the expected dimension.

Hence balanced **genus-zero** six-curves exist (numerically), with the Klein symmetry, as smooth
points of an 8-dimensional family. The arithmetic question for them (Section 6.2) is the same as
at `k = 7`. They do not change the status of `(L_6)`, which L8 settled.

**`k = 7`.** No formulation produced a nondegenerate balanced curve.

| `newton_beta.py` | toric chart (`x_i = c_i prod_{A ∋ i}(t - t_A)`), 39 equations | 16/40 converged, all degenerate (merged special points) |
| `newton_log.py` | same, log-ratio form | 10/60 converged, all degenerate: many `s_T` merge at a point where all five light points coincide |
| `newton_bar.py` | same plus log-barrier against merging | 0/3 |

The same `t`-coordinate barrier solver succeeds at 5% at `k = 6` and 0% at `k = 7`. This is weak
evidence that Klein seven-curves (expected dimension 0) may not exist. It is not evidence against
the general 8-dimensional family. Every `k = 7` formulation is attracted to degenerate strata,
which the barrier and normalisation choices only partly suppress. As in L8 §5, the numerics are
inconclusive.

**No geometric obstruction was found.**

* **Real loci (exact, `realwalk.py`).** A real balanced curve meets every `D_S` once at a real
  point. Its real locus is then a closed walk on the 360 associahedral cells of `Mbar_{0,7}(R)`,
  reversing one cyclic interval per step and using every split exactly once. Depth-first search
  finds such walks for `n = 5, 6, 7` (11, 26 and 64 search nodes).
* **BMY (hand computation).**
  * Take the universal family `S -> P^1` over a class-`beta` curve. It has `e(S) = 60` and
    `K_S^2 = -48`, with 7 disjoint sections of self-intersection `-psi_i.beta = -15`. Then
    `(K_S + sum sigma_i)^2 = 29 <= 3 e(S \ sum sigma_i) = 138`.
  * For the cubic model on `P^1 x P^1` (7 sections of bidegree `(3,1)`, 35 triple points,
    21 double points) with Kummer weights `1 - 1/n`, the slack in BMY is
    `88 - 168/n + 224/n^2 > 0` for all `n`.
  * So neither log nor orbifold BMY, in these forms, forbids balanced rational seven-curves. This
    contrasts with the Kummer bound of `boolean_section_kummer_uniform_bound.md`, which needs
    Gaussian structure.

## 8. Heuristics, with exact bookkeeping (`classes7.py`)

* **Lattice count.** In the anchored chart with `Y_j = 1`, the blocks `n_T ≈ e^w` (56 of them)
  impose `X_i ≡ X_j (mod n_T)`. The lattice index is
  `prod n_T^{|T|-1} = e^{124 w}`, and the box has volume `e^{75w}`. The expected number of
  solutions is `e^{(56 - 124 + 75) w} = e^{7w}`, or `e^{8w}` with the top block. The latter agrees
  with the exponent `1 - K.beta = 8` of `ptolemy.md` §4.
* **Naive count.** Treating the 10 generating three-term relations as independent events of
  probability `e^{-8w}` gives `e^{(56-80)w} = e^{-24w}`.
  * The discrepancy is **exactly 31w**: `sum_{T ⊆ {2..6}, |T| >= 3} (C(|T|,2) - (|T|-1)) = 31`.
    These are the transitively implied congruences.
  * The relation variety `Y_7 ⊂ A^{56}` (10 relations) has dimension `46 = dim Mbar_{0,7} +
    rank Pic`, as a universal torsor would. Its balanced integral points are the configurations
    sought. Counting them is a Manin-type problem far beyond current torsor methods. I do not
    claim that `Y_7` is the universal torsor; the Cox ring of `Mbar_{0,7}` is not generated by
    boundary sections alone.
* **Lifting one point is overdetermined.**
  * Fix a balanced six-configuration at scale `2w`. The 7th point `u/v` (`u ~ e^{15w}`,
    `v ~ e^{w}`) must satisfy 25 congruences modulo divisors of size `e^{w}` of the six-point
    blocks. The expected count is `e^{(16-25)w} = e^{-9w}`. This is the integer shadow of the
    `-8` in the table of Section 5.
  * So any proof by fibration over lower `k` must average over the base. That means
    equidistribution of CRT residues of cluster values. I did not attempt this.

## 9. What would decide (L_7)

* **Disproof.** Any one of the following suffices.
  1. A `Q`-unirational family, of dimension at least 3, of rational curves of class
     `q beta` (any `q`) satisfying (iii) of Theorem 2(a). A family with `Q`-dense parameter
     points also suffices.
  2. The same with approximately balanced classes, the imbalance tending to 0.
  3. A dominant product map as in Theorem 2(b). By Proposition 3 it must contain a ruling family
     at least as triple-rich as `beta`, and one of Kapranov degree at least 5 in each model.
  4. A dense set of positive-rank elliptic curves of class `∝ beta`, as in Theorem 2(c). By
     Corollary 6 these can carry at most two forgetful involutions, so the `(Z/2)^3` common-argument
     construction is excluded.

  The most promising concrete lead found here is that `beta` lies in the cone of explicit,
  plausibly covering, low-degree families (Section 4). A product structure is what is missing.
* **Proof.** Theorem A of L8 rules out Liouville arguments. Corollary 1 shows that any proof must
  bound the finite boundary heights of rational points of `M_{0,7}` near the ray `w beta`, off a
  fixed `Z`, with `K.beta < 0`. No conjecture of Vojta type predicts this.
  * The `D = 0` inequality is vacuous since `h_K = -7w`.
  * The truncated `abc`-type form with `D = B` reads `49 w <= 56 w`, also vacuous for squarefree
    contacts.

  The heuristic count `e^{7w}` predicts that `(L_7)` is false.
