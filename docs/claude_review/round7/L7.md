## 2. Theorem A: a Grassmannian model of Mbar_{0,7} for every arrangement of seven planes

Let `A = (H_0, ..., H_6)` be planes in `P^3`, defined over `Q`, with equations `l_a in (Q^4)^*`.
For a line `R` in `P^3` put

```text
Phi_A(R) = [ R ; R ∩ H_0, ..., R ∩ H_6 ]      (seven points on R ≅ P^1, up to PGL_2).
```

For a configuration `x = (x_0, ..., x_6)` of seven distinct points of `P^1`, with homogeneous
vectors `v_a in K^2`, let `M(x)` be the `7 x 8` matrix of the linear system

```text
l_a( L v_a ) = 0      (a = 0..6),     unknown L in Hom(K^2, K^4) ≅ K^8.             (2.1)
```

**Theorem A.** Assume

* (A1) no point of `P^3` lies on six of the planes;
* (A2) for one configuration `x^0` the matrix `M(x^0)` has rank 7 and the line spanned by its kernel
  is contained in none of the `H_a`.

Then `Phi_A: G(2,4) ⇢ X` is birational and defined over `Q`. Its inverse is
`Psi_A(x) = image of the kernel vector of M(x)`. For every field `K ⊇ Q` and every
`x in X(K)` in the domain of `Psi_A`, the line `R_x = Psi_A(x)` is defined over `K`.

*Proof.*
1. *`Psi_A` is well defined.* Replacing the vectors `v_a` by `g v_a` (`g in GL_2(K)`) or by scalar
   multiples replaces the kernel `L` by `L g^{-1}` or leaves it unchanged, so the image of `L` depends
   only on the point `x in M_{0,7}`. Let `U` be the set of `x` with `rank M(x) = 7` and
   `im L ⊄ H_a` for all `a`. It is open, and nonempty by (A2).
2. *`L` has rank 2 on `U`.* If `L = p mu^T` had rank 1, then `l_a(p) mu(v_a) = 0` for all `a`. The
   nonzero form `mu` vanishes on at most one of the pairwise non-proportional `v_a`, so `p` would
   lie on six planes, against (A1).
3. *`Phi_A ∘ Psi_A = id` on `U`.* For `x in U` the map `L: P^1 -> R_x` is an isomorphism sending
   `v_a` to a point of `R_x ∩ H_a`. Since `R_x ⊄ H_a`, that point is `R_x ∩ H_a`. So
   `Phi_A(R_x) = x`.
4. *`Psi_A ∘ Phi_A = id` on `Phi_A^{-1}(U)`.* If `Phi_A(R) = x in U`, any parametrisation
   `L: P^1 -> R` sending `v_a` to `R ∩ H_a` lies in the kernel of `M(x)`. The kernel is a line, so
   `Psi_A(x) = R`.
5. Both maps are rational maps between irreducible fourfolds and are mutually inverse on dense
   open sets, so they are birational. The kernel of a matrix with entries in `K` is spanned by a
   `K`-rational vector (signed maximal minors), which gives the last statement. `[]`

**Dictionary.** For `R` in the domain, `x_a = x_b` exactly when `R` meets the axis
`l_ab = H_a ∩ H_b`. So pair divisors correspond to the hyperplane sections `sigma_1(l_ab)` of the
Plücker quadric. A triple collision `x_a = x_b = x_c` happens when `R` passes through the point
`P_abc = H_a ∩ H_b ∩ H_c`, a codimension-2 condition in `G(2,4)`. **The exception is a coaxial
triple** (`H_a, H_b, H_c` share a line `l`): then every line meeting `l` gives the triple collision,
so the triple divisor `D_abc` becomes the hyperplane section `sigma_1(l)`, and a curve of degree
`d` in `G(2,4)` meets it `d` times **with no condition at all**. This is the source of richness
below.

**Remarks.**
1. In this model the rational curves of lowest degree are cheap over `Q`. A conic in `G(2,4)` is a
   ruling of a quadric surface `Q ⊂ P^3`, and "the conic meets `{R : R ∋ P}`" is the **linear**
   condition `P in Q`.
2. Coaxial triples are limited. Dually, they are collinear triples among seven points of `P^3`. If
   six of the points are coplanar, (A1) fails. The arrangement used below has three coaxial triples,
   and I found no arrangement satisfying (A1) with four. This is not proved to be impossible; it is
   not needed.

## 3. Theorem B: a rich, addable, Q-rational family

### 3.1 The arrangement

Labels `O, a1, a2, b1, b2, c1, c2` (indices 0..6). With `X3` the last coordinate form and
`m1 = (1,2,-3,0)`, `m2 = (2,-1,5,0)`, `m3 = (-3,4,1,0)`:

```text
H_O = {X3 = 0},
H_a1 = {m1 + 2 X3 = 0},  H_a2 = {m1 - 3 X3 = 0},
H_b1 = {m2 + 5 X3 = 0},  H_b2 = {m2 -   X3 = 0},
H_c1 = {m3 + 7 X3 = 0},  H_c2 = {m3 - 4 X3 = 0}.
```

This is a *star*: three lines `l_1, l_2, l_3` in the plane `H_O`, forming a triangle, and two more
planes through each of them. Data (`verify_VA.py`):

* (A1) holds: every six of the planes are independent.
* The coaxial triples are exactly `{O,a1,a2}`, `{O,b1,b2}`, `{O,c1,c2}`.
* The points on five planes are the three vertices `l_i ∩ l_j` of the triangle.
* The non-degenerate triple points are the eight points `P_{ai bj ck} = H_ai ∩ H_bj ∩ H_ck`. They are
  the base locus of the net `<H_a1 H_a2, H_b1 H_b2, H_c1 H_c2>` and lie on 12 planes four at a time
  (the combinatorics of the vertices of a cube).

Choose the six event points

```text
E = { P_{a1 b1 c1}, P_{a1 b1 c2}, P_{a1 b2 c1}, P_{a1 b2 c2}, P_{a2 b1 c1}, P_{a2 b2 c1} }.
```

Data: the quadrics through `E` form a 4-dimensional space `Lambda`, i.e. a web `P(Lambda) ≅ P^3`.
*(Three of the 28 six-element subsets of the eight points are unions of two faces of the cube. For
those, the quadric through a general line and the six points is a pair of planes, which is why the
choice matters. Any other subset works in the checks of `star_dbg3.py`.)*

### 3.2 The family

For a smooth quadric `Q in P(Lambda)` and a ruling `rho` of `Q` (a conic in `G(2,4)`), let
`M_{Q,rho}` be the closure of `Phi_A(rho)` in `X`. Along `rho` each marked point
`R ∩ H_a` moves by a Möbius transformation of the ruling parameter, so these are **Möbius curves**
in the sense of Section 6.

**Theorem B.**

1. *(Unique member through a general point, over the residue field.)* There is a dense open
   `U_B ⊂ X` such that for `x in U_B` with residue field `K`:
   * `R_x = Psi_A(x)` is defined;
   * there is exactly one `Q_x in P(Lambda)` containing `R_x`;
   * `Q_x` is smooth.

   Then `Q_x` is defined over `K` and contains the `K`-line `R_x`, so it is split over `K`. Its ruling
   `rho_x ∋ R_x` is a conic over `K` with a `K`-point, so `rho_x ≅ P^1_K`. The member
   `M_x = M_{Q_x, rho_x}` is the **only** member through `x`.
2. *(Class.)* For `(Q, rho)` in a dense open subset of the 3-dimensional parameter space, `M_{Q,rho}`
   is a good curve of the class `gamma_A` given by

   | divisor | `D.gamma_A` |
   |---|---|
   | `D_{O a1 a2}`, `D_{O b1 b2}`, `D_{O c1 c2}` (coaxial) | 2 each |
   | the six `D_{ai bj ck}` of `E` | 1 each |
   | `D_{a2 b1}`, `D_{a2 b2}`, `D_{b1 c2}`, `D_{b2 c2}` | 1 each |
   | `D_{a2 c2}` | 2 |
   | the other 42 | 0 |

   So `pair(gamma_A) = 6`, `triple(gamma_A) = 12`, `-K.gamma_A = 2` and
   **`(B+8K).gamma_A = +2`**. The member meets `∂` in 18 distinct points, each transversally and on
   exactly one divisor.
3. *(Addable.)* `ev: U -> X` is birational, and its inverse
   `sigma(x) = (Q_x, rho_x, position of R_x)` is a rational map defined over `Q`. In r7a's
   terminology `V_A = {M_{Q,rho}}` is a **rich addable Q-family**.

*Proof.*
1. `R_x` is defined on the open set of Theorem A. "`Q ⊇ R_x`" is 3 linear conditions on `Lambda`
   (vanishing at three points of `R_x`). Uniqueness of `Q_x` (rank 3 of these conditions) and
   smoothness (`det ≠ 0`) are open conditions. They hold at the three rational points `x` of
   `verify_VA.py` (Data), so on a dense open set. The field-of-definition statements are linear
   algebra over `K`. A quadric containing a `K`-line `R` is split, and its ruling containing `R` has
   the `K`-point `R`. Now let `M_{Q,rho}` pass through `x in U_B`, where `U_B` is also inside the

2. Every member has, by construction, the coaxial events and the six events of `E`. These are closed

   are distinct and transversal, and that no contact is at the parameter `infinity` are open
   conditions on the parameters. `verify_VA.py` computes the member through three random rational

   over triples and quadruples, squarefreeness of the product of all contact polynomials). It finds
   the table above each time. So the open set is nonempty, and the class is constant on the family.
   *(Data: 3 of 3 points; `star_run.py` gives the same table for two further points and a different random star arrangement.)*
3. By 1, a general `x` lies on exactly one member, and `sigma` is given by the linear algebra of 1,

## 4. r7a Theorem 3 is not correct: hypothesis (G1) is not generic

### 4.1 The foliation of a family and its degree on curves

Let `V` be a covering Q-family of rational curves with **exactly one member through a general point**
(r7a: "3-dimensional addable"). The tangent lines of the members define a rank-1 saturated subsheaf
`F_V ⊂ T_X`, a foliation with algebraic leaves. Its singular set `Sing(F_V)` (where `F_V` is not a
subbundle) has codimension `>= 2`. Define the linear functional on `N_1(X)`

```text
lambda_V(g) := c_1(F_V) . g = -K_{F_V} . g .
```

* `lambda_V(gamma_V) = 2`: a general member is a smooth leaf `P^1` avoiding `Sing(F_V)`, and
  `F_V` restricts to its tangent bundle.
* If `V` is given by a morphism `pi: X -> B` to a smooth threefold whose fibres are reduced conics
  (a standard conic bundle), then `F_V = T_{X/B}` and `-K_{F_V} = -K_X + pi^*K_B`.

**Lemma 4.1 (forgetful fibres).** For the forgetful map `pi_i: X = Mbar_{0,7} -> Mbar_{0,6}`
(universal curve, reduced nodal fibres), `-K_{F} = -omega_{pi_i} = -psi_i + sum_{j != i} D_{ij}`. Hence

```text
lambda_{f_i}(beta) = -15 + 6 = -9,     lambda_{f_i}(f_j) = 0 (j != i),     lambda_{f_i}(f_i) = 2.
```

*Proof.* `psi_i = omega_{pi_i}(sum_{j != i} D_{ij})` is the standard comparison on the universal
curve, with `D_{ij}` the image of the `j`-th section. `psi_i.beta = 15` follows from
`psi_i = sum_{T ∋ i, T ∌ j,k} D_T` (fixed `j, k != i`; 15 subsets). `D_{ij}.beta = 1`.
`psi_i.f_j = 1` for `j != i`: in the formula take `j` as one of the two excluded labels. The
divisors meeting `f_j` are the `D_{jm}`, and the only one of the form `D_T` with `T ∋ i`, `T ∌ j, k` is `D_{jk}`. `D_{ij}.f_j = 1`. `-omega` restricted to a fibre has degree 2. `[]`

### 4.2 The self-intersection of the section is `lambda_V(C)`

**Lemma 4.2.** Let `f: P^1 -> X` be a good curve, `C = f(P^1)`, with `C ∩ Sing(F_V) = ∅`. Let
`p: Y -> P^1` be a smooth projective surface with connected fibres of arithmetic genus 0, let
`Sigma ⊂ Y` be a section, and let `iota: Y ⇢ X` be a rational map, defined along `Sigma`, with
`iota|_Sigma = f` and mapping the general fibre birationally onto the member of `V` through the
corresponding point of `C`. Then

```text
Sigma^2 <= lambda_V(C),
```

with equality if `iota` is étale along `Sigma`.

*Proof.* Along `Sigma`, `N_{Sigma/Y} = T_{Y/P^1}|_Sigma` (a section of a fibration meets the
fibres transversally, at smooth points). The differential of `iota` sends `T_{Y/P^1}` into
`F_V` (fibres go to leaves). Along `Sigma` this is a nonzero map of line bundles
`T_{Y/P^1}|_Sigma -> f^*F_V`, because the general fibre maps nonconstantly. `F_V` is a line
subbundle near `C`. So `deg N_{Sigma/Y} <= deg f^*F_V = lambda_V(C)`, with equality if the map
has no zeros, which holds where `iota` is étale. `[]`

**Corollary 4.3 ((G1) fails when `lambda_V(C) < 0`).** In r7a Lemma 4.2 the surface is
`Y = C x P^1` and `Sigma` is the graph of a map of degree `delta >= 0`, so `Sigma^2 = 2 delta >= 0`.
If (G1) holds, i.e. `iota` is defined along `Sigma`, then Lemma 4.2 gives
`lambda_V(C) >= 2 delta >= 0`. **So (G1) fails for every curve `C` with `lambda_V(C) < 0` that
avoids `Sing(F_V)`.** `[]`

`lambda_V` is linear in `[C]`. The curves of r7a's iteration have classes that grow towards
`L beta`, so for large steps `lambda_V(C) ≈ L·lambda_V(beta)`. For forgetful fibres this is
`-9L` (Lemma 4.1, exact). So (G1) fails for every forgetful step applied to a curve of a late step
(avoiding the codimension-2 set `Sing(F_V)`). For the rich family of Theorem B,
`lambda_A(beta) <= -23` (Proposition 5.4), and the same conclusion holds for `V_A`-steps.
So r7a's claim that (G1)–(G3) "fail only when a curve meets a fixed codimension-2 locus or is
itself special" is false for (G1). Whether r7a's hypothesis "the curves satisfying (G1)–(G3) have
dense union" can hold at every step, for some ordering of the steps, is decided in Corollary 4.5
by the sign of `lambda` on the last curves.

### 4.3 What happens without (G1): contacts are lost

Without (G1), r7a's construction still produces curves, but not of class `[C] + m[V]` with the
contacts of `C` preserved.

*Example (forgetful fibre, explicit).*
1. Let `C` have a transversal contact with `D_{ijk}` at `c` (labels `i, j, k` collide at `c`), and
   add the forgetful family `f_i` in r7a's trivialisation `C x P^1 -> X`. That trivialisation moves
   point `i` to `s`, keeping the other six points of `f(c')`.
2. For `c' -> c` with `s` fixed and `s` different from the collision value, the limit has `j, k`
   collided and `i` elsewhere. So the fibre over `c` maps into the **pair** divisor `D_{jk}`, and
   `(c, s_c)` is an indeterminacy point.
3. Blowing it up, the exceptional curve maps onto the curve `e_{ijk}` of bubble configurations
   `(i, j, k, node)` inside `D_{ijk}`. This curve has `D_{ijk}.e = -1`,
   `D_{ij}.e = D_{ik}.e = D_{jk}.e = +1`, and all other degrees 0.
4. The general section of r7a's linear system avoids the blown-up point. So it has class
   `[C] + m f_i + e_{ijk}`: the triple contact is replaced by three pair contacts.

The mechanism is general. Let `D` be a boundary divisor with `D.gamma_V = 0` and `c` a contact of
`C` with `D`. Any flat limit of members through points of `C` near `c` has class `gamma_V` and passes
through `f(c) in D`. A cycle with no component inside `D` would then have `D`-degree `>= 1 > 0`. So
the limit has a component inside `D`.

For `V_A` this applies to the 42 divisors with `D.gamma_A = 0`. At an uncovered triple divisor
`D_abc`, the limit is the special member through `R_{f(c)}` (whose quadric contains `P_abc`) plus
the fibre `e_abc` of `D_abc` over `R_{f(c)}`. A section that avoids the fibre component through
`f(c)` loses the contact.

### 4.4 The correct necessary condition

**Proposition 4.4.** Let `C` be as in Lemma 4.2 and transverse to `F_V`. Let `Y -> P^1` be a
smooth model of the surface swept by the members through the points of `C`, on which `iota` is
étale along `Sigma`. Such a model exists: resolve the normalisation of the closure, which is smooth
near `Sigma` because, near `c`, it is the product of `C` with the germ of the leaf. Suppose a good
curve `C'` is the image of a section `sigma' ≠ Sigma` that meets every fibre in the same component as
`Sigma` (in particular this holds if `C'` keeps every contact that `C` has at points where the fibre
is reducible). Then

```text
[C'] = [C] + m gamma_V     with     m >= -lambda_V(C).
```

*Proof.*
1. `Y` is a rational surface fibred over `P^1` with fibres trees of rational curves. So `Num(Y)` is
   spanned by `Sigma` and the fibre components.
2. `D := sigma' - Sigma` has `D.F = 0`, and `D.Gamma = 0` for every fibre component `Gamma`, since
   both sections meet the same component once. By Zariski's lemma, `D ≡ m F` for some `m`.
3. Then `sigma'.Sigma = Sigma^2 + m >= 0`, since the curves are distinct and irreducible.
   `Sigma^2 = lambda_V(C)` by Lemma 4.2, and `iota_*[F] = gamma_V`. `[]`

Conversely, for `m >= max(1, -lambda_V(C))` the system `|Sigma + mF|` on `Y` is base-point-free:
* `H^1(O_Y(mF)) = 0` gives surjectivity onto `H^0(O_Sigma(Sigma^2 + m))`;
* off `Sigma` it contains `Sigma + |mF|`.

It is defined over `Q`, and its general member is an irreducible section whose image keeps all
contacts of `C`. Under the genericity statement (H) of Section 5.1, the image is a good curve.
So **adding `V` to `C` is possible exactly when `m >= -lambda_V(C)`**. Off the
cases with `lambda_V(C) >= 0`, this needs `m` of the order of the size of `C`.

**Corollary 4.5 (no ordering repairs r7a Theorem 3).** Let `Y -> P^1` be any smooth surface with
a rational map `iota: Y ⇢ X` whose general fibre maps birationally onto members of `V`. Let `C'` be
the image of a section `sigma'` along which `iota` is defined, with `C' ∩ Sing(F_V) = ∅`. If
`sigma'` **moves** in a positive-dimensional family of sections (as every density argument needs),
then `sigma'^2 >= 0`, and Lemma 4.2 applied to `sigma'` gives

```text
lambda_V(C') >= sigma'^2 >= 0.
```

* For `C'` within `eps` of `L beta`, this forces `lambda_V(beta) >= -O(eps)`.
* For the sections `sigma' ∈ |Sigma + mF|` of Proposition 4.4, `sigma'^2 = Sigma^2 + 2m >= m`.

Consequences:
* The forgetful families (`-9`) **cannot be the last family added**. The same holds if, at reducible
  fibres, the section meets the other component; that only changes `sigma'`, not the inequality.
* In r7a's iteration with `V = V_A`, every forgetful step needs `lambda_{f_i}(C) >= 0` at the curve
  `C` it is applied to (Corollary 4.3). By the exact values `lambda_{f_i}(tau gamma_A) in {-2,-3,-4}`
  (`lamf.py`) and `lambda_{f_i}(f_j) in {0, 2}`, this forces essentially all forgetful steps to come
  before the `V_A`-steps.
* The `V_A`-steps then start from a curve containing `≈ (L/12) mu` and end at `≈ L beta`. They need
  `lambda_A(mu) >= -o(1)` and `lambda_A(beta) >= 0`. Both are false: `lambda_A(mu) <= -10` and
  `lambda_A(beta) <= -23` (Proposition 5.4). So r7a Theorem 3 does not apply to `V_A`, in any
  ordering. `[]`

## 5. The corrected criterion and the invariant lambda_V(beta)

### 5.1 Sufficiency (conditional on genericity)

**Proposition 5.1.** Let `V_1, ..., V_r` be Q-families, each with exactly one member through a
general point and a rational section over `Q`, with fibre classes `gamma_j`. Suppose

```text
beta = sum_j c_j gamma_j  with rational c_j > 0,      and      lambda_{V_j}(beta) > 0  for all j.
```

Assume the genericity statement (H). At every step, the general member of the linear system of
Proposition 4.4, taken over a dense set of the curves of the previous step:
* avoids `Sing(F_{V'})` for the family `V'` added next;
* is transverse to `F_{V'}` outside finitely many points;
* has a `V'`-leaf surface meeting `∂^{(2)} = ∪_{S != S'} D_S ∩ D_{S'}` in finitely many points.
Then `(L_7)` is false.

*Proof (given (H)).*
1. Work in rounds. In round `r`, add `V_1, ..., V_r` in turn with multiplicities `N c_j` (`N` fixed,
   clearing denominators), via Proposition 4.4.
2. Before step `j` of round `r`, the current curve has class `seed + (r-1) N beta + O(N)`. So
   `lambda_{V_j}(C) = (r-1) N lambda_{V_j}(beta) + O(N)`. This is positive for `r >= r_0`, and
   then any `m >= 1` is allowed.
3. In the first `r_0` rounds, take `m_j >= -lambda_{V_j}(C)`. The excess is bounded independently
   of the total number of rounds.
4. After `r` rounds the class is `r N beta + O(1)`, so `eps -> 0`.
5. Each step produces a dense set of Q-rational good curves in the leaf surfaces (Proposition 4.4
   and (H)). The leaf surface of `C` contains `C`, so by induction the union of the curves of every
   step is Zariski dense.
6. Round 4 Theorem 2(a) concludes. `[]`

(H) is a genuine open-condition statement, unlike r7a's (G1). I have not proved it for any
specific family, and it is not needed below: no family with `lambda_V(beta) > 0` is known.

### 5.2 Product charts satisfy the condition automatically

**Proposition 5.2.** Let `Psi: (P^1)^4 ⇢ X` be birational over `Q` with ruling classes
`gamma_1, ..., gamma_4` and `beta = sum c_k gamma_k` (`c_k >= 0`), the input of round 4 Theorem 2(b).
Let `V_k` be the family of `t_k`-ruling lines. Then

```text
lambda_{V_k}(beta) >= 2 c_k.
```

*Proof.*
* `lambda_{V_k}(gamma_k) = 2`.
* For `j != k`, a general `t_j`-line `l_j` is a section of the surface `{t_i = const, i != j, k}
  ≅ P^1 x P^1`, which is ruled by `t_k`-lines. The map is defined along `l_j` and étale there, and
  `l_j^2 = 0` on `P^1 x P^1`. Lemma 4.2 gives `lambda_{V_k}(gamma_j) >= 0`.
* Sum with weights `c_j`. `[]`

So the condition `lambda_V(beta) > 0` is exactly what makes Theorem 2(b) work. Proposition 5.1
says that fibrations with this property can be combined **one at a time**, without a global product
structure. Corollary 4.5 says that without it, neither method can use the family `V` as its last
(resp. as a ruling) family.

### 5.4 The sign of `lambda_A(beta)`: an exact upper bound by local frames

Items 3–4 above reduce `lambda_A(beta)` to unknown local orders `c_E`. This subsection computes
it directly on test curves instead. The computation gives a **proved upper bound** because of the
following lemma.

**Lemma 5.3 (frame bound).**
* *Setting.* Let `F ⊂ T_X` be a saturated rank-1 subsheaf (a foliation) on `X = Mbar_{0,7}`. Let
  `f: P^1 -> X` be a morphism whose image is not contained in `Sing(F)`, and whose points all lie
  on at most one boundary divisor. Let `v` be a nonzero rational section of `f^*F`.
* *Components.* For each of the 35 four-element sets `k`, let `cr_k: X -> Mbar_{0,4} = P^1` be the
  forgetful map, and let `v_k` be the image of `v` in `f^* cr_k^* T_{P^1}`.
* *Count.* Set `comp(f, v) := sum over t in P^1 of min_k ord_t(v_k)`, where `ord_t` is the order
  in a local frame of `T_{P^1}` at `cr_k(f(t))`.

Then

```text
lambda_F(f_*[P^1]) = deg f^*F  <=  comp(f, v),
```

with equality iff `f(P^1) ∩ Sing(F) = ∅`.

*Proof.*
1. *The cross-ratio map is an immersion at every point of `X` lying on at most one boundary
   divisor.* (Globally `X -> prod_k Mbar_{0,4}` is even a closed embedding, a result of Keel, but
   only this local statement is used.)
   * At an interior point, `cr_{123a}` (`a = 4..7`) are coordinates.
   * At a point of `D_{ab}` only: fix three other labels at `0, 1, inf`. Use as coordinates the
     `x_c` of the three remaining labels `c != b` (one of them is `a`) and `s = x_b - x_a`.
     * The `x_c` are cross-ratios with the fixed labels.
     * For finite `x_c, x_d` (`c, d ∉ {a, b}`), `cr(a,b;c,d) - 1` equals `s` times a unit.
   * At a point of `D_{abc}` only: fix three of the four other labels `d, e, f` at `0, 1, inf`. Use
     the coordinates `(x_a, x_g, s, w)`, where `g` is the fourth other label, `x_b = x_a + s` and
     `x_c = x_a + s w`.
     * With `cr(a,b;c,d) = [ac][bd]/([ad][bc])`, one gets `cr(a,b;c,d) = (w/(w-1))(1 + O(s))`, and
       `d/dw (w/(w-1)) != 0`.
     * `cr(a,b;d,e) - 1 = s·(x_e - x_d)/((x_a - x_d)(x_a - x_e)) + O(s^2)`, which is `s` times a
       unit.
     * The Jacobian is therefore invertible.

   So `T_X -> oplus_k cr_k^* T_{P^1}` is a subbundle along `f(P^1)`. Hence the order of a section of
   `f^*T_X` at `t` is `min_k` of the orders of its components.
2. *Comparison with `F`.* Since `f(P^1) ⊄ Sing(F)`, the map of line bundles `f^*F -> f^*T_X` is
   injective. At each `t`, `ord_t` of `v` as a section of `f^*T_X` is at least `ord_t` of `v` as a
   section of `f^*F`. Strict inequality holds exactly when the local generator of `F` vanishes in
   `T_X` at `f(t)`, i.e. `f(t) ∈ Sing(F)`.
3. Summing over `t`: `sum_t ord^F_t(v) = deg f^*F`. `[]`

*How `comp` is computed* (`lamA_exact.py`, all arithmetic in `Z[t]`).
* For `F = F_A`, the test curve `f` is a Möbius curve given by integer data. Then:
  * `R_{f(t)}` comes from the `7 x 8` kernel;
  * `Q_t` is the web quadric through `R_t`;
  * the leaf is the ruling `R(u) = span(p + u w, q_2(u))` of `Q_t`, with the formulas of
    `kfol.py`;
  * `v(t) = d/du|_{u=0}`, which is a rational section of `f^*F_A`.
* With `[p_a p_b] = (l_a ∧ l_b)(R)` (linear in Plücker coordinates), each cross-ratio is
  `cr_k = N/D` with `N, D` quadratic in the brackets. Put
  * `N_0, D_0` for the values at `u = 0`,
  * `W = N_u D - N D_u` at `u = 0`,
  * `g = gcd(N_0, D_0)`.
* Then `v_k = W/g^2` in the frame of `T_{P^1}`. So `ord_t(v_k) = ord_t(W/g^2)` for finite `t`, and
  `ord_inf(v_k) = 2 deg(cr_k ∘ f) - deg(W/g^2)`.
* The code checks that the brackets are nonzero, so `R_t` meets the seven planes in distinct points.
  `Phi` is then a local isomorphism at `f(t)` for general `t`, and `v` is a rational section of
  `f^*F_A`.
* The quadric coefficients are not all zero, so `Q_t` is the unique web quadric through `R_t`.
* Some `W_k` is nonzero, so `v(t) != 0` in `T_X` for general `t`. Hence `f(P^1) ⊄ Sing(F_A)`, as
  Lemma 5.3 requires.

*Validation (Data).* The same code with the forgetful foliation `F_i` (`v` = moving `x_i`)
reproduces, on every one of the 36 test classes and for every `i`, the exact values of Lemma 4.1:
* `lambda_{f_i}(mu) = 2`;
* `lambda_{f_i}(mu_T) - lambda_{f_i}(mu) = -[i ∈ T]`;
* `lambda_{f_i}(beta) = -9` for all seven `i` (`lamf_frame.py`, mod p; `lamA_exact.py ... forgetful`,
  exact, two seeds).

* In the Kapranov coordinates, which use different brackets (projections of the rational normal
  curve), the count gives `-9` at `p = e_0, e_3` and `-26` at a general point. This matches
  Lemma 4.1 and the local rule (`lamK_exact.py`).

This requires test curves in general position. With small integer coefficients, a first exact run
without a transversality filter gave wrong values for `F_O` and `F_b1`. Its log was overwritten; the

**Proposition 5.4 (proved, by exact computation).**

```text
lambda_A(mu) <= -10        and        lambda_A(beta) <= -23.
```

*Proof.*
1. *Decomposition of `beta`.* In `N_1(X)`,
   `beta = (35/12) avg_{sigma in S_7} sigma_*gamma_A + (1/12) mu`. Indeed the average has
   `D_S`-degree `6/21 = 2/7` on pairs and `12/35` on triples, and
   `(35/12)(2/7) + (1/12)·2 = 1`, `(35/12)(12/35) = 1`. The `D_S` span `N^1(X)`.
   * The stabiliser of the class `gamma_A` in `S_7` has order 4. So the average runs over 1260
     classes.
   * All coefficients are positive.
2. *Representatives.*
   * Each class is represented by `sigma ∘ f_x`, where `f_x` is the member of `V_A` through
     `x = (6, -6, -3, -5, 2, 0, 1)`. Its class is verified exactly to be `gamma_A`: the quadric is
     smooth; the contacts are 6 simple pair and 12 simple triple collisions at distinct finite
     parameters, so every point lies on at most one boundary divisor.
   * `mu` is represented by an integer Möbius curve that passes the transversality filter.
3. *Bound.* By Lemma 5.3 and linearity,
   `lambda_A(beta) <= (35/12) avg_sigma comp(sigma f_x) + (1/12) comp(mu)`. The exact values are:
   * `comp(mu) = -10`;
   * over the 1260 classes, `sum comp(sigma f_x) = -9576`, i.e. average `-38/5`. The values range
     from `-12` to `2`, and the value `2` is `sigma = id`, the leaf itself.

   This gives `lambda_A(beta) <= (35/12)(-38/5) + (1/12)(-10) = -23`. The bound
   `lambda_A(mu) <= comp(mu) = -10` is Lemma 5.3 directly. `[]`

**Equality (evidence, not proof).** Equality holds in both bounds if the test curves avoid the
codimension-2 set `Sing(F_A)`, which is true for general members of the test families.
1. The 36 classes `mu, mu_T` give the same values on 3 exact (`lamA_exact.py`, seeds 5, 6, 7) and
   3 mod-p (`lamA_frame.py`) random instances each:
   * `lambda_A(mu) = -10`;

2. The second computation uses `beta = sum_T mu_T - 32 mu`, which has negative coefficients and

3. The values agree with the local analysis of Section 5.3:

* The sign question of Section 5.3, item 4, is settled: `lambda_A(beta) < 0`. So no degenerate

* By Corollary 4.5, **no curve close to the ray of `beta` and avoiding `Sing(F_A)` is a moving
  section of a surface whose fibres are `V_A`-members**. In particular, `V_A` cannot be the last

* Lemma 5.3 turns "`lambda_V(beta) < 0`" into a finite exact computation for any family with
  explicitly computable leaves. It needs only a decomposition of `beta` into test classes with

## 6. Möbius curves: an incidence-geometric source of rich families

**Definition.** A *Möbius curve* is `t -> [P^1; g_0(t), ..., g_6(t)]` with `g_a in PGL_2`. The
space of such curves is `PGL_2^7 / (PGL_2 x PGL_2)`, of dimension 15.

* The pair `{a,b}` collides at the two fixed points of `g_b^{-1} g_a`. So the pure class `mu` has
  `D_ab.mu = 2` for all 21 pairs.
* A *k-event* is `k` labels colliding at one parameter. A 3-event costs 1 condition and absorbs 3
  pair zeros; a 4-event costs 2 and absorbs 6; a 5-event costs 3, absorbs 10, and is a pair-type
  contact. With `tau_k` events of each size:

  ```text
  (B+8K) = 6 tau_3 + 11 tau_4 + 15 tau_5 - 70,      expected dim = 15 - tau_3 - 2 tau_4 - 3 tau_5.
  ```
* Rich families of dimension `>= 3` therefore have `(tau_3, tau_4) = (12,0)`, `(10,1)` or `(8,2)`,
  with `(B+8K) = 2, 1, 0`. All have `pair = 6` and `-K = 2`.

**Geometry.** The graph of `g_a` is a `(1,1)`-curve on `P^1 x P^1`, i.e. a plane section `H_a ∩ Q`
of a quadric `Q ⊂ P^3`. A Möbius curve is therefore *seven planes and a quadric*:
* the ruling lines `R_t` of `Q` meet the planes in the seven points;
* a 3-event is a triple point `H_a ∩ H_b ∩ H_c` lying on `Q`;
* a coaxial triple of planes gives **two** 3-events for free, since its axis meets `Q` twice.

This is Section 2 read backwards: Möbius curves are the ruling conics of `G(2,4)`. `V_A` is the
Möbius family of type "three coaxial triples (two events each) and six point events".

**Types with 12 distinct triple events** (each pair in at most two events): 23 isomorphism types
(`enum_types.py`, exhaustive over `S_7`; `types12.txt`).

*NUMERICAL* (`grow.py`, `mono.py`). Members were found by continuation: events added one at a
time by Gauss–Newton, with every intermediate curve checked to have exactly the prescribed contacts,
all distinct.
* Nondegenerate members exist for 16 of the 23 types: 0–9, 11, 13, 17, 19, 20, 22. For the other 7
  the search was inconclusive.
* For each realised type, the system "Möbius-12 curves through a fixed general point"
  (24 equations, 24 unknowns, inverse maps `G_a(z) = (p_a z + q_a)/(z - y_a)`) has nonsingular
  Jacobian at the member. So the family dominates `X`.
* Monodromy around random loops gives these lower bounds for the number of members through a
  general point:

  | type | 1 | 11 | 2 | 17 | 13 | 5 | 6 | 20 | 0 | 22 | 7 | 3 | 9 | 19 | 8 | 4 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | `deg ev >=` | 4 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 10 | 13 | 14 | 14 | 15 | 16 | 16 | 16 | 19 | 29 |

  So none of these families is addable. `V_A` is addable because its coaxial triples are not
  "distinct events": they come from the arrangement, not from conditions.

**Through a fixed point the conditions are bilinear.** With `x = (y_a)` fixed and
`G_a(z) = (p_a z + q_a)/(z - y_a)`, an event at target position `z_e` is linear in `(p, q)`:
`(p_a z_e + q_a)(z_e - y_b) = (p_b z_e + q_b)(z_e - y_a)`. With the event positions free, the
members through `x` form an affine bundle over the `z`-space when `2 tau_3 <= 12`. Then they are
rational over `Q(x)`, but `(B+8K) <= 36 - 70 < 0`. Richness needs `tau_3 = 12`, and the system
becomes overdetermined. This is the Möbius instance of Section 7.

## 7. A barrier for "free-linear" constructions

Write a rational curve in `X` by seven graphs on `P^1_t x P^1_x` (trivial `P^1`-bundle), label `a`
moving by a rational function of degree `d_a`, `D = sum d_a`. Every rational curve has such a
presentation, after elementary transformations at points off the sections. An *event* is a cluster
`e` of `|e| >= 3` labels colliding at a point `(t_e, z_e)`. With the event positions fixed, the
condition "label `a` passes through `(t_e, z_e)`" is **linear** in the coefficients of `g_a`. Call a
family *free-linear* if its members (through a general point, or globally) are parametrised by
freely chosen event positions together with solutions of these linear systems, solvable for general
positions. This means `r_a <= 2 d_a` (through a point), where `r_a` is the number of events at `a`.

**Proposition 7.1.** A free-linear family through a general point (`r_a <= 2 d_a`) has
`(B+8K) <= -4D < 0`. A globally free-linear family (`r_a <= 2 d_a + 1`) has
`(B+8K) <= 21 - 4D`, which is negative once `D >= 6`.

*Proof.*
* The pair zeros total `sum_{a<b} (d_a + d_b) = 6D`.
* With `n_k` events of size `k`: the triple-type contacts are `n_3 + n_4`, and the pair-type
  contacts are `6D - 3n_3 - 6n_4 - 10n_5 + n_5`. So
  `(B+8K) = 6 n_3 + 11 n_4 + 15 n_5 - 10 D`.
* Free-linearity gives `3n_3 + 4n_4 + 5n_5 = sum_a r_a <= 2D` (resp. `<= 2D + 7`).
* Since `6 <= 9`, `11 <= 12` and `15 <= 15`, we get `6n_3 + 11n_4 + 15n_5 <= 3 · 2D` (resp. `<= 3(2D + 7)`). `[]`

So **richness forces the linear systems to be overdetermined at generic event positions**, and every
rich family is cut out by nonlinear conditions in the graph presentation. This explains:
* why interpolation constructions fail (r7a Theorem 5(c), round 4 §8);
* why the Möbius-12 families are not addable.

`V_A` escapes because its linear structure lives elsewhere: in the coefficients of the quadric
(Section 2, Remark 1), where "an event at `P_e`" is one linear condition with no position to fix.

## 8. Rational curves of class exactly beta

In the graph presentation of Section 7, with only 2- and 3-collisions, a curve of class `beta`
forces `d_a = 3` for every label. Label `a` lies in 15 triple events and 6 pure pair contacts, and
`5 d_a + D = 36` with `D = 21`. So class-`beta` curves are

> seven cubic graphs on `P^1 x P^1` such that every three of them are concurrent (35 points), with
> the 21 residual pairwise intersections simple and distinct.

* Expected dimension: `7 · 7 - 6 - 35 = 8`.
* Equivalent formulation: the seven cubic forms span a 7-dimensional `W ⊂ H^0(O(3,1))`, so
  `P^1 x P^1 -> P(W^*) = P^6` is a sextic surface `S`. The 35 coordinate subspaces of codimension 3
  must all meet `S`.

*NUMERICAL* (`graph.py`, `beta_grow2.py`, `beta_grow3.py`):
* Continuation adds the 35 triple events one at a time, keeping every intermediate curve
  nondegenerate (all contacts distinct; every event exactly a triple).
* First batch (`beta_grow2.py`, seeds 11–14, about 2.5 hours each, killed by the time limit)

## 9. What remains, and what the outcome means

### 9.1 Status of the known refutation mechanisms

| mechanism | requirement | status after this round |
|---|---|---|
| round 4 Thm 2(a): dense Q-rational curves of class `≈ q beta` | `Q`-points of a component `M_k` of balanced rational curves Zariski dense | open (see below) |
| round 4 Thm 2(b): birational product chart | `beta ∈` ruling cone; then each ruling family has `lambda >= 2c_k > 0` (Prop. 5.2) | no candidate: forgetful `-9`, Kapranov lines `-26`, `V_A <= -23` (Prop. 5.4) |
| corrected addition (Prop. 5.1) | addable families with `lambda_V(beta) > 0`, `beta ∈` their cone | no candidate (same reason) |
| r7a Thm 3 (one rich addable family) | — | **withdrawn** (Section 4); its hypothesis is met by `V_A`, which provably cannot be the last family of any such construction (Prop. 5.4 with Cor. 4.5) |
| round 4 Thm 2(c): positive-rank genus one | dense family | untouched this round |

* *On 2(a).* The component `M_k` of r7a Prop. 2.4 has smooth Q-points. With Theorem B, the comb
  there can be built from **Q-rational** teeth: members of `V_A` and its `S_7`-translates through
  Q-points, plus forgetful fibres. It is a free comb, hence a smooth point.
* *Conditional statement.* Suppose `M_k` is rationally connected (a geometric statement, unknown),
  and suppose the standard conjecture holds that rational points on a smooth proper rationally
  connected variety over a number field are dense once one exists (a consequence of
  Colliot-Thélène's conjecture on the Brauer–Manin obstruction). Then `M_k(Q)` is dense and
  `(L_7)` is false. Nothing proved here removes either hypothesis.

### 9.2 Open questions, sharpened

* **Q1** (Sections 5.3–5.4). Is `lambda_V(beta) < 0` for every covering family with one member
  through a general point? Yes would close the chart and addition routes. It holds for every family
  computed so far, including the only known rich addable Q-family (`V_A <= -23`, proved).
* **Q2 (flexible rich families).** For families whose members through `x` form a
  positive-dimensional, `Q(x)`-rational variety `Z_x`, the section in Proposition 4.4 may be chosen
  along `C`. The relevant invariant is then
  `lambda^max_V(C) = max over rational choices of the degree of the tangent line field`, and
  Corollary 4.5 becomes `lambda^max_V(beta) >= 0`. A natural first candidate, in the star model:
  * the rulings of cubic surfaces with a double line `L_0 ∋ P_{a1 b1 c1}` passing through the other
    seven cube points;
  * through `x`, `L_0` runs over the pencil of lines through `P_{a1b1c1}` meeting `R_x`, and the
    cubic is then unique and linear;
  * parameter count: dimension 4, `pair = 9`, `triple = 18`, `(B+8K) = +3`, `Z_x ≅ P^1_{Q(x)}`.

  *This family has not been verified.* Its `lambda^max` is unknown.
* **Q3.** Do rational curves of class exactly `beta` exist (Section 8), and is their 8-dimensional
  family unirational over `Q`?

### 9.3 Meaning for the uniform theorem and for growth

* `(L_7)` and `(L_8)` are the missing lemmas of the Ptolemy route (`threshold/L8.md` §1,
  `growth/ptolemy.md` §7). Proving either would give the uniform theorem by Steps 0–5 of ptolemy
  §6.2, and with it a growth improvement.
* This round proves neither, so **nothing changes for the uniform theorem or for growth.** The
  proved bound `M <= (2/3 + eps) log R/loglog R` and the conditional Vojta theorem are untouched.
* What changes is the map of the `k = 7` problem:
  * r7a's claim that a refutation needs only one rich addable family is withdrawn;
  * such a family now exists (Theorem B), so richness and Q-rationality together are **not**
    the obstruction;
  * the obstruction for every addition- or chart-type construction is the sign of
    `lambda_V(beta) = -K_{F_V}.beta`. It is negative for the forgetful and Kapranov fibrations, and
    **provably negative for `V_A`** (`<= -23`, Proposition 5.4). So the one rich addable Q-family
    known cannot finish any such construction.
* If `(L_7)` is false, as the heuristics suggest, the Ptolemy route must go through `k >= 8`. That
  is Vojta-strength (L8 Theorem A rules out Liouville-type arguments). If `(L_7)` is true, its proof
  must be arithmetic: the function-field analogue is false (r7a Corollary 2), and by this round it
  must in particular explain why no fibration with `lambda > 0` exists, or why its Q-points do not
  spread.
