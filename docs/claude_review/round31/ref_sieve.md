## §3. The multiplicative sieve rigidity theorem

### 3.0 Is (3) the record's (**)?
Yes. Round 20 (**) quantifies over primitive α′ coprime to S and over β coprime to S with dβ primitive. If d is
S-smooth and primitive and β is primitive and coprime to S, then dβ is primitive: a rational p | dβ would need both
π_p and π̄_p in d if p ∈ S, or p | β if p ∉ S. Conversely, if dβ is primitive then β is primitive. The graft is the
round-20 "multiplicative graft", where every primitive dβ moves to ψ_d + arg β. Hence (3) = (**), word for word.

### 3.1 Step 1 (irrational directions, Ratner). CORRECT
* H = Δ(SL₂(R)) is connected and generated by unipotent one-parameter subgroups. Γ(q)×Γ(q) is a lattice in G×G.
  Ratner (Duke 63 (1991) 235–280, topological theorem) says the closure of H·x is L·x, where L is a closed
  connected subgroup containing H and L ∩ xΓ²x⁻¹ is a lattice in L. The citation and the statement used are right.
* Intermediate Lie algebras. As a Δ-module, (sl₂⊕sl₂)/Δ ≅ adjoint, which is irreducible. So h/Δ is 0 or
  everything. This is the correct argument. It is not invalidated by the fact that sl₂⊕sl₂ is V⊕V with two
  isomorphic summands, because only the quotient matters. Connected L is then Δ or G×G.
* In the case L = Δ with x = (1,U), the stabiliser is {(g,g): g ∈ Γ ∩ UΓU⁻¹}. It is a lattice, so it has finite
  index in Γ and in UΓU⁻¹: the two groups are commensurable.
* Commensurability and rational directions. Only one direction is used: commensurable ⇒ U maps P¹(Q) to P¹(Q).
  The proof is right. Some power of each parabolic element lies in the intersection, so both groups have the same
  parabolic fixed lines. Those of Γ(q) are exactly the rational lines, and those of UΓU⁻¹ are U(rational lines).
  The converse is also true for rotations, though not needed. If U is rotation by arg(a+bi), then UΓU⁻¹ = BΓB⁻¹
  with B = [[a,−b],[b,a]] ∈ GL₂(Q), and B commensurates Γ(q). So the "equivalence" the brief asks about holds, and
  the half that is used is proved correctly.
* Density gives the vectors. Take the target (Γ,Γ) and the same g in both coordinates, so gγ₀ ≈ I and gUγ₁ ≈ I.
  - α = γ₀e_j and β = γ₁e_j are columns of SL₂(Z) matrices, so they are primitive.
  - γ ≡ I (mod q) gives |α|² ≡ |β|² ≡ 1 (mod q), so the norms are coprime to q.
  - det(α,Uβ) = det(gα, gUβ) = O(ε).
  - Choose j with |g⁻¹e_j| ≥ ‖g⁻¹‖/√2. This is possible by the Frobenius norm. Then |α| and |β| lie in
    [(1/√2−ε)‖g⁻¹‖, (1+ε)‖g⁻¹‖].
  - Since sin‖x‖_u ≤ |sin x|, the left side of (3) times max(hNβ, Nα) is ≤ |det|·h·max(|α|,|β|)²/(|α||β|) = O(hε).
  - If α ∥ Uβ exactly, ψ_d would be rational, which contradicts the hypothesis. In any case the value would be 0.

  Checked line by line.

### 3.2 Step 2 (rational direction height cost). CORRECT
* Choosing w. N(w+kv) mod p is a polynomial of degree ≤ 2 in k. It is not identically 0: if it were, the line
  w + kv would lie in the isotropic locus (two lines through 0, since p splits), forcing det(v,w) ≡ 0. Since p ≥ 5,
  a good residue exists, and CRT finishes.
* The matrices. g_n = I + nqQ·aℓ has det 1 + nqQ·ℓ(a) = 1. Also B_b⁻¹g_nB_b = I + nq·adj(B_b)aℓB_b, an integer
  matrix ≡ I (mod q). Both lie in Γ(q). Then β_n = (B_b⁻¹g_nB_b)e₁, and det(bβ_n, α_n) = det(g_nv, g_nw) = 1.
* The limits. |α_n|/|bβ_n| → |ℓ(w)/ℓ(v)|, which can be any positive rational because (v,w) is a Z-basis. The
  normalised quantity is exactly max(r, h/(Qr)), with infimum √(h/Q). Hence Q ≤ h/c². In fact the infimum *equals*
  √(h/Q), because |det| ≥ 1 gives the matching lower bound.
* Independent search (`ratcost.py`, a different construction: solve det = 1 directly for each β in a box). For
  φ₅ = arg(3+4i) at k = 2, 4, 8 and φ₅ = arg(3+2i) at k = 2, 4, the minimum of normalised²/(h/Q) is 1.0046, 1.043,
  1.057, 1.005 and 1.004. It is never below 1. This confirms the claim and the "cost compounds" remark.
* Even powers. Write arg b = arg b₀ + tπ/4, where at most one factor (1+i) is possible in a primitive element. Then
  2kφ_p ≡ arg b₀^{2k} (mod π/2), with primitive norm Q₀^{2k} ≤ p^{2k}/c² for every k. So Q₀ ≤ p. Correct.

### 3.3 Identifying the primes. CORRECT (wording nit)
* Full rank. A nonzero integer relation x among the S-restricted signed-valuation vectors gives a nonunit primitive
  S-smooth d = ∏π_p^{x_p} (conjugates for x_p < 0). The direction ψ_d has primitive representative α with zero
  signed valuation at every prime of S, so its norm is coprime to q. Then β = 1 gives an exact collision. Correct.
  *Nit:* the text says "relation among its columns"; with rows indexed by p it is a relation among rows. This is
  immaterial for a square matrix.
* Triangularity. Q_{0,p} ≤ p kills the entries at primes p′ > p. The diagonal must be nonzero, so p | Q_{0,p} ≤ p,
  hence Q_{0,p} = p and b_{0,p} ~ π_p or π̄_p. This gives (4).
* Converse. The map (signed valuations, ramified bit) → (sign-flipped valuations, ramified bit + parity) is
  injective and at most doubles the norm. Distinct primitive directions of norm ≤ 2X are at sine distance
  ≥ 1/(2X). This proves full spacing, including fake–fake pairs. Correct.
* Common-phase bookkeeping: (2a−e)tπ/4 = −etπ/4 + atπ/2. Correct, and no drifting phase is set to zero.

### 3.4 Operator bound "AX + B₀K, B₀ < 4 ⇒ spacing". CORRECT
* Suppose two angles are within arccos(b)/(4K) of each other. Every term of Σ_{|k|≤K} cos(4kΔ) is ≥ b, so the 2×2
  principal minor has top eigenvalue ≥ (2K+1)(1+b).
* With K = ⌈(A+1)X/(2(1+b)−B₀)⌉ this exceeds AX + B₀K. So the spacing is ≥ arccos(b)/(4K) ≫ 1/X, and (3) follows
  with X = max(hNβ, Nα).
* The threshold is exactly the record's. The genuine constant is D = 2K + πX/2 (round 21 Thm LS), so B₀ < 4 is the
  record's "c < 2". Its prime-only analogue is still open (round 21 `fakes.md` l.28–30; overview §5.5, second bullet).

### 3.5 The p = 13 one-prime graft. CORRECT
* The parity facts for (2+3i)^n with n odd hold: Im ≡ 0 (mod 3), Re ≡ 0 (mod 2), both nonzero. The two half-angle
  inequalities |sin 2x| ≤ 2 sin‖x‖_u and |cos 2x| ≤ 2 sin‖x−π/4‖_u are right.
  Numerically, over 1 ≤ n ≤ 300: min sin‖nφ‖_u·13^{n/2} = 1.70 and diagonal min = 1.045. Both are above the claimed
  1 and 1/√2.
* Fibre decomposition. Exponent 2h+r ↦ h is injective per class and |h| ≤ |2h+r|. Each class is a translate of a
  subset of the genuine angles. The union bound gives 2D = 4K + πX, i.e. B₀ = 4 exactly, consistent with §3.4
  being sharp.
* My check (`p13.py`), all primitive ideals:

  | X | genuine X·minspacing | graft X·minspacing |

  | 10⁴ | 1.020 | 3.2e-3 |

  Exact Gram top eigenvalues are always below 4K + πX. Example: X = 1000, K = 300 gives 1723 for the graft against

## §5. Hypothesis (5) and "(5) suffices for growth"

Every step checks:
* **Gram matrix.** ⟨v_i,v_j⟩ = 1 − 2 log d_ij/W, which is < 0 because d_ij ≥ 16√N on every edge (§2). Diagonal 1,
  so ‖G‖ ≤ 2. The argument: G ⪰ 0, so λ(I−G) ≤ 1; I−G is entrywise ≥ 0, so |xᵀ(I−G)x| ≤ |x|ᵀ(I−G)|x| ≤ 1.
  Correct.
* **Moments.** For |J| = 1, E f² = |Vu|²/m ≤ ‖G‖/m ≤ 2/m, with u a unit vector since Σw_l = W. For |J| ≥ 2,
  E f_J² = W⁻²Σ w_lw_{l′} c_{ll′}^{|J|} ≤ W⁻²Σ w_lw_{l′}c_{ll′}² = tr(G²)/m² ≤ ‖G‖·tr G/m² ≤ 2/m. Independence
  makes repeated slots harmless. Correct.
* **Markov and union bound.** Over 31 coefficients, K ≥ 16 gives failure probability ≤ 1/4.
  - Pattern weights are W/32 + O(W/√m), cut weights W/16 + O(W/√m), and W/√m ≪ √(W log W) when m ≫ W/log W.
  - Repeated rows have probability ≤ 10/m.
  - E ΣL over 10 edges of a distinct ordered tuple is ≤ 10·(mW/4)/C(m,2) = 5W/(m−1), and 0 ≤ log t ≤ L/2 (t ≥ 1 is an
    integer within one unit class).
  - So Markov gives H = O(W/m) = O(log W).

  The combined good probability is ≥ 1/2 − o(1). Correct.
* **gcd.** Dividing by the Gaussian gcd removes exactly the layers that are constant on the tuple, keeps all 15
  nontrivial cut weights and the t_ij, and leaves the primitive norm dividing N. This is the standard record fact
  (Lemma K1). Correct.
* **Distinct classes.** The first directed ratio fixes A up to sign. Then g′ = z_{i₁}/Ā lies on an arc of length
  ≤ ½N^{−1/4}√(N/d) ≤ 1/8, which holds at most one Gaussian integer. The remaining ratios determine the rest.
  Injective. Correct.
* **Schwartz–Zippel.** Homogenise to z₁^D F(z₂/z₁, …). Zeros in S⁵ number ≤ D·m⁴ = o(m⁵), since D = o(W/log W) and
  m ≫ W/log W. The ≫ m⁵ ≫ (W/log W)⁵ surviving classes contradict (5). Correct.
* **(L_5°) ⇒ (5).** Balance B√(W log W) ≤ η₀W/16 eventually, and B log W < cW/16, so all of B_B(N) lies in the
  fixed Z. One nonzero (Laurent, then cleared) polynomial in the four complex ratio coordinates vanishes on Z, and
  its degree is fixed. Correct.
* Quantifiers: o_B is uniform over parents, B is fixed by the failure size, and there is no fixed-S exceptional
  set. Fine.

**Novelty.** The proof is a routine variant of round 23 `split.md` Thm 1 (random 5-subset, averaging over threshold
layers, exact gcd division, Schwartz–Zippel) and of round 20 `gauss.md` Thm 3.4′. What is new:
* the explicit O(W/√m) balance via ‖G‖ ≤ 2;
* allowing an N-dependent exceptional polynomial of degree o(W/log W) and o(m⁵) leftover;
* replacing the linear cofactor lower bound by H ≤ B log W.

**Significance.** It is a legitimate, formally weaker sufficient target than (L_5°). It is not shown to be easier.
It is open, and no method in the record bears on it differently from (L_5°). It does not change the status of
Question B. Peripheral.

## §6. Four-point counterexample with primary generators

* **Identities.** `fourpt.py`, symbolic over Q(x,m):
  - xyz − u² = 0, with u = my for all three permutations. abc is symmetric, so "u = mb" in the text should be read
    as u = √(abc) = my.
  - 1 + (a−1)³ + (b−1)³ + c³ − 3(1+c)ab = 0.
  - Im p₁ = Im p₂ = Im p₃ = 0 for all three quartics.

  So each P_j has constant imaginary part on R (Newton). Correct.
* **Jacobian.** The exact logarithmic Jacobian over Q(i) at (x,m,s) = (−3,3,7) has determinant
  **1674 i / 1349553725 ≠ 0**. Reduced mod 101 with i ↦ 10 it gives [[15,12,93],[42,52,27],[81,73,35]] with
  det 54, identical to Astra's matrix. The seven roots i, −6−4i, −6−3i, −6+5i, −7+6i, −3−2i, −4−3i are nonreal and
  distinct up to conjugation.
* **Explicit integers.** `fourpt_int.py`: d = 4, n = 10^k + 1 with k = 3, 5, 7, 9, 12.
  - All normalised points are primary and of equal norm.
  - The normalised gcd norm is constant (2025), so the gcd is bounded.
  - diam/n³ → 4778.67 and R/n⁷ → 0.022222, i.e. exponent 3/7.
  - All six t_ab are integers and constant in n (35840, 9216, 4096, −4096, −1024, 5120). This confirms one unit
    class, integral t, and H_Σ = O(1).
* **Completion.** Dominance, preimage of a proper Z, and choice of rational (x,m) off a proper closed set: standard
  and correct. Primary quotients of primary elements are primary. Inert primes appear equally in all four points
  and are removed by the gcd. At most one of the 14 forms exceeds v_λ(D), so the gcd is bounded. Each of the seven
  cuts corresponds to one root, with weight 2 log n + O(1), and each pair is separated by 4 cuts, so
  t = O(n⁴·n³/n⁷). Correct.
* **Novelty.** The result itself is the record's Theorem 4.5 (round 20 gauss Thm 4.1, killer-checked). The family
  is different: 3 rational parameters with Newton-identity quartics, not the record's linear blocks. The
  primary-generator one-unit-class version repairs the round-20 killer minor item 2, which the record already
  judged harmless (H_Σ shifts by O(1)). Peripheral.
