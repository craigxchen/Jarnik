The target I propose (argue with it if you see a better one, but then commit to yours):

 DECIDE WHETHER INTEGRAL FAKES EXIST AT GROWTH SCALE.

 Definition. An integral fake of size m at weight W: a squarefree-or-not split parent N with log N = W, an exponent profile with pair separation, and a 2 x m integer matrix with primitive columns in a cone of angular width <= C N^(-1/4), with det(P_i,P_j) = t_ij g_ij for the prescribed g_ij, t_ij nonzero, and |t_ij| within the genuine bound. No norm condition. Genuine clusters are integral fakes, so:
   (P) a proof that integral fakes need W >= omega(m) m log m with omega -> infinity is a growth-rate theorem (this is what we are after; even omega = any unbounded function);
   (C) an integral fake with W = O(m log m) and m -> infinity is a new barrier, strictly stronger than F1: it would say that the whole determinant/Pluecker/divisibility formulation (hence Question A's data at growing size) cannot prove growth, and that exact norms are the only remaining lever.
 Either outcome is worth more than everything in Parts I-III. What I can contribute:
   - Your III.7 says that for m >~ 10^4 the labelled d_ij determine the configuration in M_{0,m}(Q). So (P)/(C) is a question about one explicit rational configuration per labelled profile: can the unique h_ij be scaled to small nonzero integers (your III.10 residual problem). Uniqueness means there is nothing left to choose except the parent and the profile; that looks like the right place for either a counting argument over profiles on a FIXED worst parent, or a construction.
   - Heuristic count. For each p | N the columns fall into two classes mod p (the cut), i.e. two points L_p^+, L_p^- of P^1(F_p). In a genuine cluster these are the two isotropic points of x^2+y^2; in an integral fake they are free: about N^2 choices in all. Column i then lies in a lattice of index N' (star norm) cut out by its side at each prime, and must be a short vector of it inside the cone: probability about N^(-1/4) each, presumably independent-looking. Expected number of integral fakes on a given profile is about N^(2 - m/4): this is 1 exactly at m = 8, which I take to be where (L_8) comes from. So for m > 8 integral fakes should not exist "generically", and genuine clusters, if they exist, exist for a structural reason. A proof of (P) therefore needs an input that is NOT a first-moment over the L_p (the adversary chooses them), and a construction for (C) needs structure replacing the Gaussian one: another quadratic order, a non-quadratic multiplicative structure on Z^2, a parametrised family as in your III.11 but at growing m, etc. III.11's construction stops at m = 6 for pair-cut-only profiles; with balanced cuts I do not know.
   - Calibration. Genuine clusters satisfy W >= 3 m log m - O(m). The record's fakes (Paley, angle models) sit at W <= (8+o(1)) m log m. Between 3 and 8 nothing is known in either direction, even for angle fakes. If your argument for (P) only improves the 3 for integral fakes, say so: a mechanism that uses integrality on every parent and moves the constant would already be new.
 Kill criteria, so that we do not drift: (i) if (P) reduces to a statement that angle fakes satisfy, stop and say which; (ii) if (P) reduces to (L_8) or to a zero estimate, state the reduction in one paragraph and stop; (iii) if (C) can only be done with a positive fraction of the log divisibility missing, give the best fraction with a proof that the method cannot do better, and stop.
