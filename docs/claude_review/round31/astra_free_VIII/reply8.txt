DECISION. The judge recommends closing the function-field programme, and I agree: seven exchanges have produced a clean and complete negative picture there, the escape that is left (exactly squarefree N in odd characteristic) has no client among integer arguments, and nothing has moved over Z. Unless you object with a reason, we stop asking function-field questions.

THE EIGHTH QUESTION. Return to the integers, with what the function-field work taught us used as a tool rather than as a subject.

Two facts frame it.
(F1) The uniform bound cannot come from any argument that is function-field valid. A proof must use characteristic 0 essentially, or the archimedean order, or transcendence input.
(F2) The GROWTH RATE is not filtered at all. Theorem 1.1 (W = log N >= 3 M log M - O(M)) transfers verbatim to every one of our models, and no function-field family comes near it: the best constructions have W about M^2 log M over F_2[t] and log W about M log M over F_3[t]. Over Z the angle-model fakes of the record sit at W <= (8+o(1)) M log M for pair, cut, monomial-gap and rational-character inputs. So there is a wide gap in which an argument, even a function-field-valid one, could improve Theorem 1.1, and the record's standing instruction is that any improvement of the rate log R/loglog R, or failing that of the constant 2/3, is progress.

I ask you to choose ONE of the following, say why, and prove a theorem about lattice points on circles over Z. In each case mark every step that fails in the F_2[t] or F_3[t] models; if no step fails there, test the statement against your own constructions before believing it.

(a) Power saving through the binary model. If you believe W(M) >= M^{1+c} over F_2[t] (the data say about M^2 log M), find the proof there, where the combinatorics is clean and there are no fakes, and port it. A proof of W >= omega(M) M log M whose steps exist over Z improves the rate. If instead you find a family with W = O(M log M) over F_2[t], then elementary collision arguments are exhausted in that model and we learn that the integer rate needs input of type (F1).

(b) The archimedean order. The M points of a cluster are ordered on the arc, consecutive chords are real and positive in a common direction up to O(R^{-1/2}), and the curvature gives the triple inequality. Theorem 1.1 uses the archimedean place only through the upper bound for the product of chords. The function-field models have no order at infinity. Is there an inequality for ordered clusters (for instance on the cut numbers S_{p,tau} restricted to intervals of consecutive points, or on the signs of the reduced chords c_ij) that fails for the Paley fakes or for your unordered function-field clusters, and that raises the constant 3? The record's fakes are angle-model fakes: check any proposed inequality against round8/lw.md Thm 6.1 and round5/.

(c) The low-height window P_+(N') <= M^{2-delta} (round21/README.md; overview/overview.md section 5.5): the one regime of the growth problem where no fake is known and no restricted theorem is known. Either prove a growth theorem there or build the fake.

(d) An arithmetic form of the characteristic-0 inputs. Your Part IV bound M(D,0) <= 24 came from Newton linearisation and orbifold BMY; the port analysis isolated uniqueness of same-degree right composition factors (Lemma 1). If any of these has an unconditional arithmetic shadow that constrains integer clusters, however weakly (a bound in a restricted class of N, a statement at one prime of good reduction, an Arakelov inequality on a specific arithmetic surface with explicit constants), state it and prove it.

If you judge all four hopeless, say so plainly and say what you would do instead; I would rather have an honest "no route" with reasons than a fifth function-field theorem.

As before: full proofs or exact reproducible computations, statements labelled proved / computed / heuristic, say plainly what you could not do, and append the result as Part VIII to answer.md. The standing cautions of the record apply: no uniform exceptional sets from fixed-S Subspace theorems, no discarded Gaussian gcd or denominator costs, no drifting common phase replaced by zero, no template-dependent threshold treated as uniform in the radius.
