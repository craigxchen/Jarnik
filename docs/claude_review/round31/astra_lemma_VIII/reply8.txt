REFEREE RESULTS ON PART VII, AND AN EIGHTH QUESTION

Seven referees and a judge went through Part VII. Nothing you claimed is false. The judge's report is in this directory as referee_report_VII.md (also /home/user/Jarnik/docs/claude_review/round31/README_VIIL.md, with ref7L_*.md beside it). The points that matter:

1. VII.2 (the sparse theorem) is recorded as a theorem. The judge checked the two steps you flagged, line by line: the moving-coefficient gcd estimate (uniformity over coefficients is genuine, because for fixed Q the exponent set of R is finite and independent of the coefficients; the v-weight bound is O(L/Q) with the b' cancelling) and the passage from one n_* to a common exceptional set (every "eventually" is over finitely many objects fixed in advance or along the one diagonal subsequence). One wording correction: the frozen family must have EXACT supports (coefficients nonzero on the support, F_i - F_1 of exact support S_i1); with supports merely contained in the frozen ones the top coefficient of F_1 can vanish and the contact drops. Additions:
   - The covering inequality holds in the sharper form 4 h_0 + 1 <= #(charged places) <= W_0; on your Pell family the log-Wronskian has ord_inf = 4h+1 exactly and W simple poles.
   - The constant 1/4 is optimal as b grows: your Pell family at fixed n composed with t^k is b-sparse with b <= 8n+2 and h/W = 4n/(16n+2).
   - Attribution: the gcd lemma is the case S = {0, infinity}, height-0 coefficients, of Guo-Sun-Wang (J. Number Theory 231 (2022), Thm 8). "Arbitrary complex coefficients" are height-0 constants, so VII.2 is not Diophantinely stronger than your VI.1; its real gain is that no honest-block hypothesis is needed.
2. (VII.4), 20h <= 9D + 18r, is a theorem. It is false with "line" in place of "circle" (five marks with all roots on the imaginary axis, D = 6, h = 3).
3. VII.5 is correct, and h = 4n holds for EVERY choice of pairwise distinct real seeds (the pair z_12, z_13 is pinned at contact exactly 4n by G_s conj(G_u) - conj(G_s) G_u = 2i(s-u)); your condition on s_j + s_k only decides whether an anchor pair reaches 4n+1. The judge reproduced D = 9, 17, 25 with independent exact arithmetic.
4. VII.6 is correct with one correction: "rank <= 1" holds for the Y = 0 slice; the rank of the whole family, source derivative included, is 2, still < 4. The covering inequality itself is false in characteristic 2 (W_0 = 30, h_0 = 8 dominates). A split family with b = 5 already refutes the sparse statement there.
5. Referee data on the unrestricted question: no five marks at D = 7, h = 4 (exact, Groebner over Q), so M(7,0) = 4. Rigid real five-mark configurations with h = (D+1)/2 exist at D = 11 and D = 13 (numerical to 40 digits, not interval-certified). So the strict inequality 2h > D is not vacuous, and any proof must put these rigid curves in the exceptional set.
6. A consequence over Q that you did not draw, now checked by three further referees with exact arithmetic. Specialising your five-mark Pell family at integers T (seeds rational, chosen after Z; gcd bounded by a Bezout integer depending on the seeds) gives, for every n and every proper closed Z, infinitely many primitive five-tuples of Gaussian integers off Z with dist^{4+1/(2n)} H_{-K} bounded. Hence alpha_ess_e(-K) <= 4 and A_5(delta) is false for every delta < 0. The known window is now 10/3 <= alpha_ess <= 4. The tuples are unbalanced (5 of 15 cuts) and not in one unit class, so nothing follows for (L_5^o) or for delta >= 0. One referee also computed that at a FIXED integer T with n -> infinity each curve has exponent exactly 4 with a seed-dependent constant (not refereed).

THE EIGHTH QUESTION: WHAT IS THE WEAKEST HYPOTHESIS, AND HOW HARD IS THE CONJECTURE?

After thirty-one rounds the record has no unconditional gain over Liouville in Question B and no growth bound beyond Theorem 1.1 (M <= (2/3 + eps) log R / loglog R). Its only conditional results use Vojta's Main Conjecture for one fixed variety and give the uniform bound outright (expert_note section 1; growth/conditional.md Thm 5.1; growth/ptolemy.md section 6; section 3 of the note). The note says: "no intermediate growth bound is known from a weaker standard conjecture." I ask you to attack that sentence from both sides. Choose (a) or (b), or do both if one is quick.

(a) A conditional theorem from a weaker or more standard hypothesis. Prove M(C) < infinity, or M = o(log R / loglog R), or even a constant below 2/3, from one of: abc over Q(i) (or the n-term abc of Browkin-Brzezinski with explicit dependence on n; uniform abc); Lang-Vojta or Bombieri-Lang for a specific variety together with a correlation or fibre-power argument of Caporaso-Harris-Mazur / Abramovich / Ascher-Turchet type, adapted to archimedean closeness rather than S-integrality; a quantitative Subspace conjecture with polynomial dependence on |S|; or any hypothesis you can defend as strictly weaker than the Vojta instances the record uses. The record's barrier 6 says where plain abc on character relations stops (abc on chord relations admits fakes and abc on character relations is equivalent to the Lang-Waldschmidt support test); read expert_note section 4 (the abc criterion), section 5 item 6 (abc on single relations) and round8/weakest.md before choosing, and say exactly which additional input your hypothesis supplies. A proof that a named hypothesis is INSUFFICIENT in a precise class (a fake satisfying it) is also a result.

(b) A hardness statement. Prove that the uniform bound, or A_5(delta) for some delta < 1/30, or (L_8), implies a recognised open statement (a case of abc with explicit exponent, a uniform S-unit bound, a statement about gaps between values of binary forms or about simultaneous approximation of logarithms of Gaussian primes, a uniform bound for integral points on a named family), or is equivalent to one. The specialisation direction of item 6 shows that explicit families transfer information from Q(i)[t] to Q; is there a family whose specialisations turn the conjecture into a statement people already study? Even a clean implication "Cilleruelo-Granville at exponent 1/2 implies X" with X believed hard would tell us how much input any proof must contain, and it would be new to the record.

In either case I want theorems about integers. If you conclude that neither side yields anything, say so with reasons and tell me what you would ask instead.

As before: full proofs or exact reproducible computations, statements labelled proved / computed / heuristic, say plainly what you could not do, and append the result as Part VIII to answer.md. The standing cautions of the record apply: no uniform exceptional sets from fixed-S Subspace theorems, no discarded Gaussian gcd or denominator costs, no drifting common phase replaced by zero, no template-dependent threshold treated as uniform in the radius.
