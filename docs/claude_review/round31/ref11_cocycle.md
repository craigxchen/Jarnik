## 0. Results

All statements fix C > 0 first, then take q = q_P with P large in terms of C, unless something else is said.

**R1. Canonical identity (Proved; also Computed).** For q an odd squarefree product of split primes, one unit eta, and marks A_i = eta prod_p f_ip with distinct rows, A_j - A_i = 2 i eta H_ij T_ij, where T_ij = -Im X_ij is a nonzero rational integer, 0 < T_ij^2 < Q_ij, and |T_ij| = gcd(Re(A_j - A_i), Im(A_j - A_i))/2. If q > C^4/16, the bound on t_ij forces every pair of the configuration to have distinct rows. Exact check: 20,000 random pairs on q_200, 0 failures.

**R2. Rigidity (XI.6) (Proved).** Her proof is correct. Corrected statement with quantifiers: let M >= 3 and q > C^4/16. Let z_1..z_M in Z[i] satisfy z_j - z_i = 2 i eta H_ij t_ij with t_ij in Z \ {0} for all i < j. Then there are lambda = u/v in Q^x (lowest terms, v > 0) and w in v^{-1} Z[i] with z_i = w + lambda A_i for every i. The proof needs only that each z_j - z_i is a real multiple of A_j - A_i (exact chord direction). Integrality of z then makes lambda rational. For M = 2 the statement is empty.

**R3. Characterisation (Proved; sharper than her text).** Let S be a set of M >= 3 marks in one unit class, and put G(S) = gcd over pairs of T_ij. The marks S carry an integral-cofactor configuration at constant C, for some lambda and w, if and only if

  diam(S) <= C q^{1/4} G(S).

The admissible lambda are exactly u/v with v | G(S) and |u| diam(S) <= v C q^{1/4}, and lambda = 1/G(S) is always the best choice. Plainly: an integral-cofactor fake is a translate of (1/v) times a set of genuine lattice points of |z|^2 = q in one unit class, pairwise "T-congruent" modulo v, which lie on an arc v times longer than critical. For gcd(v, 2q) = 1, T-congruence is ordinary congruence A_i = A_j mod v Z[i]. The fake is genuine (lambda = +-1) exactly when v = 1. Equivalently, its points are lattice points on the circle with centre w and radius sqrt(q)/v. The required exponent rows can always be realised exactly by a further translation (CRT; Section 3.3). So the row condition in (A2) adds nothing.

**R4. The s/4 bound for these configurations (Proved).** (XI.2) holds with B_ij = 2 i eta t_ij, so XI.1 applies uniformly in lambda and w. I checked every step of XI.1, including the class count modulo l^{a+1} for a = ord_l(v) >= 1. Consequence: for P >= P_0(C), on both families, every integral-cofactor configuration on q_P has at most (s/4)(1 + O_C(loglog P/log P)) points. Equivalently: for every v >= 1, at most that many marks of one unit class are pairwise T-congruent mod v within diameter v C q^{1/4}. In particular **no integral-cofactor fake with M >= (1/4 + eps) s exists for P >= P_0(C, eps)**. This answers the "M >> s" form of (A2) negatively.

**R5. Integral-cofactor fakes with M = 3 exist on q_200 (Computed, exact).** The parent is q_200 = 4810942114471671677256758751310693840381 (s = 16). Take the eta = 1 marks with row indices 436, 12399, 50763 (bit k of the index chooses pi_pk). Then G = gcd T_ij = 4391525450 = 2 * 5^2 * 13 * 101 * 151 * 443, and lambda = 1/G, w = -A_436/G. This gives

  z = (0, 0), (3439204080, 29559507552), (11203893610, 21049952704),

with t = -24, -1758853, -1. The ratios |t_ij| / ((1/2) sqrt(Q_ij) q^{-1/4}) are 3.573, 2.863, 1.383. So (A2) holds at every C >= 3.574 (in particular at C = 4 and C = 8) and fails at C = 3. All conditions were verified exactly from the rows: canonical identities, integrality, the form 2 i eta H_ij t_ij, and the bound on t_ij. After a translation the exponent rows are exact (valuation 1 at f_ip and 0 at conj f_ip, for all 16 primes, for all three points). The shape has |S_0| = 1, so the eta = 1 class contains 4 copies (flip on S_0, complex conjugation) and the four unit classes contain 16. Seen from the fake's own circle (radius sqrt(q)/G = 1.9 q^{1/4}), the three points span 0.94 of a diameter. In the genuine geometry they are three lattice points of |z|^2 = q_200, congruent modulo G and spread over almost a diameter. Compare: M_max(q_200, 8) = 1 for genuine clusters (her XI.3 and README_X). **So on q_200 the integer-cofactor cocycle without numerical residues is strictly weaker than the circle: it allows 3 points where the circle allows 1.**

**R6. Pairs are trivial (Proved; Computed).** For every pair of marks with Q_ij >= 4 sqrt(q)/C^2, the choice lambda = 1/|T_ij| (t_ij = +-1) is a 2-point integral-cofactor configuration. On q_200, 59.9% of pairs qualify at C = 4 or 8 (exactly the pairs separated at >= 8 primes) and 40.2% at C = 1.

**R7. No 4-point configuration was found (Computed, partial search).** The smooth-modulus search was exhaustive over all 65,536 base marks of the eta = 1 class on q_200 at C = 8. It covers every configuration whose modulus G(S) has odd part, built from primes < 600 and prime powers < 3000 coprime to q, at least diam/(8 q^{1/4}). It found exactly 4 triples, all copies of the R5 shape (G = 4391525450, minimal C = 3.573). It found no triple with minimal C below 3.573 and no 4-point set: for every triple, each other candidate was tested as a fourth point. An unbiased random-pair search over all moduli (`mc.c`) is reported in Section 3.4. This is a lower bound on configurations, not a proof that no 4-set exists. A 4-set is heuristically not expected on q_200 for C <= 8 (E_4 of about 2.5e-4 C^2, R8).

**R8. Size heuristic (Heuristic).** The task's heuristic needs one correction: congruence modulo v costs about 1/v per extra point, not v^{-2}. The marks lie on the norm curve modulo v, which has about v residues; equivalently, v | T_ij is one integer divisibility. With N = 2^s marks per unit class, the expected number of M-point configurations is about E_M = C^{M-2} N^M/M! (2 pi q^{1/4})^{-(M-2)}, up to a constant factor. This sum is dominated by dilations v of order q^{1/4}/C, where the "arc" is the whole circle. A test of the pair statistic on q_200 (200 random v in [1e9, 4e9)) found 352 congruent pairs against 189 predicted, a factor of about 2 explained by the 2^{|S_0|} multiplicity of pair shapes. The smooth search found far fewer triples than E_3 predicts (Section 3.5), so E_M is an upper-order estimate only. On the 1 mod 4 family, log10 E_3(C = 1) is about 3 at P = 200, 6 at P = 1000, 3 at P = 2000, -2.8 at P = 3000 and -73 at P = 10^4. log10 E_4 is about -3.6 at P = 200 and falls rapidly. The 5 mod 8 family behaves the same way. **Heuristically, the largest integral-cofactor configuration on q_P is 3 for small P (up to about 2500) and 2 for all large P.** Against the proved s/4 bound, the truth for the cocycle is heuristically bounded, as it is for the circle.

**R9. Numerical-residue pinning (Proved; her statement correct).** Corrected statement: let M >= 2 and C^2 < 4 sqrt(q). Let S be a set of primes of q. Suppose that for every p in S some pair separated at p satisfies |z_j - z_i|^2 = |A_j - A_i|^2 mod p, and that prod_{p in S} p >= min_{i<j} Q_ij. Then lambda = +-1, and z_i = w +- A_i with w in Z[i]. Every step checks: p does not divide T_ij on a pair separated at p; t^2 = T^2 mod p gives p | u^2 - v^2; v | T_ij and u | t_ij for every pair; v^2 <= T_ij^2 < Q_ij and u^2 <= t_ij^2 <= C^2 Q_ij/(4 sqrt q) < Q_ij on the pair attaining min Q; hence 0 <= |u^2 - v^2| < min Q <= prod_S p, which forces u^2 = v^2. For M = 2 the "configuration" is a pair, and the conclusion then says it is a genuine pair. The R5 fake keeps the canonical squared-chord residue at no prime of q_200: G^2 = 1 mod p fails for all 16 primes. Under her weaker "local vertex value" reading of the signed residues, dilated fakes keep the residue law, because the squared chords are all multiplied by the same G^{-2}.

**R10. What Part XI answers.** On (A1) it gives a constant, 1/4 against 1/3 (any c < 1/12), and no rate. On (A2) it builds no unbounded fake. It proves a structure theorem (R2, R3): every integral-cofactor fake is a dilated congruence family of genuine points. The same s/4 bound applies to all of them, so none has M >= (1/4 + eps) s. It does not decide whether M = O(1); heuristically yes (R8). With canonical numerical residues at a prime set of product >= min Q_ij, every configuration is a translate of a genuine cluster, so on q_200 and q_250 none with M >= 2 exists at C <= 8. Her statements "no M = o(s)" and "no unbounded integral-cofactor fake" are accurate descriptions of what was not done.

**R11. Summary sentence.** The sentence "the cocycle with integer cofactors is not weaker than the norm equation up to dilation and translation" is **correct as a structural statement for M >= 3 and false as a counting statement**. The integral-cofactor configurations are exactly the images w + (1/v) S of sets S of genuine norm-q points. But the arc condition changes from length C q^{1/4} to length v C q^{1/4} with a congruence modulo v, a union over v of different problems, and the union is strictly larger (R5: 3 points against 1 on q_200 at C = 4). Corrected summary: *the integer-cofactor cocycle with exact chord directions is equivalent to the norm equation on the family of dilated arcs {length v C q^{1/4}, congruence mod v : v >= 1}. It is equivalent to the genuine problem (v = 1) exactly when lambda = +-1 is forced, for instance by canonical squared-chord residues at primes of product >= min Q_ij.*

Separation from angle models comes from two inputs, and neither works alone. (i) Exact chord directions give rigidity: if each z_j - z_i is only required to be a real multiple of A_j - A_i, with real cofactors, the rigidity still gives z = w + lambda A, but with lambda real. Then lambda = C q^{1/4}/(2 sqrt q) puts all 2^s marks of a unit class into one configuration, so M = 2^s. (ii) Integrality of t_ij gives the collision bound. Angle models fail (i): their differences have no exact canonical direction. So it is fair to say that exact directions plus integral cofactors carry the separation, and that exact directions alone do not.

## 1. Definitions

q is an odd squarefree product of s split primes p (for q_P: all p = 1 mod 4 in [P, 2P], or all p = 5 mod 8 there). For each p a generator pi_p is fixed. A sign row r in {0,1}^s determines the canonical mark A_r = eta prod_p f_rp, with f_rp = pi_p if r_p = 1 and conj(pi_p) otherwise, and eta a fixed unit. For rows i, j: D_ij is the set of primes where they differ, Q_ij = prod_{p in D_ij} p, H_ij = prod_{p not in D_ij} f_ip (so |H_ij|^2 = q/Q_ij), X_ij = prod_{p in D_ij} f_ip, and T_ij = -Im X_ij.

An **integral-cofactor configuration** at constant C (the object of reply11.txt, (A2)) consists of distinct rows r_1..r_M and Gaussian integers z_1..z_M with z_j - z_i = 2 i eta H_ij t_ij, where t_ij in Z \ {0} and |t_ij| <= (C/2) sqrt(Q_ij) q^{-1/4}. The norms |z_i|^2 are unconstrained. The triangle identities in Z[i] hold automatically because the z_i are actual Gaussian integers. Two marks are **T-congruent mod v** if v | T_ij. When gcd(v, 2q) = 1 this is the same as A_i = A_j mod v Z[i], because the content of 2 i eta H_ij is 2.

## 2. Checks of the proofs

### 2.1 Canonical identity (Proved)
On D_ij the factors of row j are the conjugates of those of row i. So A_j - A_i = eta H_ij (conj X_ij - X_ij) = 2 i eta H_ij T_ij. T_ij = 0 would make X_ij real, so Q_ij = X_ij^2 would be a square, impossible for squarefree Q_ij > 1. The same argument rules out Re X_ij = 0. Hence 0 < T_ij^2 < Q_ij. If rows i and j agree, then Q_ij = 1 and the bound forces 0 < |t_ij| <= (C/2) q^{-1/4} < 1 once q > C^4/16, which is impossible. So all rows are distinct and every pair is separated, as she says. H_ij is primitive (no rational prime divides it), so the content of 2 i eta H_ij T_ij is 2|T_ij|. This gives the formula |T_ij| = gcd(dx, dy)/2 used in all my computations. Finally, p does not divide T_ij for p in D_ij, since Q_ij = (Re X_ij)^2 + T_ij^2 with p || Q_ij. Computed: all of this holds on 20,000 random pairs of q_200 (`checks.py`).

### 2.2 Rigidity (Proved)
The fake difference equals lambda_ij (A_j - A_i) with lambda_ij = t_ij/T_ij in Q^x. For a triple, subtract lambda_ik times A_k - A_i = (A_j - A_i) + (A_k - A_j) from the same identity for z. This gives (lambda_ij - lambda_ik)(A_j - A_i) + (lambda_jk - lambda_ik)(A_k - A_j) = 0 with real coefficients. Three distinct points of the circle |z|^2 = q are not collinear, so the two vectors are R-independent, and all three lambdas are equal. For M >= 3, the triangles {1, i, j} give lambda_ij = lambda_1i = lambda_1j for every pair, so lambda is constant. Then z_i = w + lambda A_i with w = z_1 - lambda A_1 in v^{-1} Z[i]. The argument uses only that z_j - z_i lies on the real line through A_j - A_i, which is exactly the hypothesis of (A2). It uses nothing about |z_i|, the rows of z_i, or residues.

**Several unit classes.** The (T1) form 2 i eta H_ij t_ij is defined only inside one unit class. A genuine pair with different units has a difference H(eta_j conj X - eta_i X), which is not a real multiple of 2 i eta H. So the posing of (A2) applies to one unit class; a genuine cluster contains a one-class subcluster with at least a quarter of its points. Under the natural extension "z_j - z_i in Q (A_j - A_i), and H_ij | z_j - z_i", the rigidity proof works verbatim, and XI.1 (which allows eta_i to vary) gives the same s/4 bound. The divisibility H_ij | z_j - z_i must then be imposed explicitly: it is automatic in one class but not across classes when v shares primes with q.

**M = 2.** There is one ratio and no rigidity; see R6.

### 2.3 Characterisation (Proved)
Write lambda = u/v. Then u T_ij = v t_ij, so v | T_ij and u | t_ij for every pair. Conversely, if v | G(S), set z_i = w + (u/v) A_i with w = -(u/v) A_1 + (any Gaussian integer). Then z_j - z_i = 2 i eta H_ij (u T_ij/v) lies in Z[i]. The bound |u T_ij/v| <= (C/2) sqrt(Q_ij) q^{-1/4} is equivalent to |u| |A_j - A_i| <= v C q^{1/4}, because |A_j - A_i| = 2 sqrt(q/Q_ij) |T_ij|. Taking u = 1 and v = G(S) maximises v/|u|, which proves R3.

### 2.4 The bound (XI.1) under dilation (Proved)
- Cut step. Mean log Q_ij <= m W/(2(m-1)), since p separates u_p(m - u_p) <= m^2/4 pairs. With log|B_ij| <= log C - W/4 + (1/2) log Q_ij, this gives (XI.4).
- Integrality. B_ij = 2 i eta t_ij lies in Z[i]. In general, v | D_ij by Bezout, so all marks are T-congruent mod v.
- Class count. Take an odd prime l <= m not dividing q. For a = 0: x^2 + y^2 = q mod l has l - (-1|l) <= l + 1 solutions. For a >= 1: the lifts of the common class modulo l^a satisfy 2(xh + yk) = (q - x^2 - y^2)/l^a mod l (using 2a >= a + 1), with (x, y) not both 0 mod l. So there are exactly l lifts. This uses the exact norm of the canonical marks.
- Collision. A_i = A_j mod l^{a+1} gives l^{a+1} | D_ij (H_ij is a unit mod l). From B_ij v' l^a = u D_ij with l not dividing v', it follows that l | B_ij. Distinct collision primes then multiply into |B_ij|.
- The Cauchy-Schwarz fraction and Mertens give (XI.5). Combining with (XI.4) gives (XI.3) with O_C(m) = O(m) + 4m log^+ C, uniform in lambda and w.
- Application. Subsets of a configuration are configurations with the same lambda. This justifies the preliminary subset of size floor(P/2), and the conclusion (XI.1) follows as she writes it. Since s/4 < (1/3 - 1/24) s, the value c = 1/24 is correct. For genuine circles this is the record's rough-parent estimate (`growth/auxiliary.md` line 74; `ref8_extend.md` line 185); the extension to arbitrary lambda is hers and is correct.

## 3. Computations on q_200 (s = 16; 65,536 marks with eta = 1)

### 3.1 Scripts
`marks.py` builds all marks exactly. `search.py` runs the smooth-modulus search over all bases. `verify.py` checks (A2) from first principles: rows, H_ij, Q_ij, t_ij, using the exact integer test 16 t^4 q <= C^4 Q^2. `rows.py` builds a translate with exact exponent rows. `mc.c` is an unbiased random-pair search over all moduli (128-bit exact gcd, hits re-verified in Python). `pairtest.py` tests the pair heuristic. `checks.py` checks R1 and the R9 residue fact.

### 3.2 The triple of R5
`python3 verify.py 200 4 436 12399 50763` prints "ALL CONDITIONS (A2) at C = 4 : True"; at C = 3 it prints False. The search also found the copies {2484, 14447, 52811} (the flip at the single prime of S_0, index 11) and {12724, 51088, 63051} (the complement, i.e. complex conjugation); see the found_200_*.txt files.

### 3.3 Exact rows by translation (Proved, Computed)
Take c in Z[i] with c = -z_i mod f_ip for every i and p. This is consistent: two marks with the same factor at p have z_j - z_i = 2 i eta H_ij t_ij with f_ip | H_ij. For a pair separated at p, z_i + c = -2 i eta H_ij t_ij is nonzero mod conj f_ip, because p does not divide t_ij = T_ij/v (taking u = 1). Exponent exactly 1 is arranged by lifting modulo f_ip^2, which needs M < p. Inactive conjugates are chosen to avoid all z_i. For the R5 triple, `rows.py` gives valuation (1, 0) at every prime for all three points. The norm defect of that translate is |z|^2/q of about 1.7e118; the norm is unconstrained in (A2).

### 3.4 Search coverage
- Smooth search (`search.py 200 8 i0 i1`, ranges 0-1000, 1000-20000, 20000-65536): exhaustive within its class of moduli (R7). It found 4 triples, one shape, and no 4-sets. The class is restrictive: the factor 2 of G and every prime factor above 600 are missed. Section 3.5 compares the yield with the heuristic.
- Random pairs (`mc.c`, all moduli, exact 128-bit gcd): 10^5 random ordered pairs (two seeds) at C = 8 found 0 hits. Each triple is hit by 6 of the N(N-1) ordered pairs, so the expected number of hits is about 1.4e-4 times the total number T_8 of triples at C = 8. Zero hits gives T_8 < about 2e4 at 95% confidence. This is consistent with the 4 triples found and rules out only the top of the E_3 range. The search is too thin to find further shapes.
- Small dilations: every odd v coprime to q whose prime factors are below 600 is inside the smooth class. So there are no triples at small v; the heuristic expectation there is about 5e-8 C^2 per v.

### 3.5 Comparison with the heuristic
For C = 8, E_3 predicts about 10^3 to 10^4 triples on q_200 if the congruences behave like random divisibilities. The smooth class should catch a few per cent of them, but the search found 4 (one shape). So the triple heuristic overestimates by one to two orders of magnitude. A plausible reason is that T_ij <= sqrt(Q_ij) is only just above the needed v, since v ~ q^{1/4} and sqrt(Q_ij) ~ q^{1/4}. The direction of the error favours the conclusion that cocycle configurations are small. The pair statistic is close to the prediction (factor 2).

## 4. Assessment of the meaning for (A2)

1. The judge's question is "build an integral-cofactor fake with M larger than any constant, ideally M >> s, or prove none exists". The answer it now has is: **M >> s is impossible** (Proved, R4: M <= (1/4 + o(1)) s). **Small fakes exist** (Computed, R5: M = 3 on q_200 at C = 4, where genuine clusters have M = 1). **Unbounded fakes**: not decided, heuristically none (R8).
2. The proof that no fake has M >= (1/4 + eps) s uses more than "such z_i would be an actual cluster". It uses that they are an affine-rational image of a congruence family of actual norm-q points on a longer arc, together with the collision count, which survives the denominator v. It does not reduce to the genuine-cluster bound.
3. The reading "for |lambda| < 1 the fake is a set of genuine lattice points on an arc |v/u| times longer than critical, all T-congruent mod v; for lambda = +-1 it is a translated genuine cluster" is fair, with two precisions. Congruence is "v | T_ij", which is ordinary congruence mod v when gcd(v, 2q) = 1; the R5 example has v even. And u = +-1 can be assumed without loss of generality.
4. For the larger programme, the separating input identified in reply10 (T3) is confirmed but sharpened. The integer-cofactor cocycle by itself does not pin the genuine circle at the same arc (R5). It does pin genuine geometry up to the dilation parameter v, and the dilated problems obey the same proved s/4 bound and, heuristically, bounded sizes. A rate for the cocycle on q_P is therefore as hard as a rate for the union over v of congruence-restricted genuine problems, and (A1) for the cocycle is not easier than (A1) for the circle.
