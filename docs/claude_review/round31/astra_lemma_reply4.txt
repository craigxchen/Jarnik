The target I propose (argue with it if you see a better one, but then commit):

 THE NORM-DEFECT / WIDTH TRADE-OFF AT FIVE MARKS.

 Background from the other thread (its Part III, section III.11, at /tmp/claude-0/-home-user-Jarnik/ba4929a4-8665-5790-985a-3f09130a14b9/scratchpad/astra/free/answer.md; refereed in ref3_model.md): there are arbitrarily large split parents with ten pair cuts of equal weight, valuation index one, and a primitive integer 2 x 5 star matrix with every g_ij dividing its minor with bounded cofactors, angular width ~ N^(-3/10) (exponent 10/3), and |V_i|^2 = n_i (1 + c_i K^(-2) + ...), i.e. relative norm error ~ N^(-1/10), absolute defect ~ n_i^(4/5). It is also proved there that exact norms (defect 0) together with the divisibilities force a genuine cluster. Since |V_i|^2 and n_i are integers, absolute defect < 1 is defect 0.
 So define, for theta in [0,1]: e(theta) = the best (smallest) approximation exponent attained by integral five-mark models, index one, all ten divisibilities, bounded or slowly growing cofactors, whose absolute norm defects are <= n_i^theta. Known: e(4/5) = 10/3 (attained, and 10/3 is Liouville, so e >= 10/3 always). Question B is exactly: e(0) > 15/4 off a proper closed set, where e(0) means genuine clusters.
 I would like e(theta) determined, or bounded, as a function:
   (a) Can the defect exponent 4/5 be lowered at exponent 10/3? Is there a family at theta = 1/2, at any theta > 0? If integral models exist at exponent 10/3 for every theta > 0, then Question B lives entirely in the last step from defect < n^epsilon to defect 0, and that is a sharp statement of why it is hard (and a new barrier: no argument tolerant of n^epsilon norm errors can prove it).
   (b) If instead the defect cannot be lowered, prove a trade-off: width <= N^(-3/10 + eta) forces defect >= n^(theta(eta)), by any means. Such an inequality is the first kind of statement that is violated by angle fakes (they have no integer matrix) AND by integral fakes with large defect, and satisfied by genuine clusters. Then extrapolate: what does it give at defect 0? Even a non-trivial e(0) > 10/3 + c at index one for squarefree ten-pair profiles would be the first improvement on Liouville there without a fixed plane.
   (c) The defect has a clean algebraic meaning that may help: mod each p | n_i the columns on one side of the cut are parallel, so the two sides define two points L_p^+, L_p^- in P^1(F_p), hence one quadratic form Q_p mod p up to scalar; by CRT one form Q mod N with Q(V_i) = 0 mod n_i for all i. A genuine cluster is the case where Q has a representative with bounded coefficients (x^2+y^2 after an integral change of basis). So the defect measures how short the shortest representative of the class {lambda Q + N * forms} is, in a rank-3 lattice of covolume ~ N^2. In III.11 what is the shortest representative? Is there an inequality between the successive minima of that lattice and the width of the cone, using that five (or m) columns in a cone of width Delta are simultaneously isotropic for Q mod N? This is geometry of numbers in dimension 3 with five simultaneous conditions; it may be elementary.
 Kill criteria: (i) if (b) reduces to a statement that integral fakes with large defect also satisfy, stop and say which; (ii) if lowering theta is blocked exactly by a known identity (your (8), the Pluecker quadric, the sextic), state the mechanism in a paragraph; (iii) if the answer to (a) is yes for all theta > 0, give the family and stop: that is a complete answer.
