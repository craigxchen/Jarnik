### A finite cutoff on one fixed parent

**PROVED.** Write \(p\in S\) for the label and let all parent primes in \(S\) be split. Suppose \(r=\pi\gamma/\bar\gamma\), \(\operatorname{Norm}\pi=p\), \(\operatorname{Norm}\gamma\le H\), supplies the residues at every odd external prime power through \(X\). It suffices to require this at primes where the rational expression in lowest terms has a unit denominator. Allowing arbitrary residues at the finitely many primes dividing that denominator makes no difference to the proof. Write \(r=(x+iy)/b=\alpha/b\) with minimal positive integer \(b\), and \(J=x^2+y^2=p b^2\). Then \(b\le H\), since \(r=\pi\gamma^2/\operatorname{Norm}\gamma\), and \(\gcd(x,y)=1\). Indeed a prime common to both coordinates must divide \(b\) since \(p\) is squarefree, contradicting minimality. The norm \(J\) is odd since an even \(b\) would give both coordinates even. Over each prime dividing \(J\), exactly one of the split Gaussian factors divides \(\alpha\). In particular \(\alpha\) and \(\bar\alpha\) have disjoint supports.

Put \(T=\max_{\ell\in S} v_2(\ell-1)\ge2\). At the dyadic exponent \(n=2^{T-1}\), the two e-all tests with units \(i,-i\) alone force \(b=1\) as soon as
\[
                 X\ge \sqrt2\,(pH^2)^{n/2}.                       \tag{*}
\]
More precisely one can take any \(n=2^k\) for which no \(\ell\in S\) prime to \(J\) has \(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(\alpha/\bar\alpha)=4n\). Thus it also suffices to use the minimum of \(2^{T-1}\) and \(2^{|S|}\) in (*): among \(k=0,\ldots,|S|\) one order is absent. All these bounds are uniform over candidate lifts of the given height (the order-adaptive bound uses the actual lift).

*Proof.* The coordinates \(x_n,y_n\) of \(\alpha^n\) have opposite parity. They are coprime because \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\) have disjoint supports. Both \(x_n-y_n\) and \(x_n+y_n\) are odd nonzero integers, of absolute value at most \(\sqrt2 J^{n/2}\). Nonvanishing follows since disjoint prime supports also make \(\alpha/\bar\alpha\) nontorsion. No prime dividing \(J\) divides either integer: if it did then the rational prime would divide \(\alpha^n\mp i\bar\alpha^n\), impossible at the Gaussian prime dividing \(\alpha\). In particular denominator primes are harmless. At a parent prime \(\ell\nmid J\) a divisor of either integer gives \((\alpha/\bar\alpha)^n=\pm i\bmod\ell\), of order four. As \(n\) is a power of two, this requires \(\operatorname{ord}_\ell(\alpha/\bar\alpha)=4n\). The order divides \(\ell-1\) in the split norm-one group. Our choice of \(n\) rules this out. Thus every prime power factor of each integer is external, has a nonsingular denominator, and lies below (*). The two strengthened e-all sine inequalities give
\[
 |\sin(n\phi-\pi/4)|\ge\frac{|x_n-y_n|}{\sqrt{2p^n}},\qquad
 |\sin(n\phi+\pi/4)|\ge\frac{|x_n+y_n|}{\sqrt{2p^n}} .
\]
Squaring and adding yields \(1\ge J^n/p^n=b^{2n}\). This proves integrality and requires no archimedean choice of \(\phi\). \(\square\)

**PROVED (which direction is pinned).** When the fixed parent is comparable with \(\min S>\sqrt{2\max S}\), the genuine lift at label \(p\) also forces \(\phi\equiv\pm\arg r\pmod{\pi/2}\) already at the singleton test and cutoff \(\sqrt{2p}\). Indeed \(|x\pm y|\) are now odd and smaller than every parent prime. Charge both completely at \(n=1\) as in the proof. Equality in the sum of squares forces both angular comparisons to be equalities, hence \(\sin(2\phi)=\sin(2\arg r)\). The congruence gives genuine angles up to units and conjugation. A priori the lift can be any associate of \(\pi\) or \(\bar\pi\): requiring \(\gamma/\bar\gamma=\pm1\) as literally written would even exclude \(\gamma=1+i\) and \(\gamma=\bar\pi\), which work at every height.

**PROVED (fixed dense families).** On the product of all primes \(\ell\equiv5\bmod8\) in \([P,2P]\), \(T=2\) and \(n=2\). For every label and polynomial lift height \(H\le P^h\), a single common cutoff \(X\ge 2\sqrt2 P^{1+2h}\) forces all lifts genuine and pins all angles for \(P>4\). This is an explicit dense deterministic family (half of the split primes asymptotically). Products of all the split primes in \([P,2P]\) with \(v_2(\ell-1)\le T_0\) likewise give every fixed density \(1-2^{1-T_0}\) relative to the split primes, for fixed \(T_0\ge2\). Given a polynomial lift bound, the cutoff exponent is fixed, depending only on \(T_0,h\), and can be selected after that bound. Throughout the comparable interval, even without any congruence hypothesis, a candidate with \(\sqrt{2p}\,b<\min S\) is forced genuine at \(X\ge\sqrt{2p}\,b\) by the singleton diagonal tests.

**HEURISTIC (scope).** This quantitative exclusion applies to the rational lifts, including the right-inverse witnesses of IX.3 when their heights are bounded as above. Arbitrary P3-all* data have rational lifts of height exponential in the cutoff, so (*) cannot be substituted into that CRT bound to exclude such data. On the explicit dense family a lower bound for the growth cost of data with bounded lifts still entails a rate bound for genuine clusters there. The theorem does not provide that lower bound. The angular slab identities use the real trigonometric targets at the Gaussian units; the twin and function-field models do not impose them.

Use the squarefree two-label parent \(5p\) and rows \((0,0),(1,1)\). Put \(\omega=2+i,\ \phi_5=\arg\omega\), and take \(r_5=\omega,\ r_p=\pi_p\omega/\bar\omega\). At internal prime 5 the definition omits the label 5 and needs a unit of norm \(p\) for the remaining label. Use the actual \(\pi_p\) there. The two rows have different levels at every parent prime, so they impose no same-level clause. Put \(\phi_p=-\phi_5+t\) with \(t\) uniform on \([0,(5p)^{-1/4}/4]\). This example allows an exceptional internal prime, as necessarily in the all-height right-inverse construction. It tests the assertion for rational lifts of the *external* witnesses. **PROVED (fixed-parent information lower bound).** Even on a fixed parent let \(L_X\) be as above. For any genuine factor \(\pi\), choose \(\gamma=1+iL_X\), where \(X\ge2\). Then \(\gamma/\bar\gamma\equiv1\) at every modulus through \(X\), since the numerator and denominator reduce to 1, whereas the rational twisting multiplier is nontrivial. For a fixed \(p=|\pi|^2\), the lifted factor is nonintegral for all sufficiently large \(X\): a fixed integral factor other than \(\pi\) differs from it at one exterior prime, and all those candidate reductions agree with \(\pi\) eventually only for the factor \(\pi\) itself. Equivalently the finite list of integral factors leaves a simple absolute-value comparison with \(L_X\). Thus the same entire finite datum on any genuine cluster is compatible with a non-genuine lift of norm cost \(1+L_X^2\). Since \(\log L_X\sim X\), no worst-lift rigidity threshold uniform in heights \(\operatorname{Norm}\gamma\le H\) can have order \(o(\log H)\), even on that fixed parent. This changes the chosen lift, and preserves every finite residue and arc condition.

**PROVED (restricted rate for simultaneous local residue groups).** Let \(T_\ell\) be the norm-one group at an inert prime, of order \(\ell+1\). For a fixed parent define \(G(x)\) to be the subgroup of \(\prod_{\ell\le x,\,\ell\equiv3(4)}T_\ell\) generated by its physical-label vectors \((r_j(\ell)/\bar r_j(\ell))_\ell\). Suppose that for each fixed \(K>0\) these subgroups at \(x=K\log M\) can be generated by \(O_K(1)\) elements along a family. If the models pass the e-all test for all physical differences \(e_i-e_j\) at unit one and these prime cutoffs, as well as the hypotheses of VIII.1, then \(W/(M\log M)\to\infty\). In fact on comparable parents with \(p_j\le s^A\) this is impossible for unbounded \(M\asymp s\). This excludes in particular designs derived from a bounded number of common profinite exponents and arbitrary generator choices at the individual inert primes.

*Proof.* Let \(L(x)=\mathrm{lcm}_{\ell\le x,\,\ell\equiv3(4)}(\ell+1)\). Then \(\log L(x)=o(x)\). To see this, fix \(B>2\). Powers no larger than \((x+1)/B\) contribute at most \(\psi((x+1)/B)=O(x/B)\) to its logarithm, where \(\psi\) is Chebyshev's function. Bases of prime powers of exponent at least two dividing some \(\ell+1\) are at most \(\sqrt{x+1}\) and contribute \(O(\sqrt x\log x)\). A prime \(r>(x+1)/B\) dividing \(\ell+1\) satisfies \(\ell=h r-1\) for some \(1\le h\le B\). For each *fixed* \(h\), the number of such primes \(r\le x+1\) with \(h r-1\) prime is \(o(x/\log x)\). Indeed for any fixed finite set of odd primes \(t\nmid h\), one of the \(t-1\) invertible residue classes for \(r\) gives \(t\mid h r-1\). Use the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions for the product of this fixed set of moduli and let the set increase. The upper density among primes is bounded by \(\prod_t(1-1/(t-1))\to0\); values equal to one of the finitely many sieving primes contribute \(O(1)\). The large prime contributions to \(\log L\) are thus \(o_B(x)\). Taking \(B\to\infty\) proves the claim.

If \(W\le A M\log M\) on a subsequence, VIII.1 and the factorial bound give \(s\asymp_A M\) and \(s\ge M-O_C(1)\). At least half the labels have \(\log p_j\le 2W/s\le(2A+o(1))\log M\). Take \(K>4A+2\) and \(x=K\log M\). A subgroup of exponent dividing \(L(x)\) generated by \(d=O_K(1)\) elements has size at most \(L(x)^d=M^{o(1)}\). Two of these small labels have exactly the same residue ratio at every tested inert prime. For their difference word the joint colliding modulus thus has log at least \(\sum_{\ell\le x,\,\ell\equiv3(4)}\log\ell=(K/2+o(1))\log M\), by PNT in this progression. But the necessary e-all height budget at unit one is \((\log p_i+\log p_j)/2\le (2A+o(1))\log M\). This contradiction proves the assertion. In particular every actual dense configuration of *distinct physical primes*, irrespective of the cluster, has growing residue rank on these small moduli. This is an obstruction to residue designs, not a new claim of existence or a geometric restriction on such primes forming a cluster. \(\square\)
