## The cost of the Paley owner witnesses on a fixed parent, including finite CRT lifts

**PROVED.** Let \(q\equiv3\bmod4\) be prime, \(I=\mathbb F_q^*\), \(K_{ik}=\chi(k-i)\), and take \(S\) in the positive class of column 0 as in IX.3. Put \(m=|S|\), \(a_{ik}=1+K_{ik}\), and let \(p_k\in[P,2P]\), \(k\in I\), be distinct split primes with chosen factors \(\pi_k\). Write \(T=\sum_{k\in I}\log p_k\), \(B=\sum_{k\in I}(\log p_k)^2\), \(P_{\max}=\max p_k\), and \(\gamma_k=\pi_k/\bar\pi_k\).

Suppose that for every exterior odd prime \(\ell\le X\) the Gaussian rationals \(r_k\) are units locally, \(r_k\bar r_k=p_k\), and that their ratios give the owner design there. Precisely, for every \(i,i'\in S\),
\[
 \prod_{k\in I}(r_k/\bar r_k)^{a_{ik}-a_{i'k}}\equiv u_{ii'}\gamma_i/\gamma_{i'}\pmod{\ell}
\]
with the fixed units \(u_{ii'}\) allowed by the windings. This asks merely that the *point ratios* of the candidate lift match the IX.3 design, and only modulo primes. We use neither the particular IX.3 right inverse nor specified prime-factor residues at those primes. Represent \(r_k/\pi_k=u_kG_k/\bar G_k\) with \(u_k\) a Gaussian unit and \(G_k\) conjugate-primitive, by collecting the signed prime valuations of this norm-one rational number. Put \(H_k=|G_k|^2\), the product of the split primes raised to the absolute values of those valuations. For any unnormalized Hilbert-90 representative its norm is at least \(H_k\). Put \(L_X=\sum_{2<\ell\le X,\;\ell\notin\{p_k\}}\log\ell\). Then either
\[
 2\sum_{k\in I}\log H_k\ge L_X-\tfrac12(q+3)\log(2P)-\log 2 ,
\]
or the pair identities hold exactly in \(\mathbb Q(i)\), and
\[
 \sum_{i\in S}\log H_i\ \ge\ \frac12\{mT-\sqrt{m q B}-m\log P_{\max}\}.
\]
In the second case, for \(0<\eta<1/2\), all but at most \(qB/(2\eta T-\log P_{\max})^2\) of the owners have \(\log H_i\ge(1/2-\eta)T\), when the denominator's inner difference is positive. For \(m\asymp q\), \(P=q^{O(1)}\) and \(X/(q\log P)\to\infty\) this gives \(\sum_k\log H_k\gg\min(X,q^2\log P)\). For exact global identities the normalized twists of all but \(O(\eta^{-2})\) owners have exponentially large norm in \(q\log P\). In particular the bound is intrinsic to the design, including after optimization over all right inverses.

*Proof.* For a pair write \(d=a_i-a_{i'}\), \(h=e_i-e_{i'}\). Then \(\|d\|_1=q+1\), \(\|d\|_\infty\le2\), \(d\equiv h\bmod2\). The length follows by the off-diagonal inner product \(-1\) of two full \(K\)-rows, with the deleted coordinate equal. At exterior \(\ell\le X\), all \(G_k\) are units by conjugate-primitivity and the local unit hypotheses. The difference of the two rational sides after clearing conjugate denominators is \(A-u\bar A\) up to unit, where the norm of \(A\) is at most
\[
 (2P)^{q+3}\prod_k H_k^{2|d_k|}.
\]
Indeed take \(\pi_k\) or \(\bar\pi_k\) according to the sign of \(d_k\), take twice those multiplicities for \(G_k\) or its conjugate, and multiply by \(\bar\pi_i\pi_{i'}\) to move the target ratio to the left. Thus if any pair fails as an exact identity then
\(L_X\le\log(2|A|)\le\log2+(q+3)\log(2P)/2+2\sum_k\log H_k\).
For prime powers one may replace \(L_X\) by the corresponding log lcm, although this is unnecessary here.

For exact identities let \(V_{jk}=v_{\pi_k}(r_j/\pi_j)\). At these primes the valuation matrix of \(r_j/\bar r_j\) is \(I+2V\). Consequently \(d(I+2V)=h\). Reduction modulo four gives
\[
  V_{ik}-V_{i'k}\equiv(h_k-d_k)/2\pmod 2 .
\]
At a fixed \(k\), \(V_{ik}\bmod2\) thus agrees with \((\mathbf1_{i=k}-a_{ik})/2\bmod2\) up to a common additive constant across \(i\in S\). In particular \((-1)^{V_{ik}}=\sigma_k K_{ik}\) for a column sign \(\sigma_k\) at all \(i\ne k\); the one diagonal entry is \(\pm1\). Orienting the signed vector \(f_k=\sigma_k\log p_k\) and setting \(f_0=0\), we have
\[
 \sum_{k\in I: V_{ik}\;\mathrm{odd}}\log p_k
  =\tfrac12(T-(Kf)_i+\epsilon_i),\qquad |\epsilon_i|\le\log P_{\max}.
\]
The left side bounds \(\log H_i\) from below in the sense \(\log H_i\ge\sum_{k:V_{ik}\;\mathrm{odd}}\log p_k\), since \(H_i\) pays \(|V_{ik}|\) at each physical prime. The full Paley identity \(K^TK=qI-\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\) gives \(\sum_i|(Kf)_i|^2\le q B\). Sum by Cauchy–Schwarz or count the entries above \(2\eta T-\log P_{\max}\) by the squared sum. This yields both claims. Finally \(L_X\ge\vartheta(X)-T-\log2\), so \(L_X=(1+o(1))X\) in the stated range. The second-case estimate is \((1/2-o(1))mT\). \(\square\)

**Scope.** These are lower bounds on auxiliary rational heights on one fixed parent, even on the best finite-level simultaneous lift. They do not bound the probability or the existence of small *collision moduli* on noncharacters. Raising \(H_k\) costs the model no physical prime weight. The IX.3 fake passes the theorem, the twin has no Gaussian prime lift of this specified form, and the mod-four algebra is an integer valuation argument over both quadratic constant-field models whenever the same prescribed owner identities and ordinary Paley signs are imposed.

## All-height ray tests with incoherent witnesses on a fixed growth-scale parent

**PROVED.** For each sufficiently large \(q\equiv3\pmod4\) prime there is a single replicated Paley parent with \(s=3(q-1)\) physical primes, \(m=(\rho/2+o(1))q\) marks and \(W=O(m\log m)\) on which P3-char holds at *every* finite cutoff, with compatible factored data, and the strengthened e-all inequalities hold for every unit at *every* cutoff on every multiple of each of
\[
 e_j,\qquad e_j+e_b,\qquad e_j-e_b\quad(j\ne b),
\]
where \(b\) is a fixed physical prime (the hub). All ordinary monomial gaps hold, on and off these rays. In particular the e-all rays include the entire coercive set of IX.7 and both signs on the edges of one spanning tree. Each finite snapshot has norm-correct rational lifts by the referee's CRT lemma. A compatible all-height lift, even up to finitely many exceptions for each label, need not exist. This construction does not impose the strengthened inequalities on all words.

*Proof.* Fix any \(0.99<\rho<1\). Start with IX.3's replicated Paley profile, three physical labels per coarse column, distinct primes in \([P,2P]\), \(P=q^3\), in the angular and radial bin of IX.5. Choose \(S\), the prime assignment and the offsets as in IX.3 with the product of the three owner primes as each coarse owner (details of the replicated profile, IX.5). This construction supplies compatible factored data at all odd primes, with the correct point ratio on every exterior prime power, including all four collision units. Keep these data unchanged at the parent primes (so the internal level clause is unchanged).

At a fixed exterior odd prime \(\ell\) identify the norm-one group at level \(\ell^a\) with \(\mathbb Z/m_a\), \(m_a=(\ell-\chi_4(\ell))\ell^{a-1}\), with compatible choices of generators. Indeed the norm-one group over \(\mathbb F_\ell\) is cyclic. The norm-one principal units form a copy of \(\mathbb Z_\ell\) under the \(\ell\)-adic logarithm on units congruent to 1 modulo \(\ell\), or by lifting a nontrivial trace-zero direction and taking powers via the binomial theorem. The tame factor lifts by Teichmüller. We work in the inverse limit \(\mathbb Z/(\ell-\chi_4(\ell))\times\mathbb Z_\ell\) of these additive exponent groups. A physical label \(j\) requires its residue ratio exponent \(x_j\) to have the parity of the genuine \(\pi_j/\bar\pi_j\). Exactly this condition suffices: choose a norm-one unit \(u_j\) with \(u_j^2\) equal to the quotient of desired and genuine ratios and take the local label \(r_j=\pi_j u_j\). Squares in this inverse-limit group are exactly the elements of even exponent. For each block prescribe the sum of its three exponents to equal the sum of its old IX.3 exponents. These targets have the required sum of parities. Any rearrangement with these sums preserves every exterior point ratio, since the three physical exponent columns are identical.

At each odd prime factor \(r\) of the exponent group, including \(r=\ell\) for the pro-\(\ell\) component, and at its two-part, we choose the exponents separately by CRT. First choose the hub exponent \(x_b\) prime to every odd factor, with 2-adic valuation zero or one as dictated by its parity (if the 2-part is exhausted, this means the gcd takes at most 2). In a component of prime-power order \(r^d\) with \(r\ge7\), disallow the residues \(0,x_b,-x_b\bmod r\) for each nonhub label. The allowed fraction exceeds one half since \(r-3>r/2\). At \(r=3,5\) disallow these three classes modulo \(r^2\) if \(d\ge2\); otherwise there is no restriction. The allowed fraction again exceeds one half. At 2, within the parity class prescribed for each nonhub label, disallow the residues \(0,x_b,-x_b\bmod 16\) when \(d\ge4\), and impose no further constraint at the smaller two-parts. This removes at most three of eight possible residues, and the allowed fraction of the parity coset exceeds one half. For a pro-\(\ell\) component implement the corresponding rules modulo \(r\) or \(r^2\) and lift arbitrarily.

Any two such allowed sets of densities exceeding one half in the same finite group (or in the two parity cosets, translated into the even subgroup) sum to the full group (or sum coset), by intersection with a reflected translate. In a block first prescribe any remaining coordinate except two, then choose the final two by this observation to meet the target. In the hub block precisely two coordinates remain after the hub is fixed. Higher lifts preserve the allowed residues and can achieve the prescribed sum in the full component. This gives a compatible vector at all levels. For every tested primitive ray \(v\) from the displayed list, its exponent \(x=\langle v,(x_j)\rangle\) now satisfies
\[
                   \gcd(x,m_a)\mid G_0=8\cdot3\cdot5
\]
at every level. Indeed the excluded classes are precisely those where a coordinate or its sum or difference with the hub vanishes to the forbidden order. The hub itself satisfies the same bound.

If a monomial \(nv\) collides with any Gaussian unit modulo \(\ell^a\), then \(m_a\mid4nx\), hence \(m_a\mid4|n|G_0\) and \(\ell^a\le6|n|G_0\). This bound holds for *every* exterior prime. At any cutoff the product of maximal colliding powers for that word (even allowing its own best unit at every prime) thus divides \(\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,\lfloor6|n|G_0\rfloor)\). The Chebyshev bound gives an absolute \(K\) with the product at most \(\exp(K|n|)\), uniformly in the cutoff and the label. In particular genuine arithmetic and rational lifts play no role in this upper bound.

It remains to achieve all ordinary gaps together with these strengthened gaps on the fixed parent. Choose the coarse angles as in IX.5 by Haar completion on the nullspace of the anchored differences. The ordinary coarse noncharacter union costs \(O(\sum_h(2qP^{-3/2})^h)=o(1)\), and the stronger character tests are protected by the inherited IX.3 offsets because all local point ratios are preserved. Split each sum of three fine angles using independent Haar measure on its two-dimensional fibre (angles in quarter-turns). Conditional on all other blocks, any integral word nonconstant in that block has uniform phase modulo one. Every ray above is nonconstant in at least one block, because three copies are used and at most two positions occur. For a fine mixed word of length \(h\), enumerate the four forbidden arcs. All ordinary gaps have total failure probability \(O(P^{-h/2})\). For a tested ray with multiplicity \(n\), the all-height collision bound above increases this to \(O(e^{K|n|}P^{-h/2})\), and \(|n|\le h\). There are at most \((6q)^h\) signed words of length \(h\); summing \(O((6qe^KP^{-1/2})^h)\) gives \(o(1)\). Unbounded-cutoff ray tests use the single finite lcm above for each word, so no union over cutoffs is needed. This completes the fake construction. The good set on each coarse fibre has positive Haar measure, so one may choose angles outside the finite set of genuine angles at the physical labels. IX.7 then excludes simultaneous rational lifts even away from an arbitrary finite set. \(\square\)

**Scope.** The argument replaces only exterior local witnesses on a fixed parent, imposes exact norms on those witnesses and preserves all internal same-level residues. Its distinguishing feature is the cyclic decomposition of each finite norm-one group, which permits the physical labels in a repeated column to be chosen with large order without changing any row character. It cannot produce a Gaussian-integer cocycle and does not address the twin's signed-difference condition. Over the constant-field function-field models the norm-one principal units at a general prime need not be procyclic and have exponent of order the congruence level, so the implication \(m_a\mid 4|n|G_0\) does not transfer there. This is an obstruction on integer prime moduli, not a bound on genuine circles in those models.
