REFEREE RESULTS ON PART VI, AND A SEVENTH QUESTION

Six referees and a judge went through Part VI. Nothing you claimed is false and nothing is withdrawn. The judge's report is in this directory as referee_report_VI.md (read sections 0, 1 and 5). In short:

1. VI.1 is recorded as a theorem, with your hypotheses: K, S, b, eps fixed; at most one block per cut; prod |G_e|^2 <= H (primitive norm); h(eta_i) <= rho log H; each G_e a sum of at most b terms a*u, u in O_{K,S}^*, h(a) <= rho log H; Delta <= H^{-1/4-eps} forces the tuple into an ineffective proper closed Z(K,S,b,eps). The judge rechecked the step "formal logarithmic Jacobian vanishes => a fixed P in K[Y] vanishes on the tail" (construction of the field k from a diagonal subsequence, the Jacobian criterion, passage from formal to numerical) and found it correct. Wording corrections: "remove zero subsums" should read "take a representation of minimal length"; the scope sentence should say traces and Pell constructions are covered provided the gcd-reduced blocks are still bounded fixed-field S-unit sums, or the raw Gaussian gcd is H^{o(1)}. A referee's corollary: tuples built from at most m_0 template blocks, each a sum of at most b S-units with free exponents, pairwise coprime up to H^{o(1)}, with distances <= R^{1/2-eps}, lie in an ineffective closed Z. The golden 8-point family sits exactly at exponent 4 on a curve (Delta H^{1/4} = 3.70245917364 at n = 100, 160, 220), consistent with VI.1.

2. VI.4: A_4(delta) is false for every delta < 1/6. Reproduced with exact arithmetic (constants 12.62049 and 7.38051 at rates (1,1,1); smallest limiting constant found 4.60154 against the three-point constant 4.34737). Recorded.

3. VI.3 (triple cover of Mbar_{0,7} branched on B_2, kappa = 0) and VI.5 (the moving affine pencil; "degree at most four" should read "at most two") are correct. VI.2 is correct but is the record's endpoint chart restated. A threshold lemma from the referee: the ten-pair profile needs H >= (5*13*17*29*37)^2 ~ 1.4e12; a search to H ~ 4.6e15 found at most 8 distinct pair cuts and best width exponent 0.2299 against 0.3.

4. VI.6 is correct: non-density in (*) is EQUIVALENT to A_5(delta) for some delta in (0,1/10). So (*) is not a shortcut; "strictly weaker than Question B" becomes "logically weaker, no separation known". On the judge's recommendation the (*) sub-thread is closed.

What VI.1 leaves open is the whole difficulty: S fixed. Over Q the primes of the parent are the places that matter, and their number grows with the height.

SEVENTH QUESTION: QUESTION B OVER A FUNCTION FIELD OF CHARACTERISTIC 0.

Background from the other line of work (files ff_char0_partIV.md, the argument, and ff_char0_report.md, its referee report, are in this directory). Model: distinct z_1,...,z_m in C[t] of one degree D with z_j conj(z_j) = N, conjugation acting on coefficients (so the base is R(t), or Q(i)(t)/Q(t), with the form A^2+B^2; the place at infinity is inert), and deg(z_i - z_j) <= D/2 + c. Results so far:
   - characteristic 0: m <= 24 at c = 0, uniformly in D, by an orbifold Bogomolov-Miyaoka-Yau inequality (IV.2) on P^1 x P^1 blown up along the contact tree of the m sections. (IV.2) is vacuous for m <= 7 and at the level of unit templates.
   - over F_2(t) (norm of F_4(t)/F_2(t)) and over F_3(t) with A^2+B^2, m is unbounded, and the analogues of A_5(delta < 1/30) are FALSE: five-tuples at exponent 15/4 are dense. So no proof of Question B can be characteristic-free.
Nobody has looked at the five-point statement itself in characteristic 0. That is the question.

(a) Decide the characteristic-0 function-field analogue of A_5(delta). Five distinct primitive z_i in C[t] (with the real structure above, or first over C(t) with z_i w_i = N and the torus split, whichever is cleaner) of degree D with one norm and deg(z_i - z_j) <= (1/2 - delta) D. For which delta in (0, 1/10) do all such tuples, for all large D, lie in a proper Zariski-closed subset of T_5? Fix the right notion of "closed subset" and "dense" (closed subsets over the constant field, or over the function field) and say which you use; the analogue of Liouville/Cilleruelo-Cordoba should give delta <= 1/10, and the naive count gives expected dimension about 2D - 4(1/2 + delta)D = -4 delta D, i.e. critical at delta = 0, exponent 4 = dim, as Vojta predicts over Q.
   - If it is true for some delta < 1/30 (or for every delta > 0): give the proof and isolate the step that handles a set S of places (the roots of N) whose size is comparable to the height. That is exactly what the fixed-S Subspace argument of VI.1 cannot do. Then say what the arithmetic form of that step is: a known theorem, abc, Vojta on a named variety of some dimension, or something with no arithmetic form. Everything is a theorem here: Mason/Wronskians with truncation, BMY on surfaces, function-field Subspace (Wang), gcd bounds for S-units (Corvaja-Zannier, Levin-Wang), multiplicities of rational curves at the constant point e (Seshadri constants of -K on your X).
   - If it is false in characteristic 0 as well (excess families of rational curves through e with (-K.C)/mult_e(C) < 4 - eta, dense in T_5): exhibit them. That would say Question B is the wrong intermediate statement even where the uniform bound is a theorem, and a family over Q(i)(t) specialises to integer tuples, so say what its specialisations do to A_5 over Q.
(b) Warm-up: the same for four points (does your Pell mechanism persist, with powers of a matrix in SL_2(C[t]) or SL_2(Q[t])?) and for three.
(c) Whatever the answer, compare with characteristic p: which step of a characteristic-0 proof fails for the F_2 and F_3 families, or which characteristic-0 family is the counterpart of theirs.

Please say plainly what you could not do. As before: full proofs or exact reproducible computations, statements labelled proved / computed / heuristic, and append the result as Part VII to answer.md.
