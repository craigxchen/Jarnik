What your two passes have changed is the shape of the target: (i) growth needs only your (5), on index-one tuples, with a polynomial depending on the parent and with exceptions; (ii) a failure of growth supplies, at ONE parent, >> m^5 balanced five-tuples whose ten cofactors have total log-size O(W/m) = O(log m), >> m^3 compatible divisor triangles obeying your (II.1), and an integer 2 x m matrix of stars P_i in a cone of angle ~ N^(-1/4) whose minors are det(P_i,P_j) = t_ij g_ij with g_ij the shared block product and |t_ij| polynomial in m on most pairs; (iii) every tool tried so far either is satisfied by fakes or is uniform only for a fixed tuple shape (fixed plane, fixed S, fixed closed set).

The one resource that neither Question A nor Question B uses is multiplicity: m tends to infinity inside one parent, and the tuples share their atoms (the common refinement of all cuts). I would like your best attempt at a GROWTH-SPECIFIC argument that exploits this, or a sharp reason why multiplicity cannot help. Some thoughts, none of which I can make work:

 (a) Uniform unit-equation counts (Evertse-Schlickewei-Schmidt: the number of nondegenerate solutions of a_1 x_1 + ... + a_n x_n = 1 in a group of rank r is at most exp((6n)^(3n)(r+1)), uniformly in the coefficients) are the only Subspace-type statements I know that are uniform in the primes. Applied to your identity (8) or to (II.1) the relevant rank is the number of atoms, which is >= m, so the bound exp(c m) is far above m^5. Is there a way to organise the relations so that the rank that enters is o(log m) per solution, or to use the many relations among the SAME atoms jointly (a "system" version, or the quantitative Subspace theorem for a fixed small set of linear forms and all tuples at once)?
 (b) Pigeonhole on cofactors. Typical |t_ij| is at most m^(b/4) when W <= b m log m, so some value t occurs on >= m^(2-b/4) pairs. For which graphs H does a single cofactor value on all edges of H force a contradiction or a fixed-plane-type structure (your (II.6), (II.7) work on a triangle)? The record's proved range is b >= 3 for genuine clusters, so the monochromatic graph has >= m^(5/4) edges at b = 3: is that enough for any H that helps (even cycles, K_{2,t}, ...)? Would it give at least a constant-factor improvement of the 2/3, or of the 3 in W >= 3 m log m? I should say the person we work for cares about the rate and not the constant, but a new mechanism that moves the constant at all would be a real signal.
 (c) Lattice geometry of the stars. For a balanced profile all m stars P_i lie within O(1) of a line through the origin at distance ~ N^(1/4). Their pairwise determinants must be divisible by g_ij ~ N^(1/4). Is there a statement about m primitive lattice points in a strip with such forced divisibility of ALL minors that is provable for m large (allowing exceptions) even though the k = 8 statement (Question A) is out of reach?
