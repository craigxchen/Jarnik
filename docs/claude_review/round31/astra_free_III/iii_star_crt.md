### What the integral matrix alone gives

**PROVED — squarefree local data and an exact horizontal test.** At a prime \(p\), divisibility by \(p\) of all minors between marked primitive integer plane vectors means precisely that those vectors lie in one projective residue class modulo \(p\). Indeed each is nonzero on reduction and any two span the same one-dimensional space. At depth \(h\), the corresponding assertion modulo \(p^h\) holds since a primitive vector has a unit coordinate and determinant zero is equivalent to being a unit multiple of it. Nested compatible choices give one projective line modulo \(p^{e_p}\).

A slice of the arbitrary-projective-line problem has a scalar formulation. Let \(N\) be squarefree split, \(H=\lfloor N^{1/4}\rfloor\), and use \(P_i=(H+x_i,1)\) with distinct \(0\le x_i\le H\). Choose any finite residue line at each \(p\), corresponding to a residue of \(x_i\). CRT combines these residues into one \(r\pmod N\). The exact agreement product of row \(i\) is \(L_i=\gcd(x_i-r,N)\). If \(L_i\ge\sqrt N\) for all \(i\), then \(|P_i|^2\le5L_i\), the slopes lie in angular width \(1/H\), and \(g_{ij}=\gcd(L_i,L_j)\) divides \(x_i-x_j=\pm\det(P_i,P_j)\). In particular the binary separation labels \(L_iL_j/g_{ij}^2\) are at least \(N/H^2\). On such a list, \(\sum_{i<j}\log(|x_i-x_j|/g_{ij})\le mW/8\), since the sum of marked pair weights is at least \(W(m^2/8-m/4)\) by Jensen on the marked cardinalities with prime weights. Thus a critical-size CRT list in this slice would exhibit almost all ingredients of an integral-star fake, including polynomial-size cofactors on a positive fraction of edges. It does not require the local lines to be isotropic for the Gaussian norm. I have no unbounded construction at the rate threshold.

**PROVED — the coefficient three in a larger projective model.** Let \(N\) be any odd split parent and assign one projective residue line modulo each \(p^{e_p}\). Let \(P_i\) be primitive, mutually projectively distinct integer vectors in a cone about a real line of angular width \(C e^{-W/4}\), where \(W=\log N\). Let \(a_{ip}\) be the greatest depth \(0\le h\le e_p\) matching the line modulo \(p^h\). Suppose
\[
 \sum_i \log |P_i|^2\le\sum_{i,p} a_{ip}\log p + O(m).
\]
Then \(W\ge3m\log m-O_C(m)\), with the constant also depending on the height slack. This applies to the horizontal model above, and to the genuine Gaussian stars with equality in the height condition. It uses general projective residue lines.

Write \(s_h=\#\{i:a_{ip}\ge h\}\) at prime \(p\), and \(V=\prod_{i<j}|\det(P_i,P_j)|\). The baseline forced logarithmic divisor of \(V\) is \(D=\sum_{p,h}\binom{s_h}{2}\log p\). On the other hand every determinant is at most \(C e^{-W/4}|P_i||P_j|\), so after expanding and completing squares one obtains
\[
 \log V-D\le mW/8-\tfrac12\sum_{p,h}(s_h-m/2)^2\log p+O_C(m^2).
\]
If \(p\nmid N\), reduction into \(p+1\) projective classes gives an extra logarithmic contribution of at least \((m^2/[2(p+1)]-m/2)\log p\) by Cauchy. At \(p\mid N\), let \(n_0=m-s_1\), \(n_e=s_{e_p}\). The \(n_0\) classes outside the target split among the other \(p\) projective residues; the \(n_e\) marks in the last target class split among the \(p\) lifts to the next level. (A chart with a unit coordinate identifies lifts with the \(p\) scalar lifts.) Extra collisions beyond \(D\) give at least \(((n_0^2+n_e^2)/(2p)-m/2)\log p\). For \(e_p=1\) the positive part is at least \(m^2/(4p)\) since \(n_0+n_e=m\). For \(e_p\ge2\) add the two distinct endpoint-square terms from the preceding upper bound. Completing squares gives
\[
 \frac{n_0^2+n_e^2}{2p}+\tfrac12((n_0-m/2)^2+(n_e-m/2)^2)
 \ge \frac{m^2}{4(p+1)} .
\]
Summing up to \(m\), errors from the subtracted \(m/2\) are \(O(m^2)\) by Chebyshev. Mertens' estimate \(\sum_{p\le m}\log p/(p+1)=\log m+O(1)\) and its class \(3\pmod4\) analogue with coefficient \(1/2\) show that the baseline plus these corrections costs at least \((3/8)m^2\log m-O(m^2)\) on the right budget \(mW/8\). Every inert prime is outside \(N\). More precisely this gives
\[
 W\ge4m\log m -2m\sum_{p\mid N,\,p\le m}\frac{\log p}{p+1}-O_C(m).
\]
This recovers the known leading constant on a broader model; it gives no construction attaining it.

**PROVED — why isotropic lines change the question.** If all the projective lines to the stated depths are Gaussian isotropic lines lifted coherently, choose \(\pi_p\mid p\) defining each one. Primitive \(P_i\), viewed as Gaussian integers, then satisfy \(B_i=\prod_p\pi_p^{a_{ip}}\mid P_i\). Indeed the reduction map \(\mathbb Z[i]\to\mathbb Z[i]/(\pi_p^h)\simeq\mathbb Z/p^h\) has precisely that projective kernel on primitive points. Put \(U_i=P_i/B_i\). The aggregate height bound says \(\sum_i\log|U_i|^2=O(m)\), with nonnegative summands. A positive fraction has \(|U_i|\) bounded, hence a positive fraction has one common Gaussian multiplier. For those marks the \(B_i\) lie in the same narrow cone after rotation, and \((\prod_p\bar\pi_p^{e_p}) B_i/\bar B_i\) are distinct lattice points of norm \(N\) on a critical arc. Thus an integral fake with bounded average norm slack and genuine local isotropic lines already gives the original short-arc example up to constants. The arbitrary-line case above has no such identification.

**PROVED — quantitative partial isotropy.** Keep only a set of primes at which the lines are genuine coherent Gaussian isotropic lines. Set \(B_i=\prod_{p\ \mathrm{kept}}\pi_p^{a_{ip}}\), \(U_i=P_i/B_i\) and \(E=m^{-1}\sum_i\log |U_i|^2\). The common-direction argument gives a genuine critical subcluster of size \(\gg m e^{-E}\) on the parent restricted to the kept primes. Indeed if \(L\) is the maximum multiplicity of a projective Gaussian direction among the \(U_i\), there are at most \(cX\) possibilities of squared norm at most \(X\) for \(X\ge1\), by counting lattice points in a square. The \(j\)-th smallest squared norm is at least \(j/(cL)\), so \(E\ge m^{-1}\log(m!)-\log(cL)\ge \log(m/(cL))-1\). The total discarded marked log weight per row plus the original mean height slack bounds \(E\) from above. In particular a sequence with \(E\le\log m-\omega(1)\) would force genuinely unbounded clusters on the retained norm, even though a growth-rate transfer at constant relative density requires bounded \(E\).
