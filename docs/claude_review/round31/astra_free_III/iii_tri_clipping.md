**PROVED — the rank of the splits of even one base edge.** In a shortened one-unit cluster, fix two marks \(i,j\). Write \(d=d_{ij}\) and let \(a_x=\sqrt{d d_{ix}/d_{jx}}\), \(b_x=d/a_x\), for each possible third vertex \(x\). If \(k\) of the \(a_x\)'s belong to one multiplicative coset of rank \(r\) in \(\mathbb Q^\times\), then \(k\le8r+4\). More generally, when the other offsets \(u_x,v_x\) of (II.1) are at most \(m^B\), the same statement for \(a_xu_x/v_x\) has bound \(k\le(8+o_B(1))r+O(1)\). The normalized unit equation obtained by dividing (II.1) by one outer quadratic term contains the coordinate \(d(v_x/(a_xu_x))^2\). A fixed rank coset for those normalized equations therefore obeys this bound, even if its coefficients and group depend on \(i,j,N\).

*Proof.* Index threshold levels by \((p,h)\) with weights \(\log p/W\). The matrix of row signs times the square root of these weights has operator norm at most \(\sqrt2\), by strict pair separation and the Gram bound in Part I §5. Write \(L_p=|a_{ip}-a_{jp}|\). At a prime with \(L_p>0\), the valuation of \(a_x\) equals the number of crossed levels on the interval of these \(L_p\) active levels, measured from \(i\): this follows directly from the displayed square root and the distances between three integers. On a set of primes \(P\), form the row vectors
\[
 C_{xp}=(2v_p(a_x)-L_p)\sqrt{\log p/(W L_p)},\qquad
 A=W^{-1}\sum_{p\in P}L_p\log p .
\]
This matrix is a block-sum contraction of the weighted sign matrix (each block uses coefficients \(\pm1/\sqrt{L_p}\)), so its operator norm is at most \(\sqrt2\). Take two independent uniform rows from the chosen \(k\), with replacement. Their total mean separation divided by \(W\) exceeds \((1-1/k)/2\); the complementary levels contribute at most \((1-A)/2\), because a cut with row proportion \(z\) separates two uniform draws with probability \(2z(1-z)\le1/2\). Nestedness makes their distance on the retained levels exactly \(D=\sum_{p\in P}|v_p(a_x)-v_p(a_y)|\log p/W\). Thus \(\mathbb E D\ge(A-1/k)/2\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\|C_x-C_y\|^2\ge4D^2/A\). By Jensen this implies \(\mathbb E\|C_x-C_y\|^2\ge A-2/k\), with the inequality trivial if \(A\le 2/k\). The sum of squared lengths of the centered rows is at least \(kA/2-1\).

For the unperturbed statement take all prime blocks. Then \(A>1/2\) and the centered matrix has rank at most \(r\), since it is a diagonally rescaled valuation matrix from a rank-\(r\) coset. Its squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm is at most \(2r\), which proves the first bound. With the scalar perturbation retain primes \(p\ge\sqrt m\). The single-prime estimate \(e_p\log p\le4W/m\) proved in Part II implies \(A\ge1/2-4/\sqrt m\). Each \(u_x/v_x\) has nonzero valuations at at most \(4B\) of these primes, each of magnitude at most \(2B\). Thus replacing \(v_p(a_x)\) in \(C\) by \(v_p(a_xu_x/v_x)\) creates squared error \(O_B(1/m)\) per row since \(L_p\ge1\). The centered replacement rows lie in an \(r\)-dimensional subspace. The total squared error after centering is at most \(O_B(k/m)\). Project the original centered rows onto that subspace; their projected energy is at least \(kA/2-1-O_B(k/m)\) and at most \(2r\) by the operator bound. This proves the stated estimate.
