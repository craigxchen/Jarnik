### One fixed choice of local weights sees boundedly many pair factors

**PROVED — fixed centers.** Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_m\) be exponent rows in the parent box, \(w(v)=\sum_p|v_p|\log p\), and \(W=\sum_p e_p\log p\). Assume only \(w(a_i-a_j)>W/2\) whenever \(i\ne j\). For any \(b\in\mathbb R^\omega\) and \(\delta>0\), at most \(O(\delta^{-4})\) oriented edges \(v=a_i-a_j\) satisfy
\[
 w(v)-w(v-b)\ge\delta W. \tag{T}
\]
The bound is uniform in \(m,N,b\). Allowing the center to run along any fixed ray costs only a further factor \(O(1/\delta)\).

*Proof.* For a subset of \(s\) rows write \(X_i=a_{ip}/e_p\) at prime \(p\), and \(F_p(t)=s^{-1}\#\{i:X_i\ge t\}\). Choose \(p\) with probability \(e_p\log p/W\), and let integrals in \(t\) be on \([0,1]\). For two independent uniform indices in the subset the mean normalized distance equals \(2\mathbb E_p\int F_p(1-F_p)\) and is at least \((1-1/s)/2\). Consequently
\[
 \mathbb E_p\int(F_p-1/2)^2\le1/(4s).
\]
It follows that \(\mathbb E_p|s^{-1}\sum_i X_i-1/2|\le1/(2\sqrt s)\), and likewise \(\mathbb E_{p,i}\min(X_i,1-X_i)\le1/(2\sqrt s)\). The latter follows by integrating \(F_p(t)-F_p(1-t)\) over \(0<t<1/2\) and applying Cauchy–Schwarz on the full interval. Thus the nearest bits \(Y_i\) cost at most \(1/(2\sqrt s)\) in mean normalized distance and have \(\mathbb E_p|s^{-1}\sum_iY_i-1/2|\le1/\sqrt s\).

For a fixed center one may truncate \(|b_p|\le e_p\) coordinatewise, which only enlarges the gain. Consider a matching of \(h\) disjoint oriented edges. Round both the starting and ending rows to \(e_pY_i\). By the preceding estimates this changes the mean gain by at most \(2W/\sqrt h\), since the gain is 2-Lipschitz in its first argument. For \(d\in\{-e_p,0,e_p\}\) one has \(|d|-|d-b_p|\le b_pd/e_p\). The mean right side summed against \(\log p\) is at most \(2W/\sqrt h\) by bit imbalance on the two sets of endpoints. Thus a matching of qualifying edges has size at most \(16/\delta^2\).

For a star of \(h\) leaves oriented consistently relative to its center, round only the leaves; the mean gain costs at most \(W/\sqrt h\). For a fixed anchor coordinate \(z\in[0,e_p]\), a fair leaf bit gives the two differences \(z,z-e_p\) or their negatives. Its mean gain is nonpositive: zero belongs to the interval between them and minimizes the sum of the two distances. The actual empirical bit imbalance adds at most \(2W/\sqrt h\). Thus a coherently oriented star of qualifying edges has size at most \(9/\delta^2\). For an undirected graph of qualifying pairs choose one qualifying orientation per edge, giving maximum degree at most \(18/\delta^2\). Both orientations of the same pair cannot qualify at positive gain for a fixed center, since \(w(v-b)+w(-v-b)\ge 2w(v)\). The endpoints of any maximal matching cover every edge, giving at most \(576/\delta^4\) edges. Finally any successful center on a ray satisfies \(\delta W\le w(b)\le2W\). A net of \(O(1/\delta)\) centers on this radial interval, spaced at most \(\delta W/2\), leaves gain \(\delta W/2\) at the nearest center. This proves the ray assertion.

**PROVED — direct interpretation in a twisted height.** Take the reduced oriented Gaussian edge numerator \(A=s+it\) of norm \(\exp w(v)\), up to signs as in Part I. At every split finite place take the two linear forms \(X+iY,X-iY\) on the primitive vector \((s,t)\). Normalize the two local weights to be opposite by subtracting their mean; this leaves the global twisted height invariant when the global sum of the weights is zero. With \(h=\log Q\), write the adjustments to the two logarithms at \(\pi\mid p\) as \(+c_\pi h,-c_\pi h\). The two unadjusted logarithms are \(-v_p^+\log p/2,-v_p^-\log p/2\), with \(v_p^- =\max(-v_p,0)\). Taking their adjusted maximum gives \((|v_p-b_p|-|v_p|)\log p/4\) where \(b_p=4c_\pi h/\log p\). At the conjugate place interchange the sign in the definition of \(b'_p\). Summing gives the finite contribution
\[
 \tfrac14\big(w(v-b)+w(v-b')-2w(v)\big).
\]
At unselected or outside places with unit form values, centered adjustments contribute nonnegatively. For this test all split places supporting the parent are included with the prescribed pair of forms; at finite places outside them the primitive-coordinate norm is one in the ordinary unweighted height. If some parent places are omitted and use the ordinary norm one there, dropping their supported optimized gain cannot improve on assigning a center of zero there. At infinity use \(X,Y\); the adjusted maximum is at least \(\tfrac12\log|st|\). Since \(t\ge1\) and \(s\gg \exp(W/4)\) on these near-real edges, this is at least \(W/8-O(1)\). A twisted height below one therefore requires one of \(b,b'\) to give a gain of at least \(W/4-O(1)\). Each is confined to a fixed ray when the weight vector is fixed, even as \(Q\) varies. The ray theorem bounds the number of qualifying edges absolutely. Thus obtaining a positive power gain on a positive fraction of the \(m^2\) edges requires order \(m^2\) distinct choices of this local weight array. The statement concerns this choice of finite forms and archimedean coordinates. It does not bound systems using different additive relations or nonlinear forms.

**PROVED — fixed higher words.** Fix \(k\) and integers \(c_1,\ldots,c_k\) with \(\sum c_i=0\), and set \(v_T=\sum c_i a_{T_i}\) on ordered distinct \(k\)-tuples. For every fixed \(\delta>0\), any one center or ray has gain \(\ge\delta W\) on at most \(O_{k,c,\delta}(m^{k-1})\) tuples. Here is a direct count. For any small fixed \(\epsilon>0\), there are \(O(\epsilon^{-2})\) rows with \(D_i=\sum_p\min(a_{ip},e_p-a_{ip})\log p\ge\epsilon W\), by applying the subset bound above just to those rows. Tuples using one already number \(O_{k,\epsilon}(m^{k-1})\). For the rest, choose a uniform level \(h\in\{1,\ldots,e_p\}\) after selecting \(p\); put \(B_{i\ell}=\mathbf1_{a_{ip}\ge h}\), \(f_{i\ell}=2B_{i\ell}-1\), where \(\ell=(p,h)\). Replacing normalized \(a_{ip}/e_p\) by \(B_{i\ell}\) in the gain and averaging over the layers costs at most \(4\sum|c_i|\epsilon W\). Indeed \(\mathbb E_h|X_i-B_{i\ell}|=2X_i(1-X_i)\le2\min(X_i,1-X_i)\), and the gain is 2-Lipschitz.

Truncate \(|b_p|/e_p\le\sum|c_i|\). At a given prime the gain per unit weight on binary rows is the bounded function \(F_p(B)=|\sum c_i B_i|-|\sum c_i B_i-b_p/e_p|\). Under independent fair bits the signed sum has symmetric law because \(\sum c_i=0\); hence the constant term of its Walsh expansion is nonpositive. Every nonconstant coefficient \(u_{J,\ell}\) is bounded by \(L=\sum|c_i|\). For independent uniform row indices \(I_i\), the corresponding averaged term \(M_J=\mathbb E_\ell u_{J,\ell}\prod_{i\in J} f_{I_i,\ell}\) has second moment at most \(2L^2/m\). In detail the row Gram \(G=\mathbb E_\ell f_\ell f_\ell^t\) has diagonal one, nonpositive off-diagonals and is positive semidefinite; the nonnegative matrix \(I-G\) has spectral norm at most one by the absolute-value Rayleigh bound. Thus \(\|G\|\le2\). For a singleton \(J\), the row operator with layer columns \(\sqrt{\Pr(\ell)}f_\ell\) gives the bound. For \(|J|\ge2\), expand the second moment in two independent layers: up to the bounded weights this is a power of the empirical inner product \(m^{-1}f_\ell^t f_{\ell'}\) of order at least two. Its absolute value is at most the square, whose expectation is \(\operatorname{tr}(G^2)/m^2\le2/m\). Chebyshev on the finitely many nonconstant terms excludes all but \(O(m^{k-1})\) tuples at any fixed positive gain. Choose \(\epsilon\ll\delta/L\) and use the same ray net (with \(w(v_T)\le LW\)) to finish.

**COMPUTED — optimize over every real center in a small profile.** Give every column of the eight-mark Paley skew double core equal model weight. Enumerate all 105 perfect matchings and all 16 orientations on each. For each of the 1,680 oriented matchings the largest possible mean gain over *all real centers* is computed independently at every coordinate by a median of its four edge differences. In units of \(W/28\), the maxima are \(0,1,2,3,4,6\) with frequencies \(84,336,420,560,252,28\). Even the best of these matchings falls below mean gain \(W/4\). On every full oriented outgoing star the exact best mean is \(W/(m-1)\) at \(m=8,12,20\). These are equal-weight model tests, independent of realizability by exactly equal prime logarithms. **PROVED — continuously varying weights in a low-dimensional space.** More generally let the center vary anywhere in a real affine space of dimension \(r\), with no integrality or choice-of-height condition. At fixed \(\delta>0\) this captures at most \(O(\delta^{-4})(1+16/\delta)^r\) oriented edges. Indeed each successful center lies in the weighted \(\ell^1\)-ball of radius \(2W\). A maximal \(\delta W/2\)-separated net in its intersection with the affine space has at most \((1+16/\delta)^r\) elements: the disjoint radius-\(\delta W/4\) balls in the translated \(r\)-dimensional normed space fit in one of radius \(4W+\delta W/4\), and volumes scale as the \(r\)-th power. Assign each successful center to the nearest net point, retaining gain \(\delta W/2\). The analogous upper bound for a fixed higher word is \(O_{k,c,\delta}(m^{k-1})(1+16\sum|c_i|/\delta)^r\). In particular \(o(\log m)\) continuously adjustable parameters in one affine family of local weight arrays cannot capture polynomially many edges, or a positive fraction of all \(k\)-tuples, at fixed power gain.

**PROVED — a positive proportion needs at least square-root parameter dimension.** If centers vary over a real affine space of dimension \(r\), its qualifying pairs at fixed gain \(\delta W\) have a vertex cover of size \(O_\delta((r+1)^2)\). In particular covering a positive fraction of all pairs requires \(r\gg_{\delta,\mathrm{fraction}}\sqrt m\).

*Proof.* Normalize coordinates \(\beta_p=b_p/e_p\) and use the prime probability measure from above. They range in a linear space of dimension \(d\le r+1\); every successful center has \(\|\beta\|_1\le2\). Choose an Auerbach basis \(u_1,\ldots,u_d\) for this space with \(\|u_j\|_1=1\) and all coordinate functionals of dual norm one. For completeness, maximize the absolute determinant of \(d\) unit-ball vectors in any fixed linear coordinates. It is positive and attained, and replacing a basis vector by any unit-ball vector cannot increase it, giving the dual bound. On the set \(S\) of primes where \(\sum_j|u_j(p)|\le d/\epsilon\) the complementary probability is at most \(\epsilon\). Every admissible \(\beta\) has coefficients bounded by two, so \(\|\mathbf1_S\beta\|_2^2\le (2d/\epsilon)\|\beta\|_1\le4d/\epsilon\). The case \(d=0\) is empty.

Set \(\epsilon=\delta/4\) and discard all marks with \(D_i>\delta W/32\), at most \(O_\delta(1)\) of them by the preceding subset estimate. Normalized gain on the complement of \(S\) is at most its probability, since gain at a coordinate is at most \(|X_i-X_j|\le1\). Replacing both coordinates by bits on a common threshold layer costs at most \(4(D_i+D_j)/W\le\delta/4\). A qualifying pair among the remaining marks thus gives
\[
 \delta/2\le \mathbb E_\ell\,\mathbf1_S\,\beta_p(f_{i\ell}-f_{j\ell})/2 .
\]
Here \(|u|-|u-\beta|\le\beta u\) for \(u\in\{-1,0,1\}\) even if \(\beta\) is unbounded, which permits retaining the original linear space. Regard the restricted \(\beta\)'s as functions on threshold layers via \(\ell\mapsto p\). Let \(\Pi\) be the orthogonal projection onto their span in the layer \(L^2\) space. Cauchy–Schwarz gives one endpoint with \(\|\Pi f_i\|_2\ge \delta\sqrt{\epsilon}/(4\sqrt d)\). The positive operator \(\sum_i f_i\otimes f_i\) and the row Gram have the same nonzero eigenvalues, hence operator norm at most two. Thus \(\sum_i\|\Pi f_i\|_2^2\le2d\). At most \(32d^2/(\epsilon\delta^2)\) rows meet the endpoint threshold. Together with the discarded rows they form the required vertex cover.

**PROVED — bounded coordinates.** If the proposed \(r\)-dimensional family already has \(|b_p|\le K e_p\) at every successful center, with \(K\) fixed, the vertex cover in the preceding proof has size \(O_{\delta,K}(r+1)\). No Auerbach basis or removed prime set is needed, since \(\|\beta\|_2\le K\). All other steps, including the threshold substitution, are unchanged. A positive fraction of pairs then needs \(r\gg_{\delta,K}m\). Clipping an arbitrary center to the box can enlarge its gain, but clipping a linear family need not preserve its linear dimension, which is why the two statements have different hypotheses.
