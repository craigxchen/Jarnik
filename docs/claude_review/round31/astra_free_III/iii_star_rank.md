### A rank obstruction for two uses of uniform unit-equation counts

**PROVED — star ratios detect every participating mark.** Let \(u_i=z_i/z_1\), on the shortened one-unit cluster of Part II. Let \(a_i\) denote its Gaussian exponent row. The map \(e_i-e_j\mapsto a_i-a_j\) is injective on the rational space of zero-sum vectors, by the real affine independence proved in II.4. For any ordered \(k\)-tuple \(T\), take a tuple of multiplicative words in its phase ratios, with a zero-sum exponent vector for each word. Suppose that every mark of \(T\) occurs with nonzero coefficient in at least one of its words. The word coefficients may depend on \(T\). If all these word vectors belong, modulo roots of unity, to a single coset of a multiplicative group of rank \(r\), then the entire family uses at most \(k(r+1)\) marks and comprises at most \((k(r+1))_k\) distinct ordered tuples. Here \((n)_k=n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)\).

Indeed apply the signed prime valuations componentwise. A group coset has valuation image of rational affine dimension at most \(r\). By the injection on the zero-sum space, the list of formal exponent vectors on the original marks has the same affine dimension as its valuation image. Choose at most \(r+1\) tuples spanning this affine space. Every coefficient outside their union of marks vanishes on the affine span. The hypothesis on active marks proves the assertion. The power \(k\) is sharp for this rank argument. On all ordered \(k\)-tuples of \(n\ge k+1\) marks the formal anchored ratios have affine dimension exactly \((k-1)(n-1)\): vary just one nonanchor slot between \(i,j\) and choose its \(k-1\) companions outside \(\{i,j\}\). These differences give a spanning set in each of the \(k-1\) coordinate factors.

**PROVED — application to (8).** The five-term identity (8) in the neighboring report uses a one-unit five-tuple with only the ten simple pair cuts. Dividing by one term gives a four-variable unit equation with star ratios \(P_i/P_{i_0}\), up to rational cofactor coefficients. For any such ratios, even with tuple-dependent choices of common star normalization, the homomorphism \(x\mapsto x/\bar x\) removes all nonzero rational multipliers and recovers the original ratios \(z_i/z_{i_0}\) up to units. The preceding bound therefore applies whether one fixes those rational coefficients or absorbs them into the multiplicative variables. It applies to any other star identity with this same phase property. This argument does not assert (8) on a generic balanced five-tuple. The uniform Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt bound \(\exp((6n)^{3n}(r+1))\) for an \(n\)-variable unit equation exceeds the total possible number \((5(r+1))_5\) of five-tuples in *each* such coset, even before counting solutions or testing nondegeneracy.

**PROVED — triangle squareclasses detect the edges.** Write \(A=au^2,B=bv^2,C=cw^2,U=(uvw)^2\) as in II.5. The Heron relation is the fixed-coefficient six-variable unit equation
\[
 \frac{2AB+2BC+2CA-A^2-B^2-C^2}{4U}=1.
\]
Use \((AB/U,BC/U,CA/U,A^2/U,B^2/U,C^2/U)\) as the variables. Their first three rational squareclasses are exactly \(ab,bc,ca\), the three edge norms. At every split prime, \(v_p(d_{ij})\equiv a_{ip}-a_{jp}\pmod2\), including prime powers in the parent. In the shortened cluster the mod-two pair sum determines its unordered edge. An equality between two different pairs would give an affine dependence on two or four marks modulo two, excluded in II.4. Two consecutive labelled edges determine the ordered triple. Thus these solution vectors have different images in \((\mathbb Q_{>0}^{*}/\mathbb Q_{>0}^{*2})^6\). A coset of a rank-\(r\) subgroup of the positive rational torus has at most \(2^r\) images there. In each coset an elementary count already bounds the number of these triangle solutions by \(2^r\), even including degenerate solutions. The much larger exponential uniform unit-equation bound gives no saving in any grouping by such cosets. Moving square factors cause no change. This concerns the complete displayed word vector; an additive system combining shared atoms in another way need not retain that vector.

**PROVED — four fixed linear forms do not give a place-uniform exception bound.** Work over \(\mathbb Q(i)\). Use the coordinate forms \(X,Y\) at infinity and the two forms \(X+iY,X-iY\) at *every* chosen finite place. For an integer \(M\ge2\), take the set of all finite places over prime divisors of \(\prod_{M\le j<2M}(4j^2+1)\), and take the \(M\) primitive representatives \(v_j=(2j,1)\). Every such prime is odd split. With absolute values normalized for the product formula, the full product of the two form values over the chosen places and infinity at \(v_j\) equals \(2j/(4j^2+1)<H(v_j)^{-1}\). The finite-place contribution is the reciprocal of the norm polynomial value by the product formula on the positive rational integer \(4j^2+1\), and \(H(v_j)=2j\). These points give \(M\) different rational lines with growing heights for one finite place set, four fixed forms, bounded coefficients and a fixed exponent gain. A bound on their number depending solely on the number and heights of the forms and the gain, above an absolute height cutoff, is therefore impossible. Each individual norm has logarithm \(O(\log M)\); the example does not impose critical individual weights relative to the parent. A parametric theorem with one prescribed vector of local weights imposes another hypothesis.

**PROVED — a joint identity with one orientation kept fixed.** For any fixed \(k\) distinct primitive global stars and an integral dependence \(\sum_j c_jP_{i_j}=0\), its coefficient at slot \(j\) is divisible by
\[
 \prod_p p^{(\min_{h\ne j}a_{i_h,p}-a_{i_j,p})_+}.
\]
Here the exponent rows record exact (truncated) depth along the target projective line at \(p\), in particular for genuine Gaussian stars. A primitive linear form defining that line modulo \(p^{e_p}\) applied to the dependence shows this: if the singled-out term has smaller depth than all others, it has exact untruncated valuation at that depth, forcing the displayed difference into its coefficient. The logarithm of the divisor is the weight of the oriented cut with just slot \(j\) unmarked. By the threshold Walsh estimate of Part I §5, for fixed \(k,\eta>0\) all but \(O_{k,\eta}(m^{k-1})\) ordered tuples have weight \(\ge(2^{-k}-\eta)W\) on each such cut. Indeed the same proof gives second moment at most \(2/m\) for each nonempty Walsh coefficient with independent row indices, and a union bound applies to the fixed number of cuts. Thus at almost every tuple a nonzero coefficient of a global-star dependence has exponential size in \(W\) for fixed \(k\). Reorienting the primes for each tuple changes its stars; the small-coefficient normalization of (8) is not transferable to the whole fixed array.

**PROVED — exact cancellation in the perfectly balanced parent.** Let \(m=2k\), and assume each exponent threshold is marked on precisely \(k\) rows. With genuine stars \(P_i\), shared norms \(g_{ij}=\prod p^{\min(a_{ip},a_{jp})}\) and signed cofactors \(t_{ij}=\det(P_i,P_j)/g_{ij}\), put \(D_i=\prod_{j\ne i}t_{ij}\), \(y_i=P_i/\bar P_i\). Then
\[
 \sum_i \frac{y_i^s}{D_i}=0\qquad(-k+1\le s\le k-1).
\]
On every layer marked at \(i\), exactly \(k-1\) other rows share the mark. Hence \(\prod_{j\ne i}g_{ij}=|P_i|^{m-2}\). For a homogeneous binary form \(F\) of degree \(m-2\), Lagrange interpolation gives \(\sum_i F(P_i)/\prod_{j\ne i}\det(P_i,P_j)=0\): first go to coordinates with all ordinates nonzero, dehomogenize, and take the coefficient of degree \(m-1\) as in (8). Apply this to \(F(P)=P^{k-1+s}\bar P^{k-1-s}\). If all \(|D_i|\) are equal, the \(s=0\) equation gives \(k\) terms of either sign. The two monic degree-\(k\) polynomials on the positive and negative marks have the same interior coefficients by Newton's identities. Distinct constant terms are required since their root sets are disjoint. Each set lies on the unit circle, so \(a_j=a_0\bar a_{k-j}\) for each set. Distinct constants now force every interior coefficient to vanish. For \(k\ge2\) each set is a rotated regular \(k\)-gon, impossible inside the shortened arc. The displayed identities also hold for the real vectors in a perfectly balanced angle model if \(t_{ij}\) is defined from its real determinants. An arithmetic gain would require the cofactors' integrality.
