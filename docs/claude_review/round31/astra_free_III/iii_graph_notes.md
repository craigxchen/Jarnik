### Inverse-polynomial accuracy of all scalar cofactors can coexist with multiplicity

**PROVED.** Fix \(L,\varepsilon>0\). At arbitrarily large sizes \(m\) there is a gap-admissible angle model on the squarefree Paley skew double core, with \(W\le(8+2L+\varepsilon)m\log m\), containing a single-unit arc of angular width at most \(e^{-W/4}/2\) on \(m\) marks, and distinct positive integers \(T_{ij}\) on its unordered pairs. All the actual reduced sines in that model satisfy
\[
  \left|\sqrt{d_{ij}}\,|\sin((\delta_i-\delta_j)/2)| - T_{ij}\right| < m^{-L}.
\]
The signed \(T_{ij}\) are exactly the minors of **one integral matrix with primitive columns** \((1,b_i)^t\). The integer minors here model the scalar *cofactors*. The weighted minors \(g_{ij}T_{ij}\), the actual near-real Cartesian integrality, and the shared Gaussian stars from the question remain additional requirements.

*Construction.* Initially use \(M=q+1\) marks, \(q\equiv3\pmod4\) prime. Take \(s=\lceil C M\log M\rceil\) with a large absolute \(C\), a prime \(2s<p<4s\), and \(h_i=2pi+(i^2\bmod p)\) for \(0\le i<s\). Positive differences are distinct: their disjoint intervals around \(2p(j-i)\), of radii less than \(p\), determine \(j-i\); reducing modulo \(p\) now determines \(j+i\) since \(j+i< p\). These marks lie in \([0,10s^2]\). For \(X=10s^2\), the prime number theorem gives \(\gg X/\log X\) primes in \([X+h_i,2X+h_i]\) uniformly in \(i\), since their endpoints are in \([X,3X]\). Averaging the translates over all integers in \([X,2X]\) therefore gives one translate containing \(M\) prime marks. Take \(b_i\) in increasing order to be four times these primes. All differences remain distinct, the minimum nonzero absolute difference is at least four, and the diameter is \(O(M^2\log^2M)\).

Choose fixed \(A\) with \(4+L<A<4+L+\varepsilon/2\) and \(\gamma>0\) such that \(4+L+2\gamma<A\). Put \(P=M^A\). By the prime number theorem in the fixed progression \(1\bmod4\), there are \(\gg P/\log P\) split primes in \([P,2P]\). Subdivide on the log scale into \(O(M^{3+L+\gamma})\) intervals of width at most \(M^{-3-L-\gamma}\). One interval contains \(2(M-1)\) distinct primes. Write \(P'\) for its lower endpoint on the original scale. Assign one prime per binary cut of the skew double core, as in round 8, §6. Each pair is separated by exactly \(M\) cuts. With \(E=(P')^{M/2}\) we have
\[
 \sqrt{d_{ij}}/E=1+O(M^{-2-L-\gamma}),\quad W=2(M-1)\log P'+O(M^{-2-L-\gamma}),\quad
 E e^{-W/4}=\sqrt{P'}(1+O(M^{-2-L-\gamma})).
\]
Choose each \(\delta_i\) independently in the interval of length \(h=M^{-L}/(10E)\) starting at \(2(b_i-b_0)/E\). All intervals lie in \([0,\Delta]\), \(\Delta=e^{-W/4}/2\), since \(\sqrt{P'}\gg M^2\log^2 M\). The Taylor inequality \(|\sin x-x|\le|x|^3/6\) gives the stated simultaneous error, with \(M\) replacing \(m\); the three error contributions are \(O(M^{-L-\gamma}\log^2 M)\), at most \(M^{-L}/10\) for large \(M\), and \(O(M^6\log^6M/E^2)\).

**PROVED — simultaneous monomial gaps with the narrow intervals.** Here are details of the alteration to the established Paley construction. We use its realization torus and its proved character margin, keeping the winding \(k=e_1\). A character image is \(v(\lambda)=\sum_i\lambda_i a_i\) integral with \(\sum_i\lambda_i=0\), where \(a_i\) are the binary rows. It has \(2\langle v(\lambda),\phi\rangle=\lambda\cdot\delta+(\pi/2)\lambda_1\) in the stated torus convention. The row-difference map is injective on the zero-sum coefficients. The integral characters \(c\) of norm \(n=\|c\|_1\) satisfy
\[

\]
The other characters have \(\lambda_i\in\lambda_0+\mathbb Z\) for a common nonintegral \(\lambda_0\in(2/M)\mathbb Z\). It is enough to avoid intervals of radius \(\arcsin(e^{-w(v)/2})\le2e^{-w(v)/2}\) around \((\pi/4)\mathbb Z\); this includes the axial gaps. For a linear form with independent coordinate intervals of width \(h\) and an integral coefficient of absolute value at least one, its maximal density is at most \(1/h\), and its range has length \(O(n h)\) irrespective of its center. The probability of failure for an integral \(c\) is at most \(O((h^{-1}+n)e^{-w(v(c))/2})\). The norm \(n\) is even. At \(n=2\) the vector is a pair difference. After the fixed winding shift in \((\pi/4)\mathbb Z\), its distance to that lattice is \(|\delta_i-\delta_j|/2\). Since the rounded cofactor exceeds two, its sine exceeds \(1/\sqrt{d_{ij}}\). Fix a sufficiently large integer \(n_0=n_0(A,L)\). For \(4\le n\le n_0\), a nonzero integer \(c\cdot b\) has absolute value at least four. The linear phase after removing the winding is \((c\cdot b)/E+O(nh)\), much smaller in absolute value than one and separated from zero by at least \(1/E\) for large \(M\). Since \(E e^{-w(v(c))/2}\ll (P')^{1/2-n/4}=o(1)\), such a character passes. At \(n=4\) a zero dot product is impossible, since the Golomb property excludes equality of two (possibly repeated) pair sums except when the multisets coincide. For any even \(n\ge6\), the number of reduced coefficient vectors with \(c\cdot b=0\) is at most \(M^{n-2}\). Write their positive and negative parts as ordered lists of length \(n/2\), fix all entries except one on either side, and use the fact that the missing signed difference is nonzero and determines its two marks. The lists of a reduced vector have disjoint supports. For \(6\le n\le n_0\) their total failure probability is therefore
\[
 O_{n_0}\Big(M^L\sqrt{P'}\sum_{n=6}^{n_0} M^{n-2}(P')^{-n/4}\Big)
 = O_{n_0}(M^{L+4}/P)=o(1).
\]
For \(n>n_0\) use at most \((2M)^n\) vectors and sum the geometric tail with ratio \(2M(P')^{-1/4}\), which tends to zero polynomially. Since \(h^{-1} e^{-W/4}=O(M^L\sqrt{P'})\), a fixed sufficiently large \(n_0\) makes this tail \(o(1)\), including the harmless factor \(n+1\).

For a nonintegral character \(\lambda\), write \(n=\|\lambda\|_1\). Applying the integral margin to \((M/2)\lambda\) gives \(w(v(\lambda))\ge W/M+(n/2)\log P'-O(1/M)\). The winding term puts \(\pi\lambda_1/4\) at distance at least \(\pi/(2M)\) from \((\pi/4)\mathbb Z\). For \(n\Delta\le\pi/(4M)\), the varying term shifts it by at most \(n\Delta/2\) and \(2e^{-w(v)/2}=O((P')^{-1+1/M})\ll1/M\). For larger \(n\), one coefficient has size at least \(1/M\), so the failure probability is at most \(O(M(h^{-1}+n)e^{-w(v)/2})\). For \(j\le n<j+1\), the number of possible vectors is at most \(M(2M)^{j+M+3}\): choose the common fractional part of size less than one and enumerate the integral vector of norm at most \(j+M+1\). Since \(j\gg 1/(M\Delta)\) is exponential in \(W\), the tail from \((2M)(P')^{-1/4}<1/2\) absorbs \(h^{-1}(2M)^{M+O(1)}\). Finally the union bound for all noncharacters is at most
\[
 O\left(\prod_{p\mid N}\left(1+\frac{2}{\sqrt p-1}\right)-1\right)=o(1)
\]
by conditional Haar uniformity. Therefore a good realization exists. Omit the one exceptional winding mark to put all remaining marks in one unit class, giving \(m=M-1\). The cuts still range over both bits after that deletion. This proof does not require the additional Lang–Waldschmidt conclusion of the cited theorem.

**PROVED — parity on a genuine monochromatic graph.** On a one-unit arc at odd split norm, orient each edge angle along the arc. If \(t_{ij}\) has the same exact 2-adic valuation \(a\) on each edge of a cycle, the cycle length is even. Write the near-real factors on the oriented cycle as \(s_e+i\epsilon_e t_e\). Their product is positive real by phase closure. If \(a=0\), odd norm forces all \(s_e\) even and the factors reduce to \(i\bmod2\), whose odd powers are nonreal modulo two. If \(a>0\), all \(s_e\) are odd and the product's imaginary part divided by \(2^a\) is congruent to the number of edges mod two. Every exact-\(T\) graph is therefore bipartite. An equivalent two-edge refinement is that \(2T\mid t_{jk}\) whenever \(t_{ij}=t_{ik}=T\). Represent both oriented ratios from the center by \(C_j/\bar C_j,C_k/\bar C_k\), taking \(C_j=s_j+iT,C_k=s_k+iT\) with signed real parts. Then \(g=\mathrm N\gcd(C_j,C_k)\) is odd, prime to \(T\), and divides \(|s_j-s_k|\). The first facts follow from odd norms and \(\gcd(s_j,T)=1\); for the last, \(g\mid |\det(C_j,C_k)|=T|s_j-s_k|\). Cancelling shared factors in the ratio gives \(t_{jk}=T|s_j-s_k|/g\), which is a multiple of \(2T\) because the real parts have the same parity.

**PROVED — what pigeonholing gives at the constant boundary.** If \(E=\binom m2\) labels have average log at most \(B\), some value occurs at least \(E e^{-B-1}\) times: for maximum multiplicity \(f\), the \(k\)-th smallest label is at least \(k/f\), hence the log sum is at least \(\log(E!)-E\log f\ge E(\log(E/f)-1)\). Thus a bound \(B\le(3/4+o(1))\log m\) forces a color containing \(m^{5/4-o(1)}\) edges. The Bondy–Simonovits even-cycle bound forces a ten-cycle at this density. The rounded construction shows that such rainbow labels can satisfy considerably more than the log budget.

**PROVED — obstruction to simply narrowing the intervals at fixed height.** Consider the same core, with all its distinct supporting primes at most \(P_*\le M^A\). To prove this, write \(C_a=s_as_a^t,D_a=t_at_a^t\), so that \(\sum C_a=\sum D_a=MI-\mathbf1\mathbf1^t\). Within either family the projectors are Frobenius-orthogonal of norm \(M\). Their normalized matrix of cross inner products \(H_{ab}=(s_a\cdot t_b/M)^2\) is doubly stochastic. Its diagonal is \(h_0=(1-4/M)^2\); off the diagonal exactly one of \(H_{ab},H_{ba}\) is \(16/M^2\), the other zero. Indeed for \(a\ne b\) the cross dot product is \(-2(1+s_a(b))\), and the Paley signs \(s_a(b),s_b(a)\) are opposite. For \(\sum_a y_a=0\) the variance identity gives
\[
 \|y\|^2-\|Hy\|^2
 =\sum_a\sum_{b<c} H_{ab}H_{ac}(y_b-y_c)^2
 \ge\frac{16h_0}{M^2}\sum_{b<c}(y_b-y_c)^2

\]

\[
 \left\|\sum_a (u_a C_a+v_a D_a)\right\|_F^2

\]
Distinct prime logs in one family have spacing at least \(1/P_*\) by integration. Hence \(\|u\|^2\gg M^3/P_*^2\), using the pair-sum formula for the variance on their ordered list. ### Primitive integer stars retaining half of the shared atoms

**PROVED — partial integral lift on one parent.** In the rounded construction one can also supply a single set of primitive Gaussian integers \(P_i\), actual points of a width-\(O(1)\) strip at distance \(\asymp N^{1/4}\) from the origin. Write \(H_i=\sqrt{B_i}\) and \(g_{ij}=\prod_{p:a_{ip}=a_{jp}=1}p\). Then
\[
 |P_i|=(1+o(1))H_i,\qquad
 \left|\frac{\det(P_i,P_j)}{g_{ij}}-(b_j-b_i)\right|<m^{-L}.
\]
There is a common set \(S\) of prime columns (the first Paley family with \(O_L(\log m)\) exceptions) on which the \(P_i\) have **all** their specified Gaussian valuations exactly. In particular
\[
 g_{ij,S}:=\prod_{p\in S:\,a_{ip}=a_{jp}=1}p\ \mid\ \det(P_i,P_j),\qquad
 \log g_{ij,S}=\tfrac12\log g_{ij}+O_L((\log m)^2)
\]
on *every* pair. If instead a different bounded number of exclusions per vertex is allowed, every pair has exact divisibility by a subproduct of size at least \(\sqrt{g_{ij}}/m^{O_L(1)}\). This does not impose exact divisibility by the remaining atoms, nor membership of each \(P_i\) in its exact norm shell. The faked angles and the arguments of the integer stars agree to the precision used here up to a single common rotation; the monomial-gap assertion is for the original angle model.

*Proof.* Use any fixed slightly stronger precision in the preceding construction to absorb constants. Put \(H=N^{1/4}\) and denote the supporting prime scale by \(p_0\asymp m^A\). Each ordinary row has exactly \(M/2\) ones in the unflipped family and \(M/2-1\) in the flipped family (they differ in that row only in its own block). Two distinct ordinary rows have \(M/4\) common unflipped ones by Hadamard orthogonality, and \(M/2-1\) common bits in total by their Hamming distance. The log weights differ by \(o(1/M)\), so \(H_i/H=1+o(1)\) uniformly and \(g_{ij}\asymp H/\sqrt{p_0}\).

Fix a large constant \(k_0\) to be specified in terms of the polynomial costs below. Delete \(O_{k_0}(\log M)\) columns from the unflipped family so each row loses at least \(k_0\) ones. For example, take \(C\log M\) independent uniform first-family columns with replacement: each row sees a binomial variable of mean at least \(C\log M/2\); for a sufficiently large fixed \(C\) the probability of a failure of the \(k_0\) target on any row tends to zero by the binomial sum. With probability \(1-o(1)\) no sample repeats. Let \(S\) be the surviving first-family columns, and \(M_i=\prod_{p\in S:a_{ip}=1}p\). Then \(M_i\ll H p_0^{1/2-k_0}\). The intersection counts above prove the stated bound on \(\log g_{ij,S}\). For the vertex-dependent variant simply discard \(k_0\) first-family ones on each row; at most \(2k_0\) previously common primes are removed from any pair.

Choose an even \(q\asymp m^{L+3}\sqrt{p_0}\) such that no supporting prime divides \(q^2+1\). Among \(5M\) consecutive even candidates each of the \(2(M-1)\) large primes forbids at most two, since every prime exceeds \(10M\). Let \(v=(1,q)\), \(J=|v|\), and in orthonormal coordinates along \(v/J,(-q,1)/J\) take target vectors
\[
 V_i=(H_i\cos(\delta_i/2), H_i\sin(\delta_i/2)).
\]
Their real determinant divided by \(g_{ij}\) is exactly \(\sqrt{d_{ij}}\sin((\delta_j-\delta_i)/2)\) since \(H_iH_j/g_{ij}=\sqrt{d_{ij}}\). Their perpendicular coordinates are all bounded, as \(H_i\Delta=O(1)\).

We seek \(P_i=(x_i,qx_i+s_i)\) in the original lattice coordinates. Choose a nonzero even integer \(s_i\) within \(O(M)\) of \(JH_i\sin(\delta_i/2)\), coprime to \(M_i\). Among \(3M\) successive even candidates, each required odd prime excludes at most one; exclude zero also. Thus \(|s_i|\ll q+M\), and the perpendicular error is \(O(M/q)\).

For each selected prime choose its specified Gaussian prime and the corresponding residue \(\omega_p\) of \(i\) modulo its square as a root of \(-1\) in \(\mathbb Z/p^2\). The other conjugate uses \(-\omega_p\). For bit one impose \(x_i+\omega_p(qx_i+s_i)\equiv0\pmod p\), possible uniquely in \(x_i\) since \(p\nmid q^2+1\). Impose also \(x_i\equiv1\pmod{|s_i|}\). By CRT these give one progression of step \(M_i|s_i|\), and make \(P_i\) primitive with real coordinate odd, imaginary coordinate even. Among \(5M\) consecutive lifts in the progression nearest the desired longitudinal position, avoid the following residues. At bit one, exactly one lift index mod \(p\) makes the specified valuation at least two, since the step has \(p\)-valuation exactly one and the linear coefficient is a unit. The conjugate cannot also divide a primitive vector. At bit zero, omit the at most two lift index classes yielding a zero at either conjugate. If \(p\mid s_i\), no exclusions there are necessary, since \(x_i\equiv1\bmod p\) and \(1\pm q\omega_p\) are units. There are fewer than \(4M\) excluded indices in the range, as each prime exceeds \(5M\). This achieves all required valuations, and common Gaussian divisibility gives \(g_{ij,S}\mid\det(P_i,P_j)\) by factoring each common Gaussian prime from both stars.

The longitudinal coordinate of \(P_i\) is \(Jx_i+q s_i/J\), so its error is \(O(M q(q+M)M_i)\). This is \(O(\rho H)\) with \(\rho\le m^{-D}\) for any desired fixed \(D\) by taking \(k_0\) sufficiently large. Together with the perpendicular error \(e=O(M/q)\), both norm and strip claims follow. The error in the determinant of each pair is \(O(H e+\rho H)\): use the two orthonormal coordinates, whose target magnitudes are \(O(H)\) and \(O(1)\), respectively. Dividing by \(g_{ij}\gg H/\sqrt{p_0}\) costs only \(O(\sqrt{p_0}(M/q+\rho))=o(m^{-L})\). The choice \(q\asymp m^{L+3}\sqrt{p_0}\) and a sufficiently large fixed \(D\) suffice. This proves the lift.

### Eliminating every assignment at most parents by exposing a matching product

**PROVED.** Let a pool contain \(K\) distinct primes, and sample \(r\) of them uniformly without replacement, denoting the ordered sample by \(p_1,\ldots,p_r\). For \(s\ge1\) and \(U\ge1\), the probability that there are disjoint slot sets \(I,J,L\) with \(|J|\ge s\) and nonzero signed integers \(x,y,z\) of magnitude at most \(U\) such that
\[
 x\prod_{i\in I}p_i-y\prod_{j\in J}p_j+z\prod_{l\in L}p_l=0
\]
is at most \(4^r(2\lfloor U\rfloor)^3/\binom{K-r+s}{s}\). Fix the three sets and expose the primes on all sampled slots outside \(J\). For given coefficients the equation determines the entire product of the remaining \(|J|\) primes. Unique factorization allows at most one subset of the pool for that product. The unexposed set is uniform among \(\binom{K-r+|J|}{|J|}\) possibilities. This denominator is increasing with \(|J|\). Sum over the four states of every slot and the signed coefficients. The event is invariant under relabeling the sample and so has exactly the same probability for unordered parents.

**PROVED.** Take the ordinary Paley double core with \(r=2m\). Every quartet has all three matching classes of size \(\ge r/16\) for large \(m\) by the Hasse estimate in the main text. **PROVED — a constant bound on generic parents, uniformly over all squarefree profiles.** Draw \(r\) distinct split primes uniformly without replacement from a pool \(\mathcal P_r\) of size \(K=r^{\kappa+o(1)}\), where \(\kappa>1\); suppose all its primes satisfy \(\log p=(D+o(1))\log r\) uniformly, with fixed \(D>0\). \[
 (\kappa-1) F_M>\frac{3D}{2(M-1)},\qquad F_M=\frac{(M-1)(M-4)}{8(M-2)(M-3)}.
\]
In particular this bounds genuine arcs at any fixed arc constant after restriction to a one-unit shortened class; in the stated shortened convention the bound is literally \(M-1\). For the full dyadic prime pool \([r^D,2r^D]\), \(\kappa=D\) by the prime number theorem, and \(M=18\) suffices for all \(D\ge4\). For any sufficiently large fixed \(D\), \(M=14\) suffices. \[
