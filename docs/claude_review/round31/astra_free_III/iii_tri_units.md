### How many formal three-term unit equations do shared atoms supply?

**PROVED.** Let \(L\) be a free abelian exponent lattice and \(K_L=\operatorname{Frac} k[L]\), in characteristic zero. Use algebraically independent atom variables before numerical specialization. Assume the \(m-1\) exponent vectors of the anchored mark ratios are rationally independent, as they are for a shortened, one-unit cluster by Part II. Let \(r_0=1,r_1,\ldots,r_{m-1}\) be those Laurent monomials. Generate a multiplicative group using any atom monomials, constants, and the **whole** chord differences \(r_i-r_j\), up to atom monomials. This includes the whole reduced imaginary cofactors: if \(r_i/r_j=A/\bar A\), then \(2i\operatorname{Im}A=\bar A(r_i/r_j-1)\). Every nonconstant \(f\) for which both \(f,1-f\) belong to this group is an ordinary cross-ratio on the \(m+2\) marks \(0,1,\infty,r_1,\ldots,r_{m-1}\). In particular there are only \(O(m^4)\) nonconstant normalized solutions, irrespective of how many atom factors or known identities are multiplied together.

*Proof.* Let \(\Lambda\subset L\) be the subgroup generated by the exponents of \(r_1,\ldots,r_{m-1}\). Its fraction field \(K=k(r_1,\ldots,r_{m-1})\) is purely transcendental. Representatives for different cosets of \(L/\Lambda\) are linearly independent over \(K\): clear denominators and their Laurent supports are disjoint. This argument also applies if \(L/\Lambda\) has torsion. In \(f+(1-f)=1\), each nonconstant summand is a single atom monomial times a member of \(K\). Both cosets must be trivial. Hence \(f,1-f\) are products of integer powers of mark differences, coordinate marks and constants in \(K\).

Fix one variable \(X=r_j\) on which \(f\) depends, keeping the other marks as algebraically independent parameters. Homogenize \(f=A/B\) in \(X\), with \(A,B\) coprime binary forms of common degree \(d\). The three disjoint fibers of \(0,1,\infty\), cut out by \(A,A-B,B\), have all their roots among the other marks and \(0,1,\infty\). If a moving mark \(r_i\) is a root, differentiate with respect to that single parameter, keeping \(X\) fixed. The degree-\(2d\) binary form \(P=B\partial A-A\partial B\) vanishes at every fixed root to at least its full multiplicity: use the appropriate one of \(A,B,A-B\) in this expression. At the moving root of multiplicity \(e\) it vanishes to exactly order \(e-1\), by differentiation of \((X-r_i)^e\) in characteristic zero and disjointness of the three fibers. Thus \(P\ne0\) has at least \(3d-1\) zeros counted with multiplicity, giving \(d=1\). If the fibers use only \(0,1,\infty\), all three are totally ramified. After permuting the source marks the rational function must be \(c X^d\); its fiber over one has \(d\) distinct roots in characteristic zero, again giving \(d=1\). Its three preimages now determine the unique fractional linear map, which is exactly a cross-ratio of \(X\) and three other marked points. Conversely each such cross-ratio and its complement are products of mark differences and coordinate marks whenever those generators are included.

**PROVED — scope.** Allowing proper factors of a completed chord, specializing the independent atoms to actual Gaussian integers, or using new additive inputs can give additional equations. The theorem bounds identities that hold before specialization, including those formed jointly from an unlimited number of overlapping Plücker relations, with no restriction on individual atom monomials. It does not bound the number of arithmetically accidental solutions at one parent.

**PROVED — a quantitative obstruction to formal rank compression.** Inside any group of rank \(r\) modulo constants, the preceding nonconstant \(f\)'s number at most \(2\sum_{j=0}^4 2^j\binom rj\). Indeed in \(k[r_i]\) use the irreducible forms \(r_i,r_i-1,r_i-r_j\) as valuation coordinates, choosing a fixed sign on each. The valuation image of the intersection of the group with \(K^\times\) has rational rank at most \(r\); some \(r\) or fewer coordinates project injectively on its span. A cross-ratio of four distinct marks has at most four nonzero entries, each \(1\) or \(-1\), and multiplying out the four-mark formula gives scalar \(1\) or \(-1\) in this fixed sign convention. Count the possibilities on the projected coordinates, then the two signs. This addresses a **shared** group; the two units in any one numerical three-term equation of course generate a group of rank at most two.

**PROVED — the sharp power of the shared rank is two.** Choose a basis from the valuation vectors of the cross-ratios that actually occur. At most \(r\) vectors suffice. Each uses at most four visible pair factors, so the union graph of their supports has at most \(4r\) edges on at most \(8r\) incident marks. All the cross-ratios under consideration have visible support in this union. Each variable mark involved in one of these cross-ratios occurs on a visible edge: its two neighbors on the formal four-cycle are distinct and at most one is infinity. Add the possible edges from infinity to these marks and \(0,1\), and add \(01\), whose affine factor is constant. Now every cross-ratio determines an alternating signed four-cycle in a graph of at most \(12r+3\) edges. Given two opposite edges of a four-cycle there are at most two ways to complete it, and only constantly many orientations and orderings. The number of nonconstant solutions is consequently \(O((r+1)^2)\). This power is attained in order: the cross-ratios on \(n\) freely moving marks give \(\Theta(n^4)\) functions in the group generated by \(O(n^2)\) differences. This is about formal solutions in one common group, not the rank of the two units in a single solution.
