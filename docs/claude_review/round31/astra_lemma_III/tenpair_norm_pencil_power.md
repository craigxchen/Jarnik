# A power lower bound on each fixed rational plane of norm vectors

**PROVED.** Let \(U\subset\mathbb Q^{10}\) be a fixed rational subspace of dimension at most two. For every one-unit-class ten-pair tuple with private pairwise coprime Gaussian block norms \(n_e\), \(n\in U\), and nonzero signed cofactors \(t_e\) as in Part I, §5, one has
\[
       T=\max_e|t_e|\ \gg_U\ \big(\max_e n_e\big)^{1/10}.
\]
The implied constant may be ineffective. The proof uses Faltings' theorem for a curve of genus five, Roth's theorem on approximation to a fixed algebraic number, and the real five-star lemma proved in the accompanying note tenpair_norm_pencil.md.

**PROVED — integral parameters.** In dimension one coprimality leaves at most one positive integral vector on the fixed ray. For dimension two choose once and for all a lattice basis of \(U\cap\mathbb Z^{10}\), so \(n_e=L_e(p,q)\) with fixed integral linear forms. Put \(B=\max(|p|,|q|)\). The basis and its left inverse give \(B\asymp_U \max n_e\). Pairwise coprimality implies \(\gcd(p,q)=1\). Each form is nonzero whenever the family has a sample.

If two forms are proportional, their positive values have a fixed rational ratio, and coprimality fixes both values. The integer parameters therefore lie on a fixed affine line and have the form \((p_0,q_0)+v(p_1,q_1)\) for \(v\in\mathbb Z\); the origin and direction of this parameterization can be fixed from \(U\) alone. One edge \(ab\) has nonconstant norm on the line. Fix \(i=a\), \(j\notin\{a,b\}\), and a third index \(k\ne i,j\). Use the integer polynomial
\[
 4t_{ik}^2t_{jk}^2\prod_{h\notin\{i,j\}} n_{ih}n_{jh}
 -A_{ijk}(n)^2-4t_{ik}^2t_{jk}^2t_{ij}^2
 \tag{P1}
\]
from the cubic reconstruction of \(s_{ij}\) in the accompanying note. This polynomial vanishes at the sample parameter. Its coefficients as a polynomial in \(v\) are \(O_U(T^6)\) and it is nonzero because evaluating at the real zero of \(n_{ab}\) gives a strictly negative number. Its leading nonzero coefficient has absolute value at least one. Cauchy's root bound gives \(B\ll_U 1+|v|\ll_U T^6\). Henceforth all ten forms are pairwise nonproportional.

**PROVED — forcing a line in the Grassmannian.** Write \(M_{ij}(p,q)=t_{ij}L_{ij}(p,q)\), with skew-symmetric extension in the two indices. For each quartet its Plücker relation is a homogeneous quadratic binary form \(E\), with integral coefficients of absolute value \(O_U(T^2)\). At the sample parameters it vanishes since the entries are the minors of \(P_1,\ldots,P_5\). If any \(E\) is a nonzero polynomial then \(B\ll_U T^2\): remove any powers of the two coordinate variables, apply the rational-root theorem to the resulting polynomial, and use primitivity. On a coordinate axis \(B=1\). We may assume all relations vanish identically in the two parameters.

The coefficient bivectors of \(p,q\) in \(M\) are independent since the forms span the dual parameter plane. Both bivectors are decomposable and their sum is too. Their wedge therefore vanishes, and their rational two-dimensional support spaces meet in a rational line. Choose a generator \(u\in\mathbb Q^5\) of this line. There are rational vectors \(v,w\) such that \(M=u\wedge(pv+qw)\). None of the five \(u_i\) vanishes, since then all the incident entries \(M_{ij}\) would be proportional binary forms. With \(A_i=(v_i/u_i,w_i/u_i)\in\mathbb Q^2\),
\[
       t_{ij}L_{ij}(p,q)=u_i u_j\langle A_j-A_i,(p,q)\rangle .
\]
Thus the ten pair directions of the five affine points are fixed by \(U\) and are all different. In particular no three of these points are collinear. The configuration is uniquely determined up to translation and nonzero homothety. After matching the first two points of any two such configurations by such a transformation, all other points agree since each is the intersection of the two specified nonparallel lines from the first two points. Fix a rational representative \(A_i^0\) of the shape from one solution, if any. With the nonzero rational antisymmetric constants \(c_{ij}\) given by \(\langle A_j^0-A_i^0,(p,q)\rangle=c_{ij}L_{ij}(p,q)\), one has
\[
       t_{ij}=\lambda u_i u_j c_{ij},\qquad \lambda\in\mathbb Q^\times.
 \tag{P2}
\]
Normalizing \(u_1=1\), the rational height (maximum of absolute coprime numerator and denominator) of each \(u_i\) is \(O_U(T)\). Indeed for \(k\ne i,1\) the ratio \(u_i/u_1=t_{ik}c_{1k}/(t_{1k}c_{ik})\).

**PROVED — finite projective cofactor patterns.** Put \(\ell_i=\langle A_i^0,(p,q)\rangle\) and \(D_i=\prod_{j\ne i}c_{ij}\). The five columns \(W_i=(u_i,u_i\ell_i)\) have all their minors equal to \(1/\lambda\) times the minors of the \(P_i\) at the sample; hence the two rank-two matrices differ by one invertible real row transformation (match two independent columns, then take minors against them). Equation (8) of Part I transfers to the \(W_i\). As \(\prod_{j\ne i}t_{ij}=\lambda^4 u_i^3D_i\prod_j u_j\), its two coordinates give
\[
       \sum_i\frac1{u_i^2D_i}=0,\qquad
       \left\langle\sum_i\frac{A_i^0}{u_i^2D_i},(p,q)\right\rangle=0.
 \tag{P3}
\]
The second equation is a linear form vanishing at \((p,q)\). Write \(u_i=a_i/b_i\) in lowest terms with \(|a_i|,|b_i|\ll_U T\). Clearing the fixed denominators and multiplying by \(\prod_i a_i^2\), the two integer coefficients are sums of terms \(O_U(b_i^2\prod_{h\ne i}a_h^2)=O_U(T^{10})\). If either coefficient is nonzero, primitivity gives \(B\ll_U T^{10}\). Otherwise both vanish.

The remaining rational projective vectors \([1/u_1:\cdots:1/u_5]\) have their coordinatewise squares on the fixed projective line \(\Lambda\subset\mathbb P^4\) cut out by the three rows with columns \((1,A_i^0)/D_i\). Each triple of columns is independent by noncollinearity. The five coordinate-zero intersections of \(\Lambda\) are therefore five distinct points, each a simple zero: two coordinates cannot vanish simultaneously, since the remaining three columns are independent. No coordinate vanishes identically, since a second coordinate would then vanish there at some projective point. Over \(\mathbb C\) the cover of the line defined by taking the four coordinate ratios' square roots is connected of degree \(16\). Indeed for \(i=1,\ldots,4\), the ratio of coordinate \(i\) to coordinate five has odd valuation at the \(i\)-th coordinate-zero point, and the other three ratios have valuation zero there. These four squareclasses are independent. The only branch points are the five coordinate zeros, each of inertia order two (units over \(\mathbb C[[z]]\) have square roots). Riemann–Hurwitz gives \(2g-2=-32+5\cdot8=8\), hence \(g=5\). All our projective points have nonzero coordinates, on the smooth étale locus, and thus lift to rational points of the smooth normalization defined over \(\mathbb Q\). Faltings' theorem gives finitely many such projective vectors. By (P2) this leaves finitely many projective cofactor vectors \([t_{ij}]\), depending only on \(U\).

**PROVED — size for each fixed pattern.** Write \(t_{ij}=\lambda t_{ij}^0\) for one of these rational patterns. For a fixed ordered pair and a third index define the real cubic \(s_{ij}(n)=A_{ijk}(t^0,n)/(2t^0_{ik}t^0_{jk})\). It is independent of \(\lambda\) and at a sample gives the true scalar product divided by the common norm. Then the rational binary sextic
\[
   F(p,q)=\left(\prod_{h\notin\{i,j\}}L_{ih}L_{jh}
                         -s_{ij}(L)^2\right)/(t_{ij}^0)^2
\]
is fixed, nonzero, and takes the value \(\lambda^2\). We claim \(|F(p,q)|\gg_F B\) at primitive parameters with \(F(p,q)\ne0\) unless \(F\) is a constant times a sixth power of a rational linear form or a cube of a nonsplit indefinite rational quadratic. Indeed away from the projective real roots, \(|F|\gg B^6\). Near a particular root of multiplicity \(m\), every other factor stays bounded away from zero on the normalized direction, so \(|F|\) is comparable to \(B^6\) times the \(m\)-th power of the projective distance to that root. For rational roots, primitivity and integrality of a rational linear form give distance \(\gg_F B^{-1}\), enough for \(m\le5\). For irrational algebraic real roots, Roth gives \(\gg_F B^{-2-1/4}\) in a fixed bounded projective chart, enough for \(m\le2\). Multiplicity three forces an irreducible quadratic with both conjugate roots of multiplicity three. Higher multiplicity is impossible for an irrational root. These cases give \(|\lambda|\gg_U B^{1/2}\), sufficient here.

For the two exceptional sextic shapes, infinitely many distinct positive integral samples for a fixed projective pattern would supply infinitely many distinct parameter rays by primitivity. For each sample rescale \((p,q)\), and therefore every norm, by \(k=|\lambda|^{-1/3}>0\), and every Gaussian block by \(\sqrt{k}\). All \(P_i\) change by \(k^2\); thus every cofactor changes by \(k^3\). The new *real* samples have fixed cofactors \(t^0\) up to a common sign; retain an infinite subsequence with one sign. Their distinct rays remain distinct, and the rescaled parameters lie on \(F=1\). In the sixth-power case they lie on one fixed affine line after passage to one of at most two lines. The nonconstant-norm root argument using (P1) excludes infinitely many points there. In the indefinite quadratic-cube case they lie on one fixed nonsplit real hyperbola. On that hyperbola the argument in tenpair_norm_pencil.md from “Diagonalize the real conic” through the real five-star lemma applies without any integrality hypothesis: all rational identities extend by infinitely many samples, all norms are positive on the chosen real branch, and their ten defining forms are pairwise nonproportional by assumption, with no form vanishing on a rational isotropic direction since there are none. The resulting star-sum contradiction excludes an infinite set. Thus for the finitely many exceptional patterns only finitely many samples need absorption into the constant. All cases give the claimed exponent. \(\square\)

**PROVED — a nonempty balanced instance.** Assign the ten ranks \(r=0,\ldots,9\) in any fixed order to the edges and take \(L_r(p,q)=p+rq\). There are arbitrarily large samples with the ten block norms distinct comparable primes \(1\bmod4\). The Green–Tao–Ziegler theorem for affine-linear forms of finite complexity applies to \(4x+4ry+1\) on boxes \(X\le x\le2X,\ X/20\le y\le X/10\). No two forms have proportional linear parts, and the local factors are positive at every prime, witnessed by \(x=y=0\) modulo that prime. Each prime supplies a Gaussian block. For these comparable norms \(n_e\asymp X\), the largest cofactor is \(\gg_U X^{1/10}\); its pair distance equals \(2|t_{ij}|/\sqrt{\prod_{a\notin\{i,j\}} n_{ia}n_{ja}}\gg_U X^{-29/10}\). Thus in the \(H=\prod_e n_e\asymp X^{10}\) normalization this family has the exponent \(100/29>10/3\). The same estimate holds for every fixed rational plane with all the block norms comparable, with the corresponding dependence on the comparison constants.
