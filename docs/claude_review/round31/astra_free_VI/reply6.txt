REFEREE RESULTS ON PART V, AND A SIXTH QUESTION

Five referees and a judge went through Part V. Nothing you claimed is withdrawn. What they found, so you can build on it:

1. V.1 is confirmed (six independent reproductions, m up to 64). Strengthened form: for every integer L >= d the m points z_H have max deg(z_H - z_H') = D/2 + S_max - L/2 with S_max = (d-2)2^{d-2}, so they form a c=0 cluster iff L >= m(r-1), and M_2(D,0) >= 2^r for every D in (2^r-1)Z with D >= 2^r(2^r-1)(r-1). N is not squarefree (deg gcd(N,N') = 8 at m=4, 36 at m=8). Common gcd of the points has degree 0,1,0,8,30 for m=4..64. Prior art for the mechanism: Ben-Sasson, Kopparty, Radhakrishnan, "Subspace polynomials and limits to list decoding of Reed-Solomon codes".

2. V.2: correct, but (i) it missed unit-twisted points when deg T < d (monic normalisation of raw marks with leading coefficient theta or theta^2); the referee repaired it with the identity F_i/theta - F_j/theta^2 = A*O, O = sum of the odd-index coefficients of the linearised polynomial P_J, deg O = deg P_J(1) - a_1, a_1 in {1,2}; loss <= 4 in (V.3). (ii) Its thresholds d >= 48, s >= 6 make it empty in every computable range. (iii) V.1 is not optimal in its own family: at d=4, Y = t^15 + theta, sides (0,0,1,1,0,0,1,1), gcd degree 16, there are 8 points of reduced degree 104 < 112.

3. An analyst proved a threshold-free replacement ("Theorem E"): let V be ANY finite F_2-subspace of F_2[t] containing 1, T arbitrary, Y = T + theta, distinct forms l_i with l_i(1)=1, sides S_i, F_i = prod_{v in S_i}(Y+v), any common divisor g, unit twists allowed. If the reduced points form a c=0 cluster of degree D', and s'' is the size of a subfamily with one side per form and a common leading unit (s'' >= ceil(s/4)), then
     D' >= (4/s'') sum_{i<j} iota(P_{J_ij}(1)) >= (3/8) s''(s''-1) floor(floor(log_2 s'')/2),
   where iota(x) is the degree of the odd-degree (inert) part of x. Proof: F_i = P_J(Y+a)X, F_j = P_J(Y+a)(X+P_J(1)), so delta_ij = deg P_J(1) - deg gcd(X, P_J(1)) is invariant under common division; an odd-degree prime stays prime in F_4[t] and never divides (T+w)+theta; then the margin identity sum_{i<j} delta_ij <= s''D'/4. So inside "hyperplane designs with translation element 1" the parent degree is Theta(s^2 log s), and the loss sits entirely at small inert primes dividing P_J(1).

4. The record's growth argument transfers to F_2[t] with this norm (odd-degree primes inert with fibre 2^e+1, even-degree split with fibre 2^e-1, no ramified prime): W = deg N >= 3M log_2 M - O(M), exact minima W_min = 223, 674, 1611, 4210 at M = 32, 64, 128, 256. Your family has W = 2m(m-1)(log_2 m - 1), a factor about (2/3)m above this.

5. Through the star dictionary your cluster satisfies every hypothesis of (IV.2) with delta = E = 0 and violates it (LHS 62016 at m=32). All ratios z_a/z_b have nonzero derivative, so the failure is separable; it is not a Frobenius phenomenon and not isotriviality. The F_2(t) analogues of the record's five-point statements A_5(delta<1/30) and (L_5°) are false on your family.

6. Exhaustive values: M_2(D,0) = 2,3,2,4,3,4,3,4,3,4 for D=1..10 and = 4 for D = 11..17. For the form A^2+B^2 over F_9(t)/F_3(t): M_3(D,0) = 2,4,3,4,3,4,4,4,4,4,4 for D = 1..11.

7. Why the mechanism needs characteristic 2 ("Lemma P"): in characteristic != 2, if A is finite, A and -A disjoint, and prod_{a in A}(X+a) - prod_{a in A}(X-a) is constant, then |A| is odd; hence three transversals of a symmetric set cannot be pairwise constant-difference. So with a reflection-type conjugation (F_9(t)/F_3(t), or Z[i]) the V.1 mechanism gives at most 2 points.

The judge's reading: a theorem in the model, and a filter. No argument valid verbatim over F_2[t] with this norm can prove the uniform bound, or any bound M <= f(W) with f = o(sqrt(W/log W)). Growth-rate arguments are untouched, because your family is far above the growth scale.

THE SIXTH QUESTION. For the integers, what I care about most is the growth rate: the best bound is M <= (2/3+eps) log R/loglog R, i.e. W >= 3M log M, and any improvement of that ORDER would be real progress. The uniform bound is the ideal. Your F_2 model is now the cleanest laboratory for both, so:

(a) GROWTH SCALE OVER A FIXED FIELD. Over F_2[t] with the norm of F_4(t)/F_2(t), let W(M) be the least deg N carrying a c=0 cluster of M points. We know 3M log_2 M - O(M) <= W(M) <= 2M^2 log_2 M. Which is the truth, or what is in between?
   - Either construct clusters with W = M^{1+o(1)} (ideally O(M log M)). By Theorem E this cannot be a hyperplane design with translation element 1: cofactor differences must avoid small inert primes, and their traces must have average degree O(log M). Candidates nobody has tried: conj(Y) = Y + B with B non-constant; non-linear transversals (there are 2^{Q/2} points of norm P_V(Y)); independent choices at each split prime; unions of several designs on one parent; prime powers used deliberately.
   - Or prove W(M) >= omega(M log M), and say exactly which steps are not available over Z. A proof whose steps are all available over Z is an improvement of the integer growth rate. That is the outcome I would value most; please treat it as the main target, and be explicit about every step that uses characteristic 2, the ultrametric at infinity, or the function field.
   Over Z the obstruction to improving 3M log M is a family of "fakes" (angle models: integer data satisfying all pair and cut divisibilities and gap inequalities at W ~ 3M log M, but not coming from lattice points on a circle). If the F_2 model has genuine clusters at growth scale, tell me what structure they have, since that is what an integer construction or an integer fake would have to imitate.

(b) ODD CHARACTERISTIC WITH A REFLECTION. For A^2+B^2 over F_9(t)/F_3(t) (conjugation i -> -i, the model closest to Z[i]): is sup_D M_3(D,0) finite? Lemma P kills the subspace mechanism. Either find another mechanism (the only escape from Lemma P inside "constant cofactor differences" is even-size sets that are not symmetric cosets; or drop constancy altogether), or find the reason it is bounded. A boundedness proof using only small residue fields and the reflection would be the first argument that survives every filter in the record and could port to Z[i]; a construction would extend the filter to everything algebraic about Z[i] that holds in F_9[t].

(c) Smaller: is sup_D M_2(D,0) still infinite for squarefree N?

Take (a) and (b) in whichever order looks more promising to you, and say plainly what you could not do. As before: full proofs or exact reproducible computations, statements labelled proved / computed / heuristic, and append the result as Part VI to answer.md.
