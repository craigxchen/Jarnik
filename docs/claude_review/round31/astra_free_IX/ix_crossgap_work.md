### Splitting a prescribed angle while enforcing the lattice cross-gap

**PROVED.** Write \(\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) in quarter-turns. There exist absolute \(c>0, K<\infty\) and \(\beta=\log(24)/\log(32)>0.91\) with the following property. For every fixed \(\Theta\in\mathbb T\) there is a Borel probability measure \(\mu_\Theta\) on \(\mathbb T\) such that \(\mu_\Theta(J)\le K |J|^\beta\) for every interval \(J\). For every \(x\) in its support, either of \(y=x,\Theta-x\) satisfies
\[
   |\sin((\pi/2)y-\arg\alpha)|\ \ge\ c/\operatorname{Norm}(\alpha)
             \qquad (0\ne\alpha\in\mathbb Z[i]).
\]
The inequality holds for every representative of \(y\), and after conjugation as well. In particular it implies the max-scale lower bound \(c/\max(p,\operatorname{Norm}(\alpha))\) for any positive prime weight \(p\).

*Proof.* A Gaussian direction modulo quarter-turns has a primitive Gaussian representative. For two distinct such directions of representative norms at most \(T\), let \(d\in[0,1/2]\) be their distance on \(\mathbb T\). After multiplying one representative by a unit, the absolute determinant gives \(\sin(\pi d/2)\ge1/T\). Therefore \(d\ge c_0/T\) with \(c_0=2/\pi\). Use the original set of directions and its reflection around \(\Theta/2\), whose centers are \(\Theta-(2/\pi)\arg\alpha\). The separation assertion holds within each family.

Start with a closed interval of length \(u_0=1/8\) and take \(R=32,\ u_n=u_0R^{-n},\ T_n=c_0/(4u_n)\). Given a surviving interval of length \(u_n\), divide it into \(R\) equal children. For each family use the directions with \(T_{n-1}< H\le T_n\), where \(H\) is the primitive norm and the initial shell is \(1\le H\le T_0\). Discard every child meeting the closed \(u_n/R\)-neighborhood of one of these directions. When the parent is enlarged by \(u_n/R\) on each side it has length less than \(4u_n\), the spacing of each family in that shell. At most two centers affect it. Each removes at most four closed children, leaving at least 24. Keep 24, for example the first 24 in interval order, and split the parent's mass equally among them. The infinite nested construction gives a probability measure with cylinder masses \(24^{-n}\); shared endpoints have mass zero. An interval of length between \(u_{n+1}\) and \(u_n\) meets at most three level-\(n\) intervals (split once at the circle origin if necessary), giving the claimed Frostman bound with an absolute constant. For \(n\ge1\), \(1/H<4R u_n/c_0\) on the shell, so \(u_n/R\ge c_0/(4R^2H)\). The initial shell has the same bound since \(u_0/R\ge c_0/(4R^2)\). All support points have at least this distance from the relevant centers. As \(\sin(\pi d/2)\ge d\) on \([0,1/2]\), this proves the claim with \(c=c_0/(4R^2)\). Conjugates and all associates are already in the tested family. Multiples of primitive directions only increase the norm. \(\square\)

**PROVED (lifting all monomial gaps).** Replace each abstract column \(j\le n\) by two copies with distinct rational prime norms \(p_{j1},p_{j2}\ge P\). Give both copies the same exponent column. Suppose a *fixed* vector of coarse quarter-turn angles \(\Theta_j\) already passes both ordinary monomial gaps with column weights \(\log(p_{j1}p_{j2})\) (as well as any required character enhancements). Independently sample \(x_j\sim\mu_{\Theta_j}\) and set \((\theta_{j1},\theta_{j2})=(x_j,\Theta_j-x_j)\), using consistent real lifts so their sum is exactly the chosen coarse lift. Then, if \(nP^{-\beta/2}=o(1)\), with probability \(1-o(1)\) the refined vector passes both monomial gaps at *every* integer vector, and deterministically preserves every mark phase, winding, character phase, and coarse character weight. Both primes in every block pass the stated lattice cross-gap. One may fix the good coarse angles before defining these measures; only a finite product of Borel probability measures depending on fixed parameters is used.

*Proof.* Write \(h=\sum_{j,k}|v_{jk}|\) and \(w=\sum_{j,k}|v_{jk}|\log p_{jk}\). If \(v_{j1}=v_{j2}\) in every block, the phase and weight are exactly their coarse values. This includes every row character. Otherwise fix a block with \(b=v_{j1}-v_{j2}\ne0\) and condition on the other blocks. The tested phase is \(b x_j+\mathrm{const}\) modulo one. The two forbidden intervals have lengths at most \(C_1 e^{-w/2}\) by the arcsine gap formulas for sufficiently large \(P\). The preimage of such an interval is \(|b|\) arcs of length at most \(C_1 e^{-w/2}/|b|\). The total risk for this vector is thus at most \(C_2 |b|^{1-\beta}e^{-\beta w/2}\le C_2 h^{1-\beta}P^{-\beta h/2}\). At length \(h\) there are at most \((4n)^h\) signed words, which overcounts all exponent vectors. Their joint union bound is \(O(\sum_{h\ge1}h^{1-\beta}(4nP^{-\beta/2})^h)=o(1)\). The cross-gap assertion holds with probability one even during conditioning. Elementary finite rational shifts of individual \(\phi_j\) by quarter-turns leave both the monomial target families and this cross-gap invariant. \(\square\)

**PROVED (coarse feasibility check).** For a saturated Paley core as in VIII.3 take weights \(\log(p_{j1}p_{j2})\). Fix valid row offsets and one coarse torus solution passing the forced characters. On its connected realization component, every integral vector outside the real row span has Haar-uniform value (a nontrivial continuous character of a connected torus maps onto the circle). Hence the probability of any ordinary noncharacter gap failure is \(O(\sum_{h\ge1}(2n/P)^h)=o(1)\). The conditional coarse character estimates use the SAME parent primes, sorted by the arguments of the products of the two Gaussian owner factors; their norms are in \([P_0^2,P_0^2 e^{2\sigma}]\). The unweighted pair surplus is 2; hence the weighted surplus is \(2\log(P_0^2)-o(1)\) after a permutation of blocks. Prime norms \(P=q^3\), \(n=q-1\) suffice for the coarse and refined union bounds. This route has no need for the moment digits of Part VIII.
