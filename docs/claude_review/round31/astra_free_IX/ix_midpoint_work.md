### A growth-scale fake in a prescribed rational-direction window

**PROVED.** Fix the Gaussian integer \(b=2+i\), \(\beta=\arg b\). There are \(A_1(2m)\) fakes with \(s=9m-8\), \(W=O(m\log m)\), all \(m\) marks in
\[
 [\beta+m^{5/4}e^{-W/4},\ \beta+(m^{5/4}+1)e^{-W/4}].
\]
In particular the elementary real interval containing all their \(\operatorname{Re}(\bar b z)\) has length \(O(m^{5/4})\). For any genuine integer marks the larger sieve on this projection would give \(m\ll m^{5/6}\), impossible. The unrefined bound by the length of the interval itself gives no such conclusion. This is a restricted theorem on the scale of orientations, without a parent-only consequence. The parent primes of this particular construction can be larger than in the deep window.

*Proof.* Take the fully flipped squarefree Paley profile of round 5, construct.md, Theorem 2, with \(m=q+1\), \(q\equiv3\pmod4\) prime, and eight unflipped copies per label. Its sign matrix \(S\) has \(s=9m-8\) columns. For each row \(i\) two columns differ by \(\pm2e_i\). In particular \(S\) has full row rank. Fix a sufficiently large absolute exponent \(A\) as specified below and use the prime-window lemma there to choose the \(s\) primes in an interval \([P,P e^{1/(4s)}]\), with \(m^A\le P\le2m^A\). Write \(W=\sum\log p\), \(\Delta=e^{-W/4}\). The round-5 hypotheses for \(C=1,Q_0=2m\) hold for all sufficiently large \(m\) if \(A>4\). Let \(\epsilon\) have independent uniform coordinates on \([0,\Delta]\). Steps 1–4 of that proof give probability greater than one half that every zero-sum integral character satisfies its two monomial gaps and its point-residue inequalities for all four units. Indeed all these requirements on \(S\phi=\theta_0\mathbf1+\epsilon\) depend on \(\epsilon\) only. The witnesses are independent of \(\theta_0\) and the winding is zero in this real lift. Set \(\theta_0=\beta+m^{5/4}\Delta\).

We must protect the extra monomials forced when \(\theta_0\) is prescribed. For an integral \(v\ne0\) in the real row span write \(v=S^T c/2\). The vector \(c\) is unique and integral by the twin-column differences. Put \(n=\sum c_i\), \(B=\|c\|_\infty\), \(t=\|c\|_1\le mB\). For each coordinate \(i\), two entries of \(v\) differ by \(\pm c_i\), so \(w(v)\ge B\log P\). The \(n=0\) case is exactly the zero-sum character already treated. For \(n\ne0\), the two-logarithm inequality used in VIII.2 gives absolute \(a>0,K\ge1\) with
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(n\beta/2,(\pi/4)\mathbb Z)\ge a(2+|n|)^{-K}.
\]
Indeed \(e^{4in\beta}=((2+i)/(2-i))^{2n}\) and the base is nontorsion of fixed height. Since \(|e^{iu}-1|\le\operatorname{dist}(u,2\pi\mathbb Z)\), apply the inequality with exponent \(2|n|\), using complex conjugation if needed.

For \(t\le e^{\kappa W}\), where \(\kappa=1/(16(K+1))\), the displacement of \(v\cdot\phi\) from \(n\beta/2\) is at most \((1+m^{5/4})t\Delta/2=o((2+t)^{-K})\), uniformly as \(m\to\infty\). Choosing \(A>8(K+1)\) makes \(2P^{-B/2}\le (a/4)(2+mB)^{-K}\) for all integers \(B\ge1\) and large \(m\), as \(2+mB\le(3m)^B\). Thus the actual distance to \((\pi/4)\mathbb Z\) is greater than \(\arcsin(e^{-w(v)/2})\); this suffices for both gaps. For \(t>e^{\kappa W}\) there are at most \((2B+1)^m\) vectors at each \(B\ge e^{\kappa W}/m\). Condition on every offset except one with \(|c_i|=B\). The phase \(n\theta_0/2+c\cdot\epsilon/2\) is then uniform on an interval of length \(B\Delta/2\), so the bad probability is \(O((1+\Delta^{-1}) P^{-B/2})\) by counting complete and endpoint periods. The sum over \(B\ge e^{\kappa W}/m\) is \(o(1)\). For example on this tail \(W/4+m\log(2B+1)\le B\log P/4\) uniformly, since \(\log(2B+1)/B\) is decreasing and the initial value of \(B\) grows exponentially in \(W\). This is an unconditional probability estimate under the original offset law. It intersects the round-5 event of probability greater than one half. Fix successful offsets.

Choose a real solution of \(S\phi=\theta_0\mathbf1+\epsilon\) and add Haar measure on the image modulo \(2\pi\) of the real kernel of \(S\). Any integral monomial outside the full row span is a surjective circle character of this connected torus and hence uniform. The union bound over these monomials is at most a constant times \(\prod_p(1+2/(\sqrt p-1))-1=o(1)\). The full-span monomials were handled in the chosen real lift and remain fixed on this torus. Distinctness of the marks follows from the pair gaps, or from genericity of the offsets. This proves the fake claim with an explicit moving common phase. Taylor's theorem on that arc gives a projection interval of length \(O(m^{5/4})\), since \(\sqrt R=e^{W/4}\). \(\square\)
