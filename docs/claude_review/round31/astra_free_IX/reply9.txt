WHERE THIS LEAVES THE GROWTH RATE

The person I work for cares about the RATE: any M = o(log R/loglog R), restricted or conditional on something checkable, is progress; the constant 2/3 is not the goal. Putting your two Part VIIIs together, the rate question has a sharper form than before.

(G1) Write s = omega(N'). Since N' is a product of s distinct primes = 1 mod 4 with multiplicity, W >= (1 + o(1)) s log s. So ANY bound M = o(s) gives M = o(log R/loglog R).
(G2) You proved M <= s + 1 + K_C. Your Legendre fakes have M ~ s/2 and satisfy everything in A_{2M}. So inside A_{2M} the ratio M/s is pinned between two constants, and the growth-rate problem is: beat the rank (Plotkin-type) bound by an unbounded factor, using at least one input outside A_{2M}.
(G3) The same thing in Gram form. Put sigma_{p,tau}(i) = +1 if a_ip >= tau, else -1 (1 <= tau <= e_p), and G = sum_p log p sum_tau sigma_{p,tau} sigma_{p,tau}^T. Then G is positive semidefinite of rank <= Omega(N'), G_ii = W, and for i != j, exactly,
      G_ij = W - 2 d_ij = 4 log l_ij - 4 log|b_ij|,   l_ij = |z_i' - z_j'|/N'^{1/4} <= C'.
   So G = W*I + 4(L - B) off the diagonal: an archimedean kernel L (logarithms of normalised chord lengths of M points on an interval of length C') minus an arithmetic kernel B >= (1/2) log 2 (logarithms of the reduced chords). Positivity on the all-ones vector gives mean_{i != j} log|b_ij| <= W/(4(M-1)) + log C'. Hence a lower bound mean log|b_ij| >= f(M) log M with f -> infinity ALSO gives the rate. Theorem 1.1 is of this type with f a constant (collisions at primes <= M, with the chain lemma handling the small primes that divide N'); in every fake of the record the analogue of mean log|b_ij| is O(log M).
(G4) What cannot do it (proved): anything derived from pair instances of (R) at moduli <= Q_0; single linear forms in logarithms; S-unit or Subspace counts with thresholds depending on S only through |S| (they give M <= s at best, which is now your theorem); the fixed-threshold QS. What is not excluded: genuine prime arguments (the association of each prime norm with its actual Gaussian argument), prime-level residues (P3-all*), point residues at moduli M^A with A not fixed in advance, the archimedean order, primes of N' above 2M, the max-scale cross-gap, and the kernel structure in (G3) used beyond the all-ones vector (L is a logarithmic kernel of points on a line, G lies in a cut cone with weights that are logarithms of distinct primes, and b_ij is an actual Gaussian integer determined by the rows and the primes, with the cocycle relation g_ij b_ij + g_jk b_jk + g_ki b_ki = 0).

THE NINTH QUESTION

Prove M = o(omega(N')), or mean log|b_ij| / log M -> infinity, in ANY setting that is not already killed by a fake. In order of value:

(i) Unconditionally in a regime with no known fake. The natural one is the deep window: (2 - o(1)) M log M <= P_+(N') <= M^{23/12}, and first its bottom, P_+(N') <= M^{1+eps} or even P_+(N') <= M (log M)^A. There N' is supported on a fixed explicit set of small Gaussian primes, your fakes do not reach (the digit sup norm E = q^{1.5+a} must fit below P), and (VIII.4) already gives boundedness below (2 - eta) M log M. A restricted theorem "P_+(N') <= M (log M)^A implies M = o(log R/loglog R)" would be the first growth theorem inside the window. If instead you can push the fake down to P = M^{1+eps}, say so; that closes the window as a place to look.

(ii) Conditionally on ONE named genuine property that your own fakes (VIII.3 and the spread version) provably violate: state the property as an axiom, prove that genuine clusters satisfy it, prove M = o(omega(N')) from A_{2M} plus the axiom, and verify that the fake fails it. Even if the axiom is as crude as the max-scale cross-gap, a theorem of this shape would tell us for the first time which genuine input carries the rate. If you find that the axiom you need is P3-all* or the full problem in disguise, say so plainly.

(iii) If you conclude that neither is within reach, give the strongest obstruction you can prove: for example a fake in A_{2M} plus the max-scale cross-gap, or plus residues at moduli M^A with A growing, or a proof that the Gram constraints in (G3), with b_ij merely nonnegative reals satisfying the collision counts, are feasible at W = O(M log M) for every prime set.

A secondary item, only if it comes cheaply on the way: the judge's own first choice was the constant. The bracket for A_{2M} is [3, 23/3]; the analyst referee shows the constant 3 moves only through small split primes dividing N' with balanced levels (W >= 4 M log M - O(M) when all primes of N' exceed 5M/4). Either an unconditional constant above 3, or an A_1 fake at (3 + o(1)) M log M, would close it. Do not spend the session on this.
