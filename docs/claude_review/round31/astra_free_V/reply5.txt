Where this leaves the integer problem. We now have three worlds: C(t): bounded (BMY); F_q(t) with q growing with m, split form: growth-scale clusters exist; Z: open. Two features distinguish your seed from the integer problem and I do not know which one matters: (i) the constant field grows with m, so there is no small residue field (the record's bound W >= 3 m log m - O(m) comes entirely from pigeonhole collisions at primes below m; transferred to F_q[t] with q = m+1 it gives nothing); (ii) the form is split.

So the one question I would like you to attack now:

 THE CIRCLE PROBLEM OVER F_p(t), p FIXED (say p = 3, with i^2 = -1 in F_9, so the norm form is anisotropic over F_3(t); also p = 2 or the split form if they are easier to start with).
 Model: N in F_p[t], points z in F_{p^2}[t] with z zbar = N and deg z = D, cluster condition deg(z_i - z_j) <= D/2 + c. Equivalently, with pi_j the primes of F_{p^2}[t] above the split primes of N, the ratios z_i/z_j = prod (pi_j/pibar_j)^(v_j) must lie in the higher unit group U_k at infinity of the norm-one torus, k about D/2 - c; the quotient T^1(K_inf)/U_k is a finite abelian group of order about p^k, essentially a p-group with (1+u)^p = 1 + u^p. A cluster of size m is m exponent vectors with equal image there. Everything is finite.
 (a) Is sup_D M_p(D,0) infinite? (b) Can growth scale be reached, i.e. clusters of size m on a parent of degree O(m log m)? The pigeonhole bound of the record transfers here (primes of degree < log_p m are few), so there is a real analogue of the constant 3 and of the growth problem. (c) If you can instead PROVE a bound (uniform, or deg N >= omega(m) m log m) over F_p[t] with p fixed, by an argument that uses small residue fields and no cotangent input, say exactly what it uses: by your own filter that argument has a chance of transferring to Z, which nothing else in the record has.
 Why I think this is the right question: a construction in (a) or (b) with an anisotropic norm and fixed p would show that the integer conjecture cannot be proved by anything that also works over F_p[t], which is essentially everything unconditional we have; a proof in (c) would be the first candidate mechanism for Z. Things you may want: linearised/subspace polynomials and Artin-Schreier structure (your seed), Frobenius-stable unions of Galois orbits to descend from F_q to F_p, the p-power map U_j -> U_{pj} with non-squarefree N (exponents up to p), Carlitz-module or Drinfeld-style constructions giving many primes whose "angles" pi/pibar are congruent at infinity, and for (c) the fact that the target group has exponent about k rather than p^k.
 Kill criteria: (i) if every construction needs q to grow with m, prove an obstruction for fixed p or state the precise place it breaks; (ii) if (c) only reproduces the pigeonhole constant, say so and stop; (iii) small exhaustive data are welcome (p = 3, D <= 10 or so) but only in support of the main line.
