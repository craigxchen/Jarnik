# Referee report on round21/fakes.md (low-height window, fake side)

Referee: adversarial pass. Scripts and outputs: `referee_fakes_checks/` (all written from scratch; the author's
code was used only to read the stored angle vectors). Labels: proved / exact / evidence / heuristic.

## 0. Verdict

**The result survives as a partial result, with three overstatements that must be corrected before it enters
the overview.** No fatal error was found. Every numbered proof (Theorem A, Corollaries A.1-A.2, Proposition A',
Proposition B, Theorem C, Corollary C, Lemma T, Corollary T, Lemma LS1, Theorem LS) was re-derived with all
quantifiers and is correct as stated. Every number I recomputed agrees (§7). The corrections concern what the
theorems are said to *imply*:

1. **(major) "The record's constructions provably fail in the window."** Theorem A is an upper bound
   `<= exp(-kappa sum_J p_j^(-1/2))` on the Haar success probability, not a proof that it is zero. Haar
   completion with rejection still yields a fake whenever `nu(G ∩ coset) > 0`, which is exactly scenario P.
   What fails provably is the record's existence proof (the union bound (2.1)), together with any completion that
   needs success probability `>= exp(-o(M^(delta/2)))` when `m >= cM`.
2. **(major) "The large sieve adds nothing / is implied by the pair gaps."** Theorem LS proves two things: the
   cluster-only inequality at the genuine constant `D = 2K + pi X/2`, and the union inequality (cluster angles
   grafted into all split-prime angles) at `2D`. The genuine angle function satisfies the union inequality at
   `D` (Theorem LS(1) applied to genuine angles). At `K <= X^(1/2)`, `D >= (pi/4)(X + K X^(1/2))`, so this is a
   `(c, gamma)`-robust input (R1) of `round10/analytic.md` Def. 3.3 with `c = pi/4`. Overview row 7 says such
   inputs are capped by grafts, but only for grafted primes `>= M^A`, `A > 2` (Thm B). In the window, Theorem LS
   gives a factor 2, not `1 + o(1)`. So **the union large sieve at its genuine constant, and every row-7 (R1)
   input whose constant lies below `2D`, remains uncovered in the window.** The body admits this ("the sharp
   `c < 2` form ... stays unclassified"), but the summary, §6.3(ii) and the proposed overview wording claim more.
3. **(major) "(Q) yes ⇒ window fakes exist and the window is capped."** A non-arithmetic point of
   `G(p) ∩ Real(delta)` for a barrier-scale profile is an angle model. It caps row 1 (`A_0`), and row 3 only if
   the profile also meets the row-3 structure inputs. It does **not** cap:
   * rows 2 and 4, whose residue designs need all primes `> X` (`round5/README.md` scope limits; impossible at
     `X = M^A`, `A >= 2`, in the window);
   * row 7 (item 2 above);
   * Haar-a.e. inputs on `T^r`, which may fail on a whole null coset.

   The correct form is "(Q) yes ⇒ the window is capped **for class `A_0` and the profile-level inputs**."

There are also several minor issues (§10): a false sentence about transplants and the cross-gap, the scope of the
evidence (full torus only, and complete prime segments rather than sparse ones), a non-exhaustive (Q)
dichotomy, and wording.

* (d) The two `n = 1` windows are disjoint because `arcsin 5^(-1/2) + arcsin 10^(-1/2) = pi/4`. Their

* (e) Correct.
* Corollary A.1 is correct: `(kappa/ln 2)^2 = 9.833` (recomputed). For the skew double core, `r = 2(M-1)` and
  rank `M-1` (row 3: fakes have full rank), so `m = M-1`.

## 4. Lemma T / Corollary T

Re-derived; correct. A prime `l | A_v` divides exactly one `n_j`. Inert `l` is excluded by primitivity, and
`l = 2` by odd norms. The `l`-part of `A_v` is a power of one of `pi`, `conj(pi)`. Two small points:

* *Distinctness* is proved for `Z_x = Z_y`. The points on the arc are `eps_x Z_x` with units `eps_x`, so one needs
  `eps_x Z_x != eps_y Z_y`. The same argument works: `A_d` would be a unit times a real number, i.e. on an axis,
  forcing `d = 0`. (minor)
* *Strengthening, not an error.* Coprimality and oddness are used only to prove gap-admissibility. If
  `phi_j = arg beta_j` with `Norm beta_j <= p_j` is gap-admissible and realises the profile, then the `Z_x` are
  still distinct lattice points on the circle of norm `prod n_j^(e_j) <= N`, by the identity
  `Z_x conj Z_y = A_(a_x - a_y) * (positive integer)`. So "arithmetic ⇒ actual cluster" holds for every point
  `arg beta` with `Norm beta_j <= p_j`. This is the class that scenario R must refer to (§8).

Corollary T is correct: `log R' = (1/2) log N' <= W/2`, and the conjecture's `M(C)` is exceeded for large `M`.

## 5. Theorem LS

* *Lemma LS1.* Correct:
  * `4 arcsin((p_i p_j)^(-1/2)) >= 4/X` and `4 arcsin(1/p_j) >= 4/X`;
  * for genuine angles, `pi_i conj(pi_j)` and `pi_j^2` are primitive of odd norm.
* *Theorem LS(1).* The Montgomery–Vaughan/Selberg constant `N - 1 + delta^(-1)` holds in the dual form by duality.
  With `N = 2K + 1` (so `K` must be an integer; unstated) and `delta = 2/(pi X)`, it gives `2K + pi X/2`.
  Correct.
* *Theorem LS(2).* Correct via `|a+b|^2 <= 2|a|^2 + 2|b|^2`. Scope: split primes only. Inert primes (all at
  angle 0, a heavily repeated point) and `1 + i` are not in `Theta^ext`. An "all Gaussian primes" input has to be
  restricted accordingly.
* *Non-triviality.* The trivial bound for the cluster sum is `(2K+1) * 2r * sum|c|^2`, not `(2K+1) r`. The
  conclusion (non-trivial when `r >> P^(1/2)`) is unchanged.
* **What LS does not cover (issue 2 of §0).**
  * Genuine angles satisfy the union inequality at `D`; fakes are proved to satisfy it only at `2D`.
  * A factor-2 loss is not cosmetic. With an exact collision (a cluster angle equal to an external prime's
    angle), coefficients `(a, a)` on the doubled points give `4 sum_k |sum_j a_j e^(ik theta_j)|^2` on the left.
    Bounding this by `D(1 + o(1)) * 2 sum|a_j|^2` requires the cluster's own sum to be `<= (D/2) sum|a_j|^2`.
    That is a concentration statement the gaps do not give.
  * So, in the window, no current argument shows that window fakes satisfy row 7 at the genuine constants.
* **False remark (§4, last bullet).** "It is satisfied by the transplant points of Lemma T." The cross-gap
  bound `|Im(beta_j conj varpi)| >= 1` fails exactly when `beta_j` is associate to `varpi` or `conj varpi`, and
  the write-up states that exception. But the most natural transplants (`beta_j` a Gaussian prime above some
  `l_j <= p_j` with `l_j` not dividing `N`) are exactly such collisions: `varpi_(l_j)` lies in `Theta^ext`. For
  them the cross-gap fails (`sin = 0`). The remark is true only for transplants none of whose `beta_j` is
  associate to an external prime (or its conjugate) of norm `<= X`. (minor; remark only)

## 6. Proposition B, Theorem C, Corollary C, the cascade

* **Proposition B.** Re-derived; correct. The `n = 1` windows at 5 are `(-arctan(1/2), arctan(1/2))`,
  `(arctan(1/2), arctan 2)` and `(arctan 2, pi/2 + arctan(1/2))`. The two endpoints are admissible for all `n`:
  `(2+i)^n` is primitive of odd norm, so `|X|, |Y|, |X ± Y| >= 1`.
* **Theorem C.** Re-derived step by step; correct.
  * *Step 1.* A primitive `z` is `u(1+i)^eps z_0` with `eps` in `{0, 1}`, because `2 | z` is excluded. `S`
    contains every split prime `< p`, so `conj(z_0)` is some `A'_(v')` for **any** choice of conjugation branch.
    The axis gap (`eps = 0`) or the diagonal gap (`eps = 1`) gives `|Im(z e^(-i theta))| >= p^(-1/2)`.
  * *Step 2.* The area is `4(1 + eps)(1 - eps/2) > 4`, and `Norm z < p + 1` for `eps < 1/(3p)`. A primitive `z`
    of norm `p` is an associate of `pi_p` or of `conj(pi_p)`.
  * *Step 3.* `R = p` is impossible since `p` does not divide `p^2 + 1`. `Norm z_± = (R^2 + 1)/p <= p - 2 + 2/p`.
    `cos eta - R sin eta = sqrt(1 + R^2) cos(eta + arctan R)` reaches `-1` at `eta = 2 arctan(1/R)` and is monotone
    before that. And `2 arctan(1/R) > arcsin(1/p)`.
  * *Corollary C.* Correct by induction, with base `S = ∅` (only `z = 1, 1+i`).
* **Independent check (exact up to mpmath at 40 digits; `ref_thmC.py`, `ref_thmC_out.jsonl`).** Step 1 shows that
  the hypotheses of Theorem C imply the **branch-independent** condition `|sin(theta - arg z)| >= (p Norm z)^(-1/2)`
  for all primitive `z` with `Norm z < p`. I checked directly that these open arcs cover `R/(pi/2)Z` except
  **exactly** the two points `arg pi_p` and `pi/2 - arg pi_p`, for **all 180 split primes `p <= 2477`**. There
  were no gaps; the only touching points are the expected ones. This is much stronger than the author's 63-branch
  certificate up to 53. It also shows that the conjugation branches are irrelevant to Theorem C, so the
  63-branch enumeration is redundant (harmless). I also confirmed that all 63 author certificates are flagged
  `certified`.
* **Remark for the author (not an error).** `round8/stability.md` Thm 5.2 says that near the 2/3 bound
  (`A <= 3 + delta`) all but a `delta`-fraction of the split primes `<= M` divide `N'`. So actual clusters at the
  sharpest ratio have **almost complete** initial segments, and a sparse or almost-complete form of Theorem C
  (open point 6.4.1) is exactly what would bite there. At general `A` (which a growth improvement needs) the
  write-up's "sparse" description is correct.

## 8. Evidence sections, and the (Q) dichotomy

* **Scope of the evidence (minor, but it changes the reading).**
  * Every Monte Carlo and sequential computation is on the **full torus `T^r`**, with no profile and no
    realisation coset. (Q) is about cosets, so the "Poisson law" says nothing direct about
    `nu(G ∩ coset)`. Scenario P's formula `exp(-c(Pi_nc - 1))` is an extrapolation of an extrapolation.
  * The sequential runs use `13..73`, `13..97`, `13..109`. These are **complete** segments of split primes
    except 5, not "sparse prime sets like the window's" (summary wording). Only the `97..149`-type rows are
    window-like, and they were not used for the sequential construction.
  * The fit `c ∈ [1.1, 1.8]` rests on 9 points with `Lambda <= 1.03`. Calling it a "law" and extrapolating to
    `Lambda -> infinity` is heuristic, and the write-up does say "the data do not decide".
* **Evidence phrased as fact.** In §5.4, "a strong rigidity statement ... is **false**", and in §5.5 (bold),
  "Points of `G(p)` that are neither Haar-findable nor arithmetic **exist**". Both rest on finite truncations
  (`w <= 33/34`; infinitely many monomials remain). Positivity of `mu(G)` for `{13, 17, 29}` is not covered by
  Proposition A': `kappa_1 Pi_{<2} = 3.89 > sqrt 17 - 1`. Both should carry the label "evidence". The verification
  itself is correct (§7).
* **(Q) dichotomy.**
  * "Arithmetic" must be defined. The implication "only arithmetic points ⇒ actual clusters" holds for
    `phi_j = arg beta_j` with `Norm beta_j <= p_j` (Lemma T and §4 above), and not for "transplant-like" or
    near-arithmetic points.
  * Points of the form `arg beta_j` with `Norm beta_j > p_j`, and rigid (`m = 0`) realisations at rational
    angles, fall on the "yes" side but are not shown to be fakes for anything beyond `A_0`.
  * With issue 3 of §0, the honest dichotomy is:
    * (Q) yes ⇒ window fakes **for `A_0`** (and row 3 if the profile meets it);
    * (Q) no ⇒ window angle models are actual clusters.
  * Neither branch decides row 7 or rows 2 and 4 in the window.
* **"Cannot come from a positive-probability random choice"** (§5.1): a random choice with probability
  `exp(-exp(M^(delta/2)/4)) > 0` is still positive-probability. Also conditional on the extrapolated law. This is
  wording.
* **Grouping heuristic (§2).** The claim that "none of the three explanations is operative" rests, for the
  window structure, on an unproved extraction of `r` disjoint equal-weight blocks and an unproved margin transfer.
  It is labelled heuristic in the ledger but stated flatly in §0 and the summary. It should be labelled there too.
