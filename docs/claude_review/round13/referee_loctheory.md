## 1. Definitions re-derived

These agree with README §1, round12, and loccompute §1.
* `T = 1`, `w = sqrt(8 d ln 4r)`, `j = floor(w/2)`, `m' = ceil(4w)`, so `8j <= m' < 8j + 9`.
* For a sign column `|<k,beta_p>| <= w` iff `|k(S_p)| <= j`.
* `J(X)` is the leading-form ideal and `rho` the interpolation degree.
* Families `F` of integer directions have images `B_F = pi_F(A)` and local ideals `J_F = pi_F^* J(B_F) ⊂ J(A)`.
  `J_F` depends only on the `Q`-span of `F`.

**One point the write-up uses without saying it.** If `G` is a family of directions in the image space of `F`, then
`G∘pi_F` is a family of `A` with image `pi_G(B_F)`. So every sub-projection of an image (along arbitrary, non-column
directions) is a legitimate `Loc` family, with the same `rho`. All my locality tests (§5.3 and §7.1) use this.

Where `pi_s(A) = P_s` holds (Lemma 5.1 and Thm 3.4(ii)), tests on `P_s` transfer verbatim to `A`.

All my computations are in `referee_loctheory_checks/`. `rlib.py` and `echelon.py` are my own library: exact `fmpz`
ranks and nullspaces, and an incremental mod-`p` echelon with `p = 2^31 - 1`. I did not import the author's `lfi.py`.

**Rigour conventions, as in §1.4 of the write-up.**
* Generators of every tested sub-ideal are computed **exactly over `Q`**, so they lie in `J`.
* Target dimensions `dim (J/grid)_D` are **exact**: from exact ranks, or from Thm 3.3/3.4, which I verified.
* So a mod-`p` rank equal to the target certifies equality.
* A mod-`p` rank below the target is **evidence** only. Every non-containment claim marked "exact" uses an exact

## 3. Section 3: full-Venn, parity sections, orbit Hilbert function

**Lemma 3.1: correct.** The hypothesis is: every Venn cell, base point included, has `>= 2j` points. Under it, the
vector `k = sum_l (e_{x_l} - e_{x'_l})` is balanced, has `|k|_1 <= 2j`, and satisfies
`|<k,beta_p>| <= |k|_1 <= 2j <= w`. So it lies in `A`, and its class sums are `t`. The grid statement then follows.

**Lemma 3.2: correct.**
* (a) The weighted alternating sum kills `f (eps f)` when `deg f + deg(eps f) < N - 1`. Since `sum w f^2 > 0` for
  `f != 0`, this gives (a).
* (b) is a dimension count.
* (c) The sign `lambda_j = (-1)^j` is derived from Thm 3.4(i) at `b = 1`. This is **not circular**: the proof of (i)
  never uses the value of `lambda_j`.

**Theorem 3.3: correct.**
* The first part: after grid reduction, `sum_y w eps_s g_0^2` factors over the monomials of `g_0^2`. Some coordinate
  `q in s` has exponent `< N_q - 1`, so its alternating binomial sum vanishes.
* The circuit part: linkage (Thm 2.2(b)) plus the parity count
  `|P_s| = ((2j+1)^c + (-1)^{jc})/2`. This gives `H_{P_s}(jc) = (H_Gamma(jc) + (-1)^{jc})/2 > 0` and
  `H_{Q_s}(jc) = (H_Gamma(jc) - (-1)^{jc})/2 > 0`, because the central coefficient is `>= 2` for `c >= 2`.

**Theorem 3.4: correct.** I re-derived (i)-(iii).
* (i) The `Pi f_e` for `e` in one orbit are `±` one vector. `lambda` restricted to the stabiliser is a character, and
  it is independent of the orbit point, because `C` is abelian and `lambda_{s+t,e} = lambda_{s,e} lambda_{t,s.e}`.
  So `g_O = 0` iff the orbit is killed.
* (iii) The three cases are complete:
  1. non-minimal `e`: a lowering circuit exists, because degree changes add over disjoint circuits;
  2. killed orbits: a killing circuit exists, because signs multiply over disjoint circuits and each circuit inside
     a stabilising codeword stabilises `e`;
  3. minimal elements: they are connected by degree-preserving circuit moves, which gives codimension `<= 1`.

  The leading form of `f_e - lambda f_{s.e}` lies in the ideal generated by `pi_s^* J(P_s)`, by multiplicativity of
  leading forms.

**Independent checks** (my code):
* `t_orbit.py`: the orbit count (i) equals the exactly computed Hilbert function in **16/16** cases. These are 8 codes
  (single circuits of size 2-4, two overlapping circuits, a 3-generator code, codes with weight-2 words) at `j = 1, 2`.
* `t_loc34.py`: (iii) is certified (sandwich) for the code `<{1,2,3},{3,4,5}>` at `j = 1` and `j = 2`, in all degrees.
  Removing the 4-circuit leaves a mod-`p` deficit of `[28,52,16]` in degrees 8-10 at `j = 2`. That is evidence, and it

**Corollary 3.5: correct as stated** (an upper bound: `F` is replaced by its circuits). The last sentence ("not
generated by its own proper sub-families") is also correct, since those images are full grids. **It says nothing
about generation by other families**, in particular by families of non-column directions. See §5.3 below.

**Proposition 3.6, Corollary 3.7: correct.**
* Prop 3.6 takes `e = (j-1,...,j-1)`. This `e` is the unique minimum of its orbit, its stabiliser is trivial, and its
  orbit is not killed.
* Cor 3.7: `(j-1)|F| < m' <= 8j+8` with `|F| = 9` forces `j < 17`. So `|F| <= 8` for `j >= 17`.

**Scope.**

## 5. Section 5: Sylvester, Fano blocks, XOR blocks

### 5.1 Lemma 5.1, Lemma 5.2: correct

**Lemma 5.1.** I re-derived it.
* The inversion `c_z = -2^{1-t} sum_{a != 0} y_a chi_a(z)` gives `|c_z| < 2j <= n/2^t`.
* Hoeffding without replacement is applied per class, with independence across classes. This gives
  `P(|hat k(a')| >= 2j) < 2 e^{-j/2^t}`, and the union bound needs `j > 2^t ln 2n`.
* `2^{t-1} e_a in Lambda_U`, so outside coordinates can be moved into `[-2^{t-2}, 2^{t-2}]`.

The quantifiers are right: `n >= 2^{t+1} j`, `2^t ln(2n) < j`, and `j >= 2^{t-2}` for the second claim.

**Lemma 5.2** is immediate.

### 5.2 The binarity census is wrong at `t = 4` (major)

§5.2 states: "`Lambda_F = Lambda_{C(F)}` in every computed family that contains no complete 3-dimensional block". §0
row 5 and the summary say "a parity lattice unless `F` contains a complete `>= 3`-block (census `t <= 4`)", and §7.3
conjectures "binary iff no complete block". **This is false.**

**Exhaustive census** (`t_binary.py`, Smith normal form of `M_F`, all spanning subsets):

| `t` | binary, block-free | non-binary, with block | **non-binary, block-free** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 4 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | 91 | 1 | 0 |
| 4 | 27476 | 2866 | **890** |

The 890 block-free non-binary families have sizes 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 (counts 15, 105, 315, 420, 35). The smallest is
**`F = AG(3,2)`**, the 8 odd-weight vectors of `F_2^4`. That is 8 columns, `F_2`-rank 4, all circuits of size 4, girth 4,
SNF `[1,1,1,1,2,2,2,4]`, index 32 against `2^{|F|-t} = 16`.

*Hand proof.* For `a` in the odd coset, `#{a in F : a.z = 1}` is `4` for `z ∉ {0, 1111}` and `8` for `z = 1111`. So
`sum_{a in F} y_a = 4 sum_{z != 0,1111} c_z + 8 c_{1111} ≡ 0 (mod 4)` on `Lambda_F`. The parity lattice only imposes
this modulo 2.

The analogous odd-coset families `AG(t-1,2)` carry a congruence modulo `2^{t-2}` with support `2^{t-1}`.

**The author's own output already contains this.** `loctheory_checks/sylvester_lattices_out.txt` line 29 reads
`t=4 b=8: SNF=[1,1,1,1,2,2,2,4] binary=False |C|=15 dmin=4 (#2)`. Girth 4 means there are no Fano lines, hence no
complete 3-block.

**This image is not local in the tested sense** (`t_ag32.py`, toy `j = 1`, exact). For `F = AG(3,2)` at `j = 1`:
* the image `Lambda_F ∩ [-1,1]^8` has 241 points;
* its description (parity on all codewords, plus `sum y ≡ 0 mod 4`) checks exactly;
* `H = [1,8,36,112,84]`.

| degree | `dim (J/grid)` | grid + all 14 four-circuits | + all width powers of `{-1,0,1}^8` directions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | 182 | 105 | 105 |
| 5 | 504 | 392 | 400 |
| 6 | 784 | 742 | 778 |

All three columns are exact `fmpz` ranks. So the circuits fail to generate `J` of this image, in the degree `4j` where
circuits first enter.

**But non-column 7-direction families close the gap** (`t_ag32_proj.py`, certified by sandwich). Adding the
pullbacks along the 7-dimensional projections with kernels `z in {-1,0,1}^8` (exact generators) reaches the exact
targets 182, 504, 784 in degrees 4, 5, 6. Only 4, 2 and 1 families respectively are needed. In degrees `>= 7` the
circuits and width powers already suffice (table above and `t_ag32.py`). So, at toy `j = 1`, the `AG(3,2)` image is
local with `b = 7 < 8`.

At `j = 2` (`t_ag32_j2.py`), full column rank mod `p` of the reduced evaluation matrix in degrees `<= 7` proves
`J(P)_{<= 7} = grid` exactly. So `AG(3,2)` enters only from degree `8 = 4j`, together with its circuits.

Consequences:
* Cor 5.3's hypothesis "binary" excludes a natural size-8 family type that is not a complete block.
* The §6.4 table row "Column families are local only up to complete blocks" is false as stated.
* The §0 bottom line ("the first genuinely non-parity images, complete XOR blocks") is false as stated. Affine
  (odd-coset) blocks `AG(t-1,2)`, i.e. the complement of a hyperplane in `F_2^t` (`sum_F y ≡ 0 mod 2^{t-2}`), are a
  second non-parity type. At `t = 4` they have `|F| = 8` and 16 classes. Their `rho` at regime `j` is not known.

### 5.3 Corollary 5.3: correct as an upper bound; the "essential"/"`b_0 = 8`" part is not proved (major)

**The proof establishes:** under Lemma 5.1, binarity and `j >= 9`, `J_F` in degrees `<= m'` is generated by width
powers and the circuits of size `<= 8`. This upper bound is correct.

**What is claimed beyond it, unproved:**
* (a) "each such circuit is essential among column families";
* (b) "`b_0 = 8` (not `m'/(2j) ~ 4`) is the right count";
* (c) §0: "the README's `m'/(2j) + O(1) ~ 4` is too optimistic by a factor 2".

On (a): the proof only shows that a circuit is not generated by **its own** sub-families. In `A` (columns form a
basis of the linear forms), other circuits sharing columns live in the same ring. I checked that the natural
restriction argument (set the variables outside `s` to zero) does **not** prove essentiality: it maps other circuits'
generators into parity ideals of `s ∩ s'`, which are not contained in `J(P_s)`. Essentiality relative to other
circuits is supported only by computation: the author's `{1..5}`, which I reproduced (`t_loc34.py`, mod `p`).

On (b) and (c): `Loc(b_0)` counts **directions of any kind**, so a lower bound `b_0 >= 8` needs essentiality against
all families of `< 8` integer directions. It fails for small circuits (`t_circuit_noncol.py`; my code, sandwich-certified:
generators exact over `Q`, mod-`p` rank equal to the exact target):

| circuit | `j` | degree `jc` | target `dim (J(P_s)/grid)_{jc}` | generated by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3-circuit (Fano line) | 1 | 3 | 4 | **width powers alone** (4 directions `(1,±1,±1)`) |
| 4-circuit (quadrangle) | 1 | 4 | 9 | **families of 2 non-column directions** (entries in `{-1,0,1}`, all used `rho <= 6`) |
| 4-circuit | 2 | 8 | 42 | **families of 3 non-column directions** (entries in `{-1,0,1}`, all used `rho <= 12 < 8j`) |

Every one of these is rigorous **for the parity sections `P_s` at these `j`**. Whenever `pi_s(A) = P_s`, each
pulled-back family `G∘pi_s` is a family of `A` with image `pi_G(P_s)` and the same `rho`. Caveat: in Sylvester regime
boxes `j >= 9`, so `j = 1, 2` are toy values (Lemma 5.1 does not reach them). These computations therefore refute the
**principle** ("a `c`-circuit cannot be generated by fewer than `c` directions"), which is what the `b_0 = 8` claim
rests on. They do not decide the regime question for `j >= 9`.

**Evidence** in the other direction (mod `p`, finite direction sets):
* Fano line, `j = 2`: 2-direction families reach `8/9` (entries in `[-2,2]`).
* Fano line, `j = 3`: they reach `16/19` (entries in `{-1,0,1}`).
* 4-circuit, `j = 2`: they reach `39/42` (entries in `[-2,2]`).

So a `c`-circuit can be replaced by `c - 1` non-column directions in the tested cases, and possibly not by fewer. What
happens for 5- to 8-circuits at regime-like `j` is unknown.
