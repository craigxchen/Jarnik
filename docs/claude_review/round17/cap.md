# Round 17 — the cap attempt for CIRCLE(q)

Status: final for this round. Unrefereed. Write-up of the round-17 cap attempt on CIRCLE(q) (`round16/README.md` §7).

## 0. Summary and verdict

**CIRCLE(q) is undecided** at every regime `q` (first regime prime `q = 3251`: `j = 257`, `m' = 2057`). No cap was
constructed, and no lower bound was proved. Following `round16/README.md` §7.4, **route 5 stops**. Nothing here moves
the growth rate or the uniform bound: `M <= (2/3 + eps) log R/loglog R` remains the best bound.

What this round settles about the cap attempt (all at `C = 1`, `q = 3251`, with `q = 5479` as a second check):

1. **Scale (proved arithmetic, §1).** For any value of the unproved H_light constant, a cap needs
   `kappa_V(hat u_theta) <= e^{-101 333}`; at the evidence value `c_0 = 0.184` it needs `e^{-481 302}`.
2. **Gauss-sum rewriting (Lemma 2.1, Cor 2.2; proved, exact check `q <= 107`).** `(1 - g) hat k(1) = 2 sum_a zeta^a S_a`
   with `S_a = k({a} u (a+QR))`, `g^2 = -q`. So `l_{u_theta} = sum_a c_a S_a` is a cosine vector of sup norm
   `2/sqrt(q+1)` in slab coordinates. The slab bounds do not constrain `hat k(1)` on `A` at all.
3. **The suggested reduction gives no traction (Lemma 3.1, Prop 3.2, Cor 3.3; proved).**
   * The vanishing of `prod_{|t| <= j}(S_a - t)` touches at most an `e^{-2770}` fraction of the `l^1` mass of the
     expansion of `(sum c_a S_a)^{m'}`.
   * Approximants certified by one relation family alone are limited by explicit mixed-difference certificates:
     `kappa >= e^{-4507}` on the cube (ternary relations only), and `kappa >= e^{-11 312}` on the slab grid (one slab
     family only). These miss the threshold by factors `e^{96 800}` and `e^{90 000}`.
   * A cap must therefore use both families jointly in top degree, which is the full tangent-cone problem.
4. **The exact joint version is a fat-point problem (Prop 3.4, 3.5; proved).** `l_psi^{m'}` lies in the ideal
   generated by the leading forms `k_x^3`, `l_{1_P}^{2j+1}` iff `[psi]` is a base point of the degree-`m'` forms with
   multiplicity `m' - 2` at the `q` coordinate points and multiplicity `m' - 2j` at the `2q` pattern points. That
   ideal's slab part has relative dimension `<= e^{-367}` in degree `m'` (exact integers). Also `H_A(e) > 0` for all
   `e <= q`, so generic directions are not tangent-cone points.
5. **`L^2` form on the circle (Prop 4.1, 4.2; proved).** For `m' < q`:
   * `Psi_A(u_theta) = 4^{-m'} sum_i binom(m',i)^2 N_i` exactly, so `Psi_A` is constant on every circle (not just up
     to `sqrt(m'+1)`).
   * Each `N_i = |top(hat k(1)^i hat k(-1)^{m'-i})|^2` is a **rational number** (Galois plus `AGL(1,q)`).
   * Survival at the `kappa_V` level follows from a lower bound on **one** of them (`ln N_0 > -166 546`). A cap needs
     **all** of them to be tiny.
6. **The other direction is blocked at degree `~ m'/2` (Prop 6.1; proved from the sdc margin).** Every zonotope
   certificate in `A` (sub-cubes, grids and pair certificates included) has degree `< 4j = 1028 < m'/2`. Support
   (Alon-Furedi) and hypercontractive mechanisms fail by large factors (Remark 6.2).
7. **Toy calibration (evidence only, `sdc7` and `sdc11` at `L = 2`).** For `m < q` the circles have the **largest**
   restriction ratio of all tested directions. Nothing near a zero appears.

**Net.** Both directions of CIRCLE(q) reduce to a degree-`m'` statement about the slab box, about twice the degree
that any explicit certificate family reaches (`m'/(2j) = 4.002`, against `< 2` for zonotopes; each
single relation family misses the scale by `e^{90 000}` or more). The record does not have the non-zonotopal tool that this gap needs.

## 2. `hat k(1)` through slab statistics (Gauss-sum identity)

**Lemma 2.1 (proved; exact check `gauss_identity_out.txt`, `q = 7..107`).** Let `g = sum_x chi(x) zeta^x` (so
`g^2 = -q`, `g = +- i sqrt q`). For every `k in Z^q`, in `Z[zeta]`:

```text
(1 - g) hat k(1) = 2 sum_a zeta^a S_a ,        (1 + g) hat k(1) = 2 sum_a zeta^a T_a .                  (2.1)
```

*Proof.* `sum_a zeta^a S_a = sum_a zeta^a (k_a + sum_{y in QR} k_{a+y}) = hat k(1) (1 + sum_{y in QR} zeta^{-y})`, and
`sum_{y in QR} zeta^{-y} = sum_{y in NQR} zeta^y = (-g - 1)/2` because `-1 in NQR` and
`sum_{QR} zeta^y - sum_{NQR} zeta^y = g`, `sum_{QR} + sum_{NQR} = -1`. So `1 + sum_{QR} zeta^{-y} = (1 - g)/2`.
The second identity is the same with `QR` and `NQR` exchanged. `g^2 = chi(-1) q = -q`. QED

**Corollary 2.2 (proved).** With `gamma := 2/(1 - g)` (`|gamma| = 2/sqrt(q+1)`) and
`c_a(theta) := Re(e^{i theta} gamma zeta^a) = |gamma| cos(theta + arg gamma + 2 pi a/q)`:

```text
l_{u_theta}(k) = sum_a c_a(theta) S_a ,    hat k(1)^{m'} = gamma^{m'} ( sum_a zeta^a S_a )^{m'} .          (2.2)
```

So in the slab coordinates `S = (S_a)` (an invertible linear change of variables: `hat S(b) = hat k(b)(1 + G(-b))`
with `|1 + G(-b)| = sqrt(q+1)/2` for `b != 0` and `(q+1)/2` for `b = 0`), the circle direction is again a cosine
vector, of sup-norm `|gamma| = 2/sqrt(q+1)`, and the box is a subset of the grid `{-j..j}^q`:

```text
A  ⊂  W^{-1}( {-j, ..., j}^q ),     W k := S,   W = circulant(1_{{0} u QR}) (rows indexed so that (Wk)_a = k(P'_a)).   (2.3)
```

*Proof.* Take real parts of `e^{i theta} gamma` times the first identity of (2.1). The Fourier factor is
`1 + G(-b) = (1 - chi(b) g)/2` for `b != 0`, of modulus `sqrt(1 + q)/2`, and `1 + (q-1)/2` at `b = 0`. QED

**Reading.** `hat k(1)` is, up to the unit-size factor `gamma sqrt(q+1)/2`, the first Fourier coefficient of the slab
vector divided by `sqrt(q+1)/2`. The slab bound `|S_a| <= j` does **not** constrain `hat k(1)` on `A`: it gives
`|hat k(1)| <= |gamma| q j = 2qj/sqrt(q+1) ~ 2.9 * 10^4` at `q = 3251`, weaker than the trivial `|hat k(1)| <= q`.
So the slab constraints are invisible at the level of values in the circle direction; any cap would have to come
from high-degree relations.

## 3. The suggested cap route: slab reduction, and what it cannot do

The route proposed in `round16/README.md` §7.3(2): write `l_{u_theta}^{m'} = (sum_a c_a S_a)^{m'}` by (2.2), reduce every
monomial divisible by some `S_a^{2j+1}` (or `T_a^{2j+1}`) with (1.1), reduce with `k_x^3 = k_x`, and bound what is
left. This section shows, with explicit constants, that each family of relations **on its own** fails by a factor
`>= e^{90 000}` at `q = 3251`, that the slab reduction touches only an `e^{-2770}` fraction of the expansion, and that
the joint (exact) version is a fat-point base-locus problem whose relevant subspace has relative dimension `<= e^{-367}`.

**Lemma 3.0 (proved; elementary).** For every `theta` and every `1 <= i <= q`, the `i`-th largest of the numbers
`|cos(theta + 2 pi x/q)|`, `x in F_q`, is `>= cos(pi i/(2q))`. The same holds for `|c_a(theta)|/|gamma|`.

*Proof.* Modulo `pi`, the points `theta + 2 pi x/q` are `theta + pi t/q`, `t = (2x mod q)`, i.e. `q` equally spaced points
of `R/piZ` with spacing `pi/q` (`q` odd). `|cos phi| >= cos(beta)` iff `dist(phi, piZ) <= beta`, an arc of length `2 beta`,
which contains at least `floor(2 beta q/pi)` of the points. With `beta = pi i/(2q)` this is `i`. QED

### 3.1 The slab relations act on a negligible part of the expansion

**Lemma 3.1 (proved; numbers in `regime_numbers_out.txt`).** In `(sum_a c_a S_a)^{m'} = sum_{|alpha| = m'} (m'!/alpha!)
c^alpha S^alpha`, the monomials divisible by some `S_a^{2j+1}` carry `l^1` coefficient mass at most

```text
binom(m', 2j+1) * q * ( max_a |c_a| / sum_a |c_a| )^{2j+1}  *  ( sum_a |c_a| )^{m'}
```

and `max |c_a| / sum |c_a| <= 1/sum_{i=1}^q cos(pi i/(2q))`. At `q = 3251` the prefactor is `<= e^{-2770}` for every
`theta` (at `q = 5479`: `e^{-4043}`).

*Proof.* Expand the product of `m'` factors `sum_a c_a S_a`; a term divisible by `S_a^{2j+1}` chooses `S_a` in at least
`2j+1` factors; fixing `a` and a set of `2j+1` such factors bounds the mass of those terms by
`binom(m',2j+1) |c_a|^{2j+1} (sum|c|)^{m' - 2j - 1}`; sum over `a`. Lemma 3.0 gives `sum_a |c_a| >= |gamma|
sum_i cos(pi i/(2q)) = |gamma| * 2069.15` and `max|c_a| <= |gamma|`. QED

So after the slab reduction the top-degree part to be approximated is `R_top = sum_{alpha_a <= 2j} (m'!/alpha!) c^alpha
S^alpha`, which agrees with `l_u^{m'}` up to an `e^{-2770}` fraction of its coefficient mass: **the reduction
re-expresses nothing of substance**, and the problem "approximate `R_top` on `A` by degree `< m'`" is the original
problem. The same holds for the `T_a`-family and for any mixture.

### 3.2 Each family of relations alone fails by a huge factor

Write `kappa^{(X)}(u) := Cheb_X(hat u)/d^{m'}` for a finite set `X` (same normalisation as `kappa_V`). Since
`Cheb_X <= Cheb_Y` for `X ⊂ Y`, an approximant that is valid on a superset `Y ⊃ A` (i.e. whose accuracy is certified
using only relations true on `Y`) is limited by `kappa^{(Y)}`.

**Proposition 3.2 (proved; numbers in `regime_numbers_out.txt`).** For every `theta`:

```text
(cube, ternary relations only)      kappa^{(T)}(u_theta)                      >= m'! 2^{-m'} d^{-m'} prod_{top} |u|^{alpha}
                                                                                 >= e^{-4 507}   (q = 3251),   e^{-6 602} (q = 5479);
(one slab family only, S-grid)      kappa^{(W^{-1}{-j..j}^q)}(u_theta)        >= m'! 2^{-m'} d^{-m'} |gamma|^{m'} prod_{top} |c/gamma|^{alpha}
                                                                                 >= e^{-11 312}  (q = 3251),   e^{-16 418} (q = 5479).
```

*Proof.* Mixed finite differences. For a multi-index `alpha` with `|alpha| = m'` and a product grid `prod_x G_x`
containing, in coordinate `x`, `alpha_x + 1` consecutive integers, the functional
`Lambda_alpha = tensor_x Delta_x^{alpha_x}` (forward differences of step 1 based at the lowest point; `|Lambda_alpha|_1 =
2^{m'}`) kills every polynomial of degree `< m'`, and on forms of degree `m'` it is `F -> partial^alpha F` (a finite
difference of order `alpha_x` kills `k_x^{b}` for `b < alpha_x` and maps `k_x^{alpha_x}` to `alpha_x!`). Hence
`Lambda_alpha(l_v^{m'} - P) = m'! v^alpha` and `Cheb >= m'! |v^alpha| 2^{-m'}`.
* *Cube:* grid `{-1,0,1}` per coordinate, `alpha_x <= 2`. Take `alpha_x = 2` on the `1028` coordinates with largest
  `|u_x|` and `alpha_x = 1` on the next one (`m' = 2057`); Lemma 3.0 bounds the product; `hat u` has `|hat u_x| >= |u_x|`.
* *S-grid:* in the coordinates `S = Wk` (an invertible linear map, so degree `< m'` in `S` = degree `< m'` in `k`) the set
  `W^{-1}{-j..j}^q` is the grid `{-j..j}^q`, `l_u = sum c_a S_a` by (2.2), `alpha_a <= 2j`: take `alpha_a = 514` on the 4
  largest `|c_a|` and `1` on the fifth. (`hat u` normalisation only helps.) QED

**Corollary 3.3 (proved).** At `q = 3251` the cap needs `kappa_V(hat u_theta) <= e^{-101 333}` (§1, for every `c_0`).
Therefore any degree-`< m'` approximant `P` meeting the cap threshold on `A` has
* error `|l_u^{m'} - P| >= e^{-4 507} d^{m'}` at some point of `T \ A` (it is **not** a cube approximant), and
* error `>= e^{-11 312} d^{m'}` at some point of the `S`-grid outside `A` (it is **not** a one-family slab approximant),

i.e. the error function `E = l_u^{m'} - P` must be `e^{-96 800}`-relatively flat on `A` while being of full size on the
removed set `B = T \ A` (whose cube measure lies in `[e^{-33.0}, e^{-24.2}]`: one slab tail, exactly, and the union bound; `slab_tail_out.txt`). Equivalently: the
cap needs an element of degree `m'` of the vanishing ideal of `A`, modulo an `e^{-10^5}`-error, whose top-degree part
is `l_u^{m'}` (mod `k^3`). This is exactly the tangent-cone question, so the suggested reduction gives no traction:
**its two ingredients, used separately, provably fail by factors `>= e^{90 000}` (slab grid) and `>= e^{96 800}` (cube)**, and their joint use is
the full problem.

### 3.3 The exact joint version is a fat-point base-locus problem

Let `J_gen ⊂ C[k]` be the homogeneous ideal generated by the leading forms of the relations used in the route:

```text
J_gen := ( k_x^3 (x in F_q) ,  l_{1_P}^{2j+1} (P in {P_a, P'_a}) )   ⊂   J(A) .
```

(Each generator is the leading form of an element of `I(A)`: `k_x^3 - k_x` and (1.1).) An *exact syntactic cap* is an
identity `l_u^{m'} = sum_x k_x^3 h_x + sum_P l_{1_P}^{2j+1} h_P` in degree `m'`; it would put `u` in `TC_V(A,m')`.

**Proposition 3.4 (proved; apolarity).** Let `Inv_{m'} := { G in C[y]_{m'} : partial_{y_x}^3 G = 0 for all x,
(sum_{x in P} partial_{y_x})^{2j+1} G = 0 for all 2q patterns P }`. For `u in C^q`:

```text
l_u^{m'} in (J_gen)_{m'}    <=>    G(u) = 0 for every G in Inv_{m'} .
```

Geometrically, `Inv_{m'}` is the space of degree-`m'` forms with multiplicity `>= m' - 2` at the `q` coordinate points
`[e_x]` and multiplicity `>= m' - 2j` (`= 1543` at `q = 3251`) at the `2q` pattern points `[1_P]` of `P^{q-1}`; an exact
syntactic cap on the circle means that `[psi]` is a base point of this linear system.

*Proof.* The apolarity pairing `<F, G> := F(partial) G` on `C[k]_{m'} x C[y]_{m'}` is perfect (monomials pair
diagonally with weights `alpha!`). For a generator `f` of degree `e` and `h` of degree `m' - e`,
`<f h, G> = <h, f(partial) G>`, and since the pairing is perfect in degree `m' - e`, `G ⊥ f C[k]_{m'-e}` iff
`f(partial) G = 0`. So `(J_gen)_{m'}^⊥ = Inv_{m'}`, and `(J_gen)_{m'} = Inv_{m'}^⊥`. Finally
`<l_u^{m'}, G> = (sum u_x partial_x)^{m'} G = m'! G(u)` for `G` homogeneous of degree `m'` (Taylor). For the geometric
reading: `G(t w + z) = sum_e t^{m'-e} G_e(z)` with `G_e` the degree-`e` part in `z`, so
`partial_w^{D+1} G = 0` iff `G_e = 0` for `e < m' - D` iff `G` has multiplicity `>= m' - D` at `[w]`. QED

**Proposition 3.5 (proved; exact integers, `regime_numbers_out.txt`).** With `H_T(e) := [t^e](1 + t + t^2)^q`
(the cube's Hilbert function):

```text
dim (J_gen)_{m'} / (k^3)_{m'}  <=  2q H_T(m' - 2j - 1) ,     dim C[k]_{m'}/(k^3)_{m'} = H_T(m') ,
ln( 2q H_T(m' - 2j - 1) / H_T(m') ) = -366.97 (q = 3251),  -657.67 (q = 5479).
```

Moreover `H_A(e) > 0` for every `e <= q`; in particular `J(A)_{m'} ≠ C[k]_{m'}`, so a generic direction is not in
`TC_V(A,m')`, and `Inv_{m'} ⊃ J(A)^⊥_{m'} ≠ 0`.

*Proof.* `(J_gen)_{m'} = (k^3)_{m'} + sum_P l_{1_P}^{2j+1} C[k]_{m'-2j-1}`, and multiplication by `l_{1_P}^{2j+1}` maps
`(k^3)_{m'-2j-1}` into `(k^3)_{m'}`, so modulo `(k^3)` each `P` contributes the image of a space of dimension
`H_T(m'-2j-1)`. For the second claim: `J(T) = (k^3)` (quoted, the cube), `J(T) ⊂ J(A)` so `H_A <= H_T` pointwise, and
`H_A(e) = 0` forces `H_A(e') = 0` for all `e' >= e`. If `H_A(e) = 0` for some `e <= q`, then
`|A| = sum_{e' < e} H_A(e') <= sum_{e' < q} H_T(e') < 3^q/2` (symmetry of the trinomial coefficients), contradicting
`|A| >= 3^q/2` (regime; t1rigour §2, quoted). QED

*Reading (evidence-level, not a proof).* The part of `J(A)` that the route can see, `J_gen`, occupies a fraction
`<= e^{-367}` of the degree-`m'` space modulo the cube relations, and nothing in the `AGL(1,q)` symmetry forces
`l_psi^{m'}` (or its isotypic companions, §4) into it. `J(A)` itself may be larger than `J_gen` (Gröbner closure;
`dim J(A)_{m'}/(k^3)_{m'} = H_T(m') - H_A(m')` is not known). An exact syntactic cap is therefore a non-generic
coincidence, and a quantitative cap at `e^{-10^5}` would be a near-coincidence of the same kind; I found no mechanism
for either.

## 4. The circle in `L^2`: exact isotypic splitting (sharpens barrier Thm 3.2 for `Psi_A`)

Let `Pi` be the orthogonal projection of `C^A` (Hermitian product `<f,g> = (1/U) sum_A f conj g`, `U = |A|`) onto
`Poly_{<m'}|_A`, and for a function `F` on `A` let `top(F) := (I - Pi) F`. For real `u`,
`Psi_A(u) = |top(l_u^{m'})|^2` (record definition).

**Proposition 4.1 (proved; numerically confirmed on toys, `toy_calibration_out.txt`).** Let `m' < q`. Put
`M_i := l_psi^i l_{conj psi}^{m'-i} = hat k(1)^i hat k(-1)^{m'-i}` and `N_i := |top(M_i)|^2`, `0 <= i <= m'`. Then

```text
Psi_A(u_theta) = 4^{-m'} sum_{i=0}^{m'} binom(m', i)^2 N_i        for every theta,        N_i = N_{m'-i} .        (4.1)
```

In particular `Psi_A` is **exactly constant** on the circle `{u_theta}` (before normalisation; after normalisation
`Psi_A(hat u_theta) = |u_theta|_inf^{-2m'} Psi_A(u_theta)` with `|u_theta|_inf in [cos(pi/(2q)), 1]`, a factor
`<= e^{0.0005}` at `q = 3251`). By the `AGL(1,q)`-invariance the same value is taken on every circle `C_a`.

*Proof.* `u_theta = (e^{i theta} psi + e^{-i theta} conj psi)/2`, so `l_{u_theta}^{m'} = 2^{-m'} sum_i binom(m',i)
e^{i theta (2i - m')} M_i`. The translation `(tau k)_x = k_{x-1}` permutes `A` (the pattern set is translation-invariant,
quoted), so `F -> F o tau` is unitary on `C^A`, preserves `Poly_{<m'}|_A`, and commutes with `Pi` and `top`. Since
`l_psi(tau k) = zeta l_psi(k)` and `l_{conj psi}(tau k) = zeta^{-1} l_{conj psi}(k)`, `M_i o tau = zeta^{2i - m'} M_i`, and
so `top(M_i)` is an eigenvector of a unitary operator with eigenvalue `zeta^{2i-m'}`. These eigenvalues are distinct
for `0 <= i <= m'` because `0 < |2(i - i')| <= 2m'` and `q` is an odd prime with `m' < q` (so `q ∤ 2(i-i')`). Eigenvectors
of a unitary operator for distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal, and `|e^{i theta(2i-m')}| = 1`, which gives (4.1).
`M_{m'-i} = conj M_i` and `Pi` is real, so `N_{m'-i} = N_i`. `|u_theta|_inf >= cos(pi/(2q))` by Lemma 3.0 with `i = 1`.
For `C_a`, use the multiplication automorphism of Prop 4.2's proof. QED

**Proposition 4.2 (proved): every `N_i` is a rational number.**

*Proof.* `Pi` has rational entries (it is the orthogonal projection onto the span of the rational vectors
`(k^alpha)_{k in A}`). The value vector `m_i = (M_i(k))_{k in A}` lies in `Q(zeta)^A`, and
`N_i = (1/U) m_i^* (I - Pi) m_i` lies in the real subfield. Let `s in F_q^*` and `sigma_s : zeta -> zeta^s`. The map
`x -> s x` permutes the pattern set: for `s in QR`, `s P_a = {sa} u (sa + NQR) = P_{sa}` and `s P'_a = P'_{sa}`; for
`s in NQR`, `s P_a = P'_{sa}` and `s P'_a = P_{sa}`. Hence `(pi_s k)_x := k_{s^{-1} x}` is a permutation of `A`
(`(pi_s k)(P) = k(s^{-1} P)`) which maps `Poly_{<m'}|_A` to itself, so its permutation matrix `R_s` commutes with `Pi`.
Since `l_psi(pi_s k) = sum_y k_y zeta^{sy} = sigma_s(l_psi(k))` for integral `k` (and likewise for `conj psi`, as
`sigma_s` commutes with complex conjugation on the abelian field `Q(zeta)`), `sigma_s(m_i) = R_s m_i`, so
`sigma_s(N_i) = (1/U) m_i^* R_s^T (I - Pi) R_s m_i = N_i`. Thus `N_i` is fixed by `Gal(Q(zeta)/Q)` and is rational. QED

**Consequences for CIRCLE(q) (proved, from (4.1) and record Thm 2.1(2): `Psi_A <= Cheb_A^2 <= U Psi_A`).** Put
`Y := d^{2m'} T*^2`, and use `Psi_A(u_theta) <= Psi_A(hat u_theta)` (`|u_theta|_inf <= 1`, homogeneity):
* **cap ("yes") needs all isotypic components small:** `kappa_V(hat u_theta) <= T*` implies
  `Psi_A(u_theta) <= Cheb_A(hat u_theta)^2 <= Y`, hence `N_i <= 4^{m'} binom(m',i)^{-2} Y` for **every** `i`; in
  particular the single power `hat k(1)^{m'}` must have `|top(hat k(1)^{m'})|^2 <= 4^{m'} Y`. Conversely, if
  `sum_i binom(m',i)^2 N_i <= 4^{m'} Y e^{-0.001}/U`, then `Cheb_A(hat u_theta)^2 <= U Psi_A(hat u_theta) <= Y`: a cap.
* **survival ("no", at the `kappa_V` level) needs one component large:** if for some `i`,
  `N_i > 4^{m'} binom(m',i)^{-2} Y`, then `Cheb_A(hat u_theta)^2 >= Psi_A(hat u_theta) >= Psi_A(u_theta) > Y`, i.e.
  `kappa_V(hat u_theta) > T*` for all `theta`.

So, at the `kappa_V` level, CIRCLE(q) is decided by the `L^2` sizes of the top components of the `m' + 1` functions
`hat k(1)^i hat k(-1)^{m'-i}` on `A`, which are rational numbers; "no" follows from a lower bound for **one** of them
(e.g. `i = 0`, the pure power `hat k(-1)^{m'}`) at the scale `ln N_0 > 2 ln T* + 2m' ln d + m' ln 4`, i.e.
`ln N_0 > -166 546` at `q = 3251` (`c_0`-free floor `tau = 62.3`). For comparison, on the uncut cube
`ln N_0^{(T)} >= ln( (m'!)^2 (2/3)^{m'} ) > 26 400` (the cube's top component is `sum_{alpha <= 2} (m'!/alpha!) conj(psi)^alpha h_alpha`
with orthogonal `h_alpha`, `|h_1|^2 = 2/3`, `|h_2|^2 = 2/9`, reduction Thm 5.1; keep one `alpha in {0,1}^q`, `|psi_x| = 1`).
The cap needs `A` to destroy all but an `e^{-193 000}` fraction of this; survival needs it to keep an `e^{-193 000}` fraction.

**Remark 4.3 (rationality gives no usable Liouville bound).** `N_i > 0` implies `N_i >= 1/(U D)` with `D` a denominator
of `I - Pi`, but `D` is bounded only by the Gram determinant of `Poly_{<m'}|_A`, of size `exp(exp(~3 * 10^3))`. This is
far worse than the referee's `e^{-Theta(q^2)}` (which itself is too weak). So an exact "circle not in `TC_V`" (all or some
`N_i > 0`) remains quantitatively useless, as round 16 said.

## 6. The other direction: why no lower bound for `kappa_V^{(A)}(u_theta)` is available

**Proposition 6.1 (proved; uses the refereed sdc margin).** Let `v_1, ..., v_m in Z^q \ {0}` and `k_0 in Z^q` with
`k_0 + sum_{i in I} v_i in A` for every `I ⊂ [m]` (a zonotope certificate `Lambda = Delta_{v_1} ... Delta_{v_m}` at `k_0`;
sub-cubes, product grids and pair certificates are special cases). Then `m <= 4jq/(q+1) < 4j`. At `q = 3251`:
`m <= 1027 < m'/2`.

*Proof.* For each pattern `P` the values `k_0(P) + sum_{i in I} v_i(P)` lie in `[-j, j]`; their range is
`sum_i |v_i(P)|`, so `sum_i |v_i(P)| <= 2j`. Summing over the `2q` patterns, `sum_i sum_P |v_i(P)| <= 4jq`. For a nonzero
integral `v`, put `lambda = (v, -sum_x v_x) in Z^n` (base coordinate last); then `lambda(P) = v(P)` for the relative
patterns, and the sdc margin `sigma_lambda = sum_P (|lambda(P)| - 1/2) >= ||lambda||_1/2` for integral `lambda != 0`
(exact.md Thm 4.1 / fake.md §1, quoted) gives `sum_P |v(P)| >= q + ||lambda||_1/2 >= q + 1`. Hence `m(q+1) <= 4jq`. QED

So zonotopal certificates — the only kind with explicit values in the record — reach degree about `m'/2`; with
fewer than `m'` generators they do not annihilate `Poly_{<m'}|_A` and so are not elements of `V(A, m')` at all. This is the sdc instance of the record's Prop 5.7 /
"non-zonotopal certificates are missing". In the language of §3.3: a zonotope certificate gives
`Phi_c = m'! prod <v_i, y>`, a product of linear forms in `J(A)^⊥`, and each factor uses `>= q + 1` units of total pattern range
(`sum_P |v(P)|`) out of a budget of `4jq`; the forms needed at degree `m'` must use the budget twice as efficiently, which
`H_A(m') > 0` (Prop 3.5) shows is possible for generic directions but not how.

**Remark 6.2 (two further lower-bound mechanisms that fail; proved negative statements).**
1. *Support / uncertainty (Alon-Furedi type [M]).* A nonzero function on `T` of degree `<= e` has support
   `>= 3^{q - e/2}` [M]. So no element of degree `<= m'` of the vanishing ideal of `A` exists if `|T \ A| < 3^{q - m'/2}`.
   Here `|T \ A| >= e^{-33.0} 3^q > 3^{q - m'/2}`, and indeed the slab polynomials `Q_a` (degree `515 < m'`) vanish on
   `A`, so the mechanism is void. On sub-boxes `A n {supp k ⊂ X}` (where the same argument would need the Hoeffding
   tail `2q * 2 e^{-j^2/(2|P n X|)}` to be `< 3^{-m'/2}`) it needs `j^2/(2|P n X|) > (m'/2) ln 3 + ln 4q`, i.e. about
   `64 > 1140` at `|X| = m'/2`, `|P n X| ~ |X|/2` — false by a factor about 18.
2. *Hypercontractive restriction (thin cuts, reduction §5.4).* Needs `mu(T \ A) <= 3^{-3m'}/4 = e^{-6781}`, while
   `mu(T \ A) >= e^{-33.0}` (exact). *Heuristic [M]:* under `p`-biased measures the slab tail improves to about
   `(e |P| p/j)^j = (17.2 p)^j`, but the `(2,4)`-hypercontractive constant costs about `p^{-Theta(m')}` with
   `m' ~ 8j`, so no `p` helps.

**What a "no" proof would need (target statement, from Prop 4.1).** A certificate `c in V(A, m')` (for `kappa_H`:
integral with `|c|_inf <= H' = e^{m'W/40}`, `ln H' ~ 10^8`, so height is not the issue) in
the translation-isotypic component that pairs only with `M_{m'} = hat k(1)^{m'}` (so that
`Phi_c(u_theta) = 2^{-m'} e^{i m' theta} Phi_c(psi)`; then `Re c`, `Im c` are real certificates and
`kappa_V(hat u_theta) >= |Phi_c(u_theta)|/(2 |c|_1 d^{m'})`) with
`|Phi_c(psi)| / |c|_1 > 2^{m'+1} d^{m'} T*`, i.e. a single non-zonotopal dual certificate for the pure power
`hat k(1)^{m'}` with value `> e^{-83 272}` per unit `l^1` mass (`q = 3251`, `c_0`-free floor). The room is enormous
(the cube admits `e^{+13 640 - 1 426}` per unit mass), but no construction of degree `> 4j` is known on any regime box.

## 8. Open points

1. **CIRCLE(q) itself (undecided).** By Prop 4.1 it is decided, at the `kappa_V` level, by the rational numbers
   `N_i = |top(hat k(1)^i hat k(-1)^{m'-i})|^2_{L^2(mu_A)}`. "No" needs one lower bound `ln N_0 > -166 546`
   (`q = 3251`); "yes" needs all `N_i <= 4^{m'} binom(m',i)^{-2} d^{2m'} T*^2`. Neither is in reach: "no" needs a
   non-zonotopal certificate of degree `m' > 4j` (Prop 6.1), "yes" needs `l_psi^{m'}` within `e^{-10^5}` of `J(A)_{m'}`,
   whose visible part `J_gen` has relative dimension `<= e^{-367}` (Prop 3.5).
2. **Exact syntactic cap (cleaner sub-question).** Is `[psi]` a base point of the fat-point system of Prop 3.4
   (multiplicity `m' - 2` at the `q` coordinate points, `m' - 2j` at the `2q` pattern points, degree `m'`, in
   `P^{q-1}`)? A "no" (one explicit `G`) would exclude every cap that uses only the relations (1.1) and `k^3 = k`
   exactly; it is weaker than survival, since `J(A)` may exceed `J_gen`. Products of linear forms cannot be such `G` when
   the factors are integral (Prop 6.1 counts the same budget); real factors and non-product forms are open.
3. **H_light** remains unproved (it is needed only for the "yes" direction to cap).
4. **`kappa_H` vs `kappa_V`.** Everything here is at the `kappa_V` level, which is exactly what the cap needs
   (`kappa_H <= kappa_V`). A survival proof would in addition need its certificate in `V_{H'}`; with `ln H' ~ 10^8` this
   is not the obstacle.

**Verdict for the program.** Per `round16/README.md` §7.4, "undecided after one round" means **route 5 stops**: its
residual is the barrier question, and on its most symmetric, most favourable test case (the `sdc_q` Fourier circle)
there is no method in either direction. The obstruction is the same in both directions and is now quantified at
`q = 3251`: every relation family and every certificate family with explicit constants reaches degree at most
`~ m'/2` in the circle direction (zonotopes `< 4j`, Prop 6.1; the slab reduction touches `e^{-2770}` of the
expansion, Lemma 3.1), while the question lives at degree `m' ~ 8j`.
