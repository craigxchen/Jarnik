# Round 9: L1/L2 for actual clusters

*Unrefereed.* Labels: **proved** (all quantifiers, for every normalised actual cluster unless stated),
**model**, **heuristic**, **evidence** (finite computation no proof uses). Every statement says
whether its inputs hold for actual circles.

## 0. Summary and verdict

**No growth improvement, and neither L1 nor L2 is proved or refuted.** What is proved is the exact
form of both lemmas for actual clusters. It shows that, on near-extremal actual clusters, L1 and L2
are each other's negation. It also locates what each one needs.

1. **The reduced chord is a rational integer (Theorem 1.1, proved).** `beta_xy = i^(k_x) gamma_s B_xy`
   with `gamma_s in {2i, 1-i, 2, 1+i}` fixed by the unit difference, and
   `B_xy = D_s(A_(a_x - a_y)) in Z \ {0}`. Unit-1 collisions at odd `q` not dividing `N` are exactly
   the prime-power divisors of `B_xy`, and `mu_xy = 2 log|B_xy| + log|gamma_s|^2 - 2 log t_xy`
   exactly (1.1). Window collisions are agreements of a **genuine linear code**
   `w_x(q) = <a_x, ell(q)> + k_x u(q)` built from discrete logarithms of `pi_j/conj pi_j` in the norm-one
   torus mod `q` (Prop 3.1).
2. **L1 is a polynomial size bound (Theorem 2.1, proved).** On near-extremal actual clusters, L1 holds
   with some fixed `K` if and only if, for some fixed `b`, every pair has `|B_xy| <= M^b` and
   `t_xy >= M^(-b)`. Equivalently, every pair gcd has `Norm g_xy >= N^(1/2) M^(-O(1))`.
3. **L1 excludes L2 (Theorem 3.2, proved).** On a near-extremal `K`-uniform actual cluster, every
   `B_xy` has fewer than `K c_0/(4(1-eta)) + o(1)` prime factors in the window. So L2 fails for large
   `M`.
4. **Theorem C for actual clusters needs no window (Theorem 3.3, proved).** `K`-uniform margins
   together with one pair with `|B_xy|/t_xy >= M^f` give `W >= (2(M-1)/K)(2f log M + log 2)`. This
   recovers the conclusion of `p3lower.md` Theorem C without its hypotheses `X >= M`, `lambda_Q`
   and `H`.
5. **Route 1 restated (Corollary 3.4).** For actual clusters, route 1 is exactly two statements
   about near-extremal clusters:
   * (R1-arch): every reduced chord and inverse chord is `M^(O(1))`;
   * (R1-arith): some reduced chord or inverse chord is super-polynomial.

   Together they are the growth statement itself, so the route does not weaken the problem; it
   only splits it into an archimedean half and an arithmetic half. L2 is one mechanism for
   (R1-arith). Neither half can be tested on actual data.
6. **Barriers and limits (proved unless labelled).**
   * *Markov uniformity is automatic and gives nothing (Prop 2.3).* Uniformity must hold at one
     specific rare pair. In the Poisson model that pair belongs to a set of `M^(eps/4 + o(1))` pairs.
   * *Ceiling for the alternative form (Prop 2.2).* Near-extremal actual clusters have
     `lambda_max(D) <= (1/2) sqrt(c_0(c_0-2)) M^(3/2) log M (1 + o(1))`.
   * *Identities on at most four points reduce to known data (Props 5.1, 5.3, with
     `growth/ptolemy.md`).* The cocycle, triangle-area, position and 4-point (Ptolemy/Plücker)
     identities reduce to the profile, the integers `B_xy` and the residue partitions. On all `M`
     points they are equivalent to the cluster itself.
   * *Exact relations exist (Prop 5.2).* Near-extremal clusters satisfy exact linear relations
     `sum c_x z_x = 0` with `|c| <= M^(c_0/2 + 1/2 + o(1))`. Their content is lattice-ness. A cut
     constraint for them is Lemma 5.3.
   * *Integrality gives only lower bounds (§5.1).* Integrality and divisibility of the `B_xy` give
     only **lower** bounds on `|B_xy|`, while L1 is an **upper** bound on every `|B_xy|`.
   * *Heuristic.* L2 cannot follow from genuineness of the labels plus profile structure: rows
     adapted to the labels avoid heavy pairs. It must use the short-arc condition, at moduli
     `exp(Theta(log^2 M/loglog M))` (Prop 3.5).
7. **New pair-level upper bound (Lemma 1.3, proved, actual only).** Every pair has
   `M <= sqrt2 C^2 |g_xy| + 2`: the bisector of two points is a lattice direction of height
   `<= sqrt2 |g_xy|`, and `outside.md` Prop C.2 applies. So `mu_xy <= W/2 - 2 log M + O(log C)`, and
   complementary pairs (`mu = W/2`) occur only when `M <= sqrt2 C^2 + 2`. This is the only known
   upper bound on an individual margin. It is far below what L1 needs.
8. **Data (evidence).** The scan covered 22,907 normalised clusters (`M = 4, 5`, `C <= 4`, split-only
   `N <= 10^9` with `tau(N) >= 16`), plus the 9- and 7-point records and the golden and Pell families.
   * Theorem 1.1 holds exactly, and (1.1) to `2e-15`.
   * The best small clusters are not uniform: `K = 5`-13.
   * In every family and in the 9-point record the maximal margin is exactly `W/2`, from a
     complementary pair. That mechanism is excluded at large `M` by Lemma 1.3.

**Status: partial.** The results are reformulations, small barriers and evidence. The growth question
is untouched.

## 1. Setup and exact identities for actual clusters

**Conventions.** As in `round7/README.md` and `round8/stability.md`. `z_1, ..., z_M` are distinct
lattice points on `x^2 + y^2 = N = R^2`, on an arc of length `<= C sqrt R`. We always normalise
(`two_thirds` Lemma 1.1, `stability.md` §1): `G = gcd(z_1, ..., z_M)`, `u_x = z_x/G`, `N' = N/|G|^2`,
`W = log N'` (we drop the prime of `stability.md`), `C' = C|G|^(-1/2) <= C`. Then `N'` is odd, every
prime factor of `N'` is `= 1 mod 4`, and

```text
u_x = i^(k_x) prod_j pi_j^(a_xj) conj(pi_j)^(e_j - a_xj),     min_x a_xj = 0,  max_x a_xj = e_j,
```

with fixed Gaussian primes `pi_j`, `Norm pi_j = p_j`. Below `z_x` means `u_x`, `N` means `N'` and `C`
means `C'`; nothing is lost because all pair data (`g_xy/G`, `beta_xy`, margins) are unchanged.

* `g_xy = gcd(z_x, z_y)`, `d_xy = sum_j |a_xj - a_yj| log p_j`, so `Norm g_xy = N e^(-d_xy)`.
* `mu_xy = d_xy - W/2` (pair margin), `mubar = binom(M,2)^(-1) sum_(x<y) mu_xy`.
* `beta_xy = (z_x - z_y)/g_xy` (reduced chord), `t_xy = |z_x - z_y| N^(-1/4)` (normalised chord).
  Since the chord is at most the arc, `0 < t_xy <= C`.
* `m_xy` = the unit-1 pair collision modulus at odd primes `q <= X`, `q` not dividing `N`, i.e. the
  largest `m = prod q^(a_q)` with `z_x = z_y mod q^(a_q)` for each such `q`; `D_xy = 2 log m_xy`.
* *Near-extremal*: `W <= c_0 M log M` with `c_0` fixed. *Window*: `Q_eta = {q prime : M^(1-eta) < q <= M,
  q odd, q not dividing N}`.

Every statement of this section is **proved** and holds for **every normalised actual cluster** (no
hypothesis on `M`, `C` or `W` unless stated). None of them holds for fake circles in the form stated,
since they use that `beta_xy` is a Gaussian integer.

**Theorem 1.1 (the reduced chord is a rational integer times a fixed small factor).** For `x != y`
put `v = a_x - a_y` and

```text
A_v = prod_(v_j > 0) pi_j^(v_j) prod_(v_j < 0) conj(pi_j)^(-v_j) = X + iY,      s = k_y - k_x mod 4.
```

Then, for the representative `g_xy = prod_j pi_j^(min(a_xj, a_yj)) conj(pi_j)^(min(e_j - a_xj, e_j - a_yj))`
of the gcd, `z_x/g_xy = i^(k_x) A_v`, `z_y/g_xy = i^(k_y) conj(A_v)`, and

```text
beta_xy = i^(k_x) gamma_s B_xy,     B_xy = D_s(A_v) in Z \ {0},
(gamma_s, D_s) = (2i, Y), (1-i, X-Y), (2, X), (1+i, X+Y)   for s = 0, 1, 2, 3.
```

In particular `|beta_xy|^2 = nu_s B_xy^2` with `nu_s = |gamma_s|^2 in {4, 2}`. For every odd prime
`q` not dividing `N` and every `a >= 1`:

```text
z_x = z_y mod q^a   <=>   q^a | B_xy .
```

Hence `m_xy` is the part of `|B_xy|` supported on odd primes `q <= X` not dividing `N`, and the
number of unit-1 window collisions of `(x, y)` is `c_xy = #{q in Q_eta : q | B_xy}`.

*Proof.* For each `j`, `min(a_xj, a_yj)` copies of `pi_j` and `min(e_j - a_xj, e_j - a_yj)` copies of
`conj pi_j` divide both points, and no more: `pi_j` and `conj pi_j` are non-associate primes since
`p_j` is odd, and the remaining factors `pi_j^(a_xj - min)` and `conj(pi_j)^(...)` of `z_x` and `z_y`
are coprime column by column. So the gcd is, up to a unit, `g_xy = prod_j pi_j^(min(a_xj, a_yj))
conj(pi_j)^(min(e_j - a_xj, e_j - a_yj))`; we take this representative (another one changes
`beta_xy` and `B_xy` by a unit, resp. a sign, and nothing below depends on it). Dividing column by
column, `z_x/g_xy = i^(k_x) A_v` and `z_y/g_xy = i^(k_y) prod_(v_j > 0) conj(pi_j)^(v_j)
prod_(v_j < 0) pi_j^(-v_j) = i^(k_y) conj(A_v)`. Then `beta = i^(k_x)(A - i^s conj A)`, and for
`A = X + iY` the four cases are `A - conj A = 2iY`, `A - i conj A = (X - Y)(1 - i)`,
`A + conj A = 2X`, `A + i conj A = (X + Y)(1 + i)`. `B != 0` because `z_x != z_y`.
For the congruence: `Norm g_xy` divides `N`, so a rational prime `q` not dividing `N` is coprime to
`g_xy` in `Z[i]`. Hence `q^a | z_x - z_y = g_xy beta_xy` iff `q^a | beta_xy`. Since `q` is odd it is
coprime to `gamma_s` (a divisor of 2), so this is `q^a | B_xy` (as Gaussian integers, equivalently as
rational integers since `B` is rational). QED

`glib.reduced_chord_data` (in `L1L2_checks/`) asserts `beta = gamma_s B` with `B` a nonzero rational
integer, in exact integer arithmetic, for every pair of every cluster of §4 (`stats.py`, `known.py`).

*Remarks.*
1. The four integers `D_s(A_v)` are the `D_0, ..., D_3 = Y, X - Y, X, X + Y` of `round7/p3G.md`;
   Theorem 1.1 says that the reduced chord of a pair is the `D_s` selected by the unit difference
   `k_y - k_x`, and that the pair's collision moduli are read off this one rational integer.
2. The `Im(z_x conj z_y)` description of `p3lower.md` captures units `+-1`; the integer `B_xy` is
   the unit-1 object (`round7/README.md` §1.1 item 4, last bullet).

**Corollary 1.2 (exact margin identity in integer form).** For every pair,

```text
mu_xy = 2 log|B_xy| + log nu_s - 2 log t_xy,                                          (1.1)
mu_xy = D_xy + 2 log(|B_xy|/m_xy) + log nu_s - 2 log t_xy  >=  D_xy + log 2 - 2 log C. (1.2)
```

*Proof.* `|z_x - z_y|^2 = Norm(g_xy) |beta_xy|^2 = N e^(-d_xy) nu_s B_xy^2`; take logarithms and
subtract `W/2 = log N^(1/2)`: `2 log t_xy = log(nu_s B^2) - d_xy + W/2`. For (1.2), `m_xy | B_xy`,
`nu_s >= 2`, `t_xy <= C`. QED

(1.1) is `stability.md` Prop 4.1(a) (`m_xy = 2 log|beta_xy| - 2 log C_xy`) with `beta` replaced by
the rational integer `B`; (1.2) is the pair inequality `p3lower.md` Lemma 1.3 with the cofactor
`|B|/m` kept, and with `c_C = 2 log C - log 2`.

**Lemma 1.3 (bisector lemma: every pair gcd is at least of size `M`).** For every cluster of `M >= 3`
points (normalised or not) on an arc of length `<= C sqrt R`, and every pair `x != y`,

```text
M  <=  sqrt2 C^2 |g_xy| + 2 .
```

In normalised margin form: `mu_xy <= W/2 - 2 log((M - 2)/(sqrt2 C^2))`. In particular a
*complementary* pair (`z_y = unit * conj z_x`, i.e. `g_xy` a unit, `mu_xy = W/2`, the largest possible
margin) can occur only if `M <= sqrt2 C^2 + 2`.

*Proof.* Write `z_x = g alpha`, `z_y = g eps conj(alpha)` with a unit `eps` (Theorem 1.1, valid for
the normalised points; for general points apply it to `u_x, u_y` and multiply by `G`). Then
`z_x z_y = eps g^2 Norm(alpha)`, so `theta_x + theta_y = 2 arg g + arg eps mod 2 pi` and the bisector
angle `(theta_x + theta_y)/2` equals `arg g + (arg eps)/2 mod pi`. Since `(arg eps)/2 in {0, pi/4,
pi/2, 3pi/4}`, the bisector line is spanned by the lattice vector `v = g` (if `eps = +-1`), `v = ig`,
or `v = g(1+i)`, `g(1-i)` (if `eps = +-i`); in all cases `|v| <= sqrt2 |g|`. The bisector angle lies
between `theta_x` and `theta_y`, hence in the arc. Let `v_0` be the primitive vector on this line,
`|v_0| <= |v|`. By `round4/outside.md` Prop C.2 (case `eta = 0`), the arc contains at most
`|v_0| L^2/R + 2 <= |v_0| C^2 + 2` lattice points (`L <= C sqrt R`). For the normalised form,
`|g_xy|^2 = N e^(-d_xy) = N^(1/2) e^(-mu_xy)`, so `M - 2 <= sqrt2 C^2 N^(1/4) e^(-mu_xy/2)`; take
logarithms and use `2 log N^(1/4) = W/2`. QED

*Remarks.*
1. Lemma 1.3 is the only pair-level **upper** bound on margins that I know for actual clusters. It
   uses the lattice (lines `Re(z conj v) = k`), so it is not available to fake circles. It is weak:
   it caps `mu_xy` at `W/2 - 2 log M + O(log C)`, whereas L1 needs `mu_xy <= K mubar = O(log M)` for
   near-extremal clusters.
2. It explains the data of §4: every record cluster and every member of the golden and Pell families
   has a complementary pair (`max mu = W/2` exactly). These clusters sit on a symmetry line of `Z[i]`,
   and Lemma 1.3 shows that this mechanism is confined to `M <= sqrt2 C^2 + 2`.
3. Check on the 22,907 clusters of §4: no violation (`stats.py`, field "lemma1.3 failures";
   floating-point check of an inequality that is proved, so evidence only of correct implementation).

## 2. Results on L1 (uniformity of pair margins)

All statements in this section are **proved** for **every normalised actual cluster**. The inputs are
§1 and `stability.md` Prop 4.1 (refereed, unconditional), whose part (d) I quote:

```text
(F)   (M/(M-1)) (log M - 7.9252) + log 2 - 2 log^+ C  <  mubar  <=  W/(2(M-1)).
```

**Theorem 2.1 (for actual clusters, L1 is a polynomial size bound on all reduced chords and gaps).**
Let `K >= 1`.

(a) If `max_(x<y) mu_xy <= K mubar` and `W <= c_0 M log M`, then for every pair, with
`b := K c_0 M/(4(M-1))`,

```text
1 <= |B_xy| <= (C/sqrt2) M^b,          sqrt2 M^(-b) <= t_xy <= C .
```

(b) Conversely, if `|B_xy| <= M^b` and `t_xy >= M^(-a)` for every pair (any `a, b >= 0`, no assumption
on `W`), then `max mu <= 2(a+b) log M + log 4`, so for `M >= M_0(C)` the margins are `K`-uniform with

```text
K  <=  (2(a+b) log M + log 4) / ((M/(M-1))(log M - 7.9252) + log 2 - 2 log^+ C)  =  2(a+b) + O_C(1/log M).
```

*Proof.* (a) By (F), `mu_xy <= K mubar <= K W/(2(M-1)) <= 2b log M`. By (1.1), `2 log|B| = mu - log nu_s
+ 2 log t <= mu - log 2 + 2 log C`, which gives the upper bound on `|B|`; `|B| >= 1` since `B` is a
nonzero integer. Again by (1.1), `2 log t = log nu_s + 2 log|B| - mu >= log 2 - mu`, which gives the
lower bound on `t`. (b) By (1.1) with `nu_s <= 4`: `mu <= 2 log|B| + log 4 - 2 log t`. Divide by the
lower bound (F) for `mubar`, which is positive for `M >= M_0(C)`. QED

*Reading.* For near-extremal actual clusters, L1 says exactly: every pair Gaussian integer `A_v`
(`v = a_x - a_y`) lies within angle `M^(O(1))/|A_v|` of the axis or diagonal selected by the unit
difference, and no two points are closer than `M^(-O(1)) N^(1/4)`. Equivalently (since
`log Norm g_xy = W/2 - mu_xy`): **every pair gcd has norm at least `N^(1/2) M^(-O(1))`**. Lemma 1.3
gives only `Norm g_xy >= (M-2)^2/(2C^4)`. The gap between `M^2` and `N^(1/2) M^(-O(1)) =
M^((c_0/2) M - O(1))` is the whole content of L1.

**Proposition 2.2 (point totals: the alternative form of L1 has an a-priori ceiling).** For every
member of `A_X` (in particular every actual cluster) and every point `x`,

```text
D_x := sum_(y != x) D_xy  <=  W/2 - (1/2) sum_s lambda_s s_x sigma(s) + (M-1) c_C
                          <=  W/2 + (1/2) sqrt(4 W Q) + (M-1) c_C ,
```

with `Q = sum_cuts w (S - M/2)^2` the cut slack. For actual clusters with `W <= c_0 M log M`
(recall that `two_thirds` forces `c_0 >= 3 - o(1)`),

```text
lambda_max(D)  <=  max_x D_x  <=  (1/2) sqrt(c_0 (c_0 - 2)) M^(3/2) log M (1 + o(1)).
```

*Proof.* The pair inequality `D_xy <= mu_xy + c_C` (`p3lower.md` Lemma 1.3; for actual clusters it is
(1.2)) summed over `y`, and the point-total identity `p3lower.md` Lemma 1.4(3), give the first line.
Then `|sum_s lambda_s s_x sigma(s)| <= sum_s lambda_s |sigma(s)| <= (sum lambda_s)^(1/2)
(sum lambda_s sigma(s)^2)^(1/2) = (W * 4Q)^(1/2)` (`sigma(s)^2/4 = (S - M/2)^2`). For actual clusters,
`stability.md` (4.1) gives `Q < M W/4 - (M^2/2) log M + 3.9626 M^2 + 2 binom(M,2) log^+ C`; with
`W <= c_0 M log M` this is `Q <= ((c_0 - 2)/4 + o(1)) M^2 log M`. Finally `lambda_max(D) <= max_x D_x` for a nonnegative symmetric matrix
(Perron-Frobenius / row-sum bound). QED

So for near-extremal actual clusters the alternative form of L1 (`lambda_max(D) >> M log M`, which
gives growth through `p3lower.md` Thm A) is not excluded, but it lives in the window
`omega(M log M) <= lambda_max(D) <= O(M^(3/2) log M)`, and it requires a point lying on the minority
side of cuts of total imbalance `>> M log M` (first line of Prop 2.2).

**Proposition 2.3 (the averaged forms of uniformity are automatic and useless).** Let
`m_0 := 2 log C - log 2`, so that `mu_xy + m_0 >= 0` for every pair of an actual cluster by (1.1)
(`|B| >= 1`, `nu >= 2`, `t <= C`).

(a) For every `K > 1`, the set `P_K = {pairs : mu_xy + m_0 <= K (mubar + m_0)}` has
`|P_K| > (1 - 1/K) binom(M, 2)` (Markov).

(b) If some pair in `P_K` has `log(|B_xy|/t_xy) >= f log M`, then
`W >= (2(M-1)/K)(2 f log M + log 2) - 2(M-1)(1 - 1/K) m_0`.

(c) *(Model statement.)* In the independent-residue model of `p3lower.md` Lemma C.3, the expected
number of pairs with `>= k` window collisions is `asymp binom(M,2) e_k((1/m_q)_q)` (for independent
Bernoulli events of probabilities `1/m_q`, `P(c >= k)` lies between `e_k prod_q (1 - 1/m_q)` and
`e_k`). At the L2 threshold `k = (2 - eps/4) log M/loglog M` this is `M^(eps/4 + o(1))`, i.e. a
fraction `M^(-2 + eps/4 + o(1))` of all pairs (the `o(1)` is `O(logloglog M/loglog M)` and decays
slowly: the exponent of `M` is about 0.69 at `log M = 100` for `eps = 0.4`, `eta = 1/2`). So (b) applies to an L2 pair only if the exceptional set `P_K^c` misses all of these
`M^(eps/4 + o(1))` pairs.

*Proof.* (a) Markov's inequality for the nonnegative numbers `mu_xy + m_0`, whose mean is
`mubar + m_0`. (b) As in `p3lower.md` Prop B.1: `W >= 2(M-1) mubar` (cut identity), and for the
pair, `K(mubar + m_0) >= mu_xy + m_0 >= 2 log(|B|/t) + log 2 + m_0` by (1.1); solve for `mubar`. (c) is a
computation in the model of `p3lower.md` Lemma C.3: by Lemma C.1 there,
`(lambda_Q - k p_max)^k/k! <= e_k <= lambda_Q^k/k!` with `lambda_Q = log(1/(1-eta)) + o(1)`, and
`log(binom(M,2) e_k) = 2 log M - k log k + O(k) = (eps/4) log M + O(log M logloglog M/loglog M)` at
that `k`. QED

*Reading.* (a) is free: "most pairs have margin `O(mubar)`" holds for every cluster, actual or fake.
Growth through pairs needs the margin of **one specific rare pair** (an L2 pair) to be comparable
to the mean. So L1 cannot be replaced by any statement about all but `eps binom(M,2)` pairs, or even
all but `M^(eps/4)` pairs, unless that statement also says where the exceptional pairs are. This is
the actual-cluster form of the "adapted margins" phenomenon of `p3lower.md` Theorem A: a cluster may
put its large margins exactly on its heavy pairs, and for actual clusters (1.2) shows it **always**
does: a pair with `c` window collisions has `mu >= 2(1-eta) c log M + log 2 - 2 log C`.

**What is not proved.** No input valid for actual clusters is known to force L1 (equivalently, by
Theorem 2.1, to force every pair gcd up to `N^(1/2) M^(-O(1))`), and none is known to force the
alternative form. Lemma 1.3 is the only upper bound on individual margins and it is off by the factor
`M^((c_0/2) M)` in the gcd. Non-uniformity cannot be proved either: the only lower bounds on
`max mu` are `mu >= 2 log((M-1)/C) + log 2` for the closest pair and `(F)`, both compatible with
`K = O(1)`.

## 3. Results on L2 (heavy pair), and the logical structure of route 1 for actual clusters

All statements are **proved** for **every normalised actual cluster** unless labelled otherwise.

**Proposition 3.1 (window collisions are agreements of a genuine linear code).** For an odd prime
`q` not dividing `N` let `T_q` be the norm-one torus of `(Z[i]/q)^*` (cyclic of order
`n_q = q - (-1/q)`), fix a generator, and write `ell_j(q) = dlog(pi_j/conj pi_j)`,
`u(q) = dlog(i)` in `Z/n_q`. Put `w_x(q) = sum_j a_xj ell_j(q) + k_x u(q)`. Then

```text
z_x = z_y mod q   <=>   w_x(q) = w_y(q)   <=>   q | B_xy .
```

So `c_xy` is the number of window coordinates in which the code words `w_x = (w_x(q))_(q in Q_eta)`
and `w_y` agree. The columns `ell_j = (ell_j(q))_q` are the genuine labels of `round7/p3G.md`
(Def. 1.1 of `p3lower.md`).

*Proof.* `pi_j` and `q` are coprime (`p_j != q`), so `rho_j = pi_j/conj pi_j` is a unit mod `q`
of norm 1, i.e. lies in `T_q`; so does `i`. By Theorem 1.1, `z_x/z_y = i^(k_x - k_y) A_v/conj(A_v) =
i^(k_x - k_y) prod_j rho_j^(v_j)` in `Q(i)`, and both sides are units mod `q` (q does not divide `N`).
So `z_x = z_y mod q` iff `prod_j rho_j^(v_j) i^(k_x - k_y) = 1` in `T_q`, iff `w_x(q) - w_y(q) = 0`
in `Z/n_q`. The last equivalence is Theorem 1.1. QED

**Theorem 3.2 (on near-extremal actual clusters, L1 excludes L2).** Fix `c_0, K, C, eta, eps`. Put
`b = K c_0 M/(4(M-1))`. If an actual cluster has `W <= c_0 M log M` and `K`-uniform margins, then
every pair has

```text
c_xy  <=  #{q > M^(1-eta) : q | B_xy}  <  (b log M + log^+(C/sqrt2)) / ((1-eta) log M)  =  K c_0/(4(1-eta)) + O(1/M) + O_C(1/log M).
```

In particular L2 (`c_xy >= (2 - eps/4) log M/loglog M` for some pair) fails for every
`M >= M_1(c_0, K, C, eta, eps)`.

*Proof.* A product of `c` distinct primes `> M^(1-eta)` dividing `B_xy` is at most `|B_xy|`, so
`(1-eta) c log M < log|B_xy| <= b log M + log(C/sqrt2)` by Theorem 2.1(a). QED

**Theorem 3.3 (Theorem C for actual clusters, with the window replaced by one large reduced chord).**
Let an actual cluster have `K`-uniform margins, and let some pair satisfy
`log(|B_xy|/t_xy) >= f log M`. Then

```text
W  >=  (2(M-1)/K) (2 f log M + log 2) .
```

If the pair satisfies L2 (`c_xy >= k` window collisions), then `f >= (1-eta) k - log C/log M`, and
with `k = (2 - eps/4) log M/loglog M` this is `W >= ((8(1-eta) - eps)/K) M log^2 M/loglog M` for
`M >= M_0`, i.e. the conclusion of `p3lower.md` Theorem C, **without** its hypotheses `X >= M`,
`lambda_Q >= lambda_0` and `H(eta, delta)`.

*Proof.* `W >= 2(M-1) mubar` (cut identity, `p3lower.md` Lemma 1.4(4)), `mubar >= mu_xy/K`, and
`mu_xy >= 2 log(|B|/t) + log 2` by (1.1). For L2: `|B_xy| >= prod (window primes) > M^((1-eta)k)`
and `t <= C`. QED

**Corollary 3.4 (what route 1 asks of actual clusters).** For `b > 0` let `(P_b)` be the property
"every pair has `|B_xy| <= M^b` and `t_xy >= M^(-b)`". Fix `c_0`. Then for actual clusters:
1. By Theorem 2.1, L1 (with some fixed `K`) on near-extremal clusters is equivalent to `(P_b)` on
   near-extremal clusters for some fixed `b`.
2. By Theorems 3.2-3.3, L1 and L2 together say that near-extremal clusters satisfy `(P_b)` and also
   violate it. L1 is equivalent to the first statement below and L2 implies the second, and the two
   statements together suffice:
   * **(R1-arch)** there is `b` such that every near-extremal actual cluster satisfies `(P_b)`;
   * **(R1-arith)** for every `b`, every near-extremal actual cluster with `M >= M_1(b)` violates
     `(P_b)`: it has a reduced chord `|B_xy| > M^b` or a chord `t_xy < M^(-b)`.

   Together they say that near-extremal actual clusters do not exist for `M >= M_1(b)`, which gives
   `2 log R >= W > c_0 M log M`, i.e. `M <= (2/c_0 + o(1)) log R/loglog R`. Conversely, if no actual
   cluster with `W <= c_0 M log M` exists for large `M`, both statements hold vacuously. So route 1 is
   **not weaker** than the growth statement at the same constant; its value is only the split into an
   archimedean half (R1-arch) and an arithmetic half (R1-arith).
3. L2 is one mechanism for (R1-arith), through window primes. By Theorem 3.3 any other mechanism that
   produces one super-polynomial `|B_xy|/t_xy` would do; the window structure is irrelevant once
   (R1-arch) is known.
4. Neither half can be **tested** on actual data at large `M`: if growth is true there is nothing to
   test. On small clusters (§4), (P_b) holds trivially for `b` of order `W/log M`.

*Proof.* Items 1-3 restate Theorems 2.1, 3.2 and 3.3 (with `W >= 2(M-1) mubar` for the growth
statement). Item 4 is a remark. QED

**Proposition 3.5 (what is known about window collisions of actual clusters).**
(a) *(Proved, actual.)* `c_xy <= log|B_xy|/((1-eta) log M) <= (mu_xy + 2 log C - log 2)/(2(1-eta) log M)`.
So an L2 pair has margin `mu_xy >= 2(1-eta)(2 - eps/4) log^2 M/loglog M - O(log C)`, and by
`stability.md` Prop 4.1(e) a near-extremal cluster has at most
`binom(M,2) (c_0/2 + o(1))/(2(1-eta) k)` candidate pairs, `k` the L2 threshold.

(b) *(Proved, actual.)* Pigeonhole gives only an average. The points occupy at most `n_q` classes mod
`q` (Prop 3.1), so `sum_(x<y) c_xy >= sum_(q in Q_eta) E(M, n_q) >= (M^2/2) sum_(q in Q_eta) 1/n_q - (M/2)|Q_eta|`
(`two_thirds` Lemma 3.4), i.e. the mean of `c_xy` is at least `lambda'_Q - o(1)`,
`lambda'_Q = sum_(q in Q_eta) 1/n_q = log(1/(1-eta)) + o(1)`. Nothing in this count prevents the
collisions from being spread evenly: the round-6 design `D_A` satisfies every point-level counting
constraint and has `max c_xy <= 18/(1-eta)` (`p3lower.md` Lemma C.2).

(c) *(Proved, counting.)* The label vector `(ell_j(q))_(q in S)` of a Gaussian prime of norm
`<= P` takes at most `P` values, while `prod_(q in S) n_q` grows like `exp(sum_(q in S) log q)`. For
`S = Q_eta` the latter is `exp((1 + o(1)) M)`. So for prime sets of polynomial size
(`stability.md` Cor 3.6: every prime of a near-extremal cluster is `<= M^(O(1))`) the labels cannot be
prescribed on more than `A/(1-eta)` window primes at once (`P = M^A`): the realisable label vectors
on `Q_eta` form a set of density at most `P e^(-(1+o(1)) M)` among all vectors. Whether a given
algebraic design (such as `D_A`, which fixes labels at every window prime) meets this thin set is not
decided here. This is a statement about designs, not a proof of L2.

(d) *(Heuristic.)* In the model where all labels of all Gaussian primes are independent and uniform,
L2 holds for every **fixed** profile (Lemma C.3), but the code words `w_x` depend on the rows `a_x`,
and rows chosen after the labels can avoid heavy pairs: a new row is excluded by an existing row
`y` only on a set of density `P(c >= k) asymp e_k = M^(-2 + o(1))`, so `M` rows chosen greedily from a
box of size `>> M^(o(1))` per row avoid all heavy pairs. Whether such adapted rows can also be
near-Plotkin-tight (as near-extremal profiles must be, `stability.md` §4) is open, and whether
actual clusters adapt their rows to their labels is exactly the question whether the archimedean
condition (all angles in one short arc) is correlated with the residues at the window primes.
**So L2 cannot follow from genuineness of the labels plus profile structure alone (heuristically);
it must use the short-arc condition**, which is the only link between the rows and the labels.

*Proof.* (a) is Theorem 1.1 with `t <= C`, (1.1) and the Markov count of `stability.md` Prop 4.1(e)
with `lambda = 2(1-eta)k`. (b) is stated with its proof. (c) is counting (a Gaussian prime of norm
`<= P` is one of at most `P` elements, and `n_q >= q - 1`).

**Status of L2 for actual clusters: open.** It is a statement about one rational integer
`B_xy = D_s(A_{a_x - a_y})` of size `M^(Theta(log M/loglog M))` having `Theta(log M/loglog M)` prime
factors in `(M^(1-eta), M]`, for clusters that (by Theorem 3.2) must then have non-uniform margins.
Equivalently, joint agreement of the genuine code of Prop 3.1 modulo
`exp(Theta(log^2 M/loglog M))`; as `p3lower.md` Remark 3 to Theorem C notes, this is beyond
equidistribution of Gaussian primes even under GRH.

## 4. Exact data from actual circles (evidence)

Scripts in `L1L2_checks/`; outputs `stats_s9_out.txt`, `known_out.txt`, `margins_detail_out.txt`.

only to select windows and to print. **Everything here is evidence**: no proof uses it, and since
actual clusters have `M <= 10` in every known range, none of it bears directly on `M -> infinity`.

**Scope of the scan** (`scan.py 1e9 16 4 4`): every `N <= 10^9` all of whose prime factors are

deduplicated under the 8 symmetries of `Z[i]`. This is **not** exhaustive in `N` (circles with
`tau(N) < 16`, or with a factor `2` or `q = 3 mod 4`, are skipped; the latter only give scaled copies

**Records and families** (`known.py`): the 9-point normalised cluster on `N = 1176852625`

**Readings (evidence only).**
1. The best small actual clusters are **not** uniform in the sense of L1: `K` is 5-13, and in every

## 5. What the exact additive identities contain

The task asked whether the exact additive structure of actual clusters (cocycles, second
differences, triangle areas, the tie between `t_xy` and `beta_xy`) can force uniformity or a heavy
pair. This section determines, with proofs, what those identities say in terms of the profile, the
integers `B_xy` and the residue partitions. All statements are **proved** for actual clusters.

**Proposition 5.1 (identities on at most three points are profile + reduced-chord + partition data).**
(a) *Cocycle.* `g_xw beta_xw = g_xy beta_xy + g_yw beta_yw` (this is `z_x - z_w = (z_x - z_y) +
(z_y - z_w)`). Its only consequence at an odd prime `q` not dividing `N` is transitivity:
`q^a | B_xy` and `q^a | B_yw` imply `q^a | B_xw`. So the collision structure at `q^a` is the partition
of the points into residue classes mod `q^a`, which is point-residue data (class `A_X`).
(b) *Triangle area.* `4 (2 Area_xyw)^2 = Norm(g_xyw)^2 |beta_xy|^2 |beta_yw|^2 |beta_xw|^2`
(`round4/outside.md` Prop C.1), and by the circumradius formula `2 Area = N^(1/4) t_xy t_yw t_xw/2`.
Combined with (1.1) these give `log Norm g_xyw = W/4 - (mu_xy + mu_yw + mu_xw)/2`, which is the
triple-cut identity of the profile. So the area identity contains nothing beyond (1.1) and the profile.
(c) *Positions.* `t_xy = 2 N^(1/4) sin(|theta_x - theta_y|/2)` with `M` real angles in an interval of
length `C N^(-1/4)`. Together with (1.1) the archimedean pair content is: `nu_s B_xy^2 = t_xy^2 e^(mu_xy)`
with the `t_xy` the chords of `M` points of one circle.

*Proof.* (a) Reduce `z_x - z_w = (z_x - z_y) + (z_y - z_w)` mod `q^a` and use Theorem 1.1.
Conversely every partition is realised by point residues, so no further pair constraint follows from
the cocycle at `q` alone. (b) Square the circumradius formula and insert `|beta|^2 = t^2 e^mu`:
`N^(1/2) (t t t)^2/4 * 4 = Norm(g_xyw)^2 (t t t)^2 e^(mu + mu + mu)`, i.e.
`2 log Norm g_xyw = W/2 - (mu_xy + mu_yw + mu_xw)`. That this is the triple-cut identity of the
profile is `round4/outside.md` (proof of C.1: at a split prime the exponent of `Norm g_xyw` is
`e - (max - min)`). (c) is the geometry of the circle. QED

**Proposition 5.2 (exact linear relations exist with polynomially bounded coefficients).** Let
`Lambda_0 = {c in Z^M : sum c_x = 0}` and `phi(c) = sum_x c_x z_x in Z[i]`. Then (for `M >= 3`) `ker phi` has rank
`M - 3` and covolume `<= M^(3/2) C^2 N^(1/2)`. Hence (Minkowski) there is a nonzero
`c in ker phi` with

```text
|c|_2  <=  sqrt(M - 3) (M^(3/2) C^2 N^(1/2))^(1/(M-3)),
```

which for `W <= c_0 M log M` is `M^(c_0/2 + 1/2 + o(1))`. Every such relation forces, with `s_x`
the positions measured from the arc's centre (`theta_x = theta_0 + s_x N^(-1/4)`),

```text
|sum c_x s_x| <= |c|_1 C^3 N^(-1/2),       |sum c_x s_x^2| <= |c|_1 C^4 N^(-1/2)    (up to factors 1 + o(1)).
```

*Proof.* `phi` maps the rank-`(M-1)` lattice `Lambda_0` (covolume `sqrt M` in `V = {sum c = 0}`) to
`Z^2`, with image of rank 2 (three points of a circle are not collinear), so `ker phi` has rank `M - 3`.
Since `ker phi` is a rational subspace, `covol(Lambda_0) = covol(ker) covol(pi(Lambda_0))`, `pi` the
orthogonal projection of `V` onto `(ker phi)^perp`, and `covol(phi(Lambda_0)) = J covol(pi(Lambda_0))`,
where `J = det(Phi Phi^T)^(1/2)` and `Phi` is the `2 x M` matrix with rows `Re(z_x - zbar)`,
`Im(z_x - zbar)` (`zbar` the mean; this is `phi` composed with the projection onto `V`). So
`covol(ker) = sqrt M * J / covol(phi(Lambda_0)) <= sqrt M * J`, since `phi(Lambda_0)` is a sublattice of
`Z^2`. By Hadamard's inequality `J <= |row 1| |row 2| <= sum_x |z_x - zbar|^2 <= sum_x |z_x - z_1|^2
<= M C^2 N^(1/2)` (chords `<= C N^(1/4)`). Minkowski's first theorem in dimension `M - 3`
(`lambda_1 <= sqrt(n) covol^(1/n)`) gives the bound on `|c|_2`. For the consequences write
`z_x = N^(1/2) e^(i theta_0) e^(i eps_x)`, `eps_x = s_x N^(-1/4)`, `|s_x| <= C`, expand
`sum c_x e^(i eps_x) = 0` to fourth order and use `sum c_x = 0`: the imaginary part gives
`|sum c_x eps_x| <= |c|_1 max|eps|^3/6 (1 + o(1))` and the real part
`|sum c_x eps_x^2| <= |c|_1 max|eps|^4/12 (1 + o(1))`. QED

*Remark.* Prop 5.2 holds for **any** `M` lattice points of size `N^(1/2)` in a window of size
`C N^(1/4)`; its content is that the points are lattice points (rank 2), not that they lie on a
circle. In a fake circle the "points" `N^(1/2) e^(i theta_x)` satisfy no exact relation in general;
this is one concrete form of the additive data of `round7/README.md` §2.1 row 9.

**Lemma 5.3 (relation-cut lemma).** Let `sum c_x z_x = 0` and let `j` be a column with `p_j` not
dividing `c_x` for every `x in supp(c)`. Then every threshold cut `(j, t)` meets `supp(c)` in either
no point or at least two points on each of its sides.

*Proof.* The high side `{a_xj >= t}` consists of multiples of `pi_j^t`, so
`sum_(x : a_xj < t) c_x z_x = 0 mod pi_j^t`. If this sum had exactly one term `c_(x0) z_(x0)`, then
`v_(pi_j)(c_(x0) z_(x0)) = a_(x0 j) < t` (`p_j` does not divide `c_(x0)`, and the other factors of
`z_(x0)` are prime to `pi_j`), a contradiction. For the high side use `conj(pi_j)^(e_j - t + 1)`,
which divides every `z_x` with `a_xj <= t - 1`. QED

*Remark (4-point relations reduce to the profile).* For `|supp c| = 4` Lemma 5.3 forces each good
column to split the four rows 4|0 or 2|2, hence the column contributions satisfy
`d^(j)_12 = d^(j)_34`, `d^(j)_13 = d^(j)_24`, `d^(j)_14 = d^(j)_23`, and therefore
`|mu_12 - mu_34| <= sum_(p_j | c_1 c_2 c_3 c_4) e_j log p_j`. But the relation on 4 points is unique up
to scaling: `c` is proportional to the signed triangle areas `(A_234, -A_134, A_124, -A_123)`, whose
`p_j`-adic valuations are `e_j - range_j(triple)` plus chord terms (Prop 5.1(b)). For four values
`a_1 <= a_2 <= a_3 <= a_4` the three triple ranges `a_4 - a_2`, `a_4 - a_1`, `a_3 - a_1` are equal iff
`a_1 = a_2` and `a_3 = a_4`. So, when the chords are prime to `p_j`, "`p_j` divides no coefficient of
the primitive relation" **is** the 2|2 condition: the lemma is the triangle identity again. A
4-point relation with small coefficients is the statement that most of the weight of the profile
restricted to the four rows is in 2|2 columns (plus small chords) — profile data. This matches
`growth/ptolemy.md` Theorem B: the quadruple (Ptolemy) relations are the Plücker relations, whose
content is the point of `M_(0,k)` and its boundary contacts.

### 5.1 Barrier summary (what the identities cannot do, and why)

The following combines Theorems 2.1, 3.2, 3.3, Props 5.1-5.3 and `growth/ptolemy.md`. The two
statements in bold are proved; the reading after them is interpretation.

1. **For actual clusters, L1 is equivalent (Theorem 2.1) to upper bounds `|B_xy| <= M^b`,
   `t_xy >= M^(-b)` for every pair.** Integrality and divisibility of the `B_xy` (Theorem 1.1, the
   cocycle, the area identity, residues at any modulus) give only **lower** bounds on `|B_xy|`
   (`|B| >= m_xy >= 1`). The only upper bounds on individual `|B_xy|` that I know are the profile
   bound `mu_xy <= W/2` and Lemma 1.3 (`mu_xy <= W/2 - 2 log M + O(log C)`, from lattice lines).
   *Reading:* an L1 proof needs a new source of **upper** bounds on reduced chords of individual pairs,
   at the scale `|B_xy| <= M^(O(1))`, i.e. `Norm g_xy >= N^(1/2) M^(-O(1))` (pair gcds of size `N^(1/4)`).
   Lemma 1.3 gets `Norm g_xy >= M^2/O(C^4)`.
2. **The additive identities on at most four points reduce to the profile, the integers `B_xy` and
   residue partitions** (Props 5.1, 5.3 and Ptolemy = Plücker); on all `M` points they are equivalent
   to the cluster itself (the points are recovered from `g`, `beta` and one point). Prop 5.2 gives
   relations of polynomial height among all points, whose content is lattice-ness.
   *Reading:* the only intermediate uses of additive structure are relations on `k` points with
   `5 <= k <= M`, and `growth/ptolemy.md` Thm C shows that for `k <= 6` such relations are satisfied by
   polynomial families with all residues bounded. No intermediate class between "pair-local" and "the
   actual problem" is known.
3. L2 is (Prop 3.1) a joint agreement statement for the genuine code `w_x(q) = <a_x, ell(q)> + k_x u(q)`
   at `Theta(log M/loglog M)` window primes, i.e. modulo `exp(Theta(log^2 M/loglog M))`. Pigeonhole and
   all point-level counting give averages only (Prop 3.5(b)); genuineness makes algebraic designs
   unrealisable (Prop 3.5(c)) but does not by itself prevent rows adapted to the labels (Prop 3.5(d),
   heuristic). *Reading:* an L2 proof must use the short-arc condition as the link between the rows
   and the window residues, at moduli beyond GRH range.
4. By Corollary 3.4, L1 and L2 cannot be pursued as independent lemmas about the same near-extremal
   clusters in the hope of verifying either one: each is the other's negation there. Any progress
   must take the form (R1-arch) or (R1-arith).

## 6. Open points

1. **(R1-arch), equivalently L1 for actual clusters.** Is there `b` such that every near-extremal
   actual cluster has `Norm g_xy >= N^(1/2) M^(-b)` for all pairs (Theorem 2.1)? The only known
   individual upper bound on margins is Lemma 1.3, which reaches `Norm g_xy >= M^2/(2C^4)`. A natural
   intermediate target: improve Lemma 1.3 to `Norm g_xy >= N^delta` for some fixed `delta > 0` (still
   far from L1, but the first bound on individual pair gcds at a power of `N`). Lemma 1.3 uses one line
   family; lattice-line arguments with directions of height `N^delta` lose because Prop C.2 is linear
   in `|v|`.
2. **(R1-arith), of which L2 is one form.** Show that a near-extremal actual cluster has one pair with
   `|B_xy|/t_xy >= M^(f(M))`, `f -> infinity`. By Prop 3.5(d) any proof must use the short-arc
   condition, not only genuineness of the window labels. Equivalently (Theorem 1.1) one rational
   integer `D_s(A_v)` among the `binom(M,2)` pair values must be super-polynomially large relative to
   the chord.
3. **The window (Prop 2.2).** For near-extremal actual clusters, is `lambda_max(D) <= O(M log M)`?
   The a-priori range is `[2 log M, O(M^(3/2) log M)]`. A proof of `O(M log M)` would close the
   alternative form of L1 for actual clusters; a cluster family with `lambda_max(D) >> M log M` would
   give growth through `p3lower.md` Theorem A.
4. **Relations on `5 <= k <= M` points.** Prop 5.2 gives exact relations of height `M^(c_0/2 + O(1))`
   on all `M` points. Do they constrain margins? Lemma 5.3 constrains every cut against their support,
   but for a full-support relation it only excludes singleton cuts at large primes.
5. **Data.** All actual clusters have `M <= 10` in the ranges searched, so neither half of route 1 can
   be tested on actual data. The small-cluster evidence (§4) shows non-uniform margins driven by
   symmetry, a mechanism that Lemma 1.3 confines to `M <= sqrt2 C^2 + 2`.
