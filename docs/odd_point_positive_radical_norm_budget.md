# Odd-point positive divided differences and the complete norm exponent

For every fixed odd `n>=7`, the positive radical construction extends
directly to primitive marked directions. Its denominator can be cleared
at subpower cost under the full marked valuation profile. The field norm
forces a degree lower bound of order `sqrt(n)`, while its possible degree
is `2^(n-1)`. Thus taking more points does not by itself make the full-degree
norm budget negative.

## 1. Exact radical and denominator control

Write `n=2k+1`. Let `v_i=(x_i,y_i)` be primitive integral marked vectors,
oriented so `x_i>0`, and let `q` be the reduced positive rational quadratic
form. Put

```text
Q_i=q(v_i),       d_ij=det(v_i,v_j),
D_i=product_(j!=i)d_ij,
rho_i=Q_i^((n-2)/2)/D_i.                           (1)
```

The denominator in (1) retains its sign. Since `n` is odd, each `rho_i`
is a rational multiple of `sqrt(Q_i)`. At a clean cut of minority size
`s<=k` and depth `e`, the marked profile gives

```text
v_p(Q_i)=e on the minority, 0 outside,
v_p(d_ij)=e for an internal minority pair, 0 otherwise.
```

Consequently

```text
v_p(rho_i^2)=(n-2s)e on the minority, 0 outside.    (2)
```

All these valuations are nonnegative. Under the full marked-profile
hypothesis that the total absolute logarithmic error is `o(w)`, the sum
of the negative valuations of the finitely many rational numbers
`rho_i^2` is `o(w)`. Their least common denominator `C` is therefore a
positive integer with `log C=o(w)`. Each `C rho_i` is an algebraic integer,
because its square is the integer `C^2 rho_i^2`.

This uses the full error hypothesis, not merely clean valuations at selected
primes. In particular, uncontrolled primes may not be dropped from `C`.

Set `t_i=y_i/x_i`, `X=product_i x_i`, and `P(t)=product_i(t-t_i)`. The
sign convention and the parity of `n` give exactly

```text
D_i=x_i^(n-2) X P'(t_i),
sum_i rho_i = X^(-1) [t_1,...,t_n] q(1,t)^((n-2)/2).  (3)
```

For a positive quadratic `q`, differentiation gives

```text
(d/dt)^(n-1) q(1,t)^((n-2)/2)
 = ((n-2)!!)^2 det(q)^((n-1)/2) q(1,t)^(-n/2) > 0.  (4)
```

The divided difference in (3) is therefore positive. If the reduced metric
and its inverse have subpower size and all marked slopes lie in a bounded
chart, (4) bounds this divided difference above and below by `exp(o(w))`.
The standard fixed-chart choice is possible for the clustered projective
directions. Thus the nonzero integral radical

```text
Z=C sum_i rho_i                                   (5)
```

has its small distinguished embedding controlled without an unproved
cancellation estimate.

## 2. Fair archimedean exponents

There are equally weighted cuts of minority sizes `1,...,k`. Define

```text
a = sum_(j=0..k-1) binomial(2k,j)
  = [4^k-binomial(2k,k)]/2,
r = 2^(n-3).
```

The full fair marked profile gives

```text
log Q_i=a w+o(w),
log |d_ij|=(a-r)w+o(w),
log x_i=(a/2)w+o(w).                              (6)
```

The pair-incidence count for the second line is
`sum_(j=0..k-2)binomial(2k-1,j)=a-r`. From (1), (3), and (6), put

```text
A=(n-1)r-na/2,      B=na/2.
```

Then

```text
log |C rho_i|=A w+o(w),
log |Z|=-B w+o(w),
A+B=(n-1)r.                                      (7)
```

In particular `A>0`, since
`A/r=n*binomial(2k,k)/4^k-1>0`; the central binomial coefficient is larger
than the mean of the `n` coefficients in the row.

## 3. All conjugates in the field norm

Let `d=[Q(Z^2):Q]`. Its field is contained in the field generated by the
pair radicals `sqrt(Q_i Q_j)`, so `d` is a power of two at most `2^(n-1)`.
Every conjugate of `Z` is a signed sum of the `C rho_i`, with the signs
restricted by the actual squareclass relations. Thus every conjugate of
`Z^2` is at most `exp(2Aw+o(w))`. Using its distinguished value from (7),

```text
1 <= Norm_(Q(Z^2)/Q)(Z^2)
  <= exp([2A(d-1)-2B]w+o(w))
   = exp([2Ad-2(n-1)r]w+o(w)).                    (8)
```

All conjugates and the denominator-clearing cost are included. Additional
small conjugates could improve (8), but are not assumed. A necessary
condition is therefore

```text
d >= (n-1)r/A
  = (n-1)/[n*binomial(2k,k)/4^k-1].               (9)
```

Because `d` is a power of two, the first few consequences are:

| n | A | B | Right side of (9), approximately | Necessary d |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 7 | 19 | 77 | 5.053 | at least 8 |
| 9 | 187/2 | 837/2 | 5.476 | at least 8 |
| 11 | 437 | 2123 | 5.858 | at least 8 |
| 25 | 12706671 | 87956625 | 7.922 | at least 8 |
| 31 | 933725324 | 7119338356 | 8.625 | at least 16 |

The right side of (9) is asymptotic to `sqrt(pi*n/2)`. The possible field
degree `2^(n-1)` grows exponentially instead. More explicitly,
`n*binomial(2k,k)/4^k` increases with odd `n` and already exceeds `2` at
`n=7`; hence the threshold in (9) is less than `n-1`, far below the maximum
degree. Inserting the maximum degree into (8) always leaves a positive
exponent for these `n`.

An independent bound on the actual radical field degree would be useful:
a uniformly bounded degree would eventually violate (9) as `n` increases.
No such bound follows from this calculation or from the fair valuation
profile; large even cut depths can also erase squareclass information.

## 4. Finite-place scope

The generic-clean-cut unit argument in
`seven_positive_radical_norm_budget.md` works here as well. At a minority
collapse to a root of `q`, all minority radicals have positive valuation
by (2). The majority radical squareclasses remain the independent classes
`q(t_j)` in their free marked variables. Thus no signed majority sum
vanishes identically, and the universal signed-sum product has generic
cut valuation zero. No additional norm divisor is forced by these cuts.

At an odd clean prime, (2) is odd exactly when the cut depth is odd.
For the field generated by these radicals, a ramified prime has tame
inertia two and discriminant exponent half the field degree. This counts
the prime once when its depth is odd, not with its full depth. As in the
seven-point case, this discriminant information is not divisibility of
the norm in (8).

The conclusion is conditional on the explicitly stated full marked and
reduced-metric profile. It establishes neither that profile for arbitrary
larger-point Gale lattices nor a lattice-circle endpoint bound.

The exact checker `check_positive_radical_norm_budget.py` verifies (4) for
odd `n=3,...,31` and the incidence and norm-budget identities for odd
`n=7,...,101`, as finite supplements to the displayed general formulas.
