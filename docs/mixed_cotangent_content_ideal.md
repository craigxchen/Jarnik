# Mixed holomorphic-antiholomorphic content still misses the endpoint order

This note treats the universal rational-circle rows

```text
H_i=X_i+iL,                 bar(H_i)=X_i-iL,
W_0=product_i bar(H_i),
W_i=H_i product_(j!=i) bar(H_j).
```

Let `I=(W_0,...,W_k)` and let `bar(I)` be the ideal generated by the
coefficient conjugates of these rows.  A mixed content certificate for
`G^a bar(G)^b` naturally uses the ideal

```text
J=I^a bar(I)^b,             n=a+b,
```

and a homogeneous polynomial of degree `kn`.  Allowing both orientations
does increase the possible contact with the coalescence divisor `L=0`.
For example, a Hermitian triangle area has order three rather than two.
Nevertheless the order is always strictly smaller than the order `2n`
required by a balanced product of `n` short chords.

## 1. Normal form of the two row ideals

Work over `K=Q(i)` and make the invertible linear change

```text
y_i=X_i-iL,                 t=2iL.
```

Put

```text
Y=product_i y_i,            Y_i=product_(j!=i)y_j,
Z=product_i(y_i+t),         Z_i=product_(j!=i)(y_j+t).
```

Subtracting the zeroth row from the other rows gives

```text
I=(Y,tY_1,...,tY_k),
bar(I)=(Z,tZ_1,...,tZ_k).                            (1)
```

The second equality follows because the conjugate zeroth row is `Z`,
whereas the conjugate of row `i` is `y_i Z_i=Z-tZ_i`.

## 2. A strict upper bound for mixed contact

**Proposition.** Let `a,b` be nonnegative integers, put `n=a+b>0`, and
let `P` be a nonzero homogeneous polynomial of degree `kn`.  Suppose that
on every projective parameter chart

```text
P belongs to the integral closure of I^a bar(I)^b.   (2)
```

If `r=ord_(t=0)(P)`, then

```text
r <= floor(2n-n/2^(k-1)) < 2n.                      (3)
```

For `k=1`, the right side in (3) is `n`.  The displayed formula is valid
without a separate convention.

**Proof.** Write `P=t^r Q`, where `Q` is homogeneous of degree `kn-r`
and is not divisible by `t`.  Dehomogenize on the chart `t=1`:

```text
q(y_1,...,y_k)=Q(y_1,...,y_k,1).
```

This is a nonzero polynomial of total degree at most `kn-r`.

Consider a point of the Boolean grid `{0,-1}^k`, and let `s` be the
number of its zero coordinates.  At this point, (1) gives

```text
ord(I)=max(s-1,0),
ord(bar(I))=max(k-s-1,0).                            (4)
```

Indeed, among `Y,Y_1,...,Y_k`, one can omit at most one of the `s`
vanishing coordinate factors.  The shifted products give the second
formula in exactly the same way.  The order here is the ordinary
maximal-ideal order in a regular local ring.  Its associated graded ring
is a polynomial ring, so it defines the divisorial order obtained by
blowing up the point.  Integral dependence in (2) therefore forces

```text
mult_y(q) >= a max(s-1,0)+b max(k-s-1,0).            (5)
```

The multiplicity Schwartz--Zippel inequality on a two-element grid, in
the form proved in
[the general polynomial barrier](hermitian_polynomial_barrier.md), says

```text
sum_(y in {0,-1}^k) mult_y(q)
    <= 2^(k-1) deg(q).                               (6)
```

The two sums on the right of (5) agree by complementation, and

```text
sum_(s-subsets) max(s-1,0)
  = k 2^(k-1)-(2^k-1)
  = 2^(k-1)(k-2)+1.                                 (7)
```

Combining (5)--(7) yields

```text
n(2^(k-1)(k-2)+1) <= 2^(k-1)(kn-r),
```

which is (3).  Since `n/2^(k-1)>0`, the integer `r` is strictly less
than `2n`.  This proves the proposition.

## 3. The triangle area is the exact first mixed case

Take `a=b=1` and `k>=2`.  The oriented Hermitian area from rows
`0,1,2` is

```text
A=(W_1-W_0)(bar(W_2)-bar(W_0))
  -(W_2-W_0)(bar(W_1)-bar(W_0)).                     (8)
```

It belongs to `I bar(I)`.  Formula (1) gives the exact factorization

```text
A=t^3 (y_1-y_2) product_(ell>=3) y_ell(y_ell+t),     (9)
```

up to the harmless choice of sign in (8).  Thus its order at `t=0` is
exactly three.  Proposition (3) gives `r<=3` for every `k>=2`, so three
is the exact maximum in this first mixed case.  In particular the naive
bound `r<=a+b` is false, but the needed order four is still impossible.

## 4. Consequence for balanced chord certificates

Keep the `X_i` fixed, positive, and distinct while `L`, equivalently
`t`, tends to zero.  The common row modulus tends to a nonzero value and
every row difference is `O(t)`.  Hence a product of any fixed number
`m>=1` of selected chord differences is `O(t^m)`.

An inequality of the balanced form

```text
product_(ell=1)^m |selected chord_ell|
    >= c |P|^(m/(2n)) rho^(m/2),                     (10)
```

with fixed `c>0`, would force `P=O(t^(2n))` on an open set of fixed
positive, distinct `X_i`.  Polynomiality then forces `t^(2n)` to divide
`P`.  This contradicts (3).  Thus replacing a pure content certificate
by any fixed mixed certificate for `G^a bar(G)^b` does not repair the
universal balanced chord-product method.

The same conclusion holds for a finite menu of such certificates, with
different mixed powers and chord selections.  Choose one generic fixed
rational tuple of positive, distinct `X_i` outside the finitely many
leading-coefficient zero sets, and then take `L>0` rational and
sufficiently small for every member of the menu.

The argument concerns chartwise integral-closure divisibility.  It does
not exclude an absolute-value estimate specialized to ordinary integer
cotangent cliques; local divisibility failure need not imply failure of
such a global norm estimate.  It also does not prove or disprove the
uniform lattice-point bound.
