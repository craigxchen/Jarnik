# Integer cotangent normalization and its exact Gaussian content

This is an exact reduction, not a radius-independent point bound or a
height obstruction. It identifies what the Gaussian-gcd requirement
retains after all finitely many imaginary residues are cleared.

## 1. Clear the pair residues as well as the anchor residues

Suppose actual anchor numerators are `h_i=x_i+i t_i`, and their reduced
pair numerators are `x_ij+i t_ij`, with all displayed imaginary parts
nonzero. Their exact equations imply

```text
x_ij/t_ij = (x_i x_j+t_i t_j)/(x_i t_j-x_j t_i).
```

Put

```text
L=lcm(|t_i|, |t_ij|),       X_i=L x_i/t_i.
```

Then `L>0`, every `X_i` is an integer, and

```text
(X_i X_j+L^2)/(X_i-X_j)=L x_ij/t_ij is an integer.       (1)
```

There are `m(m+1)/2` residues for `m` nonanchor rows. If their absolute
values are at most `T`, then

```text
log L <= m(m+1)/2 log T.
```

Thus for a fixed extracted row count, `log T=o(w)` implies
`log L=o(w)`. This assertion is not uniform when the extracted row
count itself grows.

Writing `A=X_1` and `d_j=X_j-A`, condition (1) also gives the ordered
divisor formulation

```text
d_j | A^2+L^2,
d_i-d_j | A^2+2A d_i+d_i^2.
```

These divisibilities alone have not supplied a useful uniform height
bound.

## 2. The actual Gaussian gcd is explicit

Let `X,Y,L` be arbitrary integers with `L>0`, `delta=X-Y!=0`, and
`delta | X^2+L^2`. Put

```text
H=X+iL,   K=Y+iL,
d=gcd((X^2+L^2)/delta, X, L, delta)>0.
```

Then

```text
N(gcd_G(H,K)) = |delta| d,          d | L.              (2)
```

Indeed, the Gaussian ideal `(H,K)` equals `(H,delta)`. As an integer
lattice it is generated by the four columns

```text
(X,L), (-L,X), (delta,0), (0,delta).
```

Its index is the gcd of its maximal minors, namely

```text
gcd(X^2+L^2, delta X, delta L, delta^2).
```

The Gaussian ideal is principal; its integer lattice index is the norm
of a Gaussian gcd. Factoring `|delta|` proves (2). This proof includes
all primes, common integer contents, and the ramified prime two.

For the integer

```text
Q=(XY+L^2)/(X-Y),
```

one has `d|Q` as well, since `Q=(X^2+L^2)/delta-X`. Therefore the exact
Gaussian-gcd quotient is

```text
K conjugate(H)/N(gcd_G(H,K))
    =sign(delta) (Q+iL)/d.                            (3)
```

Its imaginary part has absolute value `L/d`, a divisor of `L`.
Consequently, for the normalized numerators `X_i+iL`, the integer
cotangent condition already forces the full Gaussian-gcd quotient to
have a small imaginary part. Restoring that particular gcd condition
does not impose an additional large-height equation.

The raw normalized numerators need not themselves be conjugate-primitive:
they have been multiplied by the real integers `L/t_i`. Formula (3)
does not claim otherwise, and does not discard canonical-unit conditions
when returning to a common-unit primitive factorization.

## 3. The least primitive radius is still retained exactly

For any such integer data, consider the rational unit-circle tuple

```text
q_0=1,       q_i=H_i/conjugate(H_i).
```

Reduce each fraction over `Z[i]` as `q_i=a_i/b_i`, with coprime Gaussian
numerator and denominator. Let `B` be their Gaussian least common
multiple of the `b_i`. Then

```text
z_0=B,       z_i=B a_i/b_i
```

is Gaussian integral and primitive, and its common radius is exactly
`|B|`. To see primitivity, at every Gaussian prime dividing `B`, choose
a denominator attaining its maximum valuation; its coprime numerator
makes the corresponding coordinate a unit at that prime. A prime not
dividing `B` cannot divide `z_0`.

Every other Gaussian integral tuple on this same rational Gaussian
projective ray is a Gaussian integer multiple of this primitive tuple:
apply Gaussian Bezout to its coordinates. Hence `|B|` is its least
radius, and all primitive realizations differ by a Gaussian unit.

No estimate derived here forces this least radius to grow too rapidly
for an endpoint cluster. Such an estimate would need additional control
of the simultaneous integer clique (1), rather than merely replacing
its quotient contents with formal variables or with their exact gcds.

## 4. A residue-dependent count, and its limitation

If `p=3 mod 4` is a prime not dividing `L`, distinct `X_i` have distinct
residues modulo `p`. Otherwise (1) would force
`X_i^2+L^2=0 mod p`, impossible because `-1` is not a square and `L`
is nonzero modulo `p`. Thus

```text
m <= the least prime p=3 mod 4 not dividing L.
```

This bounds the row count when the residues are fixed, independently
of the coordinate height. It does not exclude a fixed eight-row core
with `L` of subpower height, and it supplies no improvement to the
currently audited general radius-dependent bound.

## 5. Ordered divisor complements are an exact anchor reflection

Include the anchor at infinity explicitly, and suppose the finite
integer cotangents are positive. Put `A=min_i X_i`, `N_0=A^2+L^2`.
The rational map

```text
F(x)=(Ax+L^2)/(x-A),
F(infinity)=A,             F(A)=infinity
```

permutes the role of the anchor and the row `A`. Every other row is
sent to an integer by the clique condition. Writing `x=A+d` gives

```text
F(A+d)=A+N_0/d,           d | N_0.                       (4)
```

Thus the divisor-complement operation is one simultaneous map of the
whole tuple, rather than independent replacements of selected divisors.
It is an involution. Every finite image is positive, its minimum is
again `A`, and the image of the original infinity row supplies this
minimum.

For `q(x)=(x+iL)/(x-iL)` and `q(infinity)=1`, direct multiplication
gives

```text
q(F(x)) = q(A)/q(x).                                    (5)
```

This is reflection of the unit-circle arguments, followed by a common
rational Gaussian rotation. It preserves all pair cotangent
integrality conditions, with signs reversed when both corresponding
angles are finite. It also preserves the least Gaussian primitive
radius: conjugation preserves primitivity, and multiplying a rational
Gaussian projective tuple by a common nonzero rational Gaussian scalar
does not change its primitive integral representative except by a unit.

In particular, complementing the divisors neither decreases the least
primitive radius nor increases `A/L`. The full angular span, including
the infinity anchor, remains exactly `2 arctan(L/A)`. This operation
does not itself give a height descent.

Finally, if two finite nonminimal rows have `d_i d_j=N_0`, then

```text
q(X_i) q(X_j)=q(A) q(infinity).
```

Such a complementary pair is excluded in the small arcs covered by
the existing multiplicative rectangle theorem. This excludes exact
complementary pairs, not general large collections of divisors.
