# Linear common-phase Gaussian elimination has scalar generators

Suppose several Gaussian composites are simultaneously close to prescribed
real axes. A natural joint attack is to eliminate the common axis and use
the Gaussian ideal or a resultant generated by their small imaginary
parts. For linear common-phase elimination, this produces no new primitive
arithmetic object: the elimination ideal is generated by pairwise
determinants, and every such determinant is exactly a rational content
times the imaginary part of one scalar **difference character**.

This extends the existing
[bilinear half-character reduction](additive_balanced_character_generic_content.md)
from one pair to the complete linear common-axis elimination ideal. It is an exact
limitation of that linear generator route. It does not rule out useful
nonlinear combinations of the generators, special-value divisibility,
nonlinear source identities, or an argument that uses all phases together
without eliminating their common axis. In particular it is not an endpoint
construction or a general point count.

## 1. The rank-two elimination ideal

Let `B_j=X_j+iY_j`, for `1<=j<=k`, and introduce a homogeneous real-axis
direction `[U:V]`. Exact common phase means

```text
V X_j-U Y_j=0       for every j.                         (1)
```

Over any characteristic-zero field, projectively eliminating `[U:V]`
from (1) gives the prime determinantal ideal

```text
I=(D_jl:1<=j<l<=k),
D_jl=X_jY_l-X_lY_j=Im(conjugate(B_j) B_l).              (2)
```

Indeed, (1) says that the `2 by k` matrix with columns `(X_j,Y_j)` has
rank at most one. Its rank-one affine cone is parametrized by
`X_j=U T_j,Y_j=V T_j`; the kernel of this parametrization is the ideal of
its `2 by 2` minors. Explicitly, two monomials have the same image only
if they have the same total `U` degree and the same degree in each `T_j`;
successive swaps `X_jY_l -> X_lY_j` connect them. Thus their difference
is generated by the minors, and a general kernel polynomial splits into
such binomials by its image monomial. This also proves primality because
the target polynomial ring is a domain.

In elimination notation one must saturate the equations by `(U,V)`:

```text
((V X_j-U Y_j)_j : (U,V)^infinity) intersect k[X,Y]=I.
```

Unsaturated affine elimination permits the irrelevant solution `U=V=0`
and gives the zero ideal. On the chart of any nonzero column, the saturated
assertion also follows by solving for the common slope; the minors glue
the charts including vertical directions. Thus every polynomial
consequence obtained solely by projectively eliminating the common
direction lies in the ideal generated by the pair determinants. The
binary resultant of the `j`th and `l`th equations in (1) is already
`D_jl`.

At approximate common phase, if `dist(arg B_j,theta)<=eta_j`, then

```text
|D_jl| <= |B_j B_l| (eta_j+eta_l).                     (3)
```

Applying the nonzero-integer gap separately to several minors merely sums
scalar inequalities. If one uses no information beyond those separate
gaps, replacing their product by the Gaussian or rational ideal they
generate takes a gcd and weakens that particular bound. A nonlinear
combination of the generators may have extra vanishing, cancellation, or
special evaluated divisibility; ideal membership alone does not control
those effects.

## 2. Exact threshold-allocation dictionary

Remove the complete common Gaussian factor from actual source rows. For
each varying split prime `p`, choose `pi_p` above `p` and let `a_i(p)` be
the allocation exponent at row `i`. For an integer zero-sum row vector
`L`, put

```text
c_L(p)=sum_i L_i a_i(p),
A_L=product_p pi_p^max(c_L(p),0)
              conjugate(pi_p)^max(-c_L(p),0).           (4)
```

This is the conjugate-primitive Gaussian composite of the scalar row
character. For two characters `L,K`, prime-by-prime valuation subtraction
gives the literal identity

```text
A_L conjugate(A_K)=C_(L,K) A_(L-K),                    (5)
C_(L,K)=product_p p^kappa_p,
kappa_p=(|c_L(p)|+|c_K(p)|-|c_L(p)-c_K(p)|)/2.         (6)
```

The exponents in (6) are nonnegative integers. If fixed Gaussian units
`u_L,u_K` orient the two prescribed axes, then (5) yields

```text
Im(conjugate(u_L A_L) u_K A_K)
 =C_(L,K) Im(conjugate(u_L)u_K A_(K-L)).                (7)
```

Consequently every generator of the joint common-phase ideal is exactly
a scalar difference-character imaginary part with its full rational
content retained. Its primitive log height is

```text
log Norm(A_(K-L))
 =sum_p |c_K(p)-c_L(p)| log p.                          (8)
```

Equations (5)--(7) are the determinant version of the known bilinear
half-character reduction, but apply directly to an arbitrary family of
integer row characters. They show why a determinant of two allegedly
independent small imaginary parts does not create a second height charge:
after content removal it is the already-tested character `K-L`.

## 3. The joint ideal can have only one scale of height

There is no general lower bound making the ideal of all determinants grow
like the product of the composite heights. Fix distinct positive even
integers `a_1,...,a_k` whose pairwise differences have gcd two. Let `T`
run through positive multiples of every `|a_j^2-a_l^2|`, and set

```text
H_j(T)=a_j T+i.                                         (9)
```

Each `H_j` is conjugate-primitive: its coordinates are coprime and have
opposite parity. Their rational norms are pairwise coprime. Indeed, a
prime dividing two norms divides `a_l^2-a_j^2`, hence divides `T`, while
both norms are one modulo that prime, a contradiction. Yet

```text
Im(conjugate(H_j)H_l)=(a_j-a_l)T,
gcd_(j<l) |Im(conjugate(H_j)H_l)|=2T.                 (10)
```

Thus the determinant ideal in `Z` has generator size
`log(2T)=log T+O(1)`; viewed in `Z[i]`, its ideal norm has logarithm
`2log(2T)`. Either quantity is independent of `k`, while

```text
sum_j log Norm(H_j)=2k log T+O_k(1).                   (11)
```

All phases are simultaneously `O(1/T)`. These blocks also occur in a
literal primitive circle tuple: take an anchor choosing every
`conjugate(H_j)` and, for row `j`, reverse only block `j`. The `k+1`
rows have common modulus, are distinct and primitive, and lie on an arc
of angular width `O_k(1/T)`. Their radius is `T^(k+o(1))`, so for `k>2`
this family escapes every fixed endpoint constant. It is therefore a
countermodel to extracting an `Omega(k log T)` joint resultant charge
from simultaneous angular collapse, not an endpoint counterexample.

## 4. Remaining input needed

For the canonical Paley profiles, all individual saturated characters
already have primitive height above `W/2`, while subset determinants also
pass. Sections 1--2 show that the generators produced by common-axis
elimination are scalar difference characters; the number field `Q(i)`
supplies just the two real coordinates whose minors appear in (2).
General polynomial combinations of these generators require additional
analysis and can acquire extra special-value content. A useful joint
argument must exploit precisely such information or retain data that the
rank-one elimination discards. Concrete possibilities are an extra
divisibility theorem for the **evaluated** coefficients in a nonlinear
identity, a source-specific relation among several difference characters,
or a simultaneous approximation theorem using the full prime-exponent
matrix before projection to `Q(i)`.

The [exact checker](check_gaussian_common_phase_elimination_collapse.py)
verifies (5)--(7), including all sixteen pairs of Gaussian unit twists,
on random nested allocation vectors and checks the
primitive, pairwise norm-coprime family (9)--(11). It does not certify
the standard determinantal-ideal theorem, whose elementary proof is
given above.
