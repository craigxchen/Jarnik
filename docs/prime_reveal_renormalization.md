# Prime reveal and renormalization on divisor-angle fibers

## Status

This note develops a unified stochastic/renormalization framework for the endpoint arc problem. It gives exact identities and a concrete fixed-point classification at the exact level. It does not prove the uniform endpoint bound.

## 1. Product model and rare event

Write the complete divisor-angle random variable as

\[
S=Y_1+\cdots+Y_r \pmod{2\pi},
\]

where the block variables `Y_j` are independent and each is uniform on the angle progression contributed by one split prime-power block. Let `I` be an interval of endpoint width `delta`, and let

\[
E=\{S\in I\}.
\]

If the interval contains `M` divisor points out of `R_N` total choices, then

\[
\mathbf P(E)=M/R_N.
\]

No point or edge is anchored.

## 2. Backward survival profiles

Fix an ordering of the blocks. Put

\[
S_k=Y_1+\cdots+Y_k,
\]

and define the backward survival profile

\[
h_k(x)=\mathbf P(Y_{k+1}+\cdots+Y_r\in I-x).
\]

Then

\[
h_{k-1}=\mu_k*h_k,
\]

where `mu_k` is the law of `Y_k`. The initial value is

\[
h_0(0)=\mathbf P(E),
\]

and the terminal profile is the interval indicator.

Conditioning the product process on `E` gives the exact Doob transform

\[
\mathbf Q(Y_k=y\mid S_{k-1}=x)
=\frac{\mu_k(y)h_k(x+y)}{h_{k-1}(x)}.
\]

Thus the prime-reveal process is a genuine inhomogeneous Markov chain under the conditioned law.

## 3. Likelihood martingale and entropy identity

Let

\[
Z_k=\mathbf P(E\mid Y_1,\ldots,Y_k)=h_k(S_k).
\]

Under the original product law, `(Z_k)` is a martingale. Under the conditioned law, the log-likelihood increments satisfy

\[
\log\frac{Z_k}{Z_{k-1}}
=\log\frac{\mathbf Q(Y_k\mid S_{k-1})}{\mu_k(Y_k)}.
\]

Consequently the chain rule for relative entropy gives the exact identity

\[
\log\frac1{\mathbf P(E)}
=\sum_{k=1}^r
\mathbf E_{\mathbf Q}
D\bigl(\mathbf Q(Y_k\mid S_{k-1})\,\|\,\mu_k\bigr).
\]

This is the precise version of the statement that the rare successful branch pays for its successive prime reveals.

## 4. Renormalization operator

The natural state is not a point configuration but a positive survival profile `h` on the circle, considered modulo translation and positive scaling. Revealing a block with law `mu` applies

\[
\mathcal T_\mu h=\mu*h.
\]

Conditioning on a realized branch value `y` applies the projectivized update

\[
\mathcal R_{\mu,y}[h](x)
=\frac{h(x+y)}{(\mu*h)(x_0)},
\]

with translation chosen so that the current partial sum is returned to a fixed origin. A packet renormalization composes many such updates and then rescales the spatial window to unit width.

The prime-reveal martingale and renormalization are therefore the same object viewed forward and backward:

- forward: a Doob-transformed conditioned random walk;
- backward: repeated convolution of survival profiles.

## 5. Exact fixed points

For a probability measure `mu` on a compact abelian group, suppose a nonzero nonnegative profile `h` satisfies

\[
\mu*h=\lambda h
\]

and has support strictly smaller than the whole group. Positivity implies `lambda=1` after normalization, and every translation by an element of `supp(mu)` preserves the support and the positive level sets of `h`. Hence the support is a union of cosets of the closed subgroup generated by the differences of `supp(mu)`.

In particular, exact nonconstant positive fixed profiles are subgroup/coset objects. If the block support generates a dense subgroup of the circle, the only continuous positive fixed profile is constant.

For Gaussian-prime angle blocks, an exact finite subgroup stabilization would require a nontrivial product of ratios

\[
\prod_j(\pi_j/\overline{\pi_j})^{n_j}
\]

to be a root of unity. Gaussian unique factorization forces such a relation to be trivial, apart from units. Thus the exact structured fixed points are arithmetically excluded in the diffuse primitive setting.

## 6. Approximate fixed points are the load-bearing problem

The endpoint problem therefore reduces to a stability statement:

> If repeated packet convolution and conditioning preserves a highly localized survival profile for many conductor scales, then the profile must be close to a coset-indicator fixed point with controlled denominator.

The exact theorem is not enough. One needs constants uniform when the successful mass `M/R_N` tends to zero and when the number of blocks grows.

## 7. A live-packet martingale target

Partition the prime blocks into packets of comparable conductor height. Choose a uniformly random packet order and a uniformly random live cut, following the live-coordinate principle from the quantum parallel-repetition walkthrough. The desired estimate is a probability-weighted oscillation inequality of the form

\[
\mathbf E_{\mathbf Q}
\|\Gamma(h_{k+1})-\Gamma(h_k)\|^2
\le
\frac{\Phi(h_0)-\mathbf E\Phi(h_L)}{L},
\]

where `Gamma` is a scale-resolved transform of a positive profile and `Phi` is a bounded potential. The point of a scale-resolved transform is to avoid losses from very small survival probabilities, just as resolvent purification avoids a smallest-eigenvalue loss.

A suitable scalar candidate is the family of resolvents

\[
\Gamma_u(h)=\frac{h}{h+u},\qquad u>0,
\]

integrated against a measure in `u`. The identity

\[
\int_0^\infty \left(\frac{s}{s+u}\right)^2du=s
\]

suggests a probability-preserving embedding of scalar survival weights. Whether the convolution update admits the required Bregman inequality remains unproved.

## 8. Renormalization fixed-point program

For a packet of conductor height `b`, rescale angles by the endpoint scale appropriate to the remaining conductor. The renormalized profile records the conditional probability that the unrevealed walk reaches the target interval.

A successful proof would establish:

1. compactness of normalized profiles modulo translation;
2. strict contraction away from approximate subgroup profiles;
3. classification of all limiting fixed points as finite-grid/coset profiles;
4. arithmetic exclusion of those profiles using Gaussian unique factorization and quantitative separation.

The main lesson from the walkthroughs is that a local inequality may need a remote or scale-resolved correction to become globally coercive. Here that suggests augmenting the local survival profile by a small multiscale tail recording resonance at larger angular scales, rather than studying only the endpoint window.

## 9. Concrete obstruction found

The naive Doob entropy identity alone cannot prove the theorem. Its total budget is

\[
\log(R_N/M),
\]

which may be almost the full input entropy even when `M` tends to infinity. It records how rare the fiber is, not how many successful atoms lie in it.

Likewise, exact fixed-point classification does not control approximate fixed points. The missing theorem is a uniform quantitative stability result for highly localized positive profiles under a sequence of arithmetic convolution operators.

This is, however, a genuine compression: both proposed ideas reduce to one state variable, the projective survival profile `h`, and one dynamical operation, arithmetic convolution followed by conditioning and rescaling.
