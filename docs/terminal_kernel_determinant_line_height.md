# Terminal coherent-kernel determinant degree and a height bound

The terminal coherent kernel has an exact determinant-line degree at
all row sizes `m=6k`. Its primitive polynomial Pluecker coordinates have
binary degree

```text
delta_k = h(6k-1,2k)
        = binom(6k,2k)*(2k-1)/(3*(4k+1))
```

in each row. This extends the previously established twelve-row degree
55 without assuming that localized six-row generators span at every
configuration. Keeping the **image** Pluecker factor is essential in
this calculation.

The degree gives a universal majority-core factor and the covolume bound

```text
log covol(ker_Z E_P) <= delta_k*A_0(6k)*w/2+o(w).        (1)
```

The resulting first-minimum bound remains above the actual-cut exclusion
threshold for every `k`. Additional content at coalition degenerations
is not ruled out or computed here; it is the arithmetic improvement
still needed by this approach.

## 1. Setup and the dimensions needed for one moving row

Put

```text
d=2k,       m=3d=6k,       n=m-1,
r=dim K_k = 2*(3d)!/(d! (d+1)! (d+2)!),
rho=h(m,d),       delta=h(n,d),       beta=h(n,d-1).
```

Here `h(a,c)` is the squarefree degree-`c` joint-kernel dimension
from [the Nagata balancing note](coherent_nagata_balancing.md).
Its bounded-walk formula gives

```text
rho=binom(m,d)-2binom(m,d-1)+binom(m,d-2),
delta=binom(n,d)-2binom(n,d-1)+binom(n,d-2),
beta=binom(n,d-1)-2binom(n,d-2)+binom(n,d-3),
rho=delta+beta.                                        (2)
```

A binomial coefficient with negative lower index is zero. The second
formula simplifies to

```text
delta=binom(3d,d)*(d-1)/(3*(2d+1)).                      (3)
```

Keep the directions `b_1,...,b_n` distinct, and let the last direction
be `t`. Over the field generated by the fixed directions, the coherent
matrix is a pencil

```text
E(t)=E_0+t E_1.
```

Its generic rank is `rho`. Source coordinates use one fixed rational
basis of the terminal constituent. Denote the lowering operators on
the fixed labels by `S=sum partial_i`, `T=sum b_i partial_i`.
Every coherent polynomial pencil has the form

```text
R(t)=A_0+t A_1+x_m B,
S A_1=T A_0=0,
C:=S A_0=T A_1=-B,
S C=T C=0.                                            (4)
```

Thus `C` lies in the `beta`-dimensional degree-`d-1` joint kernel on
`n` labels. If `C=0`, both `A_0,A_1` lie in the `delta`-dimensional
degree-`d` joint kernel on those labels.

## 2. The stacked rank is exactly `rho+delta`

An odd-block product of pair degree `(1^n)` and polynomial degree `d`
on `n=3d-1` labels has `d-1` blocks. Its total excess of block sizes
over three is two. Repeatedly apply the balancing identity to an
oversized block and a singleton. Once there are no singletons, the
product has one block of size five and `d-2` triple blocks. Consequently
every degree-`d` joint invariant on the fixed labels is spanned by
products

```text
f=h_I * product of (d-2) disjoint triple circuits,
|I|=5.                                                (5)
```

Add the moving label as the singleton in the balancing identity.
For any anchor `a in I`, that identity says that

```text
(t-b_a) f
```

is the coherent image of a constant source vector: its expansion
is a linear combination of products of `d` triple circuits on all
`m` labels, with coefficients depending only on the fixed `b_i`.
Choosing two distinct anchors in `I` shows that **both** constant
pencils `f` and linear pencils `t f` belong to the image of the
stacked map `Q |-> (E_0 Q,E_1 Q)`.

The kernel of the map from coherent pencils to `C` in (4) therefore
has dimension exactly `2 delta`. Its image is the whole
`beta`-dimensional joint kernel. To see surjectivity, choose a fixed
`t_0` distinct from the other directions. All-distinct coherent
surjectivity identifies the fiber image with a joint kernel of
dimension `rho`. The map from that fiber to `C=-partial_(x_m)R`
has kernel exactly the degree-`d` joint kernel on the fixed labels,
of dimension `delta`. Equation (2) now makes its image dimension
`beta`.

It follows that

```text
rank [E_0;E_1]=2 delta+beta=rho+delta.                  (6)
```

In particular, the constant polynomial kernel has dimension
`r-rho-delta`. This argument works for every distinct configuration
of the fixed directions in characteristic zero; it does not use a
numerical generic-rank extrapolation.

## 3. The image determinant line has degree `beta`

Let `H_t` be the target joint kernel. Its subspace of polynomials
independent of `x_m` is the fixed space `H_(n,d)` of dimension
`delta`. For each `C` in a basis of `H_(n,d-1)`, choose elements
of `H_(t_0)` and `H_(t_1)` having the same `x_m` coefficient `-C`,
where `t_0,t_1` are distinct allowed directions. Their linear
interpolation is a polynomial section `R_C(t)` of `H_t`:
substituting (4), or interpolating the equation
`(T+t partial_(x_m))R=0`, proves this for all `t`.

These `beta` linear sections and a constant basis of `H_(n,d)`
are independent at every finite `t`, because their `x_m`
coefficients are the independent vectors `-C`. The leading
coefficients of the linear sections are independent modulo
`H_(n,d)`: applying `T` to them recovers the independent `C`.
Their wedge therefore has degree exactly `beta` and no common
nonconstant polynomial factor in `t`. It is the primitive polynomial
Pluecker vector of the generic image line.

## 4. The source-kernel determinant line has degree `delta`

Let `l=r-rho`. The polynomial kernel of `E(t)` is a free saturated
submodule of `F[t]^r` of rank `l`. Choose a polynomial basis whose
leading coefficients are independent, by repeatedly removing a
leading-coefficient dependency to lower the sum of basis degrees.
Its wedge is primitive and its degree equals the sum of those basis
degrees. Its degree-zero basis vectors span precisely the constant
kernel. By (6), there are `r-rho-delta` such directions; each of the
remaining `delta` basis vectors has degree at least one. Thus the
primitive source-kernel wedge has degree at least `delta`.

Now retain both exterior factors. The matrix of maximal minors of
`E(t)`, of size `rho`, has rank one as an exterior tensor. It
factors over `F[t]` as

```text
(maximal minors of E(t))
 = c(t) * (primitive image Pluecker vector)
        * (primitive source-kernel Pluecker covector). (7)
```

Here the source factor uses complementary coordinates in the fixed
source volume form. The scalar `c(t)` is a polynomial: both primitive
coordinate tuples have gcd one, so a denominator could not cancel
from every entry. Each maximal minor has degree at most `rho`,
whereas the image factor has degree `beta`. Consequently

```text
deg(source wedge)+beta+deg c <= rho=delta+beta.
```

The lower bound already proved forces

```text
deg(source wedge)=delta,       deg c=0.                (8)
```

In particular, the polynomial-kernel minimal indices are exactly
`r-rho-delta` zeros and `delta` ones. This proves the degree without
requiring a pre-existing family of linear syzygies to span the whole
kernel. At twelve rows it gives 286 zeros and 55 ones, recovering
[the earlier pencil calculation](generic_terminal_coherent_syzygies.md).

The statement `deg c=0` concerns the univariate polynomial gcd when
one row moves and the other directions are fixed and distinct. It is
not a statement about the gcd of integer maximal minors after all
rows are specialized, and it does not discard either exterior factor.

There is also a global form of the exterior factor bookkeeping.
Choose primitive polynomial image and source tuples over the affine
parameter ring in all `b_i`. The scalar in their factorization of the
maximal-minor matrix is a polynomial. Applying (8) with each label
in turn as the moving row shows that this scalar has degree zero in
every `b_i`, hence is constant (depending on the fixed bases).
This is a polynomial identity, not a claim that the gcd of specialized
integer minors is constant.

For a `rho`-element set `J` of cut rows, let

```text
a_i(J)=#{S in J : i in S}.
```

The corresponding image Pluecker coordinate, in homogeneous binary
parameters, has row degree `beta-a_i(J)`: the raw minor has degree
`rho-a_i(J)` in that row, while its source factor has degree `delta`.
If this number is negative, that image coordinate is zero. The total
binary degrees of the two exterior factors are therefore

```text
source: m*delta,
image:  m*beta-rho*d,
combined: 2*d*rho,
```

exactly the degree of a rank-`rho` residual minor. At twelve rows
these are `660`, `308`, and `968`, respectively. Thus replacing
maximal minors by source-kernel coordinates without removing the
image factor would assign the wrong degree.

## 5. Homogeneous row degree and mandatory core content

Let `Delta=product_(i<j) det(P_i,P_j)` in homogeneous binary rows
`P_i=(X_i,Y_i)`. The all-distinct locus is the principal affine open
with coordinate ring `A=Q[X_i,Y_i,Delta^(-1)]`. This is a regular
unique-factorization domain, so `Pic(A)=0`. Constant rank makes the
coherent kernel a vector bundle on this open, and its determinant
is therefore a free invertible `A`-module. Choose a generator. Its
Pluecker coordinates generate the unit ideal locally, hence globally
on this affine open. Clearing only powers of `Delta` and removing
the polynomial gcd gives a primitive polynomial tuple
`W(P_1,...,P_m)` that remains nonzero at every distinct configuration.
This uses the determinant-line trivialization; polynomial gcd one
by itself would not exclude a common zero of codimension two.
The preceding degree calculation, performed over the function field
of all other rows, gives degree `delta` in each binary row. Label
symmetry gives the same degree for all rows.

The kernel is unchanged by simultaneous `SL_2` transformations of the
binary rows, since every coherent residual entry is a matching
invariant on its outside rows. A primitive polynomial coordinate
tuple is consequently transformed by a scalar character of `SL_2`.
That character is trivial. Thus every coordinate of `W` is an
`SL_2` invariant of row multidegree `(delta,...,delta)`.
After one fixed rational scaling all coordinates are integral.
Its nonvanishing on the distinct locus follows from the preceding
determinant-line construction.

For the extracted Gaussian rows, write `P_i=X_i+iY_i` in the usual
half-angle normalization. Since the coordinate shear has determinant
one,

```text
W(X_1,Y_1;...;X_m,Y_m)=W(P_1,Y_1;...;P_m,Y_m).
```

The diagonal torus forces every monomial of each coordinate to have
total `P` degree `m delta/2`. Its complement to a subset `T` can
carry at most `delta(m-|T|)` of that degree. Thus every monomial
has `P` degree on `T` at least

```text
e_T = delta*max(0,|T|-m/2).                            (9)
```

For a core factor `H_T` dividing the `P_i` with `i in T`, this
proves divisibility by `H_T^(e_T)`. The coordinate value is an
ordinary integer; primitive odd split support therefore supplies
its conjugate too. In the existing notation `n_T=Norm(H_T)`, the
common integer content has the mandatory factor

```text
G_majority=product_T n_T^(delta*max(0,|T|-3k)).         (10)
```

The same support argument adds over nested prime-allocation levels,
so (10) does not assume that every block has a new rational prime.
No exact upper bound for the specialized gcd is asserted.

## 6. Height bound and comparison with actual-cut exclusion

Use a fixed full core profile with `log n_T=w+o(w)` and correction
height `o(w)`, as in the
[higher-kernel evaluation note](higher_kernel_evaluation_heights.md).
Each bracket has logarithmic size `2^(m-2)w+o(w)`. By invariant
straightening, a fixed coordinate of `W` is a linear combination,
with constants depending only on `m`, of regular bracket monomials
with `m delta/2` factors. Thus

```text
log ||W(P)|| <= delta*m*2^(m-3) w+o(w).
```

The elementary identity

```text
sum_(t=0)^m binom(m,t) max(0,t-m/2)
 = (m/4) binom(m,m/2)
```

gives the logarithmic size of (10). Dividing the evaluated tuple
by its actual gcd, which is at least (10), gives

```text
log ||W(P)_primitive|| <= delta*A_0(m) w/2+o(w),
A_0(m)=m*2^(m-2)-(m/2)binom(m,m/2).                    (11)
```

The primitive evaluated tuple is the primitive exterior coordinate
vector of the saturated integer coherent kernel, whose Euclidean
norm is its lattice covolume. Minkowski therefore gives a nonzero
kernel vector with coefficient-height exponent at most

```text
B_k = delta_k*A_0(6k)/(2*(r_k-rho_k)).                 (12)
```

Fixed dimension and basis constants contribute only `o(w)`. This
is a covolume **upper bound**, not an exact primitive-height formula.
The actual-cut theorem excludes nonzero coherent-kernel vectors
below exponent `2^(2k+1)`. The comparison is:

| `k` | `delta_k` | `r_k-rho_k` | `B_k` | `B_k/2^(2k+1)` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 18 | 2.25 |
| 2 | 55 | 341 | 543.870968 | 16.995968 |
| 3 | 2,380 | 83,028 | 10,635.700246 | 83.091408 |
| 4 | 100,947 | 23,193,775 | 148,443.720622 | 289.929142 |
| 5 | 4,292,145 | 7,638,717,450 | 1,608,785.365571 | 785.539729 |

This particular bound fails to cross the threshold for every `k`,
not just the displayed cases. For even `d=2k>=4`,
`binom(3d,3d/2)<=2^(3d-2)`, so `A_0(3d)/2>=3d*2^(3d-4)`.
Also `binom(2d,d)<=2^(2d)`, and (3) and the source dimension give

```text
B_k/2^(d+1)
 > d*(d+1)^2*(d+2)*(d-1)/(64*(2d+1)) >= 25/8 > 1.
```

The last expression is increasing for `d>=4`; the case `k=1`
was computed exactly. Stirling's formula further gives

```text
B_k/2^(2k+1) ~ sqrt(pi)/(2 sqrt(2))*k^(9/2).
```

Thus the exact determinant degree improves raw selected-row
Hadamard bookkeeping, but the universal majority-core content alone
cannot supply the desired Minkowski contradiction. A stronger
lower bound for the actual common content of `W`, or another
arithmetic control on its primitive evaluation, could still change
that conclusion. Neither the degree argument nor the comparison
excludes such a refinement.
