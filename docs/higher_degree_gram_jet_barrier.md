# Higher-degree Gram jets and the source-product height barrier

The quadratic quotient in
[primal_dual_gram_contact_audit.md](primal_dual_gram_contact_audit.md)
extends to every binary degree and every contact-jet order. This gives
an exact higher-degree lattice sequence and a quantitative barrier for
all weighted source products, including their nonnegative combinations.
It does not rule out specially cancelling signed integer combinations
or exclude a first short frame.

All asymptotic statements below fix the row count `m`, polynomial
degree `d`, and jet order `h` before taking `w` to infinity. No
uniformity in these parameters is asserted.

## 1. The higher-degree evaluation quotient

Let `U=(z w_0) in SL_2(Z)` be one admissible integer frame, with complex
columns denoted `z,w_0` to distinguish the column from the height
parameter `w`. Write its primitive positive Gram polynomial as

```text
Q_U(X,Y)=A X^2+2BXY+C Y^2,
A=|z|^2, B=Re(bar(z)w_0), C=|w_0|^2,
AC-B^2=1.
```

Let `S_d=Z[X,Y]_d`, and define

```text
epsilon_d(P)=P(w_0,-z).
```

For `d>=1` there is an exact sequence

```text
0 -> Q_U S_(d-2) -> S_d -> Z[i] -> 0,                 (1)
```

where negative-degree spaces are zero. Indeed an integral
unimodular substitution takes evaluation at `(w_0,-z)` to evaluation
at `(1,i)`. Its image is all of `Z[i]`, and its rational kernel is
the ideal generated by `X^2+Y^2`. The transformed polynomial `Q_U`
is primitive, so Gauss's lemma gives the stated integral kernel.

Fix primitive real contact vectors `lambda_e`, their annihilating
linear forms `L_e(X,Y)=det(lambda_e,(X,Y))`, and pairwise coprime odd
split norms `d_e`. Let `D=product d_e`. Choose the orientation at
each prime power using the frame, and put `Delta=product G_e`, with
`Norm(Delta)=D`.

For positive integers `h` and `d>=h`, define the degree-`d` contact-jet
lattice

```text
Gamma_(d,h)=S_d intersect intersection_e (d_e,L_e)^h.  (2)
```

Here these are ordinary ideals of `Z[X,Y]`; the integer `d_e` has
degree zero. Then

```text
0 -> Q_U Gamma_(d-2,h-1) -> Gamma_(d,h)
  -> (Delta^h) -> 0                                  (3)
```

is exact under `epsilon_d`. For order zero use the full coefficient
lattice. When necessary, (2) also defines degrees below the jet order.

The full prime-power content of (3) can be checked as follows. In
coordinates taking `L_e` to `Y`, membership in `(d_e,Y)^h` says that
the coefficient of `X^(d-k)Y^k` is divisible by `d_e^(h-k)` for
`0<=k<h`. Consequently, for `d>=h-1`,

```text
[S_d:Gamma_(d,h)]=D^(h(h+1)/2).                       (4)
```

At a prime dividing `d_e`, write the transformed Gram polynomial as
`q_0 X^2+q_1 XY+q_2 Y^2`. The constant coefficient `q_0` is divisible
by `d_e`, while `q_1` is a unit modulo every prime of `d_e`, because
`q_1^2-4q_0q_2=-4`. It follows that

```text
((d_e,Y)^h:Q_U)=(d_e,Y)^(h-1).                        (5)
```

One elementary way to justify the colon equality is to use the
associated graded ring of the regular sequence `(d_e,Y)`. The initial
form of `Q_U` has coefficient `q_1 X` on its `Y` variable; this is a
nonzerodivisor in the polynomial ring over `(Z/d_e Z)[X]`. Thus
multiplication by `Q_U` increases ideal order by exactly one.

Equation (5) identifies the kernel in (3). Evaluation sends every
ideal `(d_e,L_e)` into `(G_e)`, so the image lies in `(Delta^h)`.
The index ratio from (4) for the source and its kernel is `D^h`,
exactly the index of `(Delta^h)` in `Z[i]`; hence the inclusion of
images is equality. This argument retains every exponent, even when
`d_e` is not squarefree.

For `P=sum p_k X^(d-k)Y^k`, use the coefficient norm

```text
||P||_B^2=sum_k p_k^2/binomial(d,k).
```

Cauchy--Schwarz and the binomial identity give

```text
|epsilon_d(P)| <= ||P||_B (|z|^2+|w_0|^2)^(d/2).
```

Thus, if `P in Gamma_(d,h)` is not divisible by `Q_U`,

```text
||P||_B >= D^(h/2)/||U||_F^d.                         (6)
```

The first-frame obstruction would be a polynomial below (6), together
with a proof that its polynomial is not a multiple of the unknown
positive Gram polynomial. Higher degree has not removed that kernel:
it is precisely the persistent principal ideal visible in (3).

The full lattice's determinant scale alone also stays above (6).
Indeed (4) gives a coefficient-volume scale
`D^(h(h+1)/(2(d+1)))`; changing to the displayed coefficient norm
costs only a fixed-degree constant. In the full profile of Section 2,
its logarithmic exponent minus the threshold exponent in (6) equals

```text
[A(d-2h)^2+A d+h(d-h)]/(d+1),
```

which is strictly positive for `d>=h>=1`. This compares the usual
volume guarantee with the required threshold. It is not a lower bound
for the actual shortest vector; in particular short vectors in the
Gram kernel still occur.

## 2. All weighted source products have an exact excess

Assume the full profile, including all private paired cuts. Put
`A=2^(m-3)`. The median clusters are exactly

```text
C=T or C={a} union T,
T subset {4,...,m},    a in {1,2,3},
C nonempty.
```

There are `4A-1` clusters. Their rational norms have
`log d_C=2w+o(w)`. The three anchor source vectors have logarithmic
height `o(w)` and each outside source vector has logarithmic height
`Aw+o(w)`; the frame itself has `log||U||_F=Aw+o(w)`.

For source forms `L_i(X,Y)=det(v_i,(X,Y))`, choose any nonnegative
integer multiplicities `n_i`, with `sum n_i=d`, and a positive jet
order `h`. Write `n(C)=sum_(i in C)n_i`. The canonical integral product

```text
P_n=M_n product_i L_i^n_i,
M_n=product_C d_C^max(h-n(C),0)                       (7)
```

belongs to `Gamma_(d,h)`. At each contact the factors from rows in
its cluster belong to `(d_C,L_C)`, and the missing factors are supplied
by the indicated power of `d_C`. No outside-unit assumption is needed
for this sufficient divisibility construction.

Since all `L_i` are real and nonzero, `Q_U` cannot divide `P_n`.
The logarithmic size of (7), in any fixed-degree coefficient norm, is

```text
log||P_n|| = [A sum_outside n_i
              +2 sum_C max(h-n(C),0)]w+o(w).         (8)
```

The product norm is within constants depending only on degree of the
product of linear-form norms. In particular coefficient cancellation
inside a product does not change this exponent.

The lower-bound threshold (6) has exponent

```text
h(4A-1)-dA.
```

Every anchor occurs in `A` clusters and every outside label in `2A`,
so

```text
sum_C n(C)=A sum_anchor n_i+2A sum_outside n_i.
```

Subtracting the threshold from (8) therefore gives the exact identity

```text
log||P_n||-[h(4A-1)-dA]w
 = [sum_C |n(C)-h|]w+o(w).                            (9)
```

This is the complete exponent audit for all multiplicities and all
jet orders. It shows the cost of both under-covering and over-covering
contacts. Merely increasing the symmetric power cannot evade it.
Three anchors, each repeated `h` times, give degree `3h` and excess
`h(A-1)w+o(w)`, improving the degree-two excess but not reaching the
threshold.

The multiplier in (7) is a specified sufficient choice. An arithmetic
reduction of it using additional valuation coincidences would need its
own local audit. No such reduction is silently included in (9).

## 3. The optimal product excess is strictly positive

For `m>=4` define

```text
c_m=min_(a,b>=0) [sum_(k=0)^(m-3) binomial(m-3,k)
                   (|kb-1|+3|a+kb-1|)-1].           (10)
```

Convexity and averaging over permutations of the anchors and of the
outside labels imply, for every multiplicity vector,

```text
sum_C |n(C)-h| >= h c_m.                              (11)
```

The two real variables in (10) are the averaged anchor and outside
multiplicities divided by `h`. This lower bound is sharp when the
rational minimizing multiplicities are cleared by a suitable fixed
choice of `h`.

The minimum is a finite rational calculation. Put `r=m-3`. It suffices
to check `(a,b)=(0,0),(1,0)` and

```text
(a,b)=(1-l/k,1/k),    1<=k<=r, 0<=l<=k.              (12)
```

These are the vertices of the absolute-value line arrangement in the
nonnegative quadrant; the objective is convex, piecewise linear, and
coercive. The first values are

| `m` | `A` | `c_m` | One minimizing `(a,b)` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 2 | 1 | `(1,0)` |
| 5 | 4 | 3 | `(1,0)` |
| 6 | 8 | 7 | `(1,0)` |
| 7 | 16 | 15 | `(1,0)` |
| 8 | 32 | 31 | `(1,0)` |
| 9 | 64 | 61 | `(2/5,1/5)` |
| 10 | 128 | `368/3` | `(1/2,1/6)` |
| 11 | 256 | `683/3` | `(1/3,1/6)` |
| 12 | 512 | 455 | `(3/7,1/7)` |

There is also a simple uniform positivity certificate. Choose one
anchor `a` and one outside label `j`; the clusters `{a}`, `{j}`,
and `{a,j}` all occur, and

```text
|n_a-h|+|n_j-h|+|n_a+n_j-h| >= h.
```

Thus `c_m>=1`, independently of the finite table. Every canonical
source product misses the ideal threshold by at least `hw+o(w)`.

The relative gap can decrease when the row count grows. For
`r=m-3`, the choice `a=0,b=2/r` gives the explicit upper bound

```text
c_m <= 8 binomial(r-1,floor((r-1)/2))-1
     = O(A/sqrt(r)).
```

For comparison, let `K` have the binomial distribution with parameters
`r,1/2`, and put `mu=E|K-r/2|`. If `b<=1/r`, the first term in
(10) is at least `A/2`; if `b>1/r`, minimization of the anchor term
about a median gives at least `3A mu/r`. Therefore

```text
c_m >= min(A/2,3A mu/r)-1,
mu=r binomial(r-1,floor((r-1)/2))/2^r.
```

In particular `c_m=Theta(A/sqrt(r))` as `r` tends to infinity. Thus
its ratio to `A` can fall inside an uncontrolled varying-row-count
profile error. This does not produce a polynomial below the ideal
threshold. Integer attainment may also require growing jet order and
degree, whereas all profile error estimates in this note fix these
parameters first.

If only nonprivate contacts are imposed, the analogous excess is
instead

```text
d+sum_(nonprivate C)|n(C)-h|.                         (13)
```

Indeed deleting the singleton clusters removes one occurrence of each
source multiplicity from the incidence sum. Formula (13) keeps the
unchanged full-profile source heights; private contact norms have
been removed only from the congruence conditions.

## 4. Nonnegative combinations cannot supply the missing cancellation

Fix a degree `d` and jet order `h`. There is a real unit vector `u`
whose angular distance from the zeros of every source linear form is
at least `pi/(2m)`, by choosing the midpoint of a largest gap between
their `m` projective zero directions. Orient each `L_i` so `L_i(u)>0`.
Then, with `c=sin(pi/(2m))`,

```text
L_i(u)>=c ||L_i||.
```

For any nonzero combination of the canonical degree-`d` products with
nonnegative integer coefficients, evaluation at `u` gives

```text
||sum_n a_n P_n||_B >= sum_n a_n P_n(u)
                    >= c^d max_(a_n>0) M_n product_i ||L_i||^n_i.
```

Together with (9)--(11), this combination still exceeds the ideal
threshold by at least `h c_m w+o(w)`. Hence a successful construction
from these products must use signed cancellation after this coherent
choice of row signs, or a separately justified reduction of their
integer multipliers. Formal Plucker identities may give the zero
polynomial, but that does not furnish the required nonzero witness.

This is a barrier for a precisely specified class of constructions.
It does not bound arbitrary signed combinations in the jet lattice,
and does not claim that the full source incidence cannot force such
a combination by an additional argument.

## 5. The full-lattice determinant scale also misses the threshold

Even the geometric-mean scale coming from the determinant of the full
jet lattice does not cross (6). Its value in coefficient space is
`D^(h(h+1)/(2(d+1)))`, up to constants for the fixed coefficient norm.
Under the full profile its logarithmic exponent minus the nonkernel
threshold is exactly

```text
h(h+1)(4A-1)/(d+1) - [h(4A-1)-dA]
 = [A(d-2h)^2+Ad+h(d-h)]/(d+1) > 0                  (14)
```

for `d>=h>=1`. At `d=2h`, this is `(2Ah+h^2)/(2h+1)`.
This is a comparison of determinant scales, not a lower bound on actual
successive minima. The Gram kernel may contain much shorter vectors;
volume alone does not control whether a short vector exits that kernel.

## 6. Exact certificate

Run `python3 docs/check_higher_degree_gram_jet_barrier.py`. The checker
constructs 21 jet lattices by exact integral congruence intersections,
verifies their full coefficient indices and Gaussian image indices,
and checks the evaluation norm inequality. It includes prime-power
depth two at 5. It also verifies 450 weighted profile exponent
identities, the displayed convex minima and their integer attainments.
The checker does not certify a bound for arbitrary signed combinations.
