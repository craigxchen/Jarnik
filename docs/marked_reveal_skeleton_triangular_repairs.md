# Marked reveal skeleton and triangular repair relations

## Status

This note develops the prime-reveal/renormalization framework using the marked-skeleton proof pattern from the supplied mathematical-discovery notes. It produces an exact independent family of repair relations along every successful leaf. It also identifies the remaining arithmetic obstruction. It does not prove the uniform endpoint bound.

## 1. Compress deterministic reveal chains

Let A be the set of successful squarefree orientation vectors whose angles lie in one endpoint interval I. Reveal the prime coordinates in any fixed order p_1,...,p_r. The successful prefixes form a rooted binary tree.

A node with only one nonempty child contributes no branching entropy and can be compressed into the next genuine branching node. The marked skeleton therefore consists only of nodes at which both signs of the current prime admit successful completions.

For a uniformly random successful leaf X,

    log |A| = sum_v P(X passes through v) h(a_v),

where a_v is the conditional mass of one child at v.

## 2. Every skeleton branch gives a repair relation

Fix a successful leaf X. At a branching node v revealing p_k, choose a successful comparison leaf Y_v in the child opposite to X. Since X and Y_v agree on all previously revealed primes, their difference vector

    r_v = X - Y_v

has zero coordinates before k, nonzero kth coordinate equal to +/-2, and coordinates in {0,+/-2} after k.

Their angles lie in I, hence

    |sum_j r_v(j) theta_j - 2 pi n_v| <= O_C(N^{-1/4})

for an integer n_v.

After division by 2, each branching node yields a bounded-coefficient repair relation

    s_v(k) theta_k + sum_{j>k} s_v(j) theta_j = pi n_v + O_C(N^{-1/4}),

where s_v(k)=+/-1 and every later coefficient lies in {0,+/-1}.

## 3. Triangular independence

Order the branching nodes encountered by X according to reveal time. Their coefficient rows are in row-echelon form: the first nonzero coordinate of the row from v is exactly the prime revealed at v.

Therefore the repair relations encountered along one successful leaf are linearly independent over every field of characteristic not equal to two.

This conclusion is deterministic and does not depend on genericity of the Gaussian-prime angles.

## 4. Why this is stronger than support overlap

Earlier repair-support arguments treated many relations as potentially dependent because subtracting two pairwise endpoint relations yields another pairwise relation. The marked-skeleton construction removes that ambiguity. Along one leaf, every new relation has a fresh pivot prime and cannot be generated by earlier rows.

Thus a leaf with b genuine branching events carries b independent, bounded-coefficient, exponentially accurate linear forms in the Gaussian-prime angles.

The branch entropy need not equal b, because highly unbalanced branches contribute little entropy. The probability-weighted version is

    log |A| = E_X sum_{v on X} h(a_v),

with each positive term attached to a distinct pivoted repair row.

## 5. Multiplicative interpretation

Let rho_j = pi_j / conjugate(pi_j). A repair row s_v gives

    eta_v = product_j rho_j^{s_v(j)}

with

    |eta_v - 1| <= O_C(N^{-1/4}).

The exponent matrix of the eta_v is triangular with diagonal entries +/-1. Hence the eta_v are multiplicatively independent as formal Laurent monomials in the rho_j.

Exact simultaneous equality eta_v=1 for even one nonzero row is ruled out by Gaussian unique factorization. The issue is quantitative simultaneous near-equality.

## 6. The endpoint calculation

For one row with conductor support height D_v,

    |eta_v - 1| >= exp(-D_v/2)

up to an absolute factor, unless eta_v=1. Since the analytic upper bound is exp(-H/4), one obtains only

    D_v >= H/2 - O_C(1).

For b rows, multiplying the individual inequalities gives

    product_v |eta_v-1| >= exp(-(1/2) sum_v D_v),

while the arc gives

    product_v |eta_v-1| <= exp(-bH/4+O_C(b)).

Because each D_v may be H/2+O(1), this remains exactly critical. Linear independence of the rows does not itself improve the product lower bound.

## 7. What a successful joint estimate must exploit

The triangular rows heavily reuse the unrevealed tail primes. Treating each row with its own denominator pays for that common tail repeatedly. A useful simultaneous estimate would have to charge shared tail arithmetic only once or sublinearly.

A sufficient inequality would have the schematic form

    product_v |eta_v-1| >= exp(-H/2 - O(b log b))

for a triangular family of b repair monomials supported in one conductor. Combined with the upper bound exp(-bH/4), this would contradict b >= 3 for large H.

However this inequality is false for arbitrary multiplicatively independent unit-circle rationals: independent near-one rational functions can share a common denominator while their numerators vanish independently. Any valid strengthening must use the conjugate-prime orientation structure and the fact that all eta_v arise from one successful-fiber reveal tree.

## 8. A renormalization-compatible joint state

For a node v, retain not only its scalar completion count but the tuple of all pivoted repair monomials generated below v. Unary reveal chains act by monomial substitution and can be compressed. A genuine branch appends one new pivoted monomial.

The natural complexity is therefore not the number of repair relations but the arithmetic complexity of their shared tail map

    tail orientation -> (eta_1,...,eta_b).

The endpoint theorem would follow from a quantitative statement that this map cannot send many successful tail orientations into a polydisc of radius O(N^{-1/4}) around (1,...,1) when its exponent matrix contains a large triangular pivot set.

This is a multivariate small-fiber theorem for a monomial map with coefficients restricted to {0,+/-1} and with Gaussian-prime specialization.

## 9. Concrete remaining target

Prove a joint Gaussian repair theorem:

> There is an absolute B such that, for every squarefree Gaussian conductor and every triangular {0,+/-1}-matrix S arising from a successful reveal skeleton, at most B rows of S can have their associated monomials simultaneously within O_C(N^{-1/4}) of 1 along one successful leaf.

A probability-weighted version bounding the total branch entropy is sufficient.

This target is genuinely stronger than the one-relation separation estimate and genuinely narrower than the original point-counting statement: it concerns an echelon family of near-one monomials generated by one filtration.

## 10. Scope check

The marked-skeleton transfer is valid and useful: it proves independence of the repair relations rather than merely abundance. But the currently available Gaussian lower bounds remain multiplicative row by row and therefore stay critical. The next advance must be a joint arithmetic estimate for the triangular family, not another scalar potential or another pairwise separation inequality.
