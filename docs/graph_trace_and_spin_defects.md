# Graph traces and integral spin defects

This continues [maximal_order_pair_defects.md](maximal_order_pair_defects.md).
There are two concrete results. Graph-product traces have an exact
dominant-cut formula, valid also for growing graphs in the extracted
regime. Three rows additionally give an integral three-radical spin
defect and an exact Heron equation. The latter uses special circle
identities, not a generic auxiliary-polynomial argument. Neither result
proves endpoint uniformity.

## 1. All graph traces are integers

Use the primitive pair data of the cited note. Thus

```text
h_ij=x_ij+i t_ij,
q_ij=N(h_ij),
G_ij=N(gcd_G(h_i,h_j)),
sqrt(q_ij)=sqrt(q_i)sqrt(q_j)/G_ij,
psi_ij=sqrt(q_ij)-x_ij>0.
```

All `x_ij` are positive and all `t_ij` are nonzero. Include the
anchor as vertex zero with `q_0=1`. Let `E` be the real multiquadratic
field generated by the row norms, and let `n=[E:Q]`.
For a simple graph `Gamma` on any of the selected vertices, put

```text
Psi_Gamma=product_(e in Gamma) psi_e,
C_Gamma=(1/n)Tr_(E/Q)(Psi_Gamma).
```

Then **`C_Gamma` is an integer**, not merely an integer divided by
the field degree. To prove this, expand the product in the radicals
`sqrt(q_e)` and integers `x_e`. Every radical monomial is an
eigenvector for a quadratic Galois character. Averaging kills every
nontrivial character. Every surviving monomial is both rational and
an algebraic integer, hence an ordinary integer.

If the norm classes of the graph's nonanchor vertices are independent,
the exact expansion is

```text
C_Gamma = sum_(F subset edges, every vertex has even degree in F)
  (-1)^(|edges|-|F|) product_(e in F)sqrt(q_e)
                         product_(e outside F)x_e.             (1)
```

With dependent norm classes the condition is instead that the edge
character of `F` is trivial. This distinction matters for a growing
graph: six-wise separation alone does not imply full independence.

## 2. A dominant-cut theorem with an explicit error

For an embedding `sigma`, write its row signs as `epsilon_i`, and
let `c_sigma` be the number of graph edges crossing that sign cut.
Let

```text
r=|edges|,
c=max_sigma c_sigma,
lambda_e=log((sqrt(q_e)+x_e)/|t_e|),
B=sum_e log|t_e|,
b=sum_e |lambda_e-W/4|.
```

Each edge defect is positive when its character is positive and
negative when its character is negative. Its absolute value has the
exact logarithm used in the pair-defect note. Consequently

```text
sign(sigma(Psi_Gamma))=(-1)^c_sigma,
log|sigma(Psi_Gamma)|
 = B+(2c_sigma-r)W/4+e_sigma,       |e_sigma|<=b.                (2)
```

Suppose

```text
rho=n exp(-W/2+2b)<1/2.                                       (3)
```

All embeddings with `c_sigma=c` have the same sign. Their total
absolute contribution is at least
`exp(B+(2c-r)W/4-b)`. All other embeddings together contribute at
most `rho` times this lower bound. Thus their signs cannot cancel
the maximal-cut contribution, and

```text
sign C_Gamma=(-1)^c,
B+(2c-r)W/4-b-log(2n)
 <= log|C_Gamma| <= B+(2c-r)W/4+b.                            (4)
```

No independence of all norm classes was used here. If every edge
character is nontrivial, character averaging gives
`average_sigma c_sigma=r/2`. For a nonempty graph the identity
embedding has zero crossing edges. Hence `c>r/2`, and the positive
integer `2c-r` is at least one.

In particular, if

```text
B+b+log n=o(W),
```

then every nonempty graph satisfies

```text
log|C_Gamma|=(2c-r)W/4+o(W)>=W/4-o(W).                       (5)
```

This conclusion applies simultaneously to **every graph** on the
centrally selected `k=O(log M)` vertices. Indeed there are at most
`binom(k,2)` edges, each pair has logarithmic error
`O(kW/sqrt(M)+1)`, and `log n<=k log 2`. Their total error is
`O(k^3 W/sqrt(M)+k^2+k)=o(W)`. The condition that all edge
characters are nontrivial follows already from the available
squareclass separation.

Thus actual circle identities cannot cause additional leading
cancellation in this particular family of traces: the maximal-cut
terms have a common sign. This does not exclude sums of distinct
graph products with carefully chosen coefficients or further valid
integral divisions of such sums.

There is a stronger exact sign statement for an Eulerian graph.
Every cut crosses an even number of edges, so `Psi_Gamma` is a
totally positive algebraic integer. This statement is valid without
any asymptotic height assumptions.

## 3. Cycle traces retain the elementary phase identity

Suppose a simple cycle has `l` vertices whose norm classes are
independent apart from the anchor. Its only even subgraphs are the
empty graph and the full cycle. Orient the cycle coherently.
Telescoping the primitive Gaussian transitions gives the positive
integer

```text
P=product_cycle h_ij
 =product_vertices q_i / product_cycle G_ij
 =product_cycle sqrt(q_ij).
```

Formula (1) becomes

```text
C_cycle=P+(-1)^l product_cycle x_ij.                         (6)
```

For an odd cycle the leading terms cancel, but exactly by the
already present identity obtained from the real part of
`product h_ij=P`. Its remaining terms have at least two primitive
residues. For an even cycle the two terms in (6) add. Accordingly
the exponents in (5) are `(l-2)W/4` for an odd cycle and `lW/4`
for an even cycle. The three-cycle trace in the earlier note is
the first instance, not an exceptional higher-order saving.

## 4. An integral three-radical spin defect

For three rows define

```text
U=q_3 G_12/(G_13 G_23),
V=q_1 G_23/(G_12 G_13),
Z=q_2 G_13/(G_12 G_23).                                     (7)
```

All three are **positive integers**, even with arbitrary prime
powers and row corrections. For example, in the Gaussian integers,

```text
gcd(h_1,h_3) gcd(h_2,h_3) divides h_3 gcd(h_1,h_2),
```

because `min(a,c)+min(b,c)<=c+min(a,b)` at every Gaussian prime.
Taking norms proves the assertion for `U`. Furthermore

```text
UV=q_13,       UZ=q_23,       VZ=q_12.                        (8)
```

Let the three arguments be ordered as `phi_1<phi_2<phi_3`, all
within a sufficiently small interval, and use their canonical
oriented pair residues. Then

```text
L=t_12 sqrt(U)+t_23 sqrt(V)-t_13 sqrt(Z)
  =sqrt(2 psi_12 psi_23 psi_13)>0.                            (9)
```

In particular `L` is an algebraic integer in the extension generated
by the three displayed radicals. To verify (9), put
`A=phi_2-phi_1`, `B=phi_3-phi_2`, and use the exact identity

```text
sin A+sin B-sin(A+B)
 =4 sin(A/2)sin(B/2)sin((A+B)/2).
```

Multiply by the positive square root of
`P=sqrt(q_12 q_23 q_13)`, an integer. Equations (7)--(8) identify
the three coefficients on the left, and
`psi_ij=2sqrt(q_ij)sin^2((phi_j-phi_i)/2)` identifies the right.

Writing `A_2=t_12^2 U`, `B_2=t_23^2 V`, `C_2=t_13^2 Z`, the
exact Heron identity is

```text
A_2^2+B_2^2+C_2^2-2A_2 B_2-2A_2 C_2-2B_2 C_2
 =-4(t_12 t_23 t_13)^2.                                    (10)
```

For a direct algebraic verification, reanchor the triple at its
first row. Its primitive numerators become `a+i b,c+i d` and
their transition, with common norm gcd `G`. Then the three
quantities `A_2,B_2,C_2` are respectively

```text
b^2(c^2+d^2)/G,       (ad-bc)^2/G,
d^2(a^2+b^2)/G.
```

The identity follows from
`A_2+C_2-B_2=2bd(ac+bd)/G` and
`(ac+bd)^2-(a^2+b^2)(c^2+d^2)=-(ad-bc)^2`.
The pair norms and residues in (7)--(10) are intrinsic under this
reanchoring, so this verifies the general case.

If the three radical classes are independent, (10) gives exactly

```text
Norm_(Q(sqrt U,sqrt V,sqrt Z)/Q)(L)
 =16(t_12 t_23 t_13)^4.                                    (11)
```

Indeed multiplying the four sign combinations modulo simultaneous
negation gives the left side of (10), and all eight conjugates
give its square. Without independence, (9)--(10) remain valid;
the eight formal sign product must not be called the field norm.

Under the extracted pair heights,

```text
log U=log V=log Z=W/4+o(W),
log L=-3W/8+o(W).
```

The tiny three-radical combination is therefore a real positive
structural constraint. In the degree-eight case its norm still
lands exactly at the residue scale: two conjugates have logarithm
`-3W/8+o(W)` and the other six have logarithm `W/8+o(W)`.
Thus this valid spin construction introduces neither an omitted
conductor denominator nor a norm contradiction. The simultaneous
compatibility of these Heron equations across many triples remains
a possible additional arithmetic problem.

## 5. All sign words in one character: exact high-order differences

Here assume the uncorrected independent-block model on `r` rows.
Let `H_S`, for `S subset [r]`, be independent, mutually
conjugate-coprime Gaussian blocks; put `n_S=N(H_S)` and
`h_i=product_(S containing i)H_S`. For a sign vector
`c in {1,-1}^r`, put

```text
l_S(c)=sum_(i in S)c_i,
g_c=product_S H_S^max(l_S(c),0)
                 bar(H_S)^max(-l_S(c),0),
Q=product_(|S| odd)n_S,
D_c=product_S n_S^floor(|l_S(c)|/2),
X_c=Re(g_c),       |g_c|=D_c sqrt(Q).                         (12)
```

These words all have the same displayed radical; `Q` is not assumed
squarefree. Write `h_i=|h_i|exp(i phi_i)`. Since `g_-c=bar(g_c)`,
the real words are indexed by signs modulo simultaneous negation.
The positive sign vector gives

```text
D_+=product_S n_S^floor(|S|/2)=lcm_c D_c.
```

For every even-cardinality subset `J subset [r]`, there is the exact
integer identity

```text
sum_(c modulo +/-) (product_(j in J)c_j)(D_+/D_c)X_c
 =2^(r-1)(-1)^(|J|/2)
       product_(j in J)t_j product_(j outside J)x_j.           (13)
```

This follows by expanding
`product_(j in J)(h_j-bar(h_j))`
times `product_(j outside J)(h_j+bar(h_j))`. Its term indexed by
`c` is the unprimitive signed product, equal to
`(D_+/D_c)g_c`. Pairing opposite signs proves (13).

In particular, when `r` is even, the highest Walsh difference has
angular order `r` and equals a small nonzero integer multiple of
`product t_i`. It reaches the residue scale exactly. The high-order
cancellation is real; it does not make that integer vanish.

### Restricted families and their denominator cost

Let `T` be a nonempty symmetric subset of the sign cube, and choose
nonzero symmetric rational weights `b_c=b_-c` with support exactly
`T`. Set

```text
a_S=max_(c in T)|l_S(c)|,
D_T=lcm_(c in T)D_c,
F(phi)=sum_(c modulo +/- in T)b_c cos(sum_i c_i phi_i).
```

Because `|l_S(c)|` has the fixed parity `|S| mod 2`, independence
of the blocks gives the exact common envelope

```text
D_T sqrt(Q)=product_S n_S^(a_S/2).                           (14)
```

Multiply the rational weights by a common integer denominator first,
and absorb that factor into `F`. The resulting integral linear
combination of the `X_c` has distinguished real value
`D_T sqrt(Q) F(phi)`. For fixed weights this is a fixed cost; moving
weights must retain the height of the denominator. Thus a restricted
family reduces the common denominator, and its available order of cancellation
must be compared with that reduction.

Let `d` be the order of the nonzero analytic function `F` at
`phi=0`. Then the following exact combinatorial inequality holds:

```text
2^(-r) sum_(S subset [r]) a_S >= d/2.                        (15)
```

Here is a proof specific to these sign-word combinations. Replace
the trigonometric expression by the nonzero multiaffine polynomial

```text
P(z)=sum_(c in T)(b_c/2) product_i(1+i c_i z_i).
```

The identity
`F(phi)=product_i cos(phi_i) P(tan(phi_1),...,tan(phi_r))`
shows that `P` also has order `d` at zero. Along the coordinate
subspace `z_i=i` for `i in S`, its generic vanishing order is

```text
nu_S=(|S|-a_S)/2.
```

To see equality, rather than just a lower bound, take the terms
with the minimum number of positive signs in `S`. Distinct
positive-sign sets give distinct lowest derivative monomials in
those variables; for each fixed set, the outside products
`product_(i outside S)(1+i c_i z_i)` are a basis, so the nonzero
weights cannot cancel them all. Symmetry gives the same order at
`z_i=-i`.

The reciprocal polynomial `R(s)=product_i s_i P(1/s)` has total
degree at most `r-d`. At a binary vertex with coordinates in
`{i,-i}`, its multiplicity is at least `nu_S+nu_(S complement)`.
Inversion preserves multiplicities at these nonzero coordinates.
The multiplicity grid inequality, with its elementary proof in
[hermitian_polynomial_barrier.md](hermitian_polynomial_barrier.md),
therefore gives

```text
2 average_S nu_S <= (r-d)/2.
```

Since `2 average nu_S=r/2-average a_S`, this is (15).
Thus the earlier general polynomial ceiling applies here only after
this explicit identification of the actual primitive word
denominators and their moment-cancellation order.

With uniform block weights `log n_S=W/2^r+o(W)` for fixed `r`,
equation (14) has logarithmic height
`(W/2) average a_S+o(W)`. Angular order `d` contributes the upper
bound `exp(-dW/4+o(W))`. Inequality (15) says the resulting
exponent is nonnegative. Consequently symbolic cancellation of
moments in any fixed restricted sign family, using this exact
common-denominator normalization, does not by itself give a
negative exponent. For even `r`, the full cube with `J=[r]` attains
equality and its exact surviving integer is (13).

This is not a lower bound for `F` at the actual angles. Additional
arithmetic cancellation at those particular angles, a further
proved integral divisor of the sum, or different moving weights
remain outside the statement. Those are the precise additional
inputs needed if this sign-word strategy is to surpass (15).

## Verification

The integrality of (7) and the exact integer identity (10) passed
1,000 Gaussian-integer triple checks with random primitive inputs,
seed 9019. The proofs above retain every prime power. No new Lean
formalization is claimed. Two independent audits checked the spin
normalization and the restricted-sign proof, including the precise
generic multiplicities. Their denominator and parity wording corrections
are included above.
