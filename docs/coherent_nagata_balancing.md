# Odd-block balancing and the full coherent differential kernel

For `m=6k` and every distinct characteristic-zero configuration `b_i`,
the terminal coherent evaluation image is exactly the squarefree
homogeneous degree-`2k` joint kernel of `D_1` and `D_b`. This holds on
the entire distinct-point locus, not just at generic configurations.
Consequently both its rank and the joint-kernel dimension are constant
on that locus, with exact value

```text
rho_k=binom(6k,2k)-2binom(6k,2k-1)+binom(6k,2k-2).
```

The proof uses the Cox–Nagata generator theorem and an
explicit determinant identity whose only division is by `b_j-b_a`.
It does not give a bounded-height inverse or generate the kernel of the
coherent evaluation map by localized six-row relations.

## 1. The precise Cox–Nagata grading

Work over a field `F` of characteristic zero. Let the `b_i` be pairwise
distinct elements of `F`, and use variables `X_i,Y_i`. The additive
plane acts by

```text
X_i |-> X_i,       Y_i |-> Y_i+(alpha+beta*b_i)X_i.
```

Write `H_I(X,Y)` for the determinant with columns indexed by an ordered
odd set `I=(i_0,...,i_(2s))` and rows

```text
X, bX, ..., b^s X, Y, bY, ..., b^(s-1)Y.                 (1)
```

Thus `H_(i)=X_i`. Its degree in each pair `(X_i,Y_i)` is one for
`i in I`, zero otherwise, and its total `Y` degree is `s`.
Reordering `I` multiplies `H_I` by the permutation sign.

The generator theorem of Castravet–Tevelev is stated with this
normalization in [Theorem 1.1 of their paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0505337).
For the hypothesis needed here, use [Sturmfels–Xu, Section 7,
Theorem 7.1 and equation (18)](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/31717?nt=1):
that section assumes every Pluecker coordinate of the acting two-plane
is nonzero. In our chart those coordinates are the differences
`b_j-b_i`. Their interleaved determinant rows differ from (1) by a
fixed sign. The theorem says that the invariant ring is generated by
all the odd determinants (1).

A squarefree polynomial `f(z_1,...,z_m)` of homogeneous degree `d`
satisfies

```text
D_1 f=D_b f=0,
D_1=sum_i partial_(z_i),       D_b=sum_i b_i partial_(z_i)
```

if and only if its homogenization

```text
f_hat(X,Y)=(product_i X_i) f(Y_1/X_1,...,Y_m/X_m)         (2)
```

is invariant. This is an isomorphism onto the invariant component of
pair multidegree `(1,...,1)` and total `Y` degree `d`. It is a
polynomial because `f` is squarefree. Infinitesimal invariance and
invariance under the additive plane agree in characteristic zero.

Since the generator degrees are nonnegative in every pair degree,
the generator theorem implies that this component is spanned by
products of `H_I` whose supports partition all `m` labels into odd
blocks. If there are `r` blocks, their degrees give

```text
d=sum_blocks (|I|-1)/2=(m-r)/2,       r=m-2d.             (3)
```

This grading statement alone does not reduce the larger blocks to
triples. The next identity does so when sufficiently many singleton
blocks are present.

## 2. A determinant balancing identity

Put `h_I(z)=H_I(1,z)`. Fix an ordered set
`I=(a,i_1,...,i_(2s))` and a label `j` outside it. For `1<=r<=2s`,
let `I_r=(i_1,...,i_(r-1),i_(r+1),...,i_(2s))`, retaining this order.
Then

```text
(b_j-b_a) h_I
 = sum_(r=1)^(2s) (-1)^(r+s-1)
     (product_(ell != r) (b_(i_ell)-b_a))
     h_(a,j,i_r) h_(I_r).                               (4)
```

Equivalently, after homogenizing all labels in `I union {j}`,

```text
(b_j-b_a) H_I X_j
 = sum_(r=1)^(2s) (-1)^(r+s-1)
     (product_(ell != r) (b_(i_ell)-b_a))
     H_(a,j,i_r) H_(I_r).                               (5)
```

For `s>=2`, each summand replaces blocks of sizes `(2s+1,1)` by
blocks of sizes `(3,2s-1)`. Its coefficients have no denominator
except the nonzero factor `b_j-b_a` when solving for `H_I X_j`.

Here is a coefficient proof, including the sign. The determinant
`h_I` is unchanged by `z_i |-> z_i+alpha+beta*b_i`, because the
changes to its lower rows are combinations of its upper rows.
First set `z_a=0`. If `J` is an `s`-subset of the ordered list
`(i_1,...,i_(2s))`, the coefficient of `z_J` in `h_I` is

```text
(-1)^(s(3s+1)/2 + sum_(i_r in J) r)
  V(J) V(I minus J),
V(c_1,...,c_t)=product_(u<v) (b_(c_v)-b_(c_u)).
```

Comparing this coefficient with those of `z_(i_r) h_(I_r)` reduces
to the elementary partial-fraction identity

```text
sum_(u in J) 1/((b_u-b_a) product_(v in J minus {u})(b_u-b_v))
 = (-1)^(s-1)/product_(u in J)(b_u-b_a).                 (6)
```

For example, (6) follows by evaluating the partial fractions of
`1/product_(u in J)(t-b_u)` at `t=b_a`. It gives

```text
h_I |_(z_a=0)
 = sum_r (-1)^(r+s-1)
     (product_(ell != r)(b_(i_ell)-b_a)) z_(i_r) h_(I_r).
                                                               (7)
```

Now subtract the unique affine function of `b` agreeing with `z`
at labels `a,j`. Both new coordinates `z'_a,z'_j` are zero, and

```text
z'_(i_r)=h_(a,j,i_r)(z)/(b_j-b_a).
```

All the determinants in (7) are invariant under this subtraction.
Substitution proves (4); homogenization proves (5). In particular,
(5) is a polynomial identity valid before dividing by any difference.

## 3. Triple generation below one third of the labels

**Balancing theorem.** If `m>=3d`, every squarefree homogeneous
polynomial of degree `d` killed by `D_1,D_b` is a linear combination
of products of `d` triple circuits on pairwise disjoint triples,
with the remaining `m-3d` labels unused. The coefficients are over
`F`; the conversion (5) uses only inverses of differences of distinct
`b_i`.

To prove this, start with an odd-block product from (3). If it has
an oversized block, meaning size at least five, it also has a
singleton. Indeed, without a singleton all `m-2d` blocks would
have size at least three, and one would have size at least five,
forcing

```text
m >= 3(m-2d)+2 > m
```

when `m>=3d`. Apply (5) to such a pair. In each resulting product,
the nonnegative quantity

```text
sum_blocks max(0,(|I|-3)/2)
```

decreases by one. Induction terminates with only triples and
singletons. The degree is `d`, so there are exactly `d` triples.
After dehomogenization each singleton is one, and

```text
h_(a,b,c)=(b_c-b_b)z_a-(b_c-b_a)z_b+(b_b-b_a)z_c.        (8)
```

This proves the theorem for every distinct configuration. It uses
no dimension formula and no generic straightening claim.

## 4. Application to the terminal coherent map and constant rank

Now let `m=6k`. For the coherent map in
[the differential note](coherent_cut_evaluation_differentials.md),
write

```text
E_b: K_k -> F[x_1,...,x_m]_(squarefree, degree 2k),
H_b=ker D_1 intersection ker D_b in that target.
```

The differential identity already gives `image(E_b) subset H_b`.
For a triple `(a,b,c)`, let

```text
T_(a,b,c)=det(nu_a,nu_b,nu_c),       nu_i=(P_i^2,P_iY_i,Y_i^2).
```

The product of `2k` such determinants on disjoint triples belongs
to `K_k`: collapsing `2k+1` rows puts two collapsed rows in one
triple. At `nu_i=(1,b_i,b_i^2)` the leading perturbation under
`nu_i |-> nu_i+lambda*x_i*(0,0,1)` is precisely the product of
the corresponding `2k` forms (8). The balancing theorem with
`d=2k` therefore proves

```text
image(E_b)=H_b                 for every distinct b.    (9)
```

There is a useful rank consequence which does not require a
Hilbert-function formula. Over the irreducible open set of distinct
configurations, both `E_b` and the matrix for `(D_1,D_b)` have
polynomial entries in `b`. Let their generic image and kernel
sizes in (9) be `rho`. At every specialization, matrix rank can
only fall from its generic maximum. Thus

```text
rank(E_b) <= rho <= dim(H_b).
```

Equality (9) forces equality throughout. Hence both dimensions
are `rho` at every distinct configuration. The same argument
applies to the disjoint-triple matrix for any `m>=3d`.

## 5. Exact rank from the caterpillar monomials

The full squarefree joint-kernel dimension is constant on the distinct
locus for any fixed `m,d`, even when `m<3d`. Indeed, the matrix whose
columns are the odd-block products from (3) has polynomial entries in
`b` and its image equals that kernel everywhere by the generator
theorem. The same rank/kernel squeeze from Section 4 applies.

[Sturmfels–Xu, Example 7.6, equation (20), and Theorem 7.10](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/31717?nt=1)
give a caterpillar Sagbi degeneration with generator monomials

```text
X_(i_0) Y_(i_1) X_(i_2) ... Y_(i_(2s-1)) X_(i_(2s)),
i_0<i_1<...<i_(2s).                                    (10)
```

One can use this degeneration to compute the generic multigraded
Hilbert function. This does not assert that the affine plane
`span{1,b}` itself has that Sagbi basis: an arbitrary two-plane whose
first row has no zero entries can be put into this form by scaling
the `Y_i`, which preserves the pair and total-`Y` gradings. The
constant-dimension result then transfers the computed dimension to
every distinct `b`.

In the squarefree pair multidegree, a monomial is a word of length
`m` with `d` letters `Y` and `m-d` letters `X`. It occurs as a
product of (10) precisely when its positions can be partitioned
into `L=m-2d` alternating odd chains, each starting and ending in
`X`. This is equivalent to its prefix height

```text
height = number of X letters - number of Y letters
```

staying in the interval `[0,L]`. Necessity follows because each
chain contributes either zero or one to any prefix height.
For sufficiency, scan the word, maintaining chains which are active
when they end in `X` and inactive when they end in `Y`. Append a
`Y` to an active chain. Append an `X` to an inactive chain if one
exists, and otherwise start a new chain. The number of active
chains is the current height. The lower bound ensures that a
`Y` always has an active chain; the upper bound ensures that
starting a new chain never creates more than `L` chains. At the
end the height is `L`, so there are exactly `L` chains and all
are active. These are the required odd chains.

Thus the dimension is the number of walks from `0` to `L` in
`m` steps, with increments `+1,-1` and constrained to `[0,L]`.
Here `m>0` and `L>=1`. If `L<=0`, no odd-block partition of
the nonempty label set exists, and the component is zero.
The two-barrier reflection formula gives

```text
h(m,d)=sum_(j in Z) [binom(m,d+j(L+2))
                    -binom(m,d-1+j(L+2))],
L=m-2d,                                               (11)
```

where binomial coefficients outside `[0,m]` vanish. Equivalently,
(11) follows from the usual difference of reflected unrestricted
walk counts and successive reflections in the barriers `-1,L+1`.
It is also characterized by the finite recurrence which starts
with one walk at height zero and at each step sends the count at
height `h` to `h-1,h+1` when those heights remain in `[0,L]`.

For the rectangular terminal case `m=6k,d=L=2k`, only `j=0,1`
contribute in (11). Symmetry of the binomial coefficients gives

```text
rank(E_b)=h(6k,2k)
 = binom(6k,2k)-2binom(6k,2k-1)+binom(6k,2k-2).         (12)
```

The first values, for `k=1,...,8`, are

```text
4, 121, 4488, 177859, 7283640, 304253964,
12880883408, 550649378199.
```

By the [Specht filtration](kernel_specht_filtration.md), the source
in this case has dimension

```text
r_k = 2*(6k)! / ((2k)! (2k+1)! (2k+2)!).
```

Hence the source-kernel dimension is exactly `r_k-h(6k,2k)`
for every distinct configuration. At twelve rows this is
`462-121=341`. The six-row rank also has the independent
[explicit minor proof](six_row_coherent_kernel_and_content.md).

## 6. Arithmetic scope and verification

The conclusion identifies the coherent image and removes its
possible rank-jump locus among distinct points. It does not yet
show that localized six-row numerical syzygies span the source
kernel at every point. Nor does (5) provide a small integral
lifting: after clearing denominators, its products of pair
separations can have substantial height and content. These
arithmetic costs must be controlled before this structural result
can imply the required short-relation descent or a radius exponent
gain.

The standard-library checker
[check_coherent_nagata_balancing.py](check_coherent_nagata_balancing.py)
checks (4) coefficientwise, including permutations and rational
configurations, and recursively balances several twelve-label
odd-block products to products of four triple circuits. It also checks
the bounded-word construction and the walk/reflection rank formulas. The proof
above is uniform in `s`; finite checks are sign and implementation
audits.
