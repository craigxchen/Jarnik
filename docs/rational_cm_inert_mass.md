# Rational CM boundary contacts with negligible inert mass

On the fixed rational curve

```text
E: y^2=x^3-2x,    P=(2,2),    [i](x,y)=(-x,iy),
x(nP)=A_n/B_n^2,    B_n>0,    gcd(A_n,B_n)=1,
```

every fixed finite set of integer shifts `K` admits positive integers
`N_j -> infinity` for which

```text
sum_(k in K) sum_(p=3 mod4) v_p(B_(N_j-k)) log p = o(N_j^2),  (1)
log B_(N_j-k) = (N_j-k)^2 hhat(P)+o(N_j^2)  for every k.     (2)
```

Here `hhat(Q)=lim_r 4^(-r)h(x(2^r Q))/2` is the absolute
degree-one canonical height. The point `P` has infinite order:
the good reduction counts at three and five are four and ten,
so prime-to-characteristic torsion injection forces rational torsion
to have order dividing two, while `P` is not two-torsion.

Thus a universal positive inert-mass proportion for rational
elliptic boundary contacts is false, even with finitely many
simultaneous rational contacts. This does not construct a lattice
arc or prove a uniform lattice-arc bound. It does not resolve the
specific denominator sequence for `y^2=x^3-2`, `P=(3,5)`.

## 1. A finite Boolean decomposition

Work over `F=Q(i)` with `O_F=Z[i]`. Fix a squarefree product `m`
of odd rational primes `q=1 mod4`. Write each `q=pi_q bar(pi_q)`
and let `J` be the set of these `2 omega(m)` Gaussian prime factors.
For `S subset J`, choose

```text
alpha_S=product_(pi in S) pi,       alpha_empty=1.
```

Generators differ only by units, which do not affect denominator
ideals. Take any nonzero integer `t`, put `R=tP`, and define `D_S`
to be the denominator ideal of `[alpha_S]R`, with every prime over
`2m` removed. A denominator exponent is
`max(0,-v(x([alpha_S]R))/2)`.

Fix a prime ideal `v` away from `2m`. If it does not divide
`D_J`, it divides none of the `D_S`. Otherwise the annihilator
of the reduction of `R`, as an `O_F`-module element, is a principal
ideal `(delta)` dividing `(m)`. It is squarefree, and its support
is a subset `U` of `J`. The reduction of `[alpha_S]R` is the identity
exactly when `U subset S`.

Moreover, whenever `U subset S`, the full denominator depth is
the same positive integer `a_v`: apply the isogeny `[m/alpha_S]`
to `[alpha_S]R` in the formal group. Its differential is a unit
at `v`, so it preserves the valuation of the formal parameter.
The result is `[m]R`, up to a unit automorphism. Consequently

```text
v(D_S)=a_v 1_(U subset S).                                  (3)
```

The annihilator is asserted to divide `(m)` only at primes dividing
`D_J`. This qualification avoids any claim about the other reduction
orders of `R`.

Define a priori fractional ideals

```text
C_T=product_(S subset T) D_S^((-1)^(|T|-|S|)),   T subset J.   (4)
```

Boolean inversion of (3) gives

```text
v(C_T)=a_v 1_(U=T).
```

Hence the `C_T` are integral and pairwise coprime, and
`D_S=product_(T subset S) C_T`. This is a decomposition of full
prime-power intersection multiplicities, not a radical identity.

The denominator/formal-group conventions and the unit-isogeny
facts are standard; see [Streng, Sections 2 and 3](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~strengtc/cmeds.pdf).
The Boolean decomposition here follows directly from (3).

## 2. Which cells can contain inert primes?

Let `p=3 mod4`, so `(p)` is one prime of `O_F`, with residue field
`F_(p^2)`. Since `R` is rational, its reduction is fixed by
the `p`-power Frobenius. Frobenius conjugates the CM action:

```text
Frob_p([alpha]R)=[bar(alpha)]R.
```

Thus its annihilator ideal is conjugation invariant. The subset
`U` in (3) must be a union of complete pairs
`{pi_q,bar(pi_q)}`. Therefore an inert prime can occur only in
a cell `C_T` whose label is a union of such pairs.

There is also an exact consistency check: rationality of `R` gives
`bar(C_T)=C_(bar(T))`. If `T != bar(T)`, pairwise coprimality
implies `gcd(C_T,bar(C_T))=1`, which excludes inert primes in that
cell. Stable cells can contain split primes too; only an upper
bound on inert mass is being inferred.

## 3. The cell masses and their normalization

Choose an infinite subsequence of integers `t` on which every
`x([alpha_S]tP)` stays bounded at the complex embeddings. Such
subsequences exist by avoiding the finitely many poles on the real
circle generated by `P`; the simultaneous progression version is
proved below.

For any point `Q in E(F)`, if `b(Q)` is its entire denominator
ideal, the exact absolute-height formula is

```text
h(x(Q))=log Norm b(Q)
         +(1/2) sum_(sigma:F->C) log max(1,|sigma(x(Q))|).
```

The finite contribution is `log Norm b`, because the x-denominator
is `b^2` and the field degree is two. Bounded complex coordinates
and canonical-height comparison therefore give

```text
log Norm b([alpha_S]tP)
 =2 Norm(alpha_S)t^2 hhat(P)+O(1).
```

For each fixed prime ideal `v`, the denominator valuation of
`[t]Q` for fixed nontorsion `Q` is `O_Q,v(log(2+|t|))`.
Indeed, the formal subgroup is open of finite index; sufficiently
deep in it the formal logarithm converts multiplication by `t`
to multiplication of its parameter by `t`. Only finitely many
shallower depths remain. This argument also covers the bad and
ramified prime over two. More explicitly one can use the bounded
valuation difference in [Streng, Lemma 3.7](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~strengtc/cmeds.pdf),
applied to the fixed point `[alpha_S]P`.

Removing primes over `2m`, followed by finite Boolean inversion,
thus yields

```text
log Norm D_S=2 Norm(alpha_S)t^2 hhat(P)+O_m(log(2+|t|)),
log Norm C_T=2 t^2 hhat(P) product_(pi in T)(Norm(pi)-1)
             +O_m(log(2+|t|)).                              (5)
```

The implicit constants also depend on the chosen compact set.
They need not be uniform as `m` grows.

All cells together have leading weight
`product_(pi in J) Norm(pi)=m^2`. The conjugation-stable cells
have leading weight

```text
W(m)=product_(q|m) (1+(q-1)^2).
```

The logarithmic Gaussian norm of an inert rational denominator
factor `p^a` is `2a log p`. Dividing the stable-cell upper bound
by two, and writing `n=mt`, gives

```text
sum_(p=3 mod4) v_p(B_n) log p
 <= hhat(P) n^2 rho(m)+O_m(log(2+|n|)),
rho(m)=W(m)/m^2=product_(q|m)(1-2/q+2/q^2).              (6)
```

Every inert prime is away from `2m`, so (6) needs no additional
inert-prime correction. Fixed exceptional primes can be removed
later at an `O(log n)` cost. The factor two cancels exactly as
shown; `log B_n` itself has leading term `hhat(P)n^2`.

The sum of reciprocals of the primes `q=1 mod4` diverges, so
`rho(m)` can be made arbitrarily small. This classical consequence
of the Euler products for `zeta(s)` and `L(s,chi_4)`, together with
`L(1,chi_4)=pi/4`, also holds after omitting any finite set of primes.

## 4. Finite shifts: CRT and one real circle

Fix a finite set `K` and a positive tolerance `epsilon`. Choose
pairwise coprime products `m_k` of odd split primes such that
`rho(m_k)<epsilon` for every `k in K`. The Chinese remainder theorem
gives an integer `N_0` with

```text
N_0=k mod m_k  for every k,      M=product_(k in K) m_k.
```

For `N=N_0+Mj` set

```text
t_k=(N-k)/m_k=a_k+b_k j,
a_k=(N_0-k)/m_k,     b_k=M/m_k>0.
```

The point `P=(2,2)` lies on the identity component `G=E(R)^0`,
which is a circle. Its infinite order implies that `jP` is dense
in `G`, and visits each nonempty open interval infinitely often
with positive `j`.

For each of the finitely many pairs `(k,S)`, consider the map

```text
z in G -> [alpha_(k,S)](a_k P+[b_k]z) in E(C).
```

Its inverse image of the identity is finite: it is a fiber of a
nonzero isogeny, restricted to a translate of a real circle.
Choose one closed interval of positive length in `G` avoiding
all these finite sets. Its images have bounded x-coordinates,
also after conjugation. Infinitely many `j` place `jP` in this
interval. Formula (6) applies simultaneously to all shifts along
these `N`, and gives

```text
sum_(k in K) sum_(p=3 mod4) v_p(B_(N-k)) log p
 <= epsilon hhat(P) sum_(k in K)(N-k)^2+O_(K,m)(log N).       (7)
```

The maps with `S=J_k` include `(N-k)P`, so bounded archimedean
coordinates also imply
`log B_(N-k)=(N-k)^2 hhat(P)+O(1)` on this fixed subsequence.

Finally repeat the construction with `epsilon=1/r`. At stage `r`
choose one member `N_r` of its infinite subsequence, larger than
`N_(r-1)`, so large that every stage-dependent error divided by
`N_r^2` is at most `1/r`. This diagonal choice proves (1) and (2).
No estimate uniform in `m`, rate of equidistribution, or elliptic
logarithm lower bound is used.

## 5. Consequence for the rational boundary model

Blowing up `O,P,3P,7P` on this CM cubic produces the same degree-five
del Pezzo construction as in
[the height compatibility note](five_row_elliptic_height_compatibility.md).
The ten boundary indices are
`K={0,1,3,7,-1,-3,-7,-4,-8,-10}`. They are distinct because `P`
has infinite order. No three of the four blow-up centers are
collinear, by the elliptic chord law.

Outside the fixed primes of bad models and the denominators of
the nonzero differences `(k-l)P`, the ten boundary contacts are
pairwise coprime. Their omitted finite-prime parts have logarithmic
size `O(log N)`. Equations (1) and (2) therefore supply simultaneous
rational boundary contacts with full quadratic height and
negligible inert mass. Their rational moduli origin is preserved.

The oriented Gaussian block allocation, a common lift with small
primitive imaginary parts, and the short-arc metric conditions
remain separate questions. This argument proves none of them.

The finite checker
[check_rational_cm_inert_mass.py](check_rational_cm_inert_mass.py)
verifies exact Gaussian denominator cells, integrality, full-depth
coprimality, conjugation, reconstruction, and the combinatorial
mass factor. The infinite subsequence is justified by the proof
above, rather than by numerical evidence.

## 6. No dominant prime power, and balanced complementary allocations

The same construction also resolves an elementary obstacle to dividing
a rational contact into two comparable oriented blocks: a single
prime power cannot dominate these selected denominators. More precisely,
the diagonal subsequence can be chosen so that, simultaneously for all
`k in K`,

```text
max_p v_p(B_(N_j-k)) log p=o(N_j^2).                        (8)
```

Here the maximum includes split, inert, and ramified rational primes.
To prove this put

```text
L(m)=product_(q|m)(1-1/q)^2 <= rho(m).
```

Every individual cell weight in (5) is at most
`product_(pi in J)(Norm(pi)-1)=m^2 L(m)`, since every factor
`Norm(pi)-1` is greater than one. Each prime away from `2m` belongs
to exactly one cell at a chosen Gaussian orientation, with its entire
denominator exponent. Therefore

```text
max_p v_p(B_n) log p
 <=2 hhat(P)n^2 L(m)+O_m(log(2+|n|)).                       (9)
```

The primes over `2m` satisfy the same bound after increasing the
error. Applying the preceding diagonal argument proves (8).

There is a stronger allocation statement that requires no factorization
inside a cell. Remove the conjugation-stable cells, the primes over
`2m`, and any desired fixed exceptional set. The total removed rational
logarithmic denominator mass is at most
`rho(m) hhat(P)n^2+O_m(log n)`. For each unordered nonstable pair
`{T,bar(T)}`, choose one cell `C_T`. These chosen cells are pairwise
coprime and coprime to every conjugate, including their own. Their
norms multiply to the retained rational denominator `B_n^*`:

```text
product_(chosen T) Norm(C_T)=B_n^*.
```

Indeed `C_T bar(C_T)=(Norm(C_T))`, and conjugation pairs the entire
trimmed rational denominator ideal. Each chosen cell has logarithmic
norm at most the right side of (9). Order the chosen cells arbitrarily
and divide them into two groups by stopping when the cumulative
logarithmic norm first reaches half of `log B_n^*`. The imbalance
is at most the largest cell logarithmic norm. Products of Gaussian
generators within the two groups give `H_n^+` and `H_n^-` satisfying,
along the diagonal subsequence,

```text
Norm(H_n^+ H_n^-)=B_n^*,
log Norm(H_n^+)=log Norm(H_n^-)=hhat(P)n^2/2+o(n^2).
```

Both products have only odd split-prime support; all their supports
and conjugate supports are disjoint. After the fixed common-contact
primes are removed, these assertions hold across all shifted
contacts as well. More generally the same cumulative allocation
gives any fixed number of prescribed proportional weights, with
an error bounded by a fixed multiple of the largest cell weight.

Thus the ten rational contacts admit twenty genuinely integral,
independent Gaussian blocks with the matched complementary norm
weights `w=H/10` of the five-row dictionary. This conclusion concerns
their norms and their divisibility in the contact integers. It does
not assert that multiplying those blocks according to their cut
labels recovers the given rational moduli point, or yields small
primitive residues.

The exact remaining compatibility condition can be stated without a
prime-distribution hypothesis. Assign `H_e^+,H_e^-` to the complementary
cut labels `e,e^c`. Supply the remaining cut blocks with their required
profile weights, and set `Z_i=K_i product_(S containing i) H_S`, where
the correction factors obey `log|K_i|=o(N^2)`. Write
`d_ij=det((Re Z_i,Im Z_i),(Re Z_j,Im Z_j))`.
If the elliptic moduli point has these two cross ratios `a_N,b_N`,
the required common rational projective transformation exists exactly
when the distinct candidate directions satisfy

```text
d_13 d_24 = a_N d_14 d_23,
d_13 d_25 = b_N d_15 d_23.                               (10)
```

The allocation just proved controls neither equation, even allowing
choices of the orientations and the remaining blocks. Small primitive
residues would further require
`|d_ij|/Norm(gcd_G(Z_i,Z_j))=exp(o(N^2))` for every pair.
The allocation removes dominant prime powers as an explanation for
failure, but does not solve these simultaneous compatibility conditions.
