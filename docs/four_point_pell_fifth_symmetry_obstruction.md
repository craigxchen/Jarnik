# No fifth point from the evident symmetries of the four-point Pell family

The shrinking four-point family in
[`four_point_bonus_counterexample.md`](four_point_bonus_counterexample.md)
cannot be extended to a fifth point by conjugating its whole Pell blocks,
by multiplying such a switch by a Gaussian unit, or by iterating the
pair-reflections generated by the four points.  A product extension by one
independent coprime binary Gaussian block of any size also has endpoint cost
bounded away from zero.  This is a result about these explicit symmetry
mechanisms, not a classification of all lattice points on the same circles.

The later [complete-neighborhood theorem](pell_four_point_complete_neighborhood.md)
goes beyond these symmetry restrictions: for Pell parameter `V>=16`, every
integer point on the same circle within chord distance `sqrt(R)/4` of the
designated anchor is one of the original four points. This includes all
switches of individual prime factors. The results below remain useful for
the separate product constructions that change the circle.

Recall

```text
P=-1+2i,
A=(U+V)+2Vi,
B=2V+(U-V)i,
C=(2U+8V)+(3U+V)i,
U^2-5V^2=1,
U+V sqrt(5)=(9+4sqrt(5))^n,       n=1 mod 10,
```

and the four orientation rows

```text
PABC,   conjugate(P) A conjugate(B) conjugate(C),
conjugate(P) conjugate(A) B conjugate(C),
conjugate(P) conjugate(A) conjugate(B) C.             (1)
```

Their common minus sign is immaterial.

## 1. The clustered limiting ray contains exactly four block switches

As `V->infinity`, put

```text
h=(1+sqrt(5))+2i,          omega=conjugate(h)/h=(1-2i)/sqrt(5).
```

The leading coefficients of `A,B,C` are positive real multiples of `h`:

```text
A/V -> h,
B/V -> ((sqrt(5)-1)/2)h,
C/V -> ((1+3sqrt(5))/2)h.                             (2)
```

Also `P=-sqrt(5) omega`, so

```text
conjugate(P)/P=omega^(-2).                             (3)
```

Start from `PABC`.  In any of the sixteen products obtained by independently
conjugating the four whole blocks, let `b` record whether `P` is conjugated
and let `m` be the number of conjugated blocks among `A,B,C`.  Equations
(2)--(3) show that its limiting phase ratio to `PABC` is exactly

```text
omega^(m-2b).                                         (4)
```

Multiplication by a Gaussian unit can align this ray with the original one
only if `epsilon omega^(m-2b)=1` for
`epsilon in {1,-1,i,-i}`.  No nonzero power of `omega` is a Gaussian unit.
For an odd power this follows already from the nontrivial `sqrt(5)` factor.
For an even power use

```text
omega^2=(1-2i)/(1+2i).
```

Unique factorization in `Z[i]` shows that no nonzero power of this quotient
is a unit.  Hence (4) can be aligned only when `m=2b`.  The solutions are

```text
(b,m)=(0,0),                 one row,
(b,m)=(1,2),                 three rows.              (5)
```

They are precisely the four rows in (1).  Every other whole-block switch,
including the full complex conjugate of any row, stays a fixed positive
angular distance from their limiting ray.  It cannot be a fifth member of
an arc whose angular width tends to zero.

## 2. Rational pair-reflections produce no new integral point

The norms of `P,A,B,C` are pairwise coprime, and each block is coprime in
`Z[i]` to its conjugate.  Record conjugation by the four bit vectors

```text
v_0=(0,0,0,0),
v_1=(1,0,1,1),
v_2=(1,1,0,1),
v_3=(1,1,1,0),                                (6)
```

with coordinates ordered as `P,A,B,C`.

Every point in the rational orbit generated by pair-reflections has an
affine exponent vector `c=(c_0,...,c_3) in Z^4`, `sum c_i=1`.  At every
Gaussian prime belonging to one block, integrality of the orbit point and
its fixed common norm require the corresponding orientation sum

```text
t=sum_i c_i v_i
```

to lie in `{0,1}`.  Put `c_0=1-c_1-c_2-c_3`.  Formula (6) gives

```text
t_P=c_1+c_2+c_3,
t_A=c_2+c_3,       t_B=c_1+c_3,       t_C=c_1+c_2.   (7)
```

Solving the last three equations gives

```text
c_1=(t_B+t_C-t_A)/2,
c_2=(t_A+t_C-t_B)/2,
c_3=(t_A+t_B-t_C)/2,
t_P=(t_A+t_B+t_C)/2.                                 (8)
```

For binary `t`, (8) is integral with binary `t_P` exactly when the number of
ones among `t_A,t_B,t_C` is zero or two.  These are again the four patterns
in (6).  Therefore the entire pair-reflection orbit has no other integral
point on this circle.  This includes arbitrarily long reflection words and
does not assume their intermediate points are integral.

## 3. Any new coprime binary block has a positive endpoint cost

There is also a size-uniform obstruction to adjoining a point by one new
binary Gaussian block in the usual product construction.  The original circle
radius and every pairwise angular separation satisfy two-sided estimates

```text
R asymp V^3,             theta_ij asymp V^(-2).       (9)
```

The upper bounds are in the counterexample note.  For one lower angular
bound, its exact displacement is

```text
z_1-z_0=(32V,-16U-16V),
```

so this chord has length at least `32V`; the upper bound `R=O(V^3)` and
`chord<=R theta` give the claim.  The same lower bound holds for every pair:
the exact table in that note writes its chord length as
`2t_ij R/|q_ij|`, where `t_ij` is a fixed nonzero integer and
`|q_ij| asymp V^2`.

Now multiply the five proposed whole-block rows by orientations of a nonzero
Gaussian integer `H`.  Assume

```text
gcd_Z[i](H,conjugate(H))=1,       gcd_Z[i](H,N_old)=1,
N_old=5abc=R^2.
```

The rational integer `N_old` contains both orientations of every old Gaussian
prime.  No relation between `|H|` and `V` is assumed.

If every row uses the same orientation, the block is a common Gaussian factor
and disappears under primitive normalization.  Otherwise it is active, so
both orientations occur.  The retained four old rows have Gaussian gcd one,
and the two coprimality assumptions prevent a new common factor.  Hence the
primitive circle radius is exactly `R'=R|H|`.  If the angular width is above
one fixed small threshold, the desired endpoint lower bound is immediate
from `R'>=R`; below we work inside that threshold.

Among the four retained old rows, two use the same one of the two
orientations.  If fixed Gaussian units are also allowed, placing all five
rows in one sufficiently short arc forces those two rows to use the same
unit; different units leave a fixed quarter-turn separation.  Multiplication
by their common factor preserves their old angular separation.  Thus the
angular width `theta` of the five rows always satisfies

```text
theta >= c_1 V^(-2).                                  (10)
```

There is a second lower bound involving the size of `H`.  For a whole-block
switch `v`, (4) and
`U/V-sqrt(5)=O(V^(-2))` give

```text
W_v/z_0=omega^j+O(V^(-2)),       j in {-2,-1,0,1,2,3}.
```

Write

```text
rho=conjugate(H)/H.
```

If the four retained old rows use both orientations of `H`, comparison of
two of them across the orientation groups forces `rho` to approach a
Gaussian unit, up to an error `O(theta+V^(-2))`.  If all four use one
orientation, a distinct fifth row must use the other: a nonclustered old
switch has fixed limiting ratio `epsilon omega^j` with `j!=0`, while a
repeated old switch has `j=0`.  Thus in every case where five distinct rows
lie in one sufficiently short arc,

```text
|rho-gamma| <= C(theta+V^(-2)),
gamma=epsilon omega^j,                                (11)
```

where `epsilon` is a Gaussian unit and, after taking the relevant quotient,
`j` belongs to `{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3}`.
If `j` is even, `gamma` lies in `Q(i)`.  Unless equality holds, clearing
its fixed denominator makes

```text
alpha=conjugate(H)-gamma H
```

a nonzero Gaussian rational of bounded denominator.  Hence

```text
|rho-gamma| >= c/|H| >= c/|H|^2.                     (12)
```

If `j` is odd, then `gamma` lies in `Q(i,sqrt(5))` but not in `Q(i)`.
Apply the nontrivial `sqrt(5)` conjugation `tau` to
`alpha=conjugate(H)-gamma H`.  After one fixed denominator is cleared,
`alpha tau(alpha)` is a nonzero Gaussian rational of bounded denominator.
Since `|tau(alpha)|=O(|H|)`, one obtains

```text
|rho-gamma| >= c/|H|^2.                              (13)
```

The constants can be chosen uniformly because the target set in (11) is
finite.  Equality in (12) or (13) cannot create an exception.  For odd `j`,
equality is impossible because `rho` lies in `Q(i)` and `gamma` does not.
For even `j`, the quotient

```text
omega^2=(1-2i)/(1+2i)
```

has Gaussian prime support only over `5`.  The equation
`rho=epsilon omega^j`, unique factorization, and
`gcd(H,conjugate(H))=1` therefore force all prime factors of `H` to lie
over `5`.  Coprimality with the old conductor then forces `H` to be a
unit.  Section 1 shows that a unit cannot create a fifth clustered
whole-block row.  Consequently (11)--(13) imply, for every eligible active
nonunit `H`,

```text
theta >= c_2 |H|^(-2)-C_2 V^(-2).                   (14)
```

These two angular lower bounds cover every size of `H`.  Choose a fixed
`epsilon_0>0` with `C_2 epsilon_0^2<=c_2/2`.  If
`|H|>=epsilon_0 V`, then (10), `R asymp V^3`, and the active radius
`R'=R|H|` give

```text
C_*=theta sqrt(R') >= c sqrt(|H|/V) >= c sqrt(epsilon_0).   (15)
```

If `|H|<epsilon_0 V`, (14) gives
`theta>=c_2/(2|H|^2)`, and therefore

```text
C_* >= c (V/|H|)^(3/2) >= c epsilon_0^(-3/2).        (16)
```

Thus `C_*` is bounded below by a positive absolute constant, for all
sufficiently large Pell indices, throughout this coprime binary whole-block
product ansatz.  The conclusion has no relative-rate hypothesis.

## 4. Scope after the complete-neighborhood theorem

The symmetry argument above does not classify switches of individual Gaussian prime
factors inside `A`, `B`, or `C`.  Their factorizations move with the Pell
index, and a partial divisor can have a phase unrelated to the whole block.
Nor does the argument cover a new factor sharing an unbounded gcd with the
old conductor or a construction that changes the original four points rather
than adjoining to them.

A narrower symbolic extension problem is available if the fifth point is
required to retain the existing integral `L=24` cotangent chart.  Its new
integer coordinate `X` would have to satisfy simultaneously

```text
X-X_i divides X_i^2+24^2,          i=1,2,3.          (17)
```

An arbitrary rational fifth circle point need not be integral in this fixed
chart: clearing its additional cotangent denominators can replace `L=24` by
a multiple and scale the three old coordinates.  Thus (17) is a useful
fixed-normalization subproblem, not an equivalent formulation of every
fifth point. The complete-neighborhood theorem now excludes every additional
integer point on the same circle within `sqrt(R)/4` of the anchor, regardless
of its cotangent normalizer or partial-factor representation. A nonunit
common multiplier changes the circle and the affine lattice index, so that
theorem does not apply uniformly to such multipliers. Neither result
classifies arbitrary five-point endpoint configurations.
