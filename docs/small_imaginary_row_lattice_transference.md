# Small imaginary coordinates and the exact row-lattice index

The common Gaussian gcd and the ordinary integer span are different
objects. For the actual conjugate-primitive Boolean rows, their quotient
index is unusually well controlled: it divides **every** determinant
residual. Nevertheless, extracting a short ordinary lattice basis from
the given rows necessarily costs at least half a block in its integer
coefficients. Thus replacing the rows by a short basis does not preserve
their small imaginary-coordinate bounds.

The index and coefficient-cost statements below retain the full prime
incidence, including primes shared with correcting factors. An explicit
balanced two-row, three-block family attains the critical scale with
constant residuals. It is not a counterexample with arbitrarily many
rows and does not settle the uniform endpoint bound.

## 1. The quotient index divides every residual

Let the nonzero Gaussian rows be conjugate primitive, with nonzero pair
determinants and exact factorizations

```text
P_i = K_i product_(T containing i) H_T,
N_i = Norm(P_i),       n_T = Norm(H_T),
Delta_ij = Im(bar(P_i) P_j)
         = t_ij product_(T containing i,j) n_T.
```

Assume the nonempty blocks have pairwise disjoint rational-prime
support. Put

```text
H = gcd_Z[i](P_1,...,P_m),       d = Norm(H),
Q_i = P_i/H,
Lambda = span_Z{Q_1,...,Q_m} subset Z^2,
F = gcd_(i<j) |Delta_ij|,       h = F/d.
```

The gcd-of-minors formula gives

```text
[Z^2:Lambda] = h.
```

This uses all pair determinants and their actual common divisor. It does
not identify the ordinary span with the Gaussian ideal generated by the
rows. The latter is the unit ideal for the `Q_i`.

**Exact index lemma.**

```text
gcd(h, product_i Norm(Q_i)) = 1,
h divides gcd_(i<j) |t_ij|.                              (1)
```

To prove the first assertion, fix a rational prime `p|h`. Each `Q_i`
has primitive integer coordinates, because it divides the
conjugate-primitive `P_i`. Its reduction modulo `p` is consequently
nonzero. All these reductions lie on one projective line, since all
their two-by-two determinants vanish modulo `p`. That line cannot be
isotropic for `x^2+y^2`: at an odd split prime this would make the same
Gaussian prime divide every `Q_i`; at an inert prime there is no nonzero
isotropic vector; and at 2 it would make `1+i` divide every `Q_i`.
Each possibility contradicts their Gaussian gcd being a unit. Thus
`p` divides none of their norms.

Consequently `v_p(N_i)=v_p(d)` for every `i`. If `p` belongs to the
unique block `n_T`, set `e=v_p(n_T)`; an incident row gives
`v_p(d)>=e`. For every pair,

```text
v_p(t_ij) = v_p(Delta_ij) - e 1_(i,j in T)
           >= v_p(d)+v_p(h)-e 1_(i,j in T)
           >= v_p(h).
```

If `p` belongs to no block, use `e=0`. This proves (1), with no
coprimality assumption on the `K_i` and the core norms.

There is also a useful audit of the prime incidence: if `T` is proper
and `p|h` also divides `n_T`, then

```text
v_p(Norm(K_j)) = v_p(d) >= v_p(n_T)   for every j outside T. (2)
```

Thus the portion of a proper block supported on primes of `h` is
already paid for by each outside row's correcting norm. It cannot
silently carry a full block of height in the residual-free limit.

The common-rotation classification gives
`n_[m] | d` and `d/n_[m] | product_i Norm(K_i)`. Hence in the
balanced extracted profile, for fixed `m`,

```text
log d = w+o(w),       log h <= min_(i<j) log|t_ij| = o(w). (3)
```

The individual-residual bound in (3) avoids an unnecessary product of
all pair residuals. The exact lemma also applies when `m` varies.

## 2. A short basis costs half a block in row coefficients

Write `H=a+ib` and set `Y_i=Im(P_i)`. Suppose `|Y_i|<=B`. Let
`v_1,v_2` be any ordinary integer basis of `Lambda`, expressed using
**real integer** coefficients as

```text
v_j = sum_i c_ji Q_i,
|v_j| <= L,
C = max_(j=1,2) sum_i |c_ji|.
```

Then

```text
C >= h sqrt(d)/(2 L B).                               (4)
```

Indeed the real linear functional `ell(x,y)=b x+a y` has Euclidean
norm `sqrt(d)`, while `|ell(v_j)|<=BC`. The determinant of the two
basis vectors has absolute value `h`. Inverting their two-by-two
matrix gives

```text
sqrt(d) <= (|v_1|+|v_2|) BC/h <= 2 L BC/h.
```

An integer lattice of index `h` has a Hermite basis of lengths at most
`sqrt(2)h`: write it as `(u,0),(r,v)` with `uv=h` and `0<=r<u`.
Thus a basis of residual height exists. But **every** such choice with
`L<=sqrt(2)h` requires

```text
C >= sqrt(d)/(2 sqrt(2) B).                            (5)
```

For the endpoint profile this is
`log C >= w/2-o(w)`. More generally any basis of logarithmic height
`o(w)` has the same lower bound. A short Euclidean basis and a short
integer coefficient lift from the prescribed generators are distinct
requirements. Transference or Hermite reduction supplies the first;
(4) proves that the second is incompatible with the proposed subpower
coefficient budget.

Gaussian coefficients cannot be substituted into this argument: their
imaginary parts multiply the large real coordinates of `P_i`, so they
do not preserve the small-value estimate in the first place.

## 3. What the rank-two small-value lattice actually says

Write `Q_i=u_i+i v_i`, and define the injective real-linear map

```text
A(s,t) = (v_i s+u_i t)_i,
L_0 = A Z^2 subset Z^m,
L_sat = span_R(L_0) intersect Z^m.
```

The gcd of all entries of `A` is one, and the gcd of its maximal minors
is `h`. Consequently

```text
[L_sat:L_0] = h,
covol(L_sat) = sqrt(sum_(i<j) Delta_ij^2)/(d h).          (6)
```

Let `g=gcd_i|Y_i|`, assuming the imaginary vector is nonzero. Since
`H` has primitive integer coordinates, extend `(a,b)` to a unimodular
integer basis. The first column of `A` in that basis is `Y`; thus
`g` divides every maximal minor, and

```text
g | h,       y=Y/g is primitive in L_sat.                (7)
```

Every `z in L_sat` independent of `y` obeys the elementary area bound

```text
||z||_2 >= g sqrt(sum Delta_ij^2)/(d h ||Y||_2).          (8)
```

For fixed `m>=3`, write `A_m=2^(m-2)`. The actual core gives

```text
log covol(L_sat) = (A_m-1)w+o(w),
log ||y||_2 = o(w).
```

Equation (8) therefore identifies a unique short rational line and
forces a long independent second vector. This is compatible with
Minkowski's product estimate: a lattice may have one short vector and
an arbitrarily long second vector. It supplies no lower bound for the
first vector.

A precise remaining route would be to prove, for some fixed `m>=4`,
that these **actual Boolean norm and oriented-divisor data** force

```text
lambda_1(L_sat) >= exp(epsilon_m w-o(w)),   epsilon_m>0. (9)
```

Such a statement would exclude the extracted `Y/g`. The subsequent
[balanced three-row polynomial family](balanced_three_row_gaussian_polynomial_family.md)
realizes all seven core blocks with bounded imaginary and determinant
residuals, so (9) is false for `m=3`; Section 4 below already rules out
`m=2`. Any valid first-minimum exclusion must begin with `m>=4`.
It must use more
than the covolume (6), the index bound (1), and two-dimensional
transference. The lattice records all rows; replacing the prime
incidence by its index alone does not establish (9).

## 4. A balanced full-incidence two-row family at the boundary

Let `a` run through positive multiples of `2210=2*5*13*17`, and put

```text
H = a+i,                  d = a^2+1,
Q_1 = 2a+1-2i,            n_1 = 4a^2+4a+5,
Q_2 = 4a+1-4i,            n_2 = 16a^2+8a+17,
P_1 = H Q_1 = 2d+a+i,
P_2 = H Q_2 = 4d+a+i.
```

Use the three blocks `H_{12}=H`, `H_{1}=Q_1`, `H_{2}=Q_2`,
and set both correcting factors equal to one. All three Gaussian
blocks and both rows are conjugate primitive. Their norms have only
odd split prime factors. The following elementary resultant bounds
show that the norm supports are disjoint:

```text
gcd(d,n_1) | 17,
gcd(d,n_2) | 65,
gcd(n_1,n_2) | 65.
```

The chosen divisibility of `a` removes these possible common primes.
For the last bound, use `n_2-4n_1=-8a-3` and reduce `16n_1`
modulo `8a+3` to obtain 65. The other two follow by reducing the
quadratics modulo `d`.

Furthermore

```text
Delta_12 = -2d,       t_12=-2,
2Q_1-Q_2=1,
span_Z(Q_1,Q_2) = Z + 2i Z,
h=2,                 Y_1=Y_2=1.
```

With `w=log d`, every block norm has logarithm `w+O(1)`, each row
has logarithmic modulus `w+O(1)`, and every correcting or imaginary
residual has bounded height. The two directions are distinct. Thus
the full balanced `m=2` pattern and exceptional small-imaginary common
rotation actually exist.

The short basis `(1,2i)` illustrates the coefficient cost exactly:

```text
1 = 2Q_1-Q_2,
2i = (4a+1)Q_1-(2a+1)Q_2.
```

The second representation is unique and costs `6a+2`, of logarithmic
size `w/2+O(1)`, despite the lattice index being the constant 2.
It therefore disproves a dimension-free inference that bounded index
and small values on the given rows imply small values on a short basis.
It does not supply the additional Boolean supports needed for `m>=3`.
Those seven supports are supplied independently by the subsequent
[three-row family](balanced_three_row_gaussian_polynomial_family.md),
which also prevents a universal profile exclusion at `m=3`.

The [exact checker](check_small_imaginary_row_lattice_transference.py)
checks the family, the existing three-row full-support fixture, and
the nonisotropic-index lemma on finite primitive Gaussian tuples.
The general statements above follow from their proofs.
