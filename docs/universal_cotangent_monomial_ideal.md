# The universal cotangent monomial ideal

Fix \(k\ge1\), work over \(K=\mathbb Q(i)\), and make the invertible
linear change of variables
\[
Y_j=X_j-iL\quad(1\le j\le k).
\]
The universal rows
\[
P_0=\prod_{j=1}^kY_j,\qquad
P_i=L\prod_{j\ne i}Y_j\quad(1\le i\le k)
\]
generate the monomial ideal
\[
I_k=(P_0,P_1,\ldots,P_k)\subset K[L,Y_1,\ldots,Y_k].
\]
The generators are all squarefree monomials of degree \(k\) in the
\(k+1\) variables \(L,Y_1,\ldots,Y_k\).

## Newton-polyhedron description

Index the variables by \(0,1,\ldots,k\), with \(Z_0=L\) and
\(Z_j=Y_j\). The exponent vectors of the generators are
\[
m_r=\mathbf 1-e_r\qquad(0\le r\le k),
\]
where \(\mathbf 1=(1,\ldots,1)\). The integral closure of a monomial
ideal is determined by its Newton polyhedron; the displayed brackets
mean the \(K\)-linear monomial ideal generated by those exponents:
\[
\overline{I_k}
=\operatorname{span}_K\left\{Z^u:u\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{k+1},\quad
u\in\operatorname{conv}(m_0,\ldots,m_k)+\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{k+1}\right\}.
\]
For \(u=(u_0,\ldots,u_k)\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{k+1}\), membership is
equivalent to
\[
\sum_{r=0}^k\max(1-u_r,0)\le1. \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, writing a point of the simplex as
\[
\sum_r\lambda_rm_r,\qquad \lambda_r\ge0,\quad\sum_r\lambda_r=1,
\]
its \(r\)-th coordinate is \(1-\lambda_r\). Allowing the positive
orthant means that one needs \(\lambda_r\ge1-u_r\), and such
\(\lambda_r\)'s exist exactly when (1) holds.

For an integer exponent vector \(u\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{k+1}\), (1) says
that at most one coordinate of \(u\) is zero. Equivalently, the
monomial is divisible by one of the \(P_r\). Consequently
\[
\boxed{\ \overline{I_k}=I_k\ }\tag{2}
\]
for every \(k\), not merely for \(2\le k\le8\). Thus the universal ideal
has no hidden integral-closure enlargement.

## All powers and normality

The same argument applies to every power. The minimal generator exponent
vectors of \(I_k^q\) are

```text
q*1-c,   c_r>=0,   sum_r c_r=q.
```

For an integer exponent vector \(u\ge0\), the Newton-polyhedron criterion
for \(\overline{I_k^q}\) is

```text
sum_r max(q-u_r,0) <= q.                              (5)
```

This is also exactly the divisibility criterion for membership in
\(I_k^q\): choose \(c_r\ge\max(q-u_r,0)\) with total \(q\), and the
resulting product of \(q\) generators divides \(Z^u\). Hence

```text
overline(I_k^q)=I_k^q for every q>=1.                   (6)
```

Thus \(I_k\) is a normal monomial ideal. If \(F\) is homogeneous of
degree \(qk\) and belongs to this closure, every monomial \(Z^u\) in
\(F\) satisfies

```text
sum_r u_r=qk,   0<=u_r<=q for every r.                  (7)
```

If some \(u_r>q\), the degree equation forces the sum of the deficits
\(\sum_s\max(q-u_s,0)\) to exceed \(q\), contradicting (5). In particular,

```text
L^(q+1) divides F  =>  F=0.                            (8)
```

The same statement holds with any \(Y_r\) in place of \(L\).

### A valuation proof of the power criterion

No general monomial-integral-closure theorem is needed here. For a
nonempty subset \(S\subseteq\{0,\ldots,k\}\), define
\[
v_S\!\left(\sum_\alpha c_\alpha Z^\alpha\right)
 =\min_{c_\alpha\ne0}\sum_{r\in S}\alpha_r.
\]
This order is additive under multiplication, since lowest-degree initial
forms multiply in the associated graded polynomial domain. Every
generator of \(I_k\) has \(v_S\)-value at least \(|S|-1\), and
one generator has exactly that value. Thus \(v_S(I_k^q)=q(|S|-1)\).
If \(F\) satisfies a monic integral equation
\[
F^n+c_1F^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n=0,\qquad c_j\in I_k^{qj},
\]
then \(v_S(F)<q(|S|-1)\) is impossible: the first term has value
\(nv_S(F)\), while the \(j\)-th later term has value at least
\[
qj(|S|-1)+(n-j)v_S(F)>nv_S(F).
\]
The unique lowest-value term cannot cancel. Hence every integral
element satisfies
\[
v_S(F)\ge q(|S|-1)\quad\text{for every nonempty }S. \tag{V1}
\]

Conversely, for a monomial \(Z^\alpha\), these inequalities are
equivalent to
\[
\sum_r\max(q-\alpha_r,0)\le q. \tag{V2}
\]
For the forward implication take \(S=\{r:\alpha_r<q\}\); the reverse
implication follows by restricting the deficit sum to an arbitrary
nonempty \(S\). If (V2) holds, choose nonnegative integers \(c_r\)
with \(\sum_rc_r=q\) and \(c_r\ge\max(q-\alpha_r,0)\). Then a product
of \(q\) generators, omitting variable \(Z_r\) exactly \(c_r\) times,
divides \(Z^\alpha\), so \(Z^\alpha\in I_k^q\). Applying this termwise
to a polynomial proves \(\overline{I_k^q}=I_k^q\) and normality directly.


## Chartwise integral closure

The conclusion survives projective charting and saturation. Let \(P\)
be homogeneous of degree \(qk\), and assume that on every chart
\(Z_s\ne0\)
\[
P/Z_s^{qk}\in\overline{(I_k/Z_s^k)^q}. \tag{9}
\]
For \(k\ge2\), set \(Z_s=1\) and evaluate at the affine coordinate
origin, where all \(Z_r\) with \(r\ne s\) vanish. The dehomogenized
generators that omit \(r\ne s\) have order \(k-1\) there; the generator
that omits \(s\) has order \(k\). Hence the chart ideal has
\(\mathfrak m\)-order \(k-1\), and its \(q\)-th power has order
\(q(k-1)\). Integral closure preserves this lower order bound (or,
equivalently, it follows directly from the monomial Newton
polyhedron). Every monomial \(Z^\alpha\) occurring in \(P\) therefore
satisfies
\[
qk-\alpha_s\ge q(k-1),\qquad\text{so}\qquad\alpha_s\le q. \tag{10}
\]
Because this holds for every chart index \(s\), all monomials of \(P\)
have total degree \(qk\) and every exponent at most \(q\). The power
criterion above then gives
\[
P\in I_k^q. \tag{11}
\]
Thus chartwise integral closure does not enlarge the homogeneous
degree-\(qk\) space: it is exactly the degree-\(qk\) part of \(I_k^q\).

For \(k=1\), \(I_1=(L,Y_1)\). On either projective chart one generator
dehomogenizes to \(1\), so the chart ideal is the unit ideal and the
condition is automatic. Every degree-\(q\) monomial in two variables
already has both exponents at most \(q\), and \(I_1^q\) contains the
entire degree-\(q\) space, giving the same conclusion.

In particular, a nonzero homogeneous degree-\(k\) chartwise-IC
certificate cannot carry \(L^2\), and a degree-\(qk\) certificate cannot
carry \(L^{q+1}\). This rules out the higher \(L\)-vanishing required by
uniform chord-content certificates while retaining the exact
chartwise hypothesis.

## Homogeneous degree-\(k\) elements

Every homogeneous degree-\(k\) polynomial in the integral closure has
the form
\[
F=c_0\prod_jY_j
+L\sum_{i=1}^kc_i\prod_{j\ne i}Y_j,\qquad c_i\in K. \tag{3}
\]
There are no other degree-\(k\) monomials in the closure: an integer
vector of total degree \(k\) satisfying (1) has exactly one zero
coordinate and all other coordinates equal to one.

The restrictions to the coordinate strata are transparent:
\[
F\big|_{L=0}=c_0\prod_jY_j,
\]
and, for \(1\le r\le k\),
\[
F\big|_{Y_r=0}
=c_r\,L\prod_{j\ne r}Y_j. \tag{4}
\]
Therefore no single coordinate hyperplane forces vanishing of every
element of \(I_k\): \(P_0\) is generically nonzero on \(L=0\), while
\(P_r\) is generically nonzero on \(Y_r=0\).

For a coordinate intersection on which a set \(S\) of \(r\) variables
vanishes, every generator, and hence every element of \(I_k\), has
vanishing order at least \(r-1\) under the weight assigning weight one
to variables in \(S\) and zero to the others. This is sharp: choose the
generator omitting one variable in \(S\). In particular, the
codimension-two strata \(L=Y_r=0\) force exactly one order of
vanishing, while the individual strata \(L=0\) and \(Y_r=0\) force
order zero.

## Consequence for the universal family

The ideal-theoretic condition \(P\in\overline{I_k}\) is therefore the
same as \(P\in I_k\) for this universal family. Any failure of a
fixed-factor criterion must come from a different choice of target
polynomial or from specialization/height issues; it cannot be hidden
inside the integral closure of this row ideal.

## Mixed holomorphic--antiholomorphic certificates

The normality calculation concerns powers \(I_k^q\) of the holomorphic
row ideal. It does not automatically apply to a mixed expression
\(G^a\overline G^{\,b}\). Such expressions remain in the same
\(K[L,X]\) after applying \(i\mapsto-i\), but form a different
bihomogeneous ideal and require a separate contact-order analysis.
Total bidegree alone does not bound contact order.
