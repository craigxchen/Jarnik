# An actual unoriented integer contact profile on the disjoint elliptic curve

The disjoint-support curve does more than supply a geometric numerical
class. Using a nontorsion rational point on its source, infinitely many
rational six-point configurations on this **fixed** curve have twenty-five
pairwise coprime integer contact cores of equal leading logarithmic size.
Their primitive integer row determinants factor into the prescribed
cores with bounded remainders. No restriction to split primes or Gaussian
orientation is asserted.

The geometry used below is established in
[the independent audit](disjoint_mixed_elliptic_six_curve_independent_audit.md).
Positive rank is proved by the exact quartic and finite-field certificate
in [Section 4 of the construction](disjoint_mixed_elliptic_six_curve.md#4-exact-quotient-quartic-and-positive-rank).
Thus the nontorsion rational point required below exists unconditionally.

## Statement and normalizations

Fix a rational origin `O` on the source and write

```text
q(Q)=hat h_[O](Q),             h_x(Q)=2q(Q)+O(1).
```

Let `P` be nontorsion and put `alpha=q(P)>0`. Index the twenty-five
boundaries by their unique side `C` containing at most one of the
three anchors `infinity,0,1`. These sides are

```text
{anchor} union S,     empty != S subset {a,b,c},
S,                   S subset {a,b,c}, |S|=2 or 3.
```

Write `B_C` for the corresponding rational contact point.
The `B_C` are all distinct and `f^*D_C=[B_C]`.

There exist a fixed finite set of rational primes `Sigma`, an unbounded
sequence of positive integers `N`, and positive integers `d_C(N)` such
that, simultaneously for every `C`,

```text
gcd(d_C(N),d_D(N))=1                         (C != D),
log d_C(N)=alpha N^2+O(N).                                      (1)
```

Choose primitive integer representatives `v_i(N)` for the six
normalized projective coordinates at `NP`, with anchors

```text
v_infinity=(1,0),      v_0=(0,1),      v_1=(1,1).
```

For every pair of distinct labels there are positive integers
`b_ij(N)`, supported on `Sigma` and bounded independently of `N`, with

```text
|det(v_i(N),v_j(N))|
    = b_ij(N) product_(C containing {i,j}) d_C(N).           (2)
```

The empty product is one. In particular the degree counts are

| Quantity | Leading logarithmic size |
|---|---|
| Each moving primitive row norm | `4 alpha N^2+O(N)` |
| Moving--moving determinant | `8 alpha N^2+O(N)` |
| Anchor--moving determinant | `4 alpha N^2+O(N)` |
| Anchor--anchor determinant | `0` |

The finite vector of bounded integers `b_ij(N)` can be made constant
by passing to a further infinite subsequence. All constants depend
on the fixed curve, maps, models, `P`, and the chosen local neighborhood;
no family-uniform estimate is implicit.

## Simultaneous local avoidance requires only compact recurrence

Enlarge `Sigma` until `E` has a smooth proper model over `Z[1/Sigma]`,
the twenty-five sections `B_C` are pairwise disjoint, and the map and
the line bundles and sections used below extend with their stated
divisor identities. This is possible by spreading out the fixed data.

Choose an integer `N_0` with `N_0P` outside all the contact points.
Only finitely many integers are excluded because `P` is nontorsion.
The group

```text
G = E(R) times product_(p in Sigma) E(Q_p)
```

is compact. Let `H` be the closure of the cyclic subgroup generated
by the diagonal point `P`. Translation by `P` on `H` returns to every
neighborhood of `N_0P` infinitely often at positive, unbounded integer
times. Equivalently, positive multiples of `P` have a subsequence
converging to zero, and translating it by `N_0P` gives the required
returns. This is the elementary recurrence of a rotation on a compact
group; it requires no density in all of `G`, and no quantitative
equidistribution assertion.

At every factor choose a compact neighborhood of `N_0P` avoiding
all the `B_C`. Infinitely many of the returns lie in their product.
On this subsequence every local Weil function for every `[B_C]` is
bounded at the real place and at the primes in `Sigma`. The same
holds for the local chordal collision functions of each pair of
normalized coordinates: their zeros lie among the `B_C`, and the
functions are continuous away from those zeros.

This argument works even if `E(R)` has two components or the diagonal
orbit has a proper closure. It does not require any `B_C` to belong
to the cyclic subgroup generated by `P`.

## Exact finite contact integers and their sizes

For `p` outside `Sigma`, let `k_(C,p)(Q)` be the intersection
multiplicity of the integral section `Q` with the section `B_C` on
the smooth proper model of `E`. Define

```text
d_C(N)=product_(p outside Sigma) p^k_(C,p)(NP).               (3)
```

Only finitely many factors are nontrivial for each `NP != B_C`.
Since the `B_C` remain pairwise disjoint on the model, a section
`NP` can meet at most one of them modulo a given good prime. This
proves the exact pairwise coprimality in (1), including all powers.

Choose a Weil height for `[B_C]` whose good-prime terms are precisely
`k_(C,p) log p`. The fixed bad-prime and real terms are bounded on
the return subsequence, hence

```text
log d_C(N)=h_[B_C](NP)+O(1).
```

The canonical translate `Q -> q(Q-B_C)` is a height for this
degree-one divisor, up to a bounded function. The quadratic-height
pairing therefore gives the sharper formula

```text
log d_C(N)
 = q(NP-B_C)+O(1)
 = alpha N^2 - 2N<P,B_C> + q(B_C) + O(1).                       (4)
```

Thus all twenty-five actual finite contact integers have the same
leading logarithmic size. No inference from global height alone is
being made: local recurrence explicitly bounds every omitted place.

## Primitive rows: why the determinant valuations are exact

Let `z_i:E -> P^1` be each normalized coordinate map, constant for
the anchors. The determinant of the two universal row sections is
a section `s_ij` of

```text
L_i tensor L_j,          L_i=z_i^*O_(P^1)(1).
```

Its divisor on `E` is exactly

```text
div(s_ij)=sum_(C containing {i,j}) [B_C].                    (5)
```

Indeed, the geometric audit exhausts all possible coordinate
collisions. At the contact indexed by `C`, precisely the coordinates
in `C` coincide in the normalized projective line. Their pairwise
differences have simple zeros. Pairs not contained in `C` remain
distinct. This also applies at the infinite anchor, using the
reciprocal coordinate.

For a direct local check, put `t=0` at `B_C`. In a finite chart,
the colliding coordinates have expansions
`r+c_i t+O(t^2)` with pairwise distinct `c_i`; a colliding fixed
anchor has coefficient zero. At infinity use rows `(1,y_i)` and
expansions `y_i=c_i t+O(t^2)`. After excluding finitely many primes,
all needed coefficients and differences are units. Thus at a good
prime where `NP` meets `B_C` to depth `k`,

```text
v_p(det(v_i,v_j)) = k  if {i,j} subset C,
                    0  otherwise.                          (6)
```

A primitive integer representative is a unimodular `Z_p` row at
every prime. Replacing it by any local unimodular lift multiplies
the determinant by a `p`-adic unit, so (6) is unaffected by globally
clearing denominators or choosing signs. This is the point where
ordinary affine numerators alone would not suffice.

Equivalently, (6) follows from the equality of the integral divisors
in (5) outside `Sigma`, using the standard integral metric on `O(1)`.
It proves (2) outside `Sigma` exactly. At a prime in `Sigma`, the
valuation of the determinant of two primitive rows is the logarithmic
chordal collision function divided by `log p`. This function is
bounded on the chosen compact neighborhood. These valuations are
nonnegative integers, so their contributions yield bounded positive
integers `b_ij(N)`. No uncontrolled denominator factor remains.

## Row heights, incidence counts, and the real place

Each moving coordinate has degree four on `E`, because
`[Q(E):Q(T)]=8` and each individual coordinate field has degree two
over `Q(T)`. Height comparison for a fixed degree-four line bundle
gives

```text
log ||v_i(N)||_infinity = 4 alpha N^2+O(N)
```

for a moving label. The three anchor row heights are zero.

A moving pair belongs to two anchored clusters for each of the
three anchors and to two unanchored clusters, for `3*2+2=8` in
total. An anchor--moving pair belongs to four clusters. No cluster
contains two anchors. Equations (1)--(2) give exactly the determinant
heights in the table.

The real avoidance also checks the normalization independently:
on the compact real subset of the open configuration space, all
six directions remain uniformly separated in the projective line.
Consequently

```text
|det(v_i,v_j)| is comparable to ||v_i|| ||v_j||
```

with fixed positive comparison constants. Thus the archimedean
part neither conceals a large cancellation in the determinant nor
changes the leading incidence counts.

## What has and has not been realized

Equations (1)--(2) realize the full twenty-five-boundary **unoriented
integer** contact pattern by actual rational points and actual
primitive integer rows on the fixed shared source. The core sizes
are simultaneous, their good-prime supports are disjoint, and all
pair determinant remainders are bounded. This is stronger than a
geometric degree computation or a formal assignment of heights.

The contact integers can contain inert rational primes. The proof
does not split any contact into two oriented Gaussian core blocks,
produce the six private singleton factors, or establish the small
original Gaussian primitive residues, first short determinant-one frame, common Gaussian
lift, and shrinking arc needed
for the original lattice problem. It supplies neither an endpoint
counterexample nor a global uniform `C sqrt(R)` lattice-arc bound.
