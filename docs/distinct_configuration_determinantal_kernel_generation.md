# Local determinant generation at every distinct configuration

Let `F` have characteristic zero, let `n,d>=2`, put `m=nd`, and fix
pairwise distinct `z_1,...,z_m` in `F`. Let `K_(n,d)` be the space of
multilinear `SL_n` invariant polynomials in the rows of an `m`-by-`n`
matrix `V`. Define

```text
E_z Q = Q((1,z_i,x_(i,3),...,x_(i,n))_i),
A_z(V) = [ V | diag(z) V ],
L_I(V) = det A_z(V)_I                         (|I|=2n).
```

For `n=2`, the coherent evaluation has no remaining variables. Then

```text
ker E_z = sum_(|I|=2n) L_I · K_(n,d-2)(I^c).           (1)
```

Here `K_(n,0)(empty)=F`. Equivalently, the kernel is spanned by a
local `2n`-row determinant times a product of `d-2` ordinary
`n`-row determinants partitioning the complementary labels. This
holds at **every** distinct configuration. In particular, the
twelve-row kernel in
[the generic syzygy note](generic_terminal_coherent_syzygies.md)
is generated by its 4620 displayed localized six-row relations at
every distinct configuration, and their span always has dimension 341.

The key scheme statement is that the ideal of maximal minors of
`A_z(V)` is geometrically prime for every distinct `z`, when `n>=2`
and `m>=2n`. This statement does not require characteristic zero;
characteristic zero enters the invariant-projection step of (1).

## 1. Dimension of the rank-defect locus

First work over an algebraically closed field `k`. Write

```text
R = k[v_(i,a)],
J_z = I_(2n)(A_z),
X = V(J_z),
D = mn-m+2n-1,
c = m-2n+1.
```

Consider the closed incidence correspondence

```text
Z = {(V,[u:w]) : V u + diag(z) V w = 0}
    subset A^(mn) × P^(2n-1).                         (2)
```

The projection `Z -> A^(mn)` is proper and has image `X`. Over the
open base `B_ind` where `u,w` are linearly independent, every vector
`u+z_i w` is nonzero. The equation for row `i` cuts out a hyperplane
in its own `k^n`. Hence `Z_ind -> B_ind` is a vector bundle of rank
`m(n-1)`. In particular, `Z_ind` is smooth and irreducible of
dimension `D`.

The dependent-pair base is the Segre variety

```text
B_dep = {[u:w]=[a q:b q]} ≅ P^(n-1) × P^1,
dim B_dep=n.
```

If every `a+z_i b` is nonzero, its incidence fibers have dimension
`m(n-1)`. If some `a+z_j b=0`, distinctness of the `z_i` implies
that exactly one row equation disappears. Such a base stratum has
dimension `n-1` and its fibers have dimension `m(n-1)+1`. Thus in
both cases

```text
dim Z_dep <= m(n-1)+n = D-(n-1) < D.                  (3)
```

The set `X_dep`, the image of `Z_dep`, is closed because its base
is projective, and satisfies the same dimension bound. Let `C` be
the closure of the image of `Z_ind`. Then `C` is irreducible and

```text
X = C union X_dep,       dim C <= D.                  (4)
```

To see that `dim C=D`, use the explicit matrix

```text
V_* = [1, z, z^3, z^5, ..., z^(2n-3)].                (5)
```

For `n=2` this means just `[1,z]`. The distinct columns of
`A_z(V_*)` are the powers `1,z,...,z^(2n-2)`, with the power `z`
duplicated. The Vandermonde determinant on any `2n-1` labels is
nonzero, so its rank is exactly `2n-1`. Its kernel line is generated
by `(u,w)=(e_2,-e_1)`, an independent pair. On the nonempty rank
`2n-1` open set, the incidence projection has one-point fibers.
Consequently

```text
dim C=dim X=D,       height J_z=c=m-2n+1.             (6)
```

This argument treats all dependent-pair possibilities, including
the case where one row is completely unrestricted. It does not
simply discard that boundary by calling it nongeneric.

## 2. The maximal-minor ideal is prime

The height in (6) is the expected height of maximal minors. The
Eagon–Northcott theorem therefore gives a free resolution of
`R/J_z` of length `c`. A precise reference is
[Eisenbud, *The Geometry of Syzygies*, Theorem A2.60, printed page 225](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~reiner/REU/REU2019notes/2005_Book_TheGeometryOfSyzygies.pdf#page=235).
The grade equals the height because `R` is regular. Equivalently,
`R/J_z` is Cohen–Macaulay of codimension `c`.

For clarity, the required unmixedness follows directly from this
resolution. If `p` is an associated prime of `R/J_z`, localization
and Auslander–Buchsbaum give

```text
height p = depth R_p
         = projective_dimension_(R_p) (R/J_z)_p <= c.
```

Every prime containing `J_z` has height at least `c`, so equality
holds. Thus all associated primes have height `c`: there are no
embedded primes and every irreducible component has dimension `D`.
Equations (3) and (4) now imply that `C` is the only component.

The scheme is generically reduced as well. Near a matrix of rank
`2n-1`, invert one nonzero `(2n-1)`-minor. Row and column elimination
put the matrix in the form

```text
[ identity_(2n-1)  0 ]
[       0         h ],                               (7)
```

where `h` is a column with `c` entries. The maximal-minor ideal is
locally generated by the entries of `h`, and the kernel line is
unique. Consequently the minor scheme is locally isomorphic to
the incidence scheme with that kernel line. At (5) the kernel pair
is independent, so the incidence is in the smooth vector bundle
`Z_ind`. Hence the minor scheme has a smooth nonempty open set.

One can also check this transversality directly. For an independent
kernel pair `(u,w)`, the map from perturbations to the normal space
factors through

```text
delta V |-> delta A_z · (u,w)
        = (delta v_i · (u+z_i w))_i in k^m.
```

Every row normal is nonzero, so this map onto `k^m` is surjective;
its composition with `k^m -> coker A_z` is therefore surjective.

An unmixed ideal with one minimal prime and a reduced generic
localization equals that prime. Thus `J_z` is prime. For the general
reducedness criterion, see
[Stacks, Lemma 10.157.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/031R).
Performing the same argument after algebraic closure proves
geometric primeness over the original field.

The restriction `n>=2` matters. If `n=1`, the minors are nonzero
scalar multiples of `v_i v_j`, and the rank-defect variety is the
union of the coordinate axes.

## 3. Coherent vanishing equals ideal membership for invariants

Let `Q` be multilinear and `SL_n` invariant, with `m=nd`. It has
the relative-invariant law

```text
Q(Vg)=det(g)^d Q(V)                (g in GL_n).         (8)
```

Assume `E_z Q=0`. On `Z_ind`, restrict to the open set where all
`v_i w` are nonzero. This is nonempty: at (5), `w=-e_1` and every
`v_i w=-1`. Choose `g in GL_n` whose first two columns are `w,-u`.
The incidence relation gives

```text
(Vg)_i = (a_i,z_i a_i,b_(i,3),...,b_(i,n)),
a_i=v_i w != 0.
```

Row multilinearity and (8) imply

```text
det(g)^d Q(V)
 = (product_i a_i) E_z Q((b_(i,a)/a_i)_(i,a>=3)) = 0.
```

This nonempty open subset is dense in the irreducible `Z_ind`, so
`Q` vanishes on `C=X`. Primeness and the Nullstellensatz give
`Q in J_z`. Conversely every `L_I` vanishes at the coherent
substitution, because column 2 of the first block equals column 1
of the second block. Therefore

```text
ker E_z = J_z intersection K_(n,d).                   (9)
```

The reasoning can be carried out over an algebraic closure.
Membership descends because field extension is faithfully flat.

## 4. Extracting the multilinear invariant component

Take `Q` in (9), and write

```text
Q = sum_(|I|=2n) L_I F_I.
```

Give each row its own degree. Projection to row multidegree
`(1,...,1)` makes `F_I` independent of the rows in `I` and
multilinear in the complementary rows. The determinant `L_I` is
`SL_n` invariant, since a column change multiplies it by
`det(g)^2`. Apply the `SL_n` Reynolds projection:

```text
Q = sum_I L_I Reynolds(F_I).                         (10)
```

The Reynolds projection preserves each row multidegree, so each
new coefficient lies in `K_(n,d-2)(I^c)`. The first fundamental
theorem for `SL_n` expresses it as a polynomial in ordinary
`n`-row determinants. Its complementary row degrees force each
term to be a product of `d-2` such determinants on disjoint sets
partitioning `I^c`. This proves (1).

For the exact invariant-theory facts used here, see
[Maithani, Theorem 3.1(b), Theorem 4.2, and Example 4.5](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1112/blms.70175):
the invariant ring is generated by maximal minors; in characteristic
zero `SL_n` is linearly reductive and its Reynolds projection is
linear over the invariant ring. Equivariance with row scalings
also follows from uniqueness of that projection.

The affine chart loses no distinct projective configurations.
Choose a projective change of binary coordinates avoiding infinity
at all finitely many directions. The corresponding invertible
change of the two column blocks, together with nonzero row
scalings, transports both the local determinants and the kernel.

## 5. Twelve rows and the remaining arithmetic issue

At `n=3,d=2`, (1) says that the six-row coherent kernel is the line
spanned by `det[V|diag(z)V]`. The polar relation `Q_I(z)` in
[the six-row calculation](six_row_coherent_kernel_and_content.md)
is nonzero at every distinct configuration and belongs to that
same line. Therefore replacing `L_I` by `Q_I(z)` does not change
the generated subspace. The five complementary gradient
invariants span `K_(3,2)(I^c)`.

At `n=3,d=4`, (1) thus proves the formerly missing all-point
localized-six generation statement. Combined with
[the coherent image theorem](coherent_nagata_balancing.md), it
gives rank 341 for that generator matrix at every distinct
characteristic-zero configuration.

This theorem supplies a field-linear presentation. It gives no
bound on the coefficients of a presentation of a prescribed
integral kernel vector, no saturation statement for the integral
span, and no quantitative control of cancellation between local
generators. Those arithmetic issues remain before one can use
this result to improve a radius exponent.

The companion
[exact checker](check_distinct_configuration_determinantal_kernel_generation.py)
audits the Vandermonde smooth-point witness, the six-row polar
normalization, and small multilinear generation cases. The
all-configuration proof is the scheme argument above, rather
than an extrapolation from those finite checks.

## 6. Exact height when directions repeat

There is a useful extension of the height calculation, separate
from the primeness claim. Allow repeated directions, and let `r`
be the largest multiplicity of any direction. For `m>=2n`,

```text
height I_(2n)([V|diag(z)V])
 = max(0, min(m-2n+1, m-r-n+1)).                     (11)
```

To prove this, keep the incidence (2). Its independent-pair part
still has dimension `D`. For a group `T` of `r_T` equal directions
`z_i=t`, the dependent kernel pairs with `u=-t w` impose exactly

```text
V_(T^c) w=0,       w != 0.
```

Their image is the ordinary determinantal locus
`rank V_(T^c)<n`, with codimension
`max(0,m-r_T-n+1)` in the full matrix space. This codimension
follows from the standard rank-stratum dimension; if fewer than
`n` outside rows remain, the condition is automatic. Dependent
pairs with no zero normal require `rank V<n` and are already
contained in every one of these images.

If `r>n`, the largest-group image has codimension strictly less
than `c=m-2n+1`, unless its codimension is already zero. It has
larger dimension than the independent-pair image, and no other
dependent-group image has larger dimension. This proves (11)
in that case. If `r<=n`, every dependent-group image has dimension
at most `D`. Hence the whole rank-defect locus has dimension at
most `D`; the general maximal-minor height bound gives the
reverse inequality and proves (11).

Thus expected height, and therefore Eagon–Northcott exactness,
persists exactly when `r<=n`. Geometric primeness does not follow
on that larger locus. For example, when `m=2n` and the rows split
into two groups of `n` equal directions each, a column operation
factors the sole maximal minor as a nonzero constant times the
product of the two ordinary `n`-row determinants. This is a
reducible hypersurface of the expected height.

For the resolution and its extension across all pair diagonals,
see [the Eagon–Northcott resolution note](coherent_kernel_eagon_northcott_resolution.md).
