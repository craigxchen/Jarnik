# Square-quartic resultants retain the triple-cut mass

The square-quartic condition is essential to inverse circle realization,
but its direct resultant and discriminant consequences do not improve the
seven-point fair exponent. They leave a specific product of triple gcds.
Moreover, the exact square-quartic condition with the fixed quadratic
`q=X^2+Y^2` permits arbitrary finitely prescribed clean cuts. Neither
statement constructs a fair endpoint family: the uncontrolled heights
and extra primes in the construction are essential limitations.

## 1. The resultant and the missing triple contribution

Use primitive integer marked vectors `v_i=(x_i,y_i)`, as in
[the majority normalization](seven_primitive_majority_normalization.md).
For an integral positive quadratic `q`, put

```text
Q_i=q(v_i),          d_ij=det(v_i,v_j),
P(X,Y)=product_i(y_i X-x_i Y),
A=Res(P,q)=product_i Q_i,
D=product_(i<j)|d_ij|,
T=product_(i<j<k) gcd(Q_i,Q_j,Q_k).                 (1)
```

Here the resultant convention is the one giving the displayed positive
product; the absolute binary discriminant of `P` is `D^2`.

At a clean split-prime cut with minority `S`, `s=|S|<=3`, and depth `e`,
the primitive normalization gives

```text
v_p(Q_i)=e  if i in S,       0 otherwise,
v_p(d_ij)=e if i,j in S,     0 otherwise.            (2)
```

The scalar used to make `q` integral is assumed a unit at this prime.
Consequently the three valuations in (1) are

```text
v_p(A)=s e,     v_p(D)=binomial(s,2)e,
v_p(T)=binomial(s,3)e.                               (3)
```

The conductor contribution is exactly

```text
e=v_p(A T/D),  since s-binomial(s,2)+binomial(s,3)=1. (4)
```

In particular `A/D` detects minority sizes one and two and entirely
misses size three. This is not repaired by replacing the resultant with
the repeated-root resultant:

```text
Res(P,q^2)=A^2,       Disc(q^2)=0.                   (5)
```

The square-free quadratic's discriminant controls the collision of its
two roots, not how many marked vectors approach either root. The latter
is exactly the information retained by `T` here.

On a clean cut, `gcd(Q_i,Q_j,Q_k)` can equivalently be replaced by
`gcd(|d_ij|,|d_ik|,|d_jk|)`. Thus the residual factor can also be read as
the product of the indices generated by each triple of primitive marked
vectors in `Z^2`. No equality of these two gcds at arbitrary primes is
asserted.

## 2. The fair budget is exact, with no resultant saving

There are `7,21,35` minority sets of sizes one, two, three. With one block
of logarithmic norm `w` on each cut, (3) gives

```text
log A=(7+42+105)w=154w,
log D=(21+105)w=126w,
log T=35w,
log N=(154-126+35)w=63w.                             (6)
```

These formulas continue with `o(w)` errors under the full-profile
hypothesis of the majority normalization note. Clear the metric using
its subpower common denominator. Fixed finite sums and minima defining
the gcds transfer the accumulated valuation error to `o(w)`; the
clearing scalar also costs only `o(w)`. This is not an assertion that
`N=A T/D` holds at uncontrolled primes without an error term.

The norm bound for the resultant is saturated at this level:
`q(v_i)=exp(22w+o(w))`, so the product in (1) is already
`exp(154w+o(w))`. Its square root after factoring over `Q(i)` has
absolute value `exp(77w+o(w))`, again exactly the product of the seven
linear-factor norms. Taking a square root does not halve the conductor
budget without also halving the corresponding norm scale.

Since `log H_Lambda=22w+o(w)`, the residual arithmetic target for this
particular route would be a bound

```text
T <= H_Lambda^(35/22-epsilon) exp(o(w))              (7)
```

for some fixed positive `epsilon` on the hypothetical fair endpoint
configurations. It would contradict their triple-cut mass and, combined
with `A/D=H_Lambda^(14/11+o(1))`, supply the desired strict saving.
Equation (7) is an unproved target, not a consequence of (1)-(5).

For comparison, at eight points the same inclusion-exclusion requires a
fourfold gcd product `U`. The clean count is
`log A=372w`, `log D=406w`, `log T=196w`, `log U=35w`, and
`log N=log(A T/(D U))=127w`. Dropping the fourfold content would be
incorrect. Any orientation of the balanced four-four cuts gives these
aggregate counts.

## 3. Exact square quartics allow arbitrary prescribed clean cuts

Fix finitely many distinct split primes `p>2n+1`, choose a nonempty
proper subset `S_p` at each, and choose positive depths `e_p`. There
exist distinct integers `t_1,...,t_n` with the following properties:

```text
q_i=1+t_i^2,
v_p(q_i)=e_p       for i in S_p,      0 otherwise,
v_p(t_i-t_j)=e_p   for i,j in S_p,    0 otherwise.    (8)
```

For proof, lift a square root `r_p` of `-1` modulo `p^(e_p+1)`.
For minority labels prescribe

```text
t_i = r_p + p^e_p u_i mod p^(e_p+1),
```

where the `u_i` are distinct nonzero residues modulo `p`. For the other
labels choose distinct residues modulo `p`, avoiding both roots of
`-1`. The derivative `2r_p` is a unit, so the norm valuations in (8)
are exactly `e_p`; the difference assertions are immediate. The Chinese
remainder theorem combines the prescriptions. Adding distinct multiples
of the common modulus makes the resulting integers distinct if needed.

With `v_i=(1,t_i)`, these are actual rational-circle marked directions
for the fixed positive quadratic `1+t^2`, whose determinant is one.
Put

```text
P_0(t)=product_i(t-t_i),
a_i=(1+t_i^2)^2/P_0'(t_i).
```

For seven points `a_i(1,t_i)` have zero row sum, and for eight points
`a_i(1,t_i,t_i^2)` have zero row sum. In both cases their interpolant is
exactly `(1+t^2)^2`. Thus the full repeated-root identity, positivity,
and square metric determinant coexist with all the prescribed cuts and
depths, even with the quadratic coefficients fixed.

This establishes a scoped obstruction: these exact algebraic conditions
do not themselves prohibit simultaneous finite clean-cut specifications.
The construction gives no small coefficient/row heights, no negligible
extra conductor, and no endpoint cluster. A successful use of the square
identity must control those global quantities, such as (7); additional
finite-place compatibility alone cannot exclude the prescribed profiles.
