# Joint affine relations on a lattice circle

## Status

The quadratic circle equation gives an exact lower bound for the
**product** of the heights of a full set of integer affine relations.
Together with the usual kernel-covolume identity, it leaves only an
`R^gamma_m` gap, where `gamma_m=O(1/m)`. This reformulates the divisor
constraint but does not improve the square-root exponent in the upper bound for
the relation-lattice covolume.

That exponent is attained already for five distinct points. The
existing rational quartic five-point construction has primitive radius
`Theta(n^8)` and relation-lattice covolume `Theta(n^4)=Theta(sqrt R)`.
Thus several relations and the exact circle equation can saturate the
determinant bound simultaneously.  This is a fixed five-point family,
not an unbounded point-count construction or a disproof of uniformity.

The exact computation for the five-point example is in
[check_joint_affine_relation_lattice.py](check_joint_affine_relation_lattice.py).

## 1. The exact kernel lattice

Let `z_i=(x_i,y_i)`, `1<=i<=m`, be distinct integer points on one
circle centered at the origin, and let `A` be the `m` by `3` matrix
whose rows are

```text
(1,x_i,y_i).
```

The rows have rank three: a line meets a circle in at most two distinct
points.  Put

```text
Lambda=ker(A^t:Z^m -> Z^3),       k=m-3,
D_I=det A_I                       (|I|=3),
g=gcd_I |D_I|,                    p_I=D_I/g.
```

The lattice `Lambda` is saturated.  Smith normal form, or primitive
exterior duality, gives

```text
det Lambda = (sum_(|I|=3) p_I^2)^(1/2).                 (1)
```

In particular this formula retains the common content of all triangle
areas.  The integer `g` is also the index in `Z^2` of the lattice
generated by all differences `z_i-z_1`.

There is a coefficient-level version of (1).  Let

```text
C=[c^(1) ... c^(k)]
```

be any `m` by `k` matrix of independent integer affine relations, and
let `q` be the index of their span in `Lambda`.  For every `k`-element
row set `J`, complementary exterior minors satisfy

```text
det C_J = +/- q p_(J^c).                                (2)
```

Thus a saturated relation basis has maximal minors exactly equal, up
to signs and complementation, to the primitive triangle determinants.
No loss depending on the coordinate size occurs in (2).

Common Gaussian scaling causes no ambiguity here.  If every point is
divided by a common `G in Z[i]`, every triangle determinant and `g` are
divided by `N(G)`, while every `p_I` and `Lambda` remain unchanged.
Primitivity is needed only to compare this invariant lattice with the
intrinsic radius after common factors have been removed.

## 2. The circle divisibility becomes a joint relation bound

Assume now that `m>=5` and the Gaussian gcd of all points is a unit, and write

```text
N=R^2,
K=binomial(m,3),
b_m=binomial(floor(m/2),3)+binomial(ceil(m/2),3).
```

The exact primitive-triangle theorem in
[affine_shape_radius_divisibility.md](affine_shape_radius_divisibility.md)
proves

```text
N^b_m divides product_(|I|=3) |p_I|.                    (3)
```

Combining (2) and (3) gives the relation-matrix form

```text
N^b_m q^K divides product_(|J|=k) |det C_J|.            (4)
```

All factors are nonzero.  Hadamard's inequality applied to every
minor, or equivalently (1) followed by AM--GM, now yields the exact
joint-height consequence

```text
product_(a=1)^k ||c^(a)||_2
    >= q det Lambda
    >= q sqrt(K) R^(2b_m/K).                            (5)
```

For coefficient sup norms, the same proof gives

```text
product_a ||c^(a)||_infinity
    >= q sqrt(K) R^(2b_m/K)/m^(k/2).                    (6)
```

This is stronger than a statement about one relation.  It holds for
every independent full-rank collection and charges a nonsaturated
collection by its exact index `q`.

On the other hand, Minkowski's second theorem followed by standard
fixed-rank basis reduction supplies a saturated basis with

```text
product_a ||c^(a)||_2 <<_m det Lambda.                  (7)
```

One explicit permissible factor in (7) is `(k+1)!/omega_k`, where
`omega_k` is the volume of the Euclidean unit ball in dimension `k`.
Indeed choose independent successive-minimum vectors `v_i` and the
saturated flags `L_i=Lambda intersect span_R(v_1,...,v_i)`. A generator
of `L_i/L_(i-1)` can be represented with its `v_i` coefficient in
`(0,1]`; subtracting integer multiples of the preceding `v_j` puts
their coefficients in `[-1/2,1/2]`. These generators form a saturated
basis with `||c^(i)||<=((i+1)/2)lambda_i`. Multiplying and applying
Minkowski's second theorem gives the stated factor. It depends on
the growing rank and must not be treated as an absolute constant.

For a minor arc of length at most `C sqrt R`, the circumradius formula
for a triangle and the two successive arc gaps give

```text
|D_I| <= C^3 sqrt(R)/8.
```

Equations (1) and (7) consequently give

```text
R^(2b_m/K)
  <<_m product_a ||c^(a)||_2
  <<_m C^3 sqrt(R)/g.                                  (8)
```

The exponent gap in (8) is

```text
gamma_m=1/2-2b_m/K
       =3/(2(m-1))  for even m,
       =3/(2m)      for odd m.                         (9)
```

After taking the geometric mean over the `m-3` relations, the gap per
relation is only `gamma_m/(m-3)=O(1/m^2)`.  But (8) is consistent: it
does not force an extra negative power of `R` in the upper bound.  Its
immediate arithmetic consequence is the exact estimate

```text
g <= (C^3/8) R^gamma_m.
```

Thus the joint relation calculation mainly controls the affine
difference-lattice index.

## 3. Why the quadratic identity adds no further linear equation

Every `c in Lambda` satisfies

```text
sum_i c_i=sum_i c_i x_i=sum_i c_i y_i=0.
```

Since `x_i^2+y_i^2=N`, one also has

```text
sum_i c_i(x_i^2+y_i^2)=N sum_i c_i=0.                  (10)
```

At the level of a fixed relation, (10) is literally the constant-column
equation again.  The equivalent anchored identity

```text
sum_i c_i |z_i-z_j|^2=0
```

follows from the same three affine equations and the common norm.
Likewise, for `c,d in Lambda`,

```text
sum_(i,l) c_i d_l |z_i-z_l|^2=0
```

is just the polarization of the affine equations.  These identities
do not provide another independent linear functional on `Lambda`.

The circle condition is nevertheless nonlinear in the entire kernel:
the three-dimensional orthogonal complement of `Lambda` must contain
integer vectors `1,x,y` with `x_i^2+y_i^2=N`.  Divisibility (3) is a
real arithmetic consequence of that nonlinear condition.  For exactly
five points, the relation rank is two and five planar points impose no
overdetermined conic condition; the five-point saturation below shows
that (3) need not reduce the covolume exponent.

## 4. Five points attain the square-root covolume

Use the five integer circle points `Z_0(n),...,Z_4(n)` from
[four_row_rational_quartic_construction.md](four_row_rational_quartic_construction.md),
with `n` tending to infinity through the even integers.  The construction
proves

```text
R_n=Theta(n^8),       arc length=O(n^4)=O(sqrt(R_n)),
```

and the five points are distinct for all sufficiently large `n`.

The checker builds the actual integer point polynomials from the eight
displayed Gaussian linear factors, without numerical root evaluation.
For all ten triples it verifies that the determinant polynomial

```text
D_I(n)=det(Z_j(n)-Z_i(n),Z_k(n)-Z_i(n))
```

has exact degree four.  It also runs the Euclidean algorithm in `Q[n]`
and verifies

```text
gcd_(|I|=3) D_I(n) = 1 in Q[n].                         (11)
```

Bezout applied to (11) shows that the gcd of the ten integer values is
bounded by one fixed nonzero integer, uniformly in `n`.  It also bounds
the norm of any common Gaussian divisor, since that norm divides every
triangle determinant.  Primitive reduction therefore leaves
`R_n=Theta(n^8)` and changes all triangle determinants only by bounded
factors.

Every determinant polynomial has degree four and at least one has a
nonzero leading coefficient, so (1) gives

```text
det Lambda_n=Theta(n^4)=Theta(sqrt(R_n)).               (12)
```

Here `Lambda_n` has rank two.  Minkowski's second theorem says that its
two successive minima satisfy

```text
lambda_1(n) lambda_2(n)=Theta(sqrt(R_n)).               (13)
```

Thus two independent integer affine relations and the exact centered
circle equation coexist at determinant saturation. An improvement must
use information not used by these covolume and joint-height inequalities,
such as additional conductor constraints or further relations among
the minors. The full affine-relation lattice determines the affine
shape; it has not been shown insufficient. Nor does this five-point
example exclude a stronger restriction on larger full-cut profiles.
